A 24-session men’s course taught at One Mission Bible Church (Hill Country lineage; Church Center channel 27610, series “Biblical Manhood - Fall 2025”). Semester 1 (01–10, Sept–Nov 2025) diagnoses wounds. ## 2. The four-mark definition, unpacked (Adam vs Jesus)

### Exact wording to memorize

**Session 14 (Tim Hawks), the line he tells them to memorize:**

> “when cut from a biblical mold, the real man is one who rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.”

**Session 14 recitation of Garrett Lewis (same four clauses):** “a real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.”

**Session 22 opening recitation (Hawks, slight article shift):** “A real man is somebody who rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects a greater reward, God's reward.”

**Session 15 recap (no “when cut…” frame):** “Rejects passivity. Accepts responsibility. Leads courageously. Expects the greater reward, God's reward.”

**Steadfast Men core line (company memorize, matching 14’s taught form):**  
**A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's. (Renamed 2026-10-01; see CANON.md.)**

Do not treat “when cut from a biblical mold” as optional decoration; it is how 14 introduces the definition. Do not treat “a greater reward” (22) as a second official text; 14 is the memorize session.

### Method (13 then 14)

**(a) Teacher claim, 13:** Core contributions are **central, not comprehensive**. Women: **nurturers of life**. Men: **bearers of destiny**. “As men go, so goes society.” Equal, not better. Mead: hardest social question is appropriate roles for men.

**(a) 13 method:** Squeeze fallen Adam and Jesus (second Adam) together. 1 Cor 15:45 — first Adam a living being, last Adam a life-giving spirit. **Herman Ridderbos** (Hawks: “Theologian Herman Ridderbos”): Adam and Christ as two figures at two creations; “all men are comprehended in them.” Spiritual destinies: Rom 5:12–19. Also two **masculine** destinies.

**First Adam (13):** (1) manhood on a natural/earthly course (money, power, now); (2) governed by instinct and reaction, not revelation; (3) draws life from others — a **taker** (stood by while Eve ate); (4) no transcendent cause — “if your vision does not exceed the borders of yourself, in time you become a very small person.”

**Second Adam (13):** (1) heavenly course (stones/bread, pinnacle, kingdoms refused); (2) based on revelation — “I come to do the will of the Father”; (3) life-giving spirit (Zacchaeus; woman taken in adultery; people comfortable he will not use them); (4) transcendent cause = the kingdom.

**14:** “Push the two examples together and four characteristics pop out.”

### Mark 1 — Rejects passivity

**(a) 14:** First Adam **fell into passivity**. Eve tempted; Adam “was with her” (Gen 3:6, already taught 09/12). He “knew what she was about to do was not the will of God, and he stood by. That plunged the human race into destruction.”

Second Adam **rejected passivity**: incarnation (“more masculinity in the manger than in Eden”); Gethsemane while disciples slept three times. “A real man rejects passivity.”

**(b)** Genesis 3 (with her; hid; blamed — 12, 14). Gethsemane narrative (no chapter number in 14).

### Mark 2 — Accepts responsibility

**(a) 14:** First Adam **disregarded** responsibility. Second Adam **accepts** it. Three responsibilities **every man has**:

1. **A will to obey.** Whose will — mine/feelings or revealed will? Gethsemane: “not my will, your will be done.” Hawks: “2.2 billion people look to Jesus as Savior…” (teacher statistic; not a cited study).
2. **A work to do.** Garden: cultivate, keep, care; also raise offspring, represent God. Jesus’ work: save the world.
3. **A woman to love.** Adam watched Eve commit “spiritual suicide.” Jesus: Eph 5, bride of Christ, gave his life for the church.

Domestic passivity: men can “run a company, raise an army, build a dam, govern a nation, invent a microchip” and still fail to lead a family, budget, romance, or a son’s character.

**(b)** Eph 5:25; Genesis garden mandate.

### Mark 3 — Leads courageously

**(a) 14, write in bold, “never apologize”:** “Men were created to lead. When men lead effectively for the right cause, society prospers, women and children thrive.”

First Adam **abandoned leadership** (hid, blamed). Second Adam **chose to lead courageously**. Three areas:

1. **Direction.** “Follow me.” Not gift-of-leadership mystique: “taking care of business, owning your deal.” Eph 5:23: husband is head as Christ is head of the church; head has eyes, ears, brain; **direction comes from revelation, not a personal empire**. Wife contributes; husband is responsible that the family has a “noble, biblical direction.”
2. **Protection.** John 10:11: good shepherd lays down his life. Garden arrest; heals Malchus; then the cross.
3. **Provision.** Fed the 5,000; fed them with his word. Eph 5:25ff: feeds and cares — provision and protection.

**(a) Greatest obstacle is feelings**, “not dad, wife, kids, government, or church.” After the Father’s blessing (Matt 3), Spirit leads Jesus into wilderness (Matt 4): bread, temple, kingdoms — three feeling-temptations. Hawks’s coward story: Spirit prompts him to open his mouth to Cindy at dinner; he stays silent; mirror: “Tim, you are a coward.”

**(b)** Eph 5:23, 5:25; John 10:11; Matt 3–4.

**Cologne illustration (14):** Danish female commentator on men in miniskirts marching in solidarity after refugee-crisis abuse — “men ought to be in the streets protecting their women.” Teacher anecdote, not a documented study in the file.

### Mark 4 — Expects the greater reward, God’s reward

**(a) 14:** First Adam **sought a greater reward** (improve his station). Second Adam **expected God’s reward**. Twofold motivation: better life now for you and those around you; eternal reward when fruit is years out (investing in a three-year-old until 33; honest path; hanging on through menopause).

Judgment seat: wood/hay/straw vs something to lay at Jesus’ feet. “Five crowns in the Bible for faithfulness” (teacher claim; not listed).

**(b)** Heb 12:1–2 (joy set before him); Heb 11:24–26 (Moses looking ahead to his reward); “2 Corinthians 2:9” as spoken — wording is “no eye has seen…” which is **1 Cor 2:9** (ASR/citation slip; flag it); 2 Tim 4:6–8 (crown of righteousness); John 10:10 (life to the full).

### Two images (14 close) — the fork

1. Adam kneeling over Abel: “This is my fault. I disobeyed God and put death in place.”
2. Youth pastor in Little Rock asks boys to define a real man; **Garrett** (Robert Lewis’s son) recites the four marks: “I learned it from my dad.”

Choose Abel-field legacy vs a son who learned manhood from his dad.

### Parallel four-mark for a real woman (19, not 14)

**(a) 19, with wife, define a real woman:** rejects worldly temptations for significance; accepts God’s design for relationships (interdependent with men, not competing); nurtures the next generation; expects God’s greater reward. Explicit parallel to the man’s four marks. From Rethinking Womanhood, taught into the men’s room so dads and wives share a definition.

### Genesis anthropology (12, with 09, 13–14, 17)

**(a/b)** Gen 1:26–27: male and female equally image (reason, will, love, moral capacity), not physical form. Dual task 1:28: fruitful **and** subdue. Gen 2 (before the fall, each detail loaded): Adam first (1 Tim 2:11–12 male elders); occupation/worship-work before Eve (2:15 work/keep); God commands the man — do not delegate the Word; naming animals = leadership; helper suitable = partner for **mission**, not cure for loneliness (Adam has God); isha/ish equal root, woman fashioned masterpiece vs man from dust; man leaves and initiates household (2:24).

Gen 3 reverses order: serpent to woman; Adam “with her” passive; God calls **the man** “Where are you?”; blame; judgment on vocation and nurture; Adam names her Eve after the fall — headship remains. Rom 5 / Gen 4 Cain: what a man does travels generations. When men surrender leadership, societies last maybe 2–3 generations (Rome, Greece, Persia — teacher claim).

Satan’s lie (12, 17): the person with responsibility is **better**. Equality discussion confuses worth with role.

Gen 3:16 is **curse, not prescription** (04, 09, 12): her desire = control (same word Gen 4); mashal = dominate. God still holds the man (Ahab “urged on by Jezebel,” 1 Kgs 21).

### Headship vs 50/50 vs boss (15, 16, 17)

Three models (15 introduces 1–2 and names 3; 17 fills 3):

2. **50/50 mythical egalitarian.** Nobody owns the whole; each mule thinks it pulls more; women ~70% more household. Murray LFPR drop. Mead: men lose a unique role.

Wife **helper** (Gen 2:18): exalted word used of the Trinity. Help fruitful/multiply and subdue. Responses: husband praise and honor (1 Pet 3:7); wife submit and respect — vote yes so he doesn’t go passive. Submit = arrange affairs under, like players with a coach. Questions about direction/money/kids **are respect**, not nagging.
