---

## Why a Texas LLC fits

| Point | Plain English |
| --- | --- |
| One owner | Texas allows a **single-member** LLC. Aaron Maberry can be the only member (100%). |
| Control | **Member-managed** means Aaron runs the company without a board of directors. |
| Selling products | An LLC is built for **commerce** — training, ebooks, apps, Stripe checkout. |
| Simpler startup | Form with Texas SOS **Form 205** ($300). No IRS Form 1023 / 1023-EZ charity application from this path. |
| Tax default | Single-member LLC is typically a **disregarded entity** for federal income tax (report on the owner’s return) unless a different election is filed later. Confirm with a tax pro after EIN. **Not tax advice.** |
| Liability wrapper | The LLC is a separate legal person for many business debts (subject to law, guarantees, and “piercing” limits). Counsel of record explains the limits. |
| Faith-based content OK | An LLC can teach faith-based material as a **business**. Being faith-based does **not** require nonprofit status. |

---

## Why 501(c)(3) nonprofit is a poor fit here

| Point | Plain English |
| --- | --- |
| No private owner | A 501(c)(3) has **no equity owner**. Assets are locked to charitable purposes; Aaron would not “own” the nonprofit the way he owns an LLC. |
| Board / governance | Charities usually need a **board**, conflict rules, and nonprofit bylaws — heavier than a solo LLC. |
| Purpose lock | Activities must stay inside **exempt purposes**. Selling training/ebooks can be allowed in some charity models, but it brings UBIT, commerciality, and private-benefit scrutiny. |
| IRS application | Seeking 501(c)(3) recognition means a formal IRS application (often Form 1023 or 1023-EZ), waiting, and ongoing charity compliance — **not** what Aaron asked for. |
| Donations vs sales | Nonprofit path is built around **charitable contributions** and public-benefit rules. Aaron’s plan is **selling** products to customers. |
| Name confusion | “Ministry-sounding” brand ≠ legal charity. Faith content can live inside an LLC. |

Aaron’s direction (14 September 2026): **LLC / not nonprofit.** Do not draft Form 202 (nonprofit corporation), Form 1023, or charity bylaws in this packet.
