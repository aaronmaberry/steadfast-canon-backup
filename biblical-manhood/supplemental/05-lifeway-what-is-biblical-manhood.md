## One-line thesis (source’s, not Steadfast’s)

Leaders should help men pursue biblical manhood through five handles — salvation, identity, service, sacrifice, and provision — because many church guys want it but struggle to connect faith to daily life.

---

## Key claims (quoted / closely paraphrased)

Anecdote: author sat with five college guys (three sophomores, two rising freshmen) from church student ministry. After hugs and prayers he drove home reflecting on 3 John 4 (CSB): “I have no greater joy than this: to hear that my children are walking in truth.” He calls them spiritual children without diminishing parents/peers/other leaders. “Their kingdom potential reminds me why my ministry matters and challenges me to continue encouraging rising generations of men to pursue biblical manhood.”

### Reaching guys

“If you’re a minister whose heart longs to see a generation of men making a difference for God’s kingdom, you’re not alone. Many leaders share that heartbeat. More importantly, God shares that heartbeat. He created every individual—including every guy—to find identity and purpose in Him.”

Barriers (quoted): “Men are busy, and men are distracted. Often, they assume they have it all together or refuse to admit their struggles. In their minds, men’s ministry is fine for ‘other guys,’ but it’s not a priority to them.”

**Lifeway Research, State of Discipleship study** (as the article reports it — do not expand beyond this page):

- “The research examines the unique ways men and women live out their faith.”
- “Men are more likely than women to have mentors who hold them accountable and disciple them. They also are more confident in sharing their faith and more intentional about praying for opportunities to talk about Jesus.”
- “Yet, men struggle to see a connection between the Bible and their daily lives. They are less likely than women to attend a Bible study class or to embrace established prayer practices. Guys are less likely to recognize God in day-to-day events and are less likely to trust His plan above their own solutions.”
- “In a nutshell, men need help seeing faith as relevant to their lives each day. They need mentors who can answer their ‘So what?’ questions before they commit fully to biblical manhood. That’s where we come in as leaders.”

Pull quote: “Men need help seeing faith as relevant to their lives each day. They need mentors who can answer their ‘So what?’ questions before they commit fully to biblical manhood.” — Bob Bunn

### Important ideas in biblical manhood

“Deep down, I think most guys in the church want to follow Jesus with all their hearts. I believe they want to pursue biblical manhood in a way that challenges them as individuals, moves them toward spiritual leadership for their families, and teaches them how to be Jesus’ hands and feet through the local church. However, as Jesus said of His disciples in Gethsemane, ‘The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak’ (Matthew 26:41, CSB).”

“As leaders, we can help men identify what biblical manhood looks like in practical terms. We can point them toward foundational elements they can live out in meaningful ways. We can offer handles for defining and demonstrating godly masculinity.”

“Here are five areas leaders can highlight…”

### 1. Salvation

“Genuine manhood took a serious hit in Eden. The corrupting influence of sin damaged God’s perfect design. However, He has provided a way to reclaim biblical manhood.” “That process begins with acknowledging sin and accepting Jesus’ sacrifice on the cross. We need to be honest and let men know that without a personal relationship with God through Christ, all talk of biblical manhood falls flat.”

### 2. Identity

“Before the fall in Genesis 3, God created all people in His image (Genesis 2:26-27).” [Page cites “Genesis 2:26-27”; standard imago Dei text is Genesis 1:26–27. Do not “correct” in notes; record what the page printed.] “Thankfully, that image was only distorted by sin, not destroyed.” “We can remind men they still carry the image of God and His image defines what true, biblical masculinity is all about. They don’t need to chase standards set by the world. God has given them everything they need to find purpose and satisfaction in Him.”

### 3. Service

“Men like to get things done. But we can’t afford to buy into the cultural myth that manliness is all about outward activity. External busyness misses the key element of internal transformation.” Ephesians 2:8–9 (saved by grace) and 2:10 (created to serve). “We can guide men to serve with proper motives, focusing on ways to bring glory to God. For believers, service is a form of worship (Romans 12:1-2), a way to connect with God by meeting others’ needs.”

### 4. Sacrifice

“The Bible says Jesus revealed the fullness of God in flesh and bone (Colossians 2:9). That means He also provides the perfect example of biblical manhood.” “Jesus didn’t define His mission through power, popularity, or wealth. He defined it through sacrifice. He came to serve and to give His life as a ransom for a world wallowing in sin (Mark 10:45).” “We are never more loving or more like our heavenly Father than when we give ourselves away for the benefit of others (John 3:16; 15:13).”

### 5. Provision

“Paul never pulled his punches. So, when he defined biblical manhood, he included our responsibility to meet the needs of those who depend on us. In fact, he said anyone shirking that responsibility was ‘worse than an unbeliever’ (1 Timothy 5:8, CSB).” “Whether it’s protecting a wife and kids or supporting an aging parent, godly men do the right thing—financially, emotionally, physically, and spiritually. It all matters for men because it all matters to God.”

### Look at yourself

Leaders (pastor, staff, lay) can influence men on their path. “While you’re at it, though, don’t forget to care for yourself. Take time on a regular basis to filter your life through these five milestones of biblical manhood, along with others God reveals to you from His Word. The best thing you can do for your ‘children’ is to maintain your own connection to God as you walk alongside them.”
