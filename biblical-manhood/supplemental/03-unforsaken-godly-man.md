## One-line thesis (source’s, not Steadfast’s)

A godly man is not defined by dominance, wealth, or image; under Christ he learns courageous obedience, holiness, self-control, sacrificial love, truthful work, and care for those entrusted to him.

## Key claims by section (quoted / closely paraphrased)

### Godly manhood begins with God

“People use the word manhood for many different things. Some mean physical strength, sexual success, money, independence, influence, or the ability to dominate a room. Scripture begins somewhere deeper. A godly man is a man who belongs to God and learns to obey Him.”

“This is good news because it frees men from both pride and despair. A loud man is not automatically strong, and a quiet man is not automatically weak. A man may be unmarried, childless, elderly, disabled, poor, unnoticed, or in a season of loss and still walk faithfully with God. Biblical manhood is not a competition for status. It is the settled direction of a redeemed life: fearing the Lord, turning from sin, telling the truth, serving others, and standing in faith when obedience is costly.”

1 Corinthians 16:13–14 quoted: “Watch, stand fast in the faith, be brave, be strong. Let all that you do be done with love.” Gloss: “Paul’s charge holds courage and love together. Neither cancels the other. Christian courage is not aggression dressed up as conviction, and Christian love is not surrender to error or evil.”

### Created in God’s image and given a calling

Genesis 1:26–28: image, male and female, fill and dominion. “Men do not create their own meaning. They receive life, identity, bodies, abilities, limits, and responsibilities from God.”

Genesis 2:15: Adam placed in the garden “to tend and keep it.” “The Hebrew verbs *abad* and *shamar* carry the ideas of serving or working and guarding or keeping. **Adam was not made for passivity.** He was given a place to cultivate, a command to obey, and a holy trust to guard.”

Word exam on page: “In Genesis 2:15, these verbs describe Adam’s work and keeping of the garden. The text presents responsible service and watchful obedience, not a mandate for harsh rule over people.”

“He does not confuse control with care. A gardener does not crush a living thing in order to prove that it is his; he serves the life God has entrusted to him.”

### Adam’s sin calls men to personal responsibility

Genesis 3: both sinned. “Scripture does not allow men to use Adam’s failure as a weapon against women, nor does it let Adam hide behind Eve.” Genesis 3:12 quoted (blame-shift). Romans 5: Adam as representative. “a godly man does not excuse his sin by pointing to his circumstances, family, temptations, or another person’s failures.” “Responsible leadership begins there.” “This is not weakness. It is the courage to stand truthfully before the God who sees the heart.”

### Courage is strength submitted to God’s Word

Joshua 1:6–9: “be strong and courageous” anchored in obeying/meditating on the Book of the Law. “Biblical courage does not begin with a man’s natural temperament. It grows as he trusts the presence and commands of God.”

1 Kings 2:2–3 quoted: “Be strong, therefore, and prove yourself a man. And keep the charge of the LORD your God.” “To prove oneself a man in this passage is not to win an argument, show contempt, or demand attention. David joins strength to keeping God’s ways…”

Temptations listed: refuse dishonest profit, admit a wrong, end a sinful relationship, seek help for an addiction, protect a child, reject crude conversation, stand against false teaching. “The question is not whether he feels impressive. The question is whether he obeys the Lord.”

### Integrity and self-control

Job 31:1 (covenant with eyes). Psalm 24 (clean hands, pure heart). Proverbs 20:7 (righteous man walks in integrity). “Sexual purity is part of that wholeness, but it is not the whole of it.” Truth-telling, restrained anger, kept commitments, appetite for money and praise, dignity toward people who cannot benefit him. Titus 2:6–8: young men sober-minded, pattern of good works. “He does not need to pretend that pain is absent. He learns not to be ruled by it.”

### Strength protects rather than preys

Psalm 82:3–4; Proverbs 31:8–9. “A man who uses his greater strength, position, income, or authority to exploit another person is not demonstrating biblical manhood. He is answering the pattern of sin.” Practical: refuse to laugh at cruelty; no intimidation for sex, silencing a spouse, frightening children, dominating a weaker person; intervene; call civil authorities when a crime is present; seek help rather than reckless heroics.

“Jesus never used His authority to feed vanity… His strength was holy. **Men who follow Him must reject both passivity toward evil and the counterfeit strength of aggression.**”

### Marriage and fatherhood are callings of sacrificial love

“Not every man is a husband or father, and a man’s standing with God is not earned by either role.” Yet clear instruction to those who are. Ephesians 5:25 quoted. “The pattern is sacrifice. A husband must never turn headship into permission for selfishness, coercion, contempt, or danger.”

1 Peter 3:7: dwell with understanding, honor, heirs together. “If there is abuse, threats, or immediate danger, safety and lawful intervention come first; Scripture never requires someone to remain accessible to violence.”

Ephesians 6:4: fathers, do not provoke; bring up in the Lord. “Children need consistency, not perfection.”

### Work, provision, and faithful presence

1 Thessalonians 4:11–12 (work with own hands, quiet lives). 1 Timothy 5:8: “In its context, Paul is addressing care within the family, especially the neglect of widows. The verse rebukes settled, hard-hearted refusal to care for those one is able and obligated to help. **It should not be used to crush a man who is unemployed, disabled, abandoned, underpaid, or facing a crisis beyond his control.** Provision includes money when money is needed, but it also includes presence, planning, truthful work, prayer, emotional steadiness, and practical care.”

### Men need the church / church needs faithful men

“Christian manhood is not a solitary project.” “The local church’s offices and authoritative teaching leadership belong to biblically qualified men, but that qualification is first a character test.” 1 Timothy 3; Titus 1. 2 Timothy 2:22. Isolation hides temptation. Quiet service (including foot-washing pattern) is not beneath a man.

### Jesus is the perfect pattern and Savior

Adam disobeyed; David sinned; Peter denied. “The answer is not a new performance system that tells men to save themselves by becoming impressive. The answer is Jesus Christ…” Philippians 2:5–8. “Salvation is in His blood, received by grace through faith, not earned through masculine achievement.” “The true measure of a godly man is therefore not whether he never fails. It is whether, when confronted by Christ, he turns from sin and follows his King.”

**Biblical conclusion (quoted):** “Be a Man Under the Lordship of Christ.” “He does not use strength to control others or hide behind weakness to avoid responsibility.”

Reflection questions (4) on image/control/money; private sin; using strength to serve; humility of Christ in work/speech/relationships/church.
