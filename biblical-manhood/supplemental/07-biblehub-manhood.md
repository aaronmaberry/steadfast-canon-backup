## One-line thesis (source’s encyclopedia block)

Biblical “manhood” is the state/quality of being a man: maturity, leadership, strength, and living by God’s commandments, illustrated by Adam and other biblical men.

**Important lexical warning (from the page itself, not Steadfast):** English “manhood” on this hub also means (a) adult maturity, (b) virility/offspring, (c) Christ’s human nature (“Godhead and Manhood”). Those are **not** the 24’s four-mark definition. Do not collapse them.

## A. Topical encyclopedia block (the essay portion)

**Definition and Overview (quoted):** “Manhood, in the biblical context, refers to the state or quality of being a man, encompassing the roles, responsibilities, and characteristics expected of men according to Scripture. It involves maturity, leadership, strength, and a commitment to living according to God's commandments. The Bible provides numerous examples and teachings that outline the expectations and virtues associated with manhood.”

Numbered teachings as printed:

1. **Adam as the First Man.** Genesis 2:7 quoted (formed from dust; living being). “Adam's role in the Garden of Eden, including his responsibilities to work and care for it (Genesis 2:15), sets a precedent for the stewardship and responsibility expected of men.”

2. **Leadership and Responsibility.** Ephesians 5:23 quoted (husband head of the wife as Christ is head of the church). “This leadership is characterized by love, sacrifice, and service, mirroring Christ's relationship with the Church.”

3. **Strength and Courage.** Joshua 1:9 quoted. “This call to strength is not merely physical but also moral and spiritual, requiring men to stand firm in their faith and convictions.”

4. **Wisdom and Understanding.** Proverbs 4:7 quoted. “Men are encouraged to seek wisdom through the fear of the Lord and to apply it in their lives, leading with discernment and integrity.”

5. **Role Models of Manhood.** Named: Abraham, Moses, David, Paul. “Each of these men demonstrated faith, obedience, and leadership in their unique contexts. For instance, David, despite his flaws, is described as a man after God's own heart (1 Samuel 13:14), exemplifying repentance and a deep relationship with God.”

6. **Fatherhood.** Ephesians 6:4 quoted. “This involves nurturing, teaching, and modeling godly behavior.”

7. **Work and Provision.** 1 Timothy 5:8 quoted. “Men are called to work diligently and provide for their families, reflecting God's provision for His creation.”

8. **Maturity and Growth.** 1 Corinthians 13:11 quoted (“When I became a man, I set aside childish ways”). “This maturity involves growing in faith, understanding, and character, moving beyond childish behaviors to embrace the responsibilities of adulthood.”

**Conclusion (quoted):** “The biblical concept of manhood is multifaceted, encompassing leadership, strength, wisdom, and responsibility. It calls men to live in accordance with God's will, serving as leaders in their families and communities, and growing in spiritual maturity. Through the examples of biblical figures and direct teachings, Scripture provides a comprehensive guide to understanding and living out the principles of manhood.”
