## One-line thesis (source’s, not Steadfast’s)

Biblical manhood is humility before God, control of appetites, protection, provision, and Christlike (not tyrannical) family leadership, approached from Scripture rather than culture or opinion.

## Key claims (quoted from the page)

Opening: “In today's postmodern world, discussions about gender can be contentious and emotionally charged, especially when considering the religious perspective. While the concept of biblical manhood may challenge our cultural norms and personal beliefs, it is important to approach it from a biblical, rather than opinion-driven, standpoint.”

### The Image of God

“According to the Bible, both men and women are created in the image of God (Genesis 1:26-27). This means that every individual is valuable and deserving of respect. However, it is important to recognize that men and women are biologically, psychologically, and emotionally distinct. These differences are not a negative aspect but rather something that God deemed ‘very good’ in His creation (Genesis 1:31). Therefore, biblical manhood should include a godly view of women, respecting their worth and acknowledging the meaningful differences between the sexes.”

Sidebar prompts: “Why This Matters…” / “Think About It: How can we honor and celebrate the differences between men and women while still recognizing their equal worth in the eyes of God?”

### Five Principles of Biblical Manhood

“Biblical manhood can be summarized by five key principles that men are expected to adhere to.”

1. **Humility before God:** “A godly man recognizes his imperfections and acknowledges his need for God's guidance. He humbly accepts Christ for salvation and depends on God in all aspects of life.”
2. **Control of appetites:** “Men are called to exercise self-control over their desires and urges. They rely on God's strength to overcome temptations rather than twisting Scripture to suit their preferences.”
3. **Protection of family:** “Men have a responsibility to protect their families, both physically and spiritually. This may involve fighting to safeguard their loved ones and ensuring their well-being.”
4. **Provision for family:** “Men are called to be the primary providers for their families. While specific circumstances may alter who contributes financially, men should take on the responsibility of providing for their own households.”
5. **Leadership of family:** “Men are expected to lead their families in a manner that reflects the relationship between Christ and the church. This leadership is characterized by sacrificial love, service, and selflessness.”

### The Role of Leadership

“Leadership is a vital aspect of biblical manhood. However, it is important to understand that biblical leadership is not about asserting dominance or enforcing authority. Instead, it is modeled after Christ's example of sacrificial love and service (Ephesians 5:25-32). Men are called to lead their families and communities with humility and selflessness.”

### Submission to God's Will

“Biblical manhood requires submission to God's will. This means acknowledging our imperfections, accepting Christ's salvation, and depending on God's guidance (Micah 6:8; Romans 3:23; 2 Peter 3:9). It also involves studying and understanding God's Word to align our actions and choices with His commands (Matthew 6:33; Hebrews 4:12).”

### Challenging Cultural Norms

“Living out biblical manhood may run contrary to cultural norms and expectations. It is essential to recognize that biblical manhood does not endorse tyranny or weak-kneed passivity, nor does it impose specific ideals of womanhood on wives. Instead, it emphasizes humility, self-control, protection, provision, and leadership. Each person is accountable to God for their own life, and men are accountable for spiritual leadership within their families.”

### Conclusion

“Understanding biblical manhood requires a holistic perspective that encompasses humility before God, control of appetites, protection and provision for family, and leadership that reflects Christ's example.” “By embracing biblical manhood, men can lead lives that honor God, serve others selflessly, and contribute positively to society.”
