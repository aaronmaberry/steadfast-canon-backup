# BIBLICAL MANHOOD: MAKING FRIENDS, FINDING A MENTOR.

 A REVIEW:

“Social, emotional, and spiritual blindspots and/or deficits caused by a lack of healthy male comradeship that results over time in foolish actions, serious setbacks, and a short-sighted masculinity.”

## I.  Ten Marks of a Mentor. 

- He clearly has something you personally need.

- He chooses to cultivate a relationship with you.

- He is willing to take a chance on you.

- He is respected by other men.

- He has a network of resources that you can draw upon.

- He is consulted by others naturally.

- In your relationship, he both talks and listens.

- He is consistent in his lifestyle.

- He is able to diagnose your real needs.

- He is concerned with your interests.

(from Howard Hendrick's book, As Iron Sharpens Iron: Building Character in a Mentoring Relationship, Moody Press, 1995.)

## 

## II. The Impact of a Mentor.

- Mentoring closes the loop on the lifecycle.

- Mentoring connects older and younger men in a symbiotic relationship.

- Mentoring allows men to teach other men from experience.

- Mentoring gives older men a significant purpose in the later half of life.

- Mentoring adds missing satisfaction to life.

 

## II. What Do You Do if You Need But Lack a Mentor.

- 
Pray for wisdom and guidance.

- (If you spot a man that you need), Go ask him.

- (If you are part of a group), Overwhelm him with numbers.

 

 

 

## QUESTIONS FOR RELECTION OR SMALL GROUP DISCUSSION

- On a scale of 1-10 (“1” being poor, “10” being rich), are you poor when it comes to male friends with whom you can share you life... rich ... or somewhere in between? Where would you rate yourself and why?

- What other men are investing in your life right now? How?

- Who is caring for your soul? Who is thinking about you and cheering for your life other than you?

- Are you willing to approach an older man to mentor you? Who are you mentoring?
