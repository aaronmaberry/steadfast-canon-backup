# 21. If There Is No Wife Yet

The lamp had sat on that box so long the cardboard held a dent. KITCHEN still faced the bed. Inside were two plates bought for a table that did not exist yet, a skillet with the sticker still on the metal, and a cloth he was saving. He said he was waiting on his life to start. I asked him what he called the room he woke up in.

He will tell you the real life starts the day a woman says yes.

The next chapters are about a wife. If you do not have one, this page is yours first. Then keep reading. You can build from every page that follows.

## You are not in a waiting room

A church can like a single man and still preach past him. The sermon says "your wife" and never looks left. After a while he believes manhood waits on the far side of a yes.

The Book will not carry that. Jesus never married, and no life has been fuller. Paul stayed single. John the Baptist stayed single. Some of the most useful men in the Book never had a wife or a child.

Watch the order in the garden. Adam received nearly the whole charge while he was still alone. Image. Blessing. Ground. Work. Word. The naming of what walked past. He was already a man under a full assignment before a bride stood beside him. She came because God named one thing that was not good. She did not arrive to switch him on.

A man with no wife yet is already in his life. The ground has his name on it. If a wife comes, she walks into work he has already begun.

## The man who unpacked the box

He cut the tape on a Saturday. No date had called. An older brother had told him to quit camping in his own rooms. He hung the skillet. He set the plates out and cooked for men who sat down. He kept a budget. He started picking up a boy from church whose father was gone, same afternoon each week. He still wanted a wife. He stopped treating her like the button that turns a man on.

He told me the week he quit waiting sat close to the week he became someone a good woman would want.

Not yet can be a true sentence. Say it if that is where you are. Then open the box. Hope and work can share the apartment.

## Three seats

Single is three men, and they are not having the same year.

One has never married. He may ache for a wife, sit half in, or have quit looking. With nobody real in the picture, he can invent a woman no living woman can match. If you are young, these years are already the work. Rooms, money you can explain, a brother, a clean conscience. That is most of a husband's job. The wife is the part still missing.

The second seat belongs to the man whose marriage ended. You are grieving what you lost, or what you broke, or both, across two addresses, while a church decides which row is yours. What I am saying to the first man is yours too.

The third man buried his wife. You lived in the house, and then the other pillow went still. People go quiet because they have no sentence. I have one. Stay in the work. Stay with us.

## What a single man builds

Work the ground and keep it. This is the shape when your name is the only one on the lease.

Make a home a man actually inhabits. Clean enough for a brother. Food you cooked. Bills paid when they are due. Too many single men keep a campsite under a roof. A wife, if she comes, will read those rooms before she reads a speech.

Set a table. Feeding people did not arrive with a license. Have the younger men over. Feed the older man who eats alone. Leave the place better than you found it.

Get men around you. You need them more than a husband does, because a quiet apartment will not notice the week you vanish. Find one man ahead of you. Do not give the drift a year.

Give the Lord the hours this stretch actually holds.

:::margin
1 Corinthians 7:35 (NIV)
"I am saying this for your own good, not to restrict you, but that you may live in a right way in undivided devotion to the Lord."
:::

A house with three little kids cannot spend an evening the way you can. Burn those hours on waiting and you have spent the one advantage this stretch gives you.

Father somebody. Boys in your church are short a man, and you have the afternoon. Throw. A tire. A prayer in the parking lot. Paul called Timothy his true son. The floor plan changes. The work does not.

Keep yourself clean. The bed is a covenant, and the keeping starts before there is a bed to share. Your eyes and your hands either store something up for that covenant or spend it early.

:::margin
1 Timothy 5:1-2 (NIV)
"Do not rebuke an older man harshly, but exhort him as if he were your father. Treat younger men as brothers, older women as mothers, and younger women as sisters, with absolute purity."
:::

As sisters, with absolute purity. That covers every woman outside a covenant with you, including the one you hope might enter one.

## If you want a wife

Most of you do. The want is good.

:::margin
Proverbs 18:22 (NIV)
"He who finds a wife finds what is good and receives favor from the LORD."
:::

Find is a moving verb. Look with honor at what will still be true when the face changes. Charm fools a room. Beauty moves on. Praise a woman who fears the Lord. Attraction is real. Weigh it last, after you know she fears God, tells the truth, and is kind with nobody watching.

Ask in the open. A named evening. A reason she can hear. A year of hovering is a stall, and so is a string of texts that never becomes a plan. She can tell pursuit from a boy in the hall.

Then decide. A week is too fast for a life. Years with no decision spend hers. If you know her character and you are still in the doorway, the true name for that is passive. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Move toward the covenant, or release her in a way she can respect.

Let an older man meet her. How she is with her father and her friends is a true preview.

Keep your hands off her. Sex before the covenant clouds your judgment and trains you both to treat the vow as optional. The men I know who crossed that line wish they had not. The men who waited are glad.

Marry when you can carry the weight. Ready is a feeling, and it will not lead. You need work, money you can account for, a home, brothers, a kept body, and a Father you talk to. If those are in place, and she fears God, and the older men say go, then go. Paul said it is better to marry than to burn. If you can carry it, pick it up.

## If you're in the divorced seat

That whole list is yours. Home, table, men, devotion, fathering across two addresses, a clean body. Single again means single.

Grief sits in the seat too, whether you filed or you fought the filing. Something died. Bury it. Men skip the funeral and frame a new house on soft ground. Weep. Tell a brother what was lost.

Then say your part. Healing can start when "I did this" stands alone, her piece left off the end.

Children come before any new woman. Dating hard while they are still finding their feet has the order upside down.

Stay in the church even if it is clumsy with you. Do not sprint at another marriage. Sit with your pastor. The man who remarries in the first raw year to stop the pain is about to hand that pain to someone else.

## If she died

It is not good for a man to be alone, and you know that in a way the younger men cannot. You had the remedy, and it is gone.

Grieve out loud, for as long as it takes. Keep a hand on what is left. Trees. Grandkids. Wednesday coffee. One brother. The Word, and a seat on Sunday. Drift likes a widower, and it usually shows up called rest.

Marrying again sits with you, with God, with your grown children, and with her if there is a her. Some men do. Some never do, and the last years are still full. Your life did not close on the day hers did.

## Why you should stay

Read what follows as a man framing the house before anyone moves in. Knowing a wife. A laid-down life. What she needs. The bed. A daughter. A son. Married men are often repairing. You can build the wall straight. Some child at your church has no father. You can be a man he watches.

Most of you will marry. Until then the ground is yours, and the reward does not wait on a second name at the mailbox.

Get up. The house needs a builder.

## Before you turn

Close this without the hum of being half a man. The fullest one who ever lived never married. Your ground needs you today. If divorce or death put you here, the grief is real and the place is still yours. May the Father who said it is not good for a man to be alone give you brothers, and handle a bride in His time, while you cut the tape and work.
