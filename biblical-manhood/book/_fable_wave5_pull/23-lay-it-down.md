# Lay It Down

He was right, and I want to start there because it matters. They were standing in an inch of water on a bathroom floor. She had taken the cheap quote. He had said the job would fail. The job had failed. He still had the estimate he'd wanted, folded in his pocket, and he could feel the sentence loading that would end it and prove the point.

He put the paper in the trash.

"I'm sorry I got loud," he said. "We'll figure it out."

His wife looked at him as though she didn't recognize him, and then she started crying. It took him a while to understand that she was crying because in years of marriage he had never once laid down a fight he was winning.

That's what this is about. You've studied her. Now you lay your life down for her, and not once, at an altar in a nice suit, but every day, in a ruined room, when you're right.

## The harder job

Here's the sentence men love to quote from Ephesians 5, and the one they quote less.

The one they love is "wives, submit." Men underline that one. Paul wrote it and it's Scripture. But the next thing he writes is addressed to us, and it's longer, and it's heavier, and I've never once heard a man underline it in the same tone of voice.

:::margin
Ephesians 5:25 (NIV)
"Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her"
:::

Gave himself up for her, just as Christ did. That is more than "love her a lot." Love her the way Jesus loved the people He died for: all the way to death, on purpose, while they were still failing Him. Men want the authority of Ephesians 5 without the cross of Ephesians 5. In Scripture, headship is a cross. The man in front is the one who takes the arrows. His whole sacrifice aims at her flourishing. That isn't a man who gets his way. That's a man who has decided that his way is her good.

:::margin
Philippians 2:5-7 (NIV)
"In your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus: Who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage; rather, he made himself nothing by taking the very nature of a servant, being made in human likeness."
:::

He had every right. He had the high ground in a way no husband ever has. And He didn't use it. He set it down and put on a towel. That's the mindset. That's what "just as Christ" means when you're standing in water with the proof in your pocket.

## What a husband lays down

"Lay down your life" sounds like something you'd do once, dramatically. It's done mostly in pieces, on ordinary days.

He lays down his right to be right. You'll be right a great deal. Most of the time it's worth nothing compared with what winning costs her. Not by pretending you're wrong. By deciding the point isn't the point. "Let's figure it out together." The second half of that sentence is the whole marriage.

He lays down his preferences. Where to eat, what to watch, how the dishes go, how the weekend goes. Cheerfully, without a scoreboard. A husband who fights for every one is telling her his comfort outranks her.

He lays down his phone. Face down when you walk in. Not in your hand while she's talking. I've watched men look at a screen through an entire conversation and not know they'd done it. She knows.

He lays down his Saturday, or whatever his day is. The golf, the shop, the game. None of those is a sin. When the house needs you, the day loses, and it loses without you sulking.

He lays down his first minutes after work. Those belong to her and not to the recliner.

He lays down his side of the story. In front of the kids, your parents, your friends, take her side. A wife whose husband defends her in public can survive a fight in private. A wife whose husband makes her the punch line learns she's alone in the room.

He lays down his mother's vote. When your mother and your wife disagree, your wife wins, and your mother hears you say so. You left. Act like it.

He lays down his sleep. When she's sick, when a child is up in the night, you get up. Guarding your sleep while she runs on almost nothing is giving her up for yourself.

He lays down his anger. Not by stuffing it. Take it to the truck and to God and to your brother, and bring her the thing that was under it.

He lays down his money. It's the house's money. She has full standing, full knowledge, full say. A husband who keeps her in the dark isn't leading. He's ruling.

And he lays down his plans. If you built the next years in your own head without her in the room, lay them down and make new ones with her in it.

## What laying down isn't

There's a counterfeit, and it's the first mark inverted, and I've watched it ruin marriages from the other direction.

Laying down your life is not abdicating. A husband who stops leading, stops deciding, stops going first, and calls it "letting her have her way" isn't giving himself up. He's disappearing. She'll feel it as abandonment even while she gets everything she asks for. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Christ gave Himself up and never once stopped leading. He led from the towel and from the cross. Laying down is a strong man choosing her good over his comfort, not a weak man choosing quiet over responsibility.

Laying down is not becoming a doormat. Jesus said hard things to the people He loved. A husband who never tells his wife the truth is laying down his job. Love speaks. Gently, at the right time, after it has listened, but it speaks.

And laying down is not self-erasure. She married a man with a spine and a direction. If you dissolve until there's nobody home, you've robbed her of a husband. Lay down your preferences, your rights, your comfort, your pride. Don't lay down yourself.

Here's the test. After you've laid something down, is she stronger or weaker? If she's more secure, more herself, more radiant, it's the real thing. If she's carrying more because you checked out and called it humility, it's the counterfeit.

## This stretch

If there's a newborn in the house, take a shift, not "I'll help when she asks." Kill the silent ledger of who slept and who got the shower. Do one unpaid thing on purpose and don't announce it. Lay down the bedroom until she's ready. Lay down the gym and the guys for this year, not forever. Love the wife who's here, not only the one you married. Nobody's clapping. Your Father sees the three o'clock.

If you've been married a long time, lay down the retirement you planned alone. Ask her what she wants the years to be. Then make them that. Lay down the remote. Lay down some of the control, because she can hold it, and she's waited. When she gets sick, laying down your life stops being a metaphor. I have watched a man bathe his wife every morning for years since the illness took most of her. She doesn't always know him. He knows her. He meant "in sickness." He just didn't know what it would look like. There is a Father who sees it every morning.

If you are in the divorced seat, you may be thinking about what you wouldn't lay down when you had the chance. Hear it, grieve it, confess your part, and don't stay there. Lay down your right to win with your children's mother. You're going to be right sometimes, and it doesn't matter. Your kids need two parents who can be in a room together. It has to be you. Lay down the narrative where it was all her. Let God hold it.

If you're single, practice anyway. A man who has never lost a preference will not suddenly become good at it on a wedding night. Lay down where you eat. Lay down a Saturday for an older man who needs help. Small deaths, daily, on purpose. That's the training ground.

## The reward for the man who loses

Jesus said something about laying down your life that sounds like a loss and is a promise.

:::margin
Matthew 16:25 (NIV)
"For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me will find it."
:::

Whoever loses their life will find it. You lay down your right to be right and you find a wife who trusts you with the truth. You lay down your Saturday and you find a marriage that's still alive later. You lay down your sleep and you find a woman who remembers that you got up.

The Father sees all of it. The laid-down fight nobody witnessed, the night shift, the bath she may not remember: none of it is wasted. One day the Man who laid down everything is going to look at a husband who laid down a little and say well done. You did it the way I did.

The man on the wet floor was right that night. He laid it down anyway. His wife told me later that it was the night she started trusting him with her fears, because a man who'd give up a fight he was winning was a man who wouldn't use what she told him against her.

You'll be right this week, more than once. Lay it down.

## Before you turn

Whoever loses his life will find it. None of it is wasted. May the Father who watched His Son lay down everything give you the strength to lay down one thing this week, and may you find that the house changes when you do.
