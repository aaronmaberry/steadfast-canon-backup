# If There Is No Wife Yet

The box still said KITCHEN. He'd been using it as a nightstand long enough that the cardboard had taken the shape of the lamp. Inside were two plates he bought for later, a skillet with the sticker on the pan, and a cloth he was saving for a reason. He said he was waiting for his life to start. I asked him where he thought he was sleeping.

That's the waiting room. It doesn't look like a lobby. It looks like a place a man refuses to inhabit because he thinks the real house begins at an altar.

I'm about to write about the house. If you're a man without a wife, don't skip it. This page is for you first. Then stay for what comes soon.

## You are not in a waiting room

Most churches treat a single man, whether they intend to or not, as a man-in-waiting. Half a man, whose real life starts when a woman says yes. The singles group is a holding pen. The sermons assume a wife. He absorbs the message that he's in the lobby of his own life.

Scripture doesn't talk like that. Jesus was single, and the fullest man who ever lived never married. Paul was single. John the Baptist was single. Some of the most useful men in the Bible had no wife and no children and served God's purpose in their generation with everything they had.

Go back to the first shape. Nearly the whole assignment was handed to Adam while he was alone: the image, the blessing, the place, the work, the word, the naming. He was a full man with a full assignment before there was a bride. She was God's remedy for a specific not-good. She wasn't what made him a man.

If there is no wife yet, you are not waiting to start. You're a man with a place that needs working today, and a wife, if she comes, will walk into a house you've already built.

## The man who unpacked the box

The man with the KITCHEN box unpacked it. Not because a date appeared. Because an older brother told him to stop treating the apartment like a tent. He hung the skillet. He set the two plates and had men over. He kept a budget. He took a boy whose father had left. He still wanted a wife. He stopped making her the on-switch.

The day he stopped waiting for a wife, he told me later, was about the day he became a man a wife would want.

Not yet is honest. Not yet is not never, and it is not nothing.

## Three seats

I said "single man" as if it were one man. It's three.

The man who has never married may want a wife badly, or be ambivalent, or have given up. You've never had the thing everyone assumes you're waiting for, so it's easy to turn her into something no real woman is. If you're young, the danger is treating the next stretch as a rehearsal. A man who lives like a man is already doing most of what a husband does, minus the wife.

The divorced seat is different. You aren't waiting for something you've never had. You're grieving something you lost, or something you broke, or both, while you manage two addresses and a church that isn't sure what to do with you. Everything I say to the first man is true of you too.

The third man is single again because his wife died. You built the house and lived in it, and then the other side of the bed went quiet. People mostly don't know what to say. I do. Stay.

## What a single man builds

Work it and keep it. Here's what that looks like without a wife.

He builds a house. Not a mortgage. A home. Rooms where a man lives, not where a boy crashes. Clean enough for company. Food you cooked. Bills paid on the day. Too many single men treat this place like a tent. It isn't. If a wife comes, she'll read this house correctly.

He sets a table. Hospitality is not a married gift. Have the young guys over. Feed the older man who eats alone. Make a place more than it was.

He builds a company. You need brothers more than a married man does. Nobody at home notices when you go quiet. Get a man ahead of you. Don't let the drift get a year's head start.

He builds a devotion.

:::margin
1 Corinthians 7:35 (NIV)
"I am saying this for your own good, not to restrict you, but that you may live in a right way in undivided devotion to the Lord."
:::

Undivided devotion isn't a consolation prize. A married man with three kids can't have it in the same measure. You have hours and an evening with nobody needing you. Spend them waiting and you've thrown away the one advantage this stretch gives you.

He builds a fatherhood. There are fatherless boys in your church. You have time. You can coach, show up, teach a boy how to throw or change a tire or pray. Paul called Timothy his true son. Fatherhood simply has a different shape.

And he builds a purity. The bed is a covenant, and the covenant starts before the bed. What you look at and where you put your hands is either building that covenant or spending it before she arrives.

:::margin
1 Timothy 5:1-2 (NIV)
"Do not rebuke an older man harshly, but exhort him as if he were your father. Treat younger men as brothers, older women as mothers, and younger women as sisters, with absolute purity."
:::

As sisters, with absolute purity. That's the standard for every woman who isn't your wife, including the one who might become your wife.

## If you want a wife

Most of you do, and it's a good thing to want.

:::margin
Proverbs 18:22 (NIV)
"He who finds a wife finds what is good and receives favor from the LORD."
:::

Finds is an active verb. He looks, with intention and with honor. Look at the right thing first. Charm is deceptive and beauty is fleeting, but a woman who fears the Lord is to be praised. What you're tempted to look at first will change. What won't is whether she fears God, tells the truth, and is kind when nobody's watching. Attraction matters. Weigh it last.

Go first, and go clearly. Ask her out plainly, for a specific evening, with your intention visible. Don't hang out for a year. Don't text for months and never ask. Women can tell a man pursuing from a boy hovering.

Don't linger. Once you're dating, a man decides. Not in a week, but not in years. If you know her character and you're still not deciding, you aren't being careful. You're being passive, and you're spending her years. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Move toward the covenant or release her honorably.

Bring older men in. Let a man ahead of you meet her. Borrow their eyes. How she is with her father and her friends is how she'll be with you later.

Keep your hands off her. Sex before the covenant clouds your judgment and teaches both of you that the covenant isn't necessary for the thing it was made to protect. Every man I know who did it wishes he hadn't. Every man I know who didn't is glad.

Marry when you can carry the weight, not when you feel ready. What you need is a place that's being worked: a job, handled money, a home, brothers, a held purity, a Father you talk to. If you have that, and she fears God, and the older men say go, then go. Paul said it is better to marry than to burn. If you can carry it, carry it.

## If you're in the divorced seat

Everything above is true for you. House, table, company, devotion, fatherhood across two addresses, purity. Single again is single. No asterisk.

You have grief, whether you wanted the divorce or fought it. Something died. It needs a funeral. Men skip this and rebuild on sand. Cry. Tell a brother. Be honest about what was lost.

You have your part. You can't heal until you've said "I did this" about the piece that's yours without attaching her piece to it.

You have children, probably, and they come before any new woman. A father in the divorced seat who's dating hard while his kids are adjusting has his priorities upside down.

Stay in the church that may not know what to do with you. Don't rush remarriage. Sit with your pastor. The man who remarries in the first raw year to stop hurting is about to hurt somebody else.

## If she died

It isn't good for a man to be alone, and you know that now in a way the younger men can't. You had the remedy and it's gone.

Grieve out loud, as long as it takes. Keep working what's left: the trees, the grandkids, the midweek coffee. Find one brother. Stay in the Word and in the church. The drift comes for widowers disguised as rest.

Whether you marry again is between you and God and your grown children and the woman, if there is one. Some men do. Some don't, and their last years are full anyway. What you can't do is decide your life ended when hers did.

## Why you should stay

Soon I'll write about knowing a wife, laying your life down, what she needs, the bed, daughters, and sons. Read every word as a man building the house before she moves in. Married men are mostly repairing. You get to build it right. Read it as a man who can father. There are children in your church who don't have one.

Not yet is honest. Most of you will marry. Not yet isn't never, and it isn't nothing. It's a place with your name on it, and a reward that doesn't depend on whether anybody ever moves in.

Get up. There's a house to build.

## Before you turn

If you're a man without a wife, close this without the hum of being half a man. You aren't one. The fullest man who ever lived never married. Your place needs working today. If you're single again by divorce or death, the grief is real and the place is still yours. May the Father who said it isn't good for a man to be alone give you brothers, and handle the question of a bride in His time, while you unpack the box and build.
