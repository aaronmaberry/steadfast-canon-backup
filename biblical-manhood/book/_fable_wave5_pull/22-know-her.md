# Know Her

He can tell you the first morning the wrens came back last spring. He has it in a notebook by the window: the date, the weather, which box they chose. He can tell a chickadee from a nuthatch by one note. He has been keeping the book since he was a boy.

He could not tell me, when I asked, what his wife was afraid of this year.

They have eaten at the same table for years. He knows where she'll sit. He thought that was the same as knowing her.

It isn't. The first thing a man owes his wife, before he lays down his life for her and before he works out what she needs, is to actually know who he's married to.

## Live with her according to knowledge

Peter wrote one sentence to husbands, and it has carried more freight than almost anything else in the New Testament about marriage.

:::margin
1 Peter 3:7 (NIV)
"Husbands, in the same way be considerate as you live with your wives, and treat them with respect as the weaker partner and as heirs with you of the gracious gift of life, so that nothing will hinder your prayers."
:::

Be considerate as you live with your wives. The older translations put it closer to the original: live with them according to knowledge. Dwell with her knowing her. Study her. Understand her. Make her a subject you're an expert in.

Look at the last clause, which men skip. So that nothing will hinder your prayers. A man who lives next to his wife without understanding her has a problem with God and not only with her. His prayers hit a ceiling. The way you know your wife is connected to the way you're heard by your Father.

Men study what they care about. A man who loves a sky can read a cloud. A man who loves a trade knows the sound that means trouble. We're built to learn the things we love, deeply, over years.

Most men stopped studying their wives somewhere in the early years. They learned enough to get along, then they stopped, and they've been running on that first data ever since. She has changed completely. They don't know it. She knows they don't.

Proximity isn't knowledge. It's true of brothers. It's truer of wives. You can share a bed with a woman for decades and not know the woman in it. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Knowing her is that work.

## What there is to know

"Know your wife" is the kind of line men nod at and do nothing with.

Know her story. How was she fathered? What did she learn about men from watching hers? What happened to her before you that she has never fully told you? Ask her to tell it again, slowly. You'll learn why she flinches at the thing you thought was nothing.

Know her fears, and not the ones you assume. Ask what she's most afraid of right now, then be quiet a long time. Given room, most wives will say something that surprises him. It's rarely money. It's often being alone, or the children, or becoming her mother, or whether he actually sees her.

Know what fills her and what drains her. Some women come alive in a room full of people and some are wrung out by it. If you don't know which she is, you'll keep planning days that exhaust her.

Know how she receives love. Men give love the way they'd want to get it, usually by doing: fixing, providing, handling. Some wives receive that as love. Some need words, or time with no agenda, or touch that isn't about the bedroom, or the thing done without being asked. Ask her when she has felt most loved by you. Do more of that.

Know her body without embarrassment. Her cycle, so you can carry more in the week she has less. Pregnancy. The stretch after a birth. The changes later, which most husbands sail past without a word. She lives in that body every hour. If you don't know what's happening in it, you don't know her.

Know her walk with God. How she prays. What she's wrestling with. What verse she's standing on. A man who knows that can pray for her with precision instead of generalities.

Know her ordinary day, hour by hour. Midafternoon with children. The hardest stretch at work. Men who don't know that day make comments that land like insults.

Know her people: her friends, her mother, what those relationships cost her. And know her dreams. What did she give up? What has she stopped saying out loud because nobody asked? A husband who honors those things, and looks for ways to hand some of them back, is a husband she'll trust with anything.

:::margin
Proverbs 20:5 (NIV)
"The purposes of a person's heart are deep waters, but one who has insight draws them out."
:::

Deep waters. She isn't going to hand you this on the surface. Drawing out takes insight, and insight takes time, and time takes deciding she's worth studying.

## How a man learns his wife

Ask, and then wait. Most men ask a question and start forming their answer before she's finished. Count to ten after she stops talking. She usually hasn't stopped. The real thing comes after the pause.

:::margin
James 1:19 (NIV)
"My dear brothers and sisters, take note of this: Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to become angry,"
:::

Listen without fixing. When she tells you something hard your whole body will want to solve it, and you mustn't, not at first. Say, "That sounds heavy. Tell me more." A woman who gets fixed every time she speaks stops speaking. A woman who gets heard tells you everything.

Watch her. What she does when she's tired. What makes her laugh when she isn't trying to. What she reaches for when she's sad. Watch her the way you'd watch a bird you haven't named yet.

Write it down. Her sizes. Her friends' names. The thing she wanted in passing months ago. The date her father died. What she said her biggest fear was when you finally asked. She'll notice when you remember what she said only once.

Ask her what you get wrong. "What do I misunderstand about you?" Then don't argue. Take notes. You'll learn more in that conversation than in a year of assuming.

And bring the questions home each week, same night. What happened. What's heavy. What's next. Answer them yourself.

## The man who opened a clean page

The man with the bird book asked her. Not a clever question. What are you afraid of this year. Then he waited past the first answer. She talked. He wrote. He opened a clean page and put her name at the top.

He's still filling it. It felt, he said, like meeting her again in the house where they had been standing next to each other the whole time.

Proximity was never the study. The study is the page you keep open.

## This stretch

If there's a newborn in the house, the woman you married is not the woman across from you right now. She hasn't slept a full night in months. She may be grieving her old self and feeling guilty for the grieving. Treat her like a woman under a load you can't see. Ask "what do you need in the next hour?" because the bigger question is too large at three in the morning. Notice when she hasn't eaten, hasn't showered, hasn't been out, hasn't laughed. Her not wanting to be touched is about exhaustion, not about you, and not permanent.

Watch for the darkness that isn't only tiredness. It has a name, it's common, and it's treatable, and the husband is usually the first to notice. If she isn't herself for weeks, if she isn't sleeping even when the baby does, if she says things about herself that frighten you, take her to a doctor and go with her. That's a man who knows his wife and keeps her.

A wife in that stretch said the sentence that would change a house: I don't need you to fix it. I need you to know how hard this is.

If you've been married a long time, the danger is that you know a version of her from years back and stopped updating. Her body has changed. Her fears have changed. Her griefs have accumulated. Ask what she wants with the years that are left. Expect different answers.

If you're in the middle years, you're likely running on old data. The question you're afraid to ask is often the one that would change the house.

If you are in the divorced seat, you may know now what you didn't know then. If you're honest that you didn't know her, that's a piece of "I did this," and it belongs in your confession and your grief. You still have to know her, differently. If you're raising children across two houses, know their mother well enough to co-parent civilly. What she's afraid of for the kids. What sets her off and what calms it down. If God gives you another wife someday, start knowing her on day one, with the page open.

If you're single, study women the right way, as sisters. Ask a question and wait for the whole answer. Practice on your mother. Practice on your sister. Learn to listen without fixing or flirting. A man who can hear women as sisters will know how to hear a wife.

## How He knows His bride

Christ knows His church specifically: every fear, every wound, every hidden thing. He knows us completely and isn't put off by what He knows, and He keeps knowing us, drawing out the deep waters.

:::margin
John 10:14 (NIV)
"I am the good shepherd; I know my sheep and my sheep know me"
:::

I know my sheep. That's the model. Not a man who married a woman once and has been guessing ever since, but a man who knows her, and keeps knowing her, and lets her know that she's known.

## Before you turn

You may feel behind. Most men are. Being behind isn't the same as being too late. She isn't keeping a ledger of the years you didn't. She's waiting to see whether you will ask. May the Father who knows His sheep by name give you the patience to draw out the deep waters, one question at a time, and may you find that the woman you've been standing next to is more than you knew.
