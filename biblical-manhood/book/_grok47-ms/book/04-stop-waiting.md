# 4. Stop Waiting

The third stair from the bottom has a voice. Put weight on it and it talks. Everybody in that house learned the skip before they learned their own spelling. The man who owns the stair could silence it before breakfast. Two long screws, a bead of glue, a drill already hanging on a peg in the garage. Ask him and he will tell you he is going to get to it. He means it. Meaning it is the whole of the repair.

I am less worried about the wood than about the lesson. A man can know a thing is broken, know the fix, and step around it until the stepping-around feels like the floor. The stair is the small version. The large versions live in the same house. A talk with his wife he has parked under "when things calm down." A brother uncalled since the funeral. A number on a statement he will not open. A son who went quiet about the time his voice changed. A church he means to walk back into. He is going to get to all of it. The years are the getting-to.

The first mark says a man rejects passivity. The word makes most men picture a couch and closed blinds. That is not the man I lose sleep over. The man I lose sleep over is up before the sun. He is useful, tired, and excellent at nearly everything except the four things that would change his house. On those he waits. He has waited so long the waiting stopped feeling like a decision.

## What it wears

Passivity will not show up dressed as laziness. Laziness you can see, and shame at least gets a man off the step. So it borrows better clothes.

It borrows patience. I am giving it time. Sometimes that is wisdom. More often time is doing the job the man refuses, and time has no judgment and loves nobody in the house.

It borrows peace. I do not want to start something. So nothing starts. The peace he is keeping is a kitchen where nobody says a sentence that costs anything.

It borrows humility. Who am I to bring it up? You are her husband. You are the boy's father. You are the one standing there. That is who.

It borrows preparation. Another book. Another podcast. Another long talk about the conversation instead of the conversation. A man can become a scholar of the thing he has never done.

It borrows faith. I am waiting on the Lord. There is a real waiting, and there is a counterfeit that signs His name to a folded hands. The difference matters, and I will get to it.

And it borrows busyness, the best costume in the closet. A man on the road before dawn, home after dark, coaching Saturday, stacking chairs Sunday, does not look passive to anyone, least of all himself. His wife could name what he is avoiding in one breath. Busy is not the opposite of passive. Often busy is the hiding place.

The first man hid too. After the fruit, when God came walking in the cool of the day, Adam was in the trees.

:::margin
Genesis 3:9 (NIV)
"But the LORD God called to the man, 'Where are you?'"
:::

God was not lost. The question was not a location. It meant, where is the man I set here to work this ground and keep it, who stood right there while it went wrong and said nothing? That question still walks through houses. It will pass your stair tonight with your name on it.

## Why we wait

No man has ever handed me the word passive as his reason. He has better words, and they sound reasonable in a driveway with the engine off.

He did not want to make it worse. That is fear of a fight, the most common reason there is. He figures the quiet he has beats the noise he would make. He does not figure that quiet has a price, and that the price compounds while nobody writes a check.

He was not sure he was right. That is fear of being wrong, wearing a responsible face. Standing still is a way of moving. It has results. He will answer for them the same way he would answer for a bad move.

Nobody asked him. This one usually starts in a boyhood where initiative got you corrected, where the safe way to be in a room was to wait for orders. Some men carry that into the office, the church, and their own marriage, still waiting to be picked. If you are young and this is you, hear it plain. Nobody issues a grown man a permit to lead his own life. The ground under your boots is yours to work.

It will sort itself out. It will not. I have never seen a field weed itself.

:::margin
Proverbs 24:30-31 (NIV)
"I went past the field of a sluggard, past the vineyard of someone who has no sense; thorns had come up everywhere, the ground was covered with weeds, and the stone wall was in ruins."
:::

Look at what the sluggard refused to do. He did not burn the field. He did not shove the wall over. He did nothing, long enough, and thorns handled the rest, because thorns do not ask permission. Neither does distance in a marriage, or interest on a debt, or the silence of a boy who has decided his father is not coming.

Under every spoken reason sits one he will not say. He is comfortable. Comfort does not argue. It holds the room at a pleasant temperature and waits for you to agree.

## The real kind of waiting

Scripture does tell a man to wait on the Lord. I will not steal that from you. Read the posture it asks for.

:::margin
Psalm 27:14 (NIV)
"Wait for the LORD; be strong and take heart and wait for the LORD."
:::

Be strong and take heart. Those are not recliner words. That is a man on his feet, doing every part that is his while he waits on the one part that is not. Abraham waited most of twenty-five years for a promised son, and in those years he moved a household to a country he had never seen, built altars, armed his own men to go get his nephew, and stood eye to eye with kings. Waiting on God never meant sitting down on the work that already had his name on it.

Here is the test. When you say you are waiting on God, look at your hands. If they are doing the next right thing you already know, you are waiting on God. If they are folded until He makes it obvious, you are waiting on comfort and putting His name on the invoice.

:::margin
Luke 9:51 (NIV)
"As the time approached for him to be taken up to heaven, Jesus resolutely set out for Jerusalem."
:::

He knew what the city held. He did not drift into it. He set his face and went.

## The same stair in every house

In a single man's apartment the stair is named "when I'm married," as if a wedding threw a switch. The man at the altar will be the man this year is building. In a house with a newborn the stair is named "when things settle," and they will not. Ten minutes at the counter after the baby is down, phones face down, is the whole season. For the man whose marriage ended, the stair is the call he will make once the anger cools. Anger cools after a man acts like a man in spite of it, not before. For the man who handed off the trucks or turned in the keys, the stair is a phone that never rings because nobody is dialing. The work is wherever he walks in and asks. A young man hides behind "once I," and the sentence never finishes. A father in the middle years waits for a quiet son to come around alone. Different step. Same skip.

## The first screw

Name the thing in words a calendar could hold. Not "my marriage." The money talk I have owed her since spring. Not "my son." Half an hour in the truck, radio off, and the question I am afraid to ask. A fog cannot be done. A named thing can.

Then ask what you are actually waiting on. Be honest. It is almost never more information. It is a feeling. Ready. Brave. Sure. None of those arrive first. They show up after the move.

Find the smallest action that is still an action. Pulling up his number is not one. The call is. Sitting with the drill across your knees is not one. The first screw is.

Do it before noon. Afternoons are where a man's resolve goes to die. By dark the couch has a lawyer.

Expect the move to feel wrong. Your nerves have been tuned to waiting for years. The first right thing will register as a mistake for about a day. That feeling is not a verdict. It is a scar stretching.

## What He sees

:::margin
James 4:17 (NIV)
"If anyone, then, knows the good they ought to do and doesn't do it, it is sin for them."
:::

That verse closes the back door. Doing nothing is not a neutral lot. God writes it in the same book as the things we do. Turn the page, though. The Father who sees what is done in secret also sees a man come down the hall with a drill before the house is awake, because he is done stepping over it. He sees the number dialed. He sees the question asked. There is a reward for that. His reward. We will sit with it when we talk about what lasts. Today, fix the step. Then make the call.

## Before you turn the page

Nobody sat you down and taught you the skip. You picked it up the way you picked up your walk, from the men around you, without a lesson. You can put it down the same way, one deliberate move at a time. The first one is the hardest by a mile. May the God who went looking for Adam in the trees find you on your feet.
