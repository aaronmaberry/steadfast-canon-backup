# 6. Go First

After midnight the wind took an oak down. Morning showed the roots in the air and the crown in a yard that was not its own. Power gone. Window to window, the block did its looking. Tree. Other windows. A silent calculation about the man who would move.

The calculation produced no one. The street cared. Caring and moving had come apart. Three thoughts ran in every house. The tree needs a man. The man should know the cut. The man is probably someone else. The faces stayed in the windows. The tree stayed in the street.

One door broke the pattern. Sleeping clothes under a coat. Gloves. A saw. No gathering on the lawn to discuss it. He went to the trunk and hunted the place to cut. Seconds piled up. He had the street to himself.

Door number two came next. Fifteen minutes and the count was six men, a second saw, a truck nosed to the curb, coffee off a camp stove for the workers. What the block had been waiting on was a door.

I have been the face in the glass. I have assigned the saw to a more qualified man and left the trunk where it lay. Courageous leading is the third mark. Men hear leadership and supply a picture of the table's head, a title, the voice that fills a room. The picture is the first open door. One man moves during the looking. He takes the exposed stretch so the man behind him has a destination.

## Watch me

Three years with the twelve, and the lesson about leading that Jesus made unmistakable involved a towel at His waist. Foot washing was the bottom task in the house. No one in the upper room was volunteering. Each man hoped the quiet would skip him. Jesus rose, filled the basin, and went to His knees.

:::margin
John 13:14-15 (NIV)
"Now that I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also should wash one another's feet. I have set you an example that you should do as I have done for you."
:::

Example, in the flesh. A memorandum on service would have been easier. He took the unwanted task before any of them did, and the instruction after was simple. Now you. Gideon's version, above an enemy camp in the dark, with three hundred men, fit in a single order.

:::margin
Judges 7:17 (NIV)
"'Watch me,' he told them. 'Follow my lead. When I get to the edge of the camp, do exactly as I do.'"
:::

Two words do the leading. Watch me. They also explain the shortage of volunteers. A table between a man and the room is cover. First position has no cover. Cold water reports to him before it reports to anyone. A wrong saw is a public error. Zero followers leaves him in the road, plan in hand, company absent. He goes out with the order and without a guarantee. His place is first.

## Where first happens

Your street may never lose an oak. The actual sites are smaller.

Apologies. Clean, and early. I am sorry I raised my voice. End it there. Hook a second clause on, the one about how she gets, and the apology becomes a brief. I have set that hook and labeled the result peace. She hears the brief arriving before your sentence closes.

Prayer where you eat. Food lands, and in plenty of houses a silence follows that everybody understands. Eyes go to one chair. Men who have prayed for strangers, myself among them, have stayed silent in front of their own last name.

Rubble, named. Night, alone, Nehemiah walked what was left of the wall, and only then did he speak. He told the crowd the fact they could all see and none of them had said.

:::margin
Nehemiah 2:17 (NIV)
"Then I said to them, 'You see the trouble we are in: Jerusalem lies in ruins, and its gates have been burned with fire. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, and we will no longer be in disgrace.'"
:::

Church has been dark for us since spring. The truck payment is behind. Our son is drinking, and the house has agreed to notice nothing. Give rubble a name and you can build. Leave it unnamed and it becomes the view.

Turning around. Cold spells in a marriage are two people parked, each waiting on the other's feet. Move yours. Greater guilt may sit on the other side of the room. You are still the man. Kitchens look short until a husband tries to cross one. I have stood on my side and let the distance win.

Direction, given by going. Nehemiah refused the porch. Come, he said, and his feet went to the wall with the word. Come, plus movement, is leading. A Sunday decision about church looks like a man in a running car. Shoes are still being found behind him.

Every season has its own handle. The requirement stays put. Single, he is the voice that says Sunday is real and then supplies the ride. New to fatherhood, he prays over a crib where he can hear his own voice, and he repeats it the following night. Divorced, he offers his children's mother the straightest apology he has ever managed, and he leaves the outcome with her. A silent teenager gets one question on a drive. An older man crosses the lobby. The younger man will not be the one who starts that walk.

## What we're afraid of

Four fears keep the handle untouched. I have fed the set.

Foolishness. The prayer stumbles. The apology sounds memorized. He reaches the tree and cannot find the cut. Refusal. He turns in the kitchen and she stays where she is. He says he has noticed, son, and receives a shrug plus a shut door. Error. So he chooses no direction, and he calls the blank a humble posture. Cost. The bill is real. Morning hours leave with the first man on the street. A winning argument leaves with the first apology. The man who goes first is the one who pays.

Fear gets an honest treatment in Scripture. Movement is never postponed until the fear leaves.

:::margin
2 Timothy 1:7 (NIV)
"For the Spirit God gave us does not make us timid, but gives us power, love and self-discipline."
:::

Afraid, and planted, is timid. Afraid, and in motion, is courageous. Heart loud in his chest, walking at the oak, that man is the definition. The noise in the chest is courage as it actually feels.

## You're never actually first

The street changes when you hear who left earlier. Moses was transferring a nation to Joshua. A river stood in front of them, and walled cities behind the river. Moses left the lecture on nerve unsaid. He identified the One already on the road.

:::margin
Deuteronomy 31:8 (NIV)
"The LORD himself goes before you and will be with you; he will never leave you nor forsake you. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged."
:::

Ahead of you. Your door opens onto a door He has opened. The hard line at your table enters a room He already occupies. Second is your real number. Cost remains. Exposure remains. He is already in the street, and He stays.

## How to go first

Bring a first line. Drafting the full conversation in advance is how men stay shut. I know the stall. Try three openers and stop. I have been wrong about something. Let me pray. Son, I have noticed. Past that line, the people in the room carry a lot of the weight.

Practice where the audience is small. Church leadership from a man who is mute at supper has the sizes backward. Truck, kitchen, a thread among three friends. Large rooms are those rooms added together.

Questions lead. Ask what this year of marriage to you has actually been like. Ask what worry has gone unspoken. You open a door and you keep your hand on it. No wife in the house yet, and the ask still works. What has friendship with me been like this year? What load have I failed to ask about?

Count on eight seconds. Your door, then a gap, then any other door. Alone in the gap is normal. Four seconds is where men retreat. The gap is the interior sensation of being first. Remain.

Pick a plain day. Fallen oaks are uncommon. Supper is tonight.

## The pioneer

:::margin
Hebrews 12:2 (NIV)
"fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith. For the joy set before him he endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God."
:::

Pioneer names the man who entered the worst stretch ahead of the rest and left a path. Eight seconds would have been a mercy. Friends slept. Friends ran. The writer gives the method. Joy was placed in front of Him. He moved with that in view. Reward is why He went first.

Third mark, held up by the fourth. Nerve is a short fuel. Somewhere near the second door it empties. Certainty about a reward does not empty. You move first because the One ahead of you can be trusted to make the cost worth paying.

## Before you turn the page

Bravest on the block is the wrong goal. Open the door with the hammering still in your chest. Even the goal leans too hard on you. The street already has Him in it. Your delay has outlasted the tree's. May the opening line be given to you, the gap after it held for you, and the next door made audible.
