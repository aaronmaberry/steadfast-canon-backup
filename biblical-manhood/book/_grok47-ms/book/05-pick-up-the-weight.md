# 5. Pick Up the Weight

One wall in your house is not available for removal. Paint matches the rest. Nail holes match too, from the years the school pictures hung there. Joists overhead dead-end on that wall. Beds, dressers, and sleeping children on the upper floor are passengers on it, thought about or not. A contractor needs a minute. Knock, glance up, read the run of the floor, and he will name the wall that carries the second floor.

You can open the kitchen only by putting new support under the load. Leave nothing under it and the upstairs floor sags until, a year later, a bedroom door will not latch.

A family, a job, and a life each have weight that has to land somewhere. Fairness does not move it.

I have argued with the location. In my own kitchen I have slid the load onto another person with a tidy sentence and called the sentence truth-telling. Accepting responsibility is the second mark. A man finds the portion that belongs to him and says, that lands on me. He can know he did not cause every pound. He can know the arrangement is unfair. He says the sentence because the wall is him. Move aside and the upstairs learns it fast.

## Not mine

After the fall, the first words a man offered God became the pattern. God asks for an account. Adam's hand comes up. The woman You gave me, she passed me the fruit. One breath, two accusations. His wife takes one. God takes the other. He keeps none. I have spoken that grammar in more than one room. Cain inherited it intact. God asks where Abel is, and the answer comes back as a question.

:::margin
Genesis 4:9 (NIV)
"Then the LORD said to Cain, 'Where is your brother Abel?' 'I don't know,' he replied. 'Am I my brother's keeper?'"
:::

The answer is yes, and it covers the field. It covers the man who says the department is someone else's, the kid is someone else's, she agreed to this, nobody briefed me.

Both men were protecting one asset. A story in which the weight had a different owner. That story is blame. Shift the weight and the afternoon feels easier.

Good men get stuck here by mixing up fault and responsibility. Fault hunts the cause. Responsibility asks for the current owner. Zero fault can attach to a layoff, a diagnosis, a daughter's anxiety, or a father who left only debts. His name can still be on every one. Court is where fairness belongs. A wall cannot use it.

## Five things with your name on them

Yours is a short list. The pounds that are not yours can wait. These five do not negotiate.

Life, the one with your name already on the birth record. Body, money, word, habits, soul. Doctor visits, card balances, and an open Bible will not be handled by a volunteer. Adulthood moved those out of your mother's grip years back.

The house, if you have one you share. Marriage has a temperature, and you answer for whether it warms or drifts. Children in the house are being formed, and the forming is yours. Teachers help. A church helps. The voice the boy grows into will still echo whoever actually lived there.

The work on your bench today. Do it at the standard you would use with the owner at your elbow. One is.

The people attached to you. A brother in trouble. A man at church whose wife walked out this month. A boy on the block whose father is a rumor. Am I my brother's keeper? Yes. The post is keeper. One Man holds the name Savior.

Then the weight men drop first, which is also the heaviest. Your own wrong. What was done to you is a different page. This page is the dinner-table sentence you cannot unsay. The years your body was home and you were not. The fact she has not been told. Set that down and the pounds do not shrink. They move onto everyone else in the house, and they grow.

Seasons swap the pounds around. They do not swap the name. Alone in an apartment, a man is mostly his own assignment, and the assignment outweighs his estimate, since an empty room never reports the hour he quits. A new husband discovers that someone else's peace now depends on his back. A newborn means he is also carrying a wife who cannot sleep. A newly licensed son means the father is carrying his own mouth. Divorce papers do not stop a man from being the floor of two addresses. Grown children leave him mostly with the fifth weight, the errors from when they were small. He can still lift that one. The tool is a phone.

## Two words for weight

Paul sets two commands near each other, and they sound like a collision.

:::margin
Galatians 6:2 (NIV)
"Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ."
:::

:::margin
Galatians 6:5 (NIV)
"for each one should carry their own load."
:::

Shoulder theirs. Shoulder yours. He can say both because the words underneath are different. One names a crush, past what a single back can bear. The other names issued gear, a pack, cargo with your number on it, meant for your shoulders alone. When Jesus called His burden light, He used the second word.

Tell the two apart and you are practicing the mark. Your pack goes on with no announcement. A brother flattened by the crush gets you underneath him, invitation or not. The mark includes both moves.

## The weight that isn't yours

Pride will load one arm until the man staggers, because any empty hand feels like surrender. Her happiness, in full. Every decision the children make. The congregation's condition. The state of his soul before God. He calls the overload devotion.

Moses reached the stagger. Desert, a nation angry about the menu, and a sentence aimed at God that pride usually blocks.

:::margin
Numbers 11:14 (NIV)
"I cannot carry all these people by myself; the burden is too heavy for me."
:::

Seventy elders arrived, assigned to share the burden so the whole of it would leave his spine. God left certain weights off your list on purpose. Grabbing them is pride. Joy in your wife was given to her and to God. Choices in your grown son were given to him. Haul what carries your name. For the remainder, ask where the seventy are.

## Providing without disappearing

One verse about providing is famous, and a paycheck is all many men were told it meant.

:::margin
1 Timothy 5:8 (NIV)
"Anyone who does not provide for their relatives, and especially for their own household, has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever."
:::

Household is the word doing the work. Everyone the roof covers. Money is a thin slice of what a father owes a child. Funding can flow into a house the funder rarely enters. Lessons can be bought for years while a folding chair at his daughter's recital stays empty, and the empty chair cancels the lessons.

Food on the table is the bottom layer, and only that layer. Being there is part of what provision means. Trade presence away to purchase a larger paycheck and you have bought a costly absence.

The man under a household owes it a floor with no give. Shoving from a teenager is a test of whether the floor stays. A hard sentence from a wife lands on a man she expects will hold his ground while she says it. You can feel weak and still stay. When the volume rises, stay again. Overhead, the beam is ignored on purpose. Feet cross the floor with no experiment. That ignorance is the beam's success.

## How to pick it up

Face the person involved and say, that's mine. Leave the polished substitute alone, the one about seeing how she might feel. That's mine.

The period is the end of the line. Add but, and you have started a second speech. Everyone in the room hears the turn. Adam's but arrived before his answer ended. Speak the claim. Shut the rest down.

Results are the thing you own. Rain through a roof is a result. Hours you intended to spend on the roof are a story. Tell your children you tried to make dinner and you have described a plan. They will describe a chair.

Pack first, then the crush. Order matters. Helping in every direction while your own pack sits in the driveway is a way of hiding, and home is where the hiding shows.

Then ask. Name the weight absent from God's list for you, and return it to Him. Two or three men can take the portion that is flattening you. Responsibility stays. Giving away the crush may be the most responsible hour you spend this year.

## The one who took it up

Carrying, in the words this chapter keeps using, was said about another man before it was said about us.

:::margin
Isaiah 53:4 (NIV)
"Surely he took up our pain and bore our suffering, yet we considered him punished by God, stricken by him, and afflicted."
:::

Took up. Bore. The weight was unearned. The wreck was not His work. He never reached for the sentence about the woman You gave me. That's mine, spoken from the cross, came from the single man who could have refused it and been accurate.

Joy sat in front of Him while He carried. Inside the second mark, the fourth one is already alive. The Father sees the beam the upstairs never mentions, and the Father is the payer. That is the reason you shoulder it.

## Before you turn the page

A settling weight, as you read, is the sensation of being the wall. It belongs on a structure made for it. God sizes a man to the issued load and posts seventy beyond it. Hold the corner with your name. When the corner gets heavy, keep your hand off the pointing. May He plant you under your share, keep you honest about the share He gave away, and teach your house that the floor can be trusted.
