# 31. Feelings Are the Dash, Not the Wheel

I was old enough to be of use the summer Vernon set me on his tractor and rode the fender. The wheel waited until I read him the gauges. Oil. Water. Amps. "Those tell you the truth about the tractor," he said. "You look at them every row." Then the furrow from the pass before. "That tells you where to go. Keep your right tire in it." I watched the cluster, lost the furrow, and bent his field. No raised voice. A knuckle on the water gauge, and the furrow pointed at again.

On a long pull the engine ran hot. The row looked straight, so I held it. He caught the sound. "You read that gauge?" I had. "Then why'd you keep going?" Nothing in me answered. A gauge tells a truth about the machine and nothing about where the field ends. Ignore the needle and the engine cooks. Steer by the needle and the pasture wanders under you. One eye on the dash. Both hands on the wheel. Know which is which.

Feelings are that dash. They report. The wheel belongs somewhere else.

## Two ways men wreck

Most men miss it in one of two directions.

One man covers the cluster. Feelings are somebody else's subject. Fine, he says, where another man would say angry. Tired, where he is sad. Busy, where he is lonely. Refusing the names does not kill the gauges. Anger is how they exit. Appetite is how they go down. Absence is how they leave the house. Claim no feelings and they are still under the hood, running the engine.

The other man gives the needle the wheel. "I don't feel in love anymore." "I don't feel like God is here." Honest reading. A ditch of a turn. Wives get left because a feeling moved. Churches get left because a feeling left. He arrives at nothing, and the honesty hides the ditch.

Scripture takes a third road. Read every gauge aloud. Say it to God. Say it to a brother. Then steer by what does not swing when the weather swings.

## What the dash is telling you

Nobody handed most men the names.

Fear means something matters, and you doubt you can protect it. Ask what you are afraid to lose. Anger means a wrong, or a wrong you only think is there. Look underneath. Fear is usually sitting there, or shame, with a hard face on. Sadness means something was lost. Grieve the thing. This is the gauge men skip, and the skip turns into drift. Loneliness means alone is not good. God said it first, which makes this the truest needle on the panel. Hear it as go find a brother. Do not hear it as a ruling on your worth. Numbness means the engine ran hot until the gauge burned out. Call that peace and you have misnamed a warning light that died. Shame says "I am a wrong thing." Lay it on the plumb line. This one lies more than the others. Guilt still works. It names a deed. Joy means you have come near the work you were made for. Notice the work you were doing when it showed.

The Psalms are hard men reading gauges aloud to God. David told God things most men will not tell a wife.

:::margin
Psalm 42:5 (NIV)
"Why, my soul, are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me? Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise him, my Savior and my God."
:::

Why are you downcast. The reading. Put your hope in God. The wheel, turned toward God and off the needle. That move has a manual. It is the Psalms.

## What the dash is not telling you

Forgiveness was settled at a cross. "I don't feel forgiven" is a number on a gauge. The number does not reopen the case.

Marriage is not told the truth by a mood. The love gauge sits on empty in every long marriage for a stretch. Follow that needle and you are divorced. Hold the covenant with both hands and years later the needle can lift, and you are holding a marriage no feeling had the power to build.

Orders do not come from moods. "I feel like God wants me to" hands a gauge the authority of God, and that is a dangerous habit in a Christian man's mouth. Test it by the Word. Test it by what your brothers see. Test it by the four marks, and watch whether it is only a door out.

:::margin
Proverbs 28:26 (NIV)
"Those who trust in themselves are fools, but those who walk in wisdom are kept safe."
:::

Sleep, food, and weather move every needle. Decide your life off a late-night reading and the weather owns you. Read it again after sleep, and again with a brother in the room.

## What the wheel is

Four things hold still.

The Word holds. Your feelings say one thing and the Bible says another. The Bible is describing reality. The feelings are describing you.

The covenant holds. You promised it before God and before witnesses. A promise exists for the day the feeling will not hold.

Brothers hold. From the other seat they can read a dash you cannot read from yours.

The four marks hold. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Unsure of the next move, do whichever of the four is in front of your boots.

Back to the tractor. The water gauge climbs on a long pull. Leave it and the engine cooks. There is no direction called "toward a gauge." Read it. Name it. Running hot. Come out of the row, let the heat fall, check the water, and return to the furrow already started. The driving changed. The destination did not.

## Elijah under the broom bush

Men die of the heavy end of this dash. Some of you are past sad. The dark has had you for a while. Elijah had just watched fire come down from heaven. One day later he walked into the desert alone and asked to die.

:::margin
1 Kings 19:4 (NIV)
"while he himself went a day's journey into the desert. He came to a broom bush, sat down under it and prayed that he might die. 'I have had enough, LORD,' he said. 'Take my life; I am no better than my ancestors.'"
:::

He read it aloud. The reading was that he wanted to die. God sent no scolding and no speech about trying harder. Bread and water came by an angel. He was allowed to sleep, then fed again. After that, a word, a job, and a man beside him.

Say it to God, and then say it to a man. If the gauge says "I've had enough," that sentence has to be heard by another human being this week. Tend the body. A prophet wanted to die, and the first two things God did were a meal and a nap.

:::margin
Psalm 103:14 (NIV)
"for he knows how we are formed, he remembers that we are dust."
:::

Then get help. A doctor sits inside faith, and so does a counselor. Both are ways bread and water get sent. Weeks of dark readings, a numbness that has settled, thoughts about ending your life, these go to a doctor, told as the truth. Medication, if he prescribes it, gets taken the way insulin gets taken. The gauge is being repaired so it can be read again. The wheel stays with the Word.

If ending it is on your mind tonight, call a man now. Your brother. Your pastor. If there is no one to call, call 988 and talk to a person whose work is sitting with you in the dark. Elijah asked God to kill him. What he received was breakfast. Let somebody find you under the bush. Then take the wheel again. He returned to the work with a companion beside him. The feeling did not leave ahead of him. He moved first, small steps, with help, and the feeling followed. A meal. A nap. A brother. The next small thing assigned to him. Remain with that until the needle is back in range.

## The dash in every season

With a baby in the house every needle is pinned and none of them can be trusted. Decide nothing about the marriage at three in the morning. Tell each other this. This is the sleep talking. We will talk after a nap.

Past a divorce, expect a year or two of readings the dash has never made. Take each one to God and to your man, and leave the wheel alone. No moving, no new marriage, no quitting, no exit from the church. Broken gauges mean straight and slow until they settle.

Tape on the cluster was how some older men were raised. The names can still be learned. Their readings include grief as friends die, fear of decline, and a widower's loneliness. That loneliness is company-hunger. God made you for people. Go find the company that remains.

Single, hear the loneliness gauge as the garden heard it. Alone is not good. Aim at brothers, and refuse the fastest thing that promises to quiet the needle.

## The Man who read every gauge

Jesus felt every gauge. Tears at a grave. Sorrow to the point of death. Hebrews says loud cries and tears in the praying. The wheel never moved with a needle. He heard let this cup pass, put the Father's will over His own, and went at the very thing the gauge begged Him to dodge.

Felt, said, and still both hands on what does not move. Read yours. Tell God and a brother. Take the wheel. Stay in the row already cut.

## Before you turn the page

Dark on a gauge for a long time means one order. Tell one man today. If it is not that dark, give God one honest sentence about the dash tomorrow morning, then take the wheel. May the Father who knows how we are formed, and remembers that we are dust, rest your body and keep you in the row.
