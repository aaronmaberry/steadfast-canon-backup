# 19. Two Men, Two Ways

Most nights, once the kids are finally down, he walks out to the strip of dirt beside the garage. Boots, no socks. He pulls the hose over, straightens the tomato cage that leans after every rain, and stands there awhile. Drive by and you'd never guess anything that matters happens there. You'd have said the same about Eden from the road. Same for an olive grove outside Jerusalem, the night a Man lay flat on that ground and prayed.

The Bible hangs the whole human story on two gardens and two men. Each had a night when everything rode on him.

The first man was given everything and lost it in one afternoon, mostly by keeping his mouth shut. The second Man arrived at His garden with almost nothing left except a choice. He made it lying on the ground, and that choice rescued the world.

:::margin
**Romans 5:19 NIV**
For just as through the disobedience of the one man the many were made sinners, so also through the obedience of the one man the many will be made righteous.
:::

So here's the question underneath this chapter. Which of those two are you standing in? And which way are you walking this week?

## Eden

The serpent slides in and skips Adam. He goes to Eve, and he goes after what God said. Did He really tell you that? A small bend in the words, and a suggestion that God is tight-fisted. And where's the man?

:::margin
**Genesis 3:6 NIV**
When the woman saw that the fruit of the tree was good for food and pleasing to the eye, and also desirable for gaining wisdom, she took some and ate it. She also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it.
:::

He was right there. Men have lived under that phrase ever since. Adam heard the command first, alone, before Eve had been made. Guarding that ground was already his job. Then the very thing he was put there to keep out came right in, and he stood and listened while God's word got twisted, and said nothing. Then he ate. The Bible's first sin has a silent man standing in the middle of it.

Most of us know that silence from the inside. Something false got said at the table, answering it would have cost the evening, and we let it ride.

When God comes looking that evening, the two of them duck out of sight.

:::margin
**Genesis 3:10 NIV**
He answered, "I heard you in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked; so I hid."
:::

Fear, then cover. The man who should have stepped out to meet that familiar voice crouched behind leaves instead. And when God asks him plainly what he's done, Adam's answer starts with her. The woman. The one You gave me. Count the names in that sentence. One is missing, and it's his own.

Under all of it sits the grab. You'll be like God, the snake had promised, and it's right there in reach. Adam swapped the Father for a piece of fruit he could hold. He wanted the gift and was willing to lose the Giver to get it.

Nobody had to teach you that groove. You found it the way rainwater finds the low spot in a yard.

Thorns follow, and death, and a shut gate. But inside the curse sit two mercies. God makes them clothes from an animal's skin, so the first covering for shame cost a life. Hold on to that. Then He turns to the snake and speaks a curse with a promise folded inside it.

:::margin
**Genesis 3:15 NIV**
And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel.
:::

A son is on the way. When the enemy comes to His garden, He won't stand there mute. It will cost Him a wound, and He will crush the thing that got in. Before the first garden stopped smoking, God was announcing the second Man.

## Gethsemane

Night, and a hillside He knows well. His friends come along, and He brings three of them in closer. Then He goes on by Himself and falls forward.

:::margin
**Matthew 26:39 NIV**
Going a little farther, he fell with his face to the ground and prayed, "My Father, if it is possible, may this cup be taken from me. Yet not as I will, but as you will."
:::

In Eden the offer was fruit that promised to make a man a god. Here it's a cup that will make God's Son look like a cursed criminal, and everything in Him pulls back. Luke says the sweat came off Him like drops of blood. And still He prays what Adam never prayed. Your will, Father, not Mine. That cup held sin He never committed, and He drank it as if the guilt were His own.

Three times He prays it. Then torches come up the path, and He stands.

:::margin
**Matthew 26:46 NIV**
Rise! Let us go! Here comes my betrayer!
:::

Rise. Let us go. He walks straight at them.

:::margin
**John 18:4 NIV**
Jesus, knowing all that was going to happen to him, went out and asked them, "Who is it you want?"
:::

He went out. No search party needed. An hour after sweating blood, He met the clubs and lanterns in the open, and while they closed in, His mind was on His men.

:::margin
**John 18:8 NIV**
Jesus answered, "I told you that I am he. If you are looking for me, then let these men go."
:::

Let these men go. Seconds from being bound, He points to His own chest. Take Me. Leave them. He plants Himself between the soldiers and the friends His Father put in His care.

Then the cross. And at the end He doesn't cry for rescue. He reports in.

:::margin
**John 19:30 NIV**
When he had received the drink, Jesus said, "It is finished." With that, he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.
:::

It is finished. Every piece done, and He says so before He dies. Adam walked off and left his assignment in the grass. Jesus completed His. The crowd filed Him away as one more failed teacher on a cross. Three days later the Father answered by raising Him.

## Whose you are

Here the Bible says something people don't expect. It doesn't stop at, go copy the second Man. It says you already live inside one of the two.

:::margin
**1 Corinthians 15:22 NIV**
For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive.
:::

Birth put you in Adam. That's why going quiet comes easy, why blame stays loaded, why ducking feels like plain sense. You were in him the way a whole family carries the name of the man at the top of the tree.

So you aren't told to try harder. Willpower never pulled one man out of Adam. God offers a transfer. By faith He lifts a man out of the first and sets him inside the second, and the second garden is counted as his. Christ's obedience goes on that man's record, and "It is finished" gets spoken over him for good. Jesus called this being born again. It's the gospel, and there isn't another door.

:::margin
**Galatians 2:20 NIV**
I have been crucified with Christ and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the body, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.
:::

That's a new address. The Spirit who raised Jesus lives in a man who belongs to Christ, and that's where his strength to stand comes from. Not grit. Life.

If you've never done this, do it now. Tell God that Eden's way is the only one you've ever lived and you're finished with it. Ask Him to put you in His Son, Christ's garden laid over yours, Christ's record where yours used to be. The door's open, and He's the one who opened it.

If you've already done that, hear the rest. Everything this book asks of a man, Jesus did first, and He did it for you. The marks in these pages aren't a ladder you climb so He'll take you. You're already His. They're the family face, starting to show on a son who belongs.

## Small gardens

Yours won't look like an olive grove. One will look like a kitchen. She says something that isn't quite fair, and going cold or firing back would both feel good. That's the garden. The other way might be one calm sentence: that one stung, could we sit down once the kids are in bed?

Another looks like a phone at eleven, with the charger down the hall where you could just walk it. Or a project gone sideways at work that has your prints on part of it, and the meeting where you say, that piece is on me.

Eden came down to one piece of fruit. Most of your days will come down to things about that size.

## Every man in the room

If you're single, remember the second Man never married, and nobody has ever been more of a man. You don't need a wife to walk His way where you already live.

If there's a baby in the house, a lot of your gardens happen at 2 a.m. He asked the Father to take the cup away, and then He stood up. You can stand up too, even if it's only to get to the end of the hall.

If you're divorced, sit a long time with let these men go. Adam's instinct is to aim a finger, at her, at the lawyers, at the God who brought her into your life. Jesus put Himself between the danger and the people He'd been given and said, Me, not them. Your kids shouldn't have to haul your war around. Shield them. Name your own part out loud. Hand hers to God.

If you're older, think about final words. Adam's last ones in Eden were an excuse. Christ's were It is finished. Cal began praying something new a while back: Lord, let me finish. Whatever's still mine to do, let me finish it. The road gets shorter, and there are still gardens on it.

## Last Adam

:::margin
**1 Corinthians 15:45 NIV**
So it is written: "The first man Adam became a living being"; the last Adam, a life-giving spirit.
:::

Paul's word is last, not next. Nobody comes after Him, because He did the job the first one dropped and carried it all the way through. Now He's drawing men into Himself and leading them through ordinary gardens, His Spirit inside them, His record covering them.

You were born in one man. You can be carried into the other. Tonight, stand where you are as someone who's in Christ. And if you've never asked Him to bring you in, don't go to bed without asking.
