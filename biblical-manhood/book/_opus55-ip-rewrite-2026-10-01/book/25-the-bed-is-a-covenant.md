# 25. The Bed Is a Covenant

"I'm doing my part, and she isn't doing hers."

That's how Mitch opened, the first time he sat down across from me. I hear some version of it from a lot of husbands when the bedroom has gone cold, usually tired and a little defensive, like a man reading off a balance sheet.

So I asked him what he'd promised her on the day itself. He knew it cold: better or worse, sickness and health, till death. Then I asked him to point to the way out in that sentence. He went back over it, slowly. There wasn't one. That vow has a single ending, and a man only reaches it standing at a graveside.

## Two kinds of promise

Contracts are built around exits. You sign for a set period, both sides watch to see whether the other delivers, and if somebody doesn't, you leave clean. Plenty of us run the bedroom on those terms without ever being taught to.

A covenant is a plan for staying. In a contract you trade goods. In a covenant two people hand themselves over, which is why it always comes with something you can see and touch. A ring. A cup. A body.

Grooms in the old English service used to put it into words while they slid the ring on: with my body I thee worship. Worship there meant honor, worth-ship. He was promising that his body would spend the rest of his life telling her what she was worth. That's what the marriage bed is for. It's the covenant sign, said again every time the two of them are able.

## Before anybody got it wrong

Our culture's loudest story says sex is recreation. Both people willing, both eager, that's the whole rulebook, and the other person becomes a means to an end. Plenty of churches told a different story for a long time, that it's faintly dirty, something decent men hurry through and feel odd about later. God told His story first, back before the fall.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:24-25 NIV**
That is why a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh. Adam and his wife were both naked, and they felt no shame.
:::

One flesh, nothing hidden, no shame, and He called it good. The God who designed it isn't embarrassed by it. He stands guard over it.

:::margin
**Hebrews 13:4 NIV**
Marriage should be honored by all, and the marriage bed kept pure, for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral.
:::

Pure means set apart for its purpose and for those two alone.

Paul quotes that same one-flesh line and then says there's a mystery folded inside it, and the mystery is Christ and His church.

:::margin
**Ephesians 5:31 NIV**
For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and the two will become one flesh.
:::

So the bed preaches. It's meant to say true things about God: He keeps one people, He gives Himself gladly, and He never punishes with silence. When a husband breaks that covenant with his body, or only with his eyes, the sermon starts lying about God.

## Keeping it

It's never leverage. No pulling away to punish her. No treating it like a bill she owes. The moment the bed becomes a bargaining chip, the covenant has walked out and two people are just negotiating. A cold bed doesn't warm up because you made her pay. Find out what broke. The break is usually older than the bed, and it runs wider.

Mitch had been keeping a count, and Dana could feel the number in his hands. I asked him to put the ledger away for a season. Nothing started with a payoff in mind. Touch that wanted nothing back. And one calm conversation, in the truck on the way home from her mother's. That's when she said it: "I want to stop feeling like a bill that's past due." A few months later he told me she comes looking for him now. He'd had the debt pointed the wrong direction. What he owed her in that bed was the truth about what she's worth.

It's generous. Paul wrote a sentence a lot of men only quote halfway.

:::margin
**1 Corinthians 7:4 NIV**
The wife does not have authority over her own body but yields it to her husband. In the same way, the husband does not have authority over his own body but yields it to his wife.
:::

Read the second half again. In Paul's world, that flipped everything. You're there for her delight; your own finish is the lesser goal. Learn how she's made. Ask what would bless her. The husband who asks ends up receiving more than the one who grabs.

It takes its time. Her clock doesn't run like yours, and she knows when she's being hurried toward your finish line. Rushing grabs what the vow promised to give. Slow tenderness is the vow wearing skin.

It talks. Away from the bed, and not in the sting right after a letdown, ask her gently what she'd like more of and less of.

It shows up in the everyday touch. Neglect quiets a marriage bed years before age gets the chance. If your hand only appears when you want something, a day comes when she flinches, and you taught her that.

And it belongs to the two of you alone. Proverbs tells a husband to drink from his own well. Jesus pushed that into the chest: a lustful look is adultery already happening in the heart. A man can keep his body at home and still be leaking out through his eyes. His wife feels the water level drop even if she never finds the crack.

## The screen

You already know the tools. One brother who knows everything. A filter whose password he holds and you don't. A charger on the kitchen counter. What changes is why you fight. A living woman is holding your covenant, and each image rips it a little while she sleeps beside you. Those pictures teach your appetite to crave whatever's new, and they whisper that she's the shortage. She isn't. You've been somewhere else.

None of this is off the clock once the lamp clicks off. Guard your eyes. Trust the Father who sees what's done in secret. And hear it plainly: He isn't running a debt you're working off. If you belong to Christ you're already His son, by grace, and every faithful night is a son living like the family he's in.

## No wife yet

Single, and hoping to marry someday? Then what you do with your body now is already shaping a covenant you haven't entered. Either you're saving toward it or you're cashing it in ahead of time. Paul said to learn control of your own body, and learning means it takes reps and time. Where good men tend to fall is the long relationship that never moves toward an altar. Late at night, behind a closed bedroom door, alone together, nobody holds out. If she's the one, and you're ready to carry what marriage asks, go marry her.

Eli showed up in chapter one, phone glowing, ten o'clock. If you're single, that hour is the whole battlefield for this chapter, not some wedding night years away. A Tuesday, roommates behind their doors, nobody needing anything from him. Wanting to win didn't win it. He lost that fight a lot first. What finally turned it was dull stuff. At night his phone now sleeps on the kitchen counter. For a year he'd hoped somebody at church would offer to keep his filter password, and nobody ever did, so he went and asked a man eleven years older to hold it. That's Stop Waiting, and it was the hinge. Some nights he'd still get up and walk back out to that counter. Then he'd text the older man, and the older man would answer. At nineteen, self-control looked like that. No victory feeling. A cord plugged in by the toaster and a friend who picks up.

## A wife once

Divorced, you're single again, and memory makes it harder. You'll hear that a guy who's done his time in a marriage has earned some freedom. He hasn't. Former husbands don't get an exception. Sex waits for a new covenant, and "we're practically married" isn't a reason to share an address. Use the same tools Eli does. The stakes are higher, too, because your kids are learning from how you handle desire when it aches.

Widowed, you're held to the same line, and I'm sorry it's so heavy. Decades of faithfulness, and still that empty pillow catches you off guard some nights. God doesn't want you calling the loneliness small. He's asking you to honor the bed through these years too. Every faithful night you gave her, He saw. He sees these.

## Her seasons, and yours

When a baby comes, remember what her body just survived. Whatever you think you're owed, drop it. She's emptied out and tired of hands on her. Quit the count. Hold her in ways that want nothing back. When she's ready, take it slow, check in, and stop the second she asks. The faithfulness you show her at her most fragile is what she leans on later, when she's strong again.

Sickness can rearrange a covenant without ending it. Gentleness that never gets as far as the bed still counts as keeping the vow.

Somewhere in midlife, her body will change in ways neither of you expected. It isn't a rejection of you. Ask her about it. Sit in the waiting room with her. Men who stay faithful to their own well still drink from it in old age, slower and still sweet. And if a day comes when making love isn't possible at all, faithfulness doesn't end. I think of a husband washing a wife who can't remember who he is. That man is honoring the marriage bed every morning. The two of them are one flesh yet.

Mitch came in hunting for a way out. The vow never had one. What it has is everything you are, already pledged, and a bed where you get to pledge it again.

## Before you turn the page

Plenty of good men have fallen here and kept it to themselves. If you're one of them, drag it out where it can be seen. David confessed, and God gave him back his footing. You can begin keeping faith again tomorrow. May the God who calls this bed honorable give you joy in your own wife, guard you from every counterfeit, and make your marriage a true sermon about Him.
