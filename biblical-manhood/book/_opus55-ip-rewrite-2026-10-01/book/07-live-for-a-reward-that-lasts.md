# 7. Live for a Reward That Lasts

I know the drive home with the radio up and a trophy riding shotgun. That night it feels permanent. Years later I saw one just like it at a yard sale, in a cardboard box on a folding table with FREE down the side in marker. A gold batter missing an arm. A bowling pin glued to marble. A sales plaque from a company that folded. Somebody's Saturdays were in that box, and nights he was sure would define him. By noon nobody had claimed it. By spring it was at the curb.

That's how the rewards you can hold tend to go. They're great for a night. Then your hand's empty and you go hunting again.

The fourth mark is Work for God's Pay: do it for the Lord's reward, not the room's applause. The other three lean on it. Stop Waiting, Pick Up the Weight, and Go First all send you a bill today, and a man keeps paying those bills only if he trusts there's a return. Why fix what nobody asked you to fix if nobody's keeping track? Why say "that's mine" if the blame would have slid right past you? Why go first if the street is all there is? Those questions don't go quiet until this mark answers them.

## What you're already working for

You're already working for some kind of pay. Every man is, from the minute his feet hit the floor. The only question is whose.

Your father's approval, alive or not. More men are on that payroll than will admit it. Comfort, which a new dad is chasing by week six and a retired man on too many afternoons. Being right, which a young husband will trade a whole evening for. The number on the screen, checked before you're out of the parking lot. The nod from across the room. Being wanted, which single men chase quietly and married men forget they're chasing. Being remembered. Your name on something.

Some of those are good in their place. Every one of them runs out, and God is a different kind of pay altogether. The nod dies in the parking lot. The bigger number shows up without the feeling that was supposed to ride along. Your father's approval, if it ever comes, is smaller than the version you rehearsed. Collect the kind of pay that lasts one night and you end up on that folding table.

## Treasure that keeps

Jesus never told men to stop wanting a reward. He told them where to put it.

:::margin
**Matthew 6:19-21 NIV**
Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moths and vermin destroy, and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where moths and vermin do not destroy, and where thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.
:::

Moths, vermin, thieves. He described the yard sale two thousand years early. Earlier in the same sermon He told them how the lasting kind gets stored.

:::margin
**Matthew 6:3-4 NIV**
But when you give to the needy, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, so that your giving may be in secret. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you.
:::

Every kind of pay on that old list needs an audience. This one asks you to send the audience home. A widow's driveway shoveled while her kitchen light is still off. A check with no story attached. Praise spoken about a man who'll never find out you said it. A single guy's Saturday spent hauling a young family's couch up to a second-floor apartment, with nobody to tell about it that night. Hidden work still has a witness, and He keeps the books.

And it's bigger than what happens at church.

:::margin
**Colossians 3:23-24 NIV**
Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving.
:::

Whatever you do. The invoice and the diaper, the spreadsheet and the graveyard shift. This pay isn't only for the guy on the stage. It's for the man on the ladder and the man at the sink with nobody watching, when he does it for the Lord. Who you're doing it for is what turns it into treasure.

## The pay is an inheritance

Hear me carefully, because men get this wrong in both directions. Paul's word for the pay is inheritance. You don't earn an inheritance. You can't clock in for it. You get it because you're in the family, and the only way into this family is grace.

:::margin
**Ephesians 2:8-10 NIV**
For it is by grace you have been saved, through faith, and this is not from yourselves, it is the gift of God, not by works, so that no one can boast. For we are God's handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do.
:::

Look at the order. Saved by grace, as a gift. Then the good works God already laid out for you. The work comes after you're His, never as the price of becoming His. So when this book says work for God's pay, it doesn't mean work hard and maybe God will let you in. It means you're already a son, so work like one, and trust your Father to remember every hidden thing. The pay isn't wages. It's a son's inheritance, and the best part of it is the Father Himself.

:::margin
**Matthew 25:21 NIV**
His master replied, "Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you in charge of many things. Come and share your master's happiness!"
:::

Well done. More put in your hands. A door open into His joy. The pay is mostly welcome. What a son wants at the end of the road is a Father glad to see him coming up the walk.

Moses ran the numbers right. He grew up in a palace with the treasury a hallway away, and he walked out to stand with slaves.

:::margin
**Hebrews 11:26 NIV**
He regarded disgrace for the sake of Christ as of greater value than the treasures of Egypt, because he was looking ahead to his reward.
:::

Looking ahead. He saw past the treasure room to something worth more, and seeing it is what let him walk away. A man can leave almost anything with something better in view. He can't leave much when all that's in the frame is what he's losing.

## What it does to a man

You don't need to interview him. A man working for God's pay shows it. He does good and doesn't bring it up. He drops arguments he could win. He stays, in his marriage and his church and his town, on days he'd have every excuse to leave. He takes the low seat without scanning the room. He forgives a man who doesn't deserve it, because the ledger that matters is his Father's and he can tear up his own. He prays for grandkids not yet born. He teaches a boy to work, and that boy will teach another long after he's gone. Mr. Pruitt keeps his pecan trees like that. The nuts are for other people. The watering is his.

If this life is all there is, that way of living makes no sense. If it isn't, it's the only math that works.

## What it doesn't promise

The loud version of this is a counterfeit, so let me say plainly what God's pay doesn't include. No promise of health, a bigger house, an easier year, or kids who land where you aimed them. Staying married doesn't promise warmth by spring. Forgiving a man doesn't promise he'll ever apologize. Preachers who sold those deals left wreckage behind them. Paul wrote down the real scale after he'd been beaten and shipwrecked.

:::margin
**2 Corinthians 4:17-18 NIV**
For our light and momentary troubles are achieving for us an eternal glory that far outweighs them all. So we fix our eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen, since what is seen is temporary, but what is unseen is eternal.
:::

Light and momentary, from a man covered in scars. He did the math with bigger numbers. He never said the trouble would vanish. He said it was producing something. The hard years stay on the record. They're the raw material the reward is made from.

## How to live for it

Every day, fix your eyes. Fix is Paul's word, and a glance won't do it. One line before your feet hit the floor: today is seen, and today counts.

Every week, one kindness nobody will ever know about. Your wife or your roommate doesn't get told. Your brother doesn't get told. The guys at work don't get told. If the story leaks, you picked the wrong kindness.

On the drive home after a day nobody thanked you for, practice hearing "well done" in His voice.

Catch yourself performing while it's starting. Jesus was plain: good done for a crowd has already been paid in full. The second you look around to see who noticed, the check cleared, and it was a small one.

Pray past Thursday. A single man's apartment is room enough for this. So is a grandfather's porch. Same audience to let go of. Same Father doing the seeing.

## One man

Put all four marks on one life and look at him. He doesn't walk around the job that needs doing. He says "that's mine" and lets it stand. He opens the first door while the street watches. And the pay he's after is out of reach of any yard sale. There's an old word for a man like that. Steadfast.

**A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.**

That's Work for God's Pay, lived out. Live for the reward that lasts. Then go back to work.

## Before you turn the page

The small rewards pay out the same night, and that's why men keep picking them. Raise your aim. The Father who sees in secret also remembers perfectly, and He isn't paying you off. He's keeping an inheritance for His son. May your eyes be fixed where moths can't get at the treasure, and may the words you're longing for be kept for you until the day He says them.
