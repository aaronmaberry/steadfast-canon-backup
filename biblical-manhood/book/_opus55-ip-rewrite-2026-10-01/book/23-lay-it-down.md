# 23. Lay It Down

She started crying, and for a minute he honestly didn't know why.

Back up ten minutes. Two people in a bathroom, water over the tile and soaking into the baseboards. Months ago she went with the cheaper plumber. He said the repair wouldn't hold. It didn't. The other quote, the one he pushed for, sat folded in his back pocket like a winning ticket. He could feel the line climbing his throat, the one that settles it. Five words and he wins.

Instead he took out the paper, crumpled it, and tossed it in the wastebasket by the sink.

"I'm sorry I got loud," he said. "We'll figure it out."

That's when the tears came. Later he worked it out. Through every year of that marriage he had never once walked away from an argument he was winning. She was looking at a husband she'd never met.

You spent the last chapter learning her. This one costs more. You lay your life down for her. The wedding, rented tux and flowers, was the easy hour of the vow. A soaked floor on a night when you're the one who's right is where the vow stops being words.

## The other half of Ephesians 5

Guys know which line in that chapter they like. It's the one telling wives to submit. It's in the Bible, Paul meant it, and I won't tiptoe around it. But read one more sentence. The part for husbands is longer than the part for wives, it asks for more, and I've never seen a man highlight it with the same enthusiasm.

:::margin
**Ephesians 5:25 NIV**
Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her
:::

Christ didn't wait for the church to clean up first. He loved people who kept failing Him, and He kept going until it killed Him. Plenty of us would like the headship without the dying. You don't get to split them. Headship is a cross. The one in front absorbs what's coming, and he does it for her sake, not to guard his position.

:::margin
**Philippians 2:5-7 NIV**
In your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus: Who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage; rather, he made himself nothing by taking the very nature of a servant, being made in human likeness.
:::

Think about who's being described. No one in history stood on higher ground. He stepped down off it, tied a towel around Himself, and washed feet. Paul says: that mind, in you. Picture it with your socks wet and your proof in your pocket.

## What it isn't

First let's clear out the fake. It has wrecked as many homes as the bully has.

The fake says, "Whatever you want, babe," and goes back to the couch. He quits making calls, quits stepping out ahead, quits deciding anything, and labels it sacrifice. It's leaving while still sitting in the house. She'll feel the hollow even on nights she gets her way. That's Stop Waiting run backward: a husband who waits forever and gives it a humble name. Jesus gave Himself up and kept leading the whole time. He led on His knees with a basin. He led nailed to a beam. Strength picks her good over its own comfort. Weakness picks a quiet house over doing the job.

The doormat is the same fake in a different coat. Jesus told people He loved things they didn't want to hear. If you never tell your wife a hard truth, you haven't laid down your life. You've abandoned your post. Love waits its turn, listens to the end, picks a good hour, keeps its voice soft. Then it says the thing.

And some men just disappear and think it's godliness. She said yes to someone with a spine and a heading. Sand yourself down until nobody's left and she's lost her husband. Let your rights go, and your comfort, and your pride. Don't let you go. She needs a man in there.

So here's how to tell, after you've given something up. Look at her. Did she get stronger, or did her load get heavier because you went missing? If she's more settled, more like herself, more awake, it was the genuine article. If the house got harder and you called your vanishing humility, it was the counterfeit.

## The small deaths

"Lay down your life" sounds like one heroic afternoon. In a marriage it mostly happens in installments, on days only she will remember.

The money might be first. If the accounts are your private country and she gets a visitor's pass now and then, that's governing her. Open the books. Give her full sight and a full vote.

Then the argument you're winning. You'll win plenty on the facts. Most of those victories charge her more than they pay you. Nobody's asking you to say the facts changed. You just decide the marriage outweighs the point. Notice the first word of "we'll figure it out." That "we" is the thing you're protecting.

Then your phone. Flip it over at the door, and pocket it while she's telling you about her day. I once sat across from a man who looked at his screen through an entire hard conversation and then insisted he'd heard every word. Neither of us believed him.

Then your mom's vote. If the two women in your life land on opposite sides, your wife gets the tie, and Mom hears it from you. You left that house on your wedding day.

Then the audience. When the kids, your parents, or the guys from work are watching, you take her side. She can survive a tough conversation in private if you stood with her in public. Make her the joke and she learns she's alone, even at a friendly table.

Then your rest. When she's sick or the baby's screaming at two, you're the one out of bed. A man who protects his sleep while she runs on nothing is using her up to keep himself fresh.

Then your weekend and your preferences. Golf isn't sin, and neither is picking the restaurant. But when home needs you, those lose, without a sulk or a scoreboard. The first fifteen minutes after you get home go to her, not the recliner.

Then the heat. Don't unload it in front of her. Take it to God, to the cab of your truck, to a brother, and come back with whatever was hiding under it.

For the high performer it's often the title, being the one who decides. And for nearly all of us it's the next ten years we mapped out in our heads with her nowhere in the room. Tear that map up. Draw a new one at the kitchen table with both your hands on it.

## The promise in it

Jesus said one sentence about this that sounds like losing and is actually a promise.

:::margin
**Matthew 16:25 NIV**
For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me will find it.
:::

Read it as a husband. Give up winning, and you find a wife who brings you the truth because she's learned you won't use it against her. Give up the Saturday, and two decades on your marriage is still alive. Give up the sleep, and she never forgets who got up.

Not one of those gets lost. Your Father notices the fight you threw, the 2 a.m. bottle, the bath she won't remember by lunch. But understand, this isn't a pay scale. Nobody gets into the Father's house by losing enough arguments. If you belong to Christ you're already a son, by grace, and what's coming is an inheritance and a "well done" from a Father to His kid. The Son who gave up everything will look at a husband who gave up a little and say, that's how I did it.

## Wherever you're standing

With a newborn at home, take the night shift before she has to ask twice. Stop keeping the silent ledger of who's had a shower. Do something unpaid and don't advertise it. Leave the bedroom alone until she's ready. Put the gym and the guys on a shelf this year. Love the tired woman in front of you. Your Father sees you at three in the morning.

Decades into a marriage, give up the retirement you dreamed up alone. Ask what she wants those years to be, then go build it. Hand over the remote, and more of the decisions too; she's able, and she's been waiting. When sickness arrives, the illustrations stop working. I know a man who has bathed his wife every morning for years, since illness took most of her away. Some mornings she has no idea who he is. He has every idea who she is. He promised "in sickness" long before he'd seen that room. The Father sees those mornings.

If your marriage ended, you might be staring at everything you wouldn't give up while you had the chance. Let that hurt. Tell God your share of it. Then take the next right step. Stop trying to beat your children's mother. You'll be right sometimes, and the kids still need a mom and dad who can sit in the same bleachers without a scene. Somebody has to make that happen, so let it be you. Drop the story where she was the whole problem. Leave the rest with God.

If you're single, or nineteen with a ring nowhere close, now is when you practice. A man who's never given up a preference won't suddenly learn between "I do" and the cake. Let the roommate choose dinner. Spend a Saturday helping an older man from church whose gutters are overflowing. Little deaths, on purpose, on regular days. That's how you train.

## Back on the wet floor

He was right that night. He threw the proof away anyway. Months later his wife told me that's when she started bringing him the things that scared her. A husband who'll let go of a fight he's winning is a husband who won't turn her honesty into a weapon.

Sometime this week, you'll be right. Let it go.

## Before you turn the page

You find your life by losing it, and the Father wastes none of the little losses no one else sees. May the One who watched His Son give up everything make you strong enough to let one thing go this week, and may you come home to a different house because of it.
