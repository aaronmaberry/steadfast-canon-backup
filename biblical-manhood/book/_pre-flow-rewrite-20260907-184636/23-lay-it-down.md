# 23. Lay It Down

*Part 4: The house*

Anthony was right.

It was a Tuesday night, a fight about money in the kitchen after the kids were down, and he'd done the math and he'd done it correctly, and his wife had made a mistake with the account and it had cost them a late fee and a little embarrassment, and he was right about all of it. He had the receipts. He had the numbers. He had the moral high ground and he was standing on it, and he could feel the sentence loading that would end the argument and prove the point.

And he said he had a strange thought, standing there. He thought about a Man on His knees in a garden with a cup He didn't want, and he thought: I'm right, and it doesn't matter. He put the sentence down. He said, "I'm sorry I got loud. We'll figure it out. It's not a big deal." And his wife looked at him like she didn't recognize him, and then she started crying, and it took him a while to understand that she was crying because in nine years of marriage he'd never once laid down a fight he was winning.

You've studied her. Now you lay your life down for her. Not once, at the altar, in a nice suit. Every day, in a kitchen, when you're right.

## The harder job

Here's the sentence men love to quote from Ephesians 5, and the one they quote less often.

The one they love is "wives, submit." Men underline that one. I'm not arguing with Paul; he wrote it and it's Scripture. But the next thing he writes is addressed to us, and it's longer, and it's heavier, and I've never once heard a man underline it in the same tone of voice.

:::margin
Ephesians 5:25 (NIV)
"Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her"
:::

Gave himself up for her. Just as Christ. That's the standard. Not "love her a lot." Love her the way Jesus loved the people He died for, which is to say: all the way to death, on purpose, while they were still failing Him.

Men want the authority of Ephesians 5 without the cross of Ephesians 5. They want the headship and not the giving up. But in Scripture, headship *is* a cross. It's the man in front, and the man in front is the one who takes the arrows. Paul goes on to say the husband gives himself up to make her holy, to wash her, to present her radiant. His whole sacrifice aims at her flourishing. That's not a man who gets his way. That's a man who's decided his way is her good.

And look at what Christ actually laid down, since He's the pattern.

:::margin
Philippians 2:5-7 (NIV)
"In your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus: Who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage; rather, he made himself nothing by taking the very nature of a servant, being made in human likeness."
:::

Did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage. He had every right. He had the high ground in a way no husband ever has. And He didn't use it. He set it down and put on a towel. That's the mindset. That's what "just as Christ" means when you're standing in a kitchen with the receipts.

## What a husband lays down

Get specific. "Lay down your life" sounds like something you'd do once, dramatically. It's mostly done in pieces, on Tuesdays.

**Your right to be right.** Anthony's kitchen. You'll be right a lot in your marriage; you're not stupid. And most of the time, being right is worth exactly nothing compared to what winning costs her. Lay it down. Not by pretending you're wrong; by deciding that the point isn't the point. "You may be right about that. Let's figure it out together." The second half of that sentence is the whole marriage.

**Your preferences.** Where to eat. What to watch. How the dishwasher gets loaded. What temperature the house is. How the weekend goes. A thousand small things a day where you'd rather it went your way. A husband who lays down his preferences daily, cheerfully, without a scoreboard, is building something she can feel even if she can't name it. A husband who fights for every preference is telling her, in a thousand small ways, that his comfort outranks her.

**Your phone.** It's the biggest competitor for your attention in your house and she knows it. Lay it down. On the counter, face down, when you walk in. In the kitchen, charging, at night. Not in your hand while she's talking. I've watched men who'd never dream of reading a newspaper while their wife talked to them look at a screen through an entire conversation and honestly not know they'd done it. She knows.

**Your Saturday.** Or whatever your day is. The hunting lease, the golf, the shop, the boat, the game. Those aren't sins. But when the house needs you and the lease is calling, the lease loses, and it loses without you sulking about it. A man who lays down his Saturday for his wife without making her pay for it in mood has given her something better than the Saturday.

**Your first fifteen minutes.** After work, you want to decompress. She's been waiting for an adult all day, or she's coming in from her own job, or she's been carrying a baby for nine hours. Lay down the decompression. The first fifteen minutes when you walk in are hers, not the recliner's. Tomás does this with his boots off and the baby on his shoulder. It costs him fifteen minutes. It's worth the whole evening.

**Your side of the story.** In front of the kids, in front of your parents, in front of your friends, take her side. Even when the story's complicated. Especially then. A wife whose husband defends her in public can survive almost any fight in private. A wife whose husband makes her the punch line at a barbecue, even gently, learns that she's alone in the room.

**Your mother's vote.** Chapter 12. When your mother and your wife disagree, your wife wins, and your mother hears you say so. Lay down the boy who still wants his mother's approval. You left. Act like it.

**Your sleep.** It applies past the newborn too. When she's sick, when she's grieving, when the kid's throwing up at 2 a.m., you get up. You lay down the sleep. A husband who guards his sleep like it's a right while his wife runs on four hours is not giving himself up for her; he's giving her up for himself.

**Your anger.** You have a right to be angry sometimes. Lay it down anyway. Not by stuffing it; by taking it to the truck, and to God, and to your brother, and bringing back to her the thing under it. Chapter 9. A man who lays down his anger before it reaches his wife has given her a safe house.

**Your money.** If you've held the money like it's yours because you earned most of it, lay it down. It's the house's money. She has full standing, full knowledge, full say. The husband who keeps his wife on an allowance and in the dark isn't leading; he's ruling, and Colossians told him not to be harsh.

**Your plans.** The retirement you've imagined. The move you've wanted. The way you pictured the next ten years. If those plans were made in your own head without her, lay them down and make new ones with her in the room. A man who unveils a plan for both their lives that he built alone has told her she's a passenger.

## What laying down isn't

Careful: there's a counterfeit, and it's the first mark inverted, and I've watched it ruin marriages from the other direction.

Laying down your life is not abdicating. A husband who stops leading, stops deciding, stops going first, and calls it "letting her have her way" isn't giving himself up for her. He's disappearing on her, which is passivity with a nicer name, and she'll feel it as abandonment even while she gets everything she asks for. Christ gave himself up and never once stopped leading. He led from the towel and from the cross. Laying down is a strong man choosing her good over his comfort. It is not a weak man choosing quiet over responsibility.

Laying down is not becoming a doormat. Jesus was no doormat. He said hard things to the people He loved. A husband who never tells his wife the truth because he's "laying down his life" is actually laying down his job. Love speaks. It speaks gently and it speaks at the right time and it speaks after it's listened, but it speaks.

Laying down is not self-erasure. She married a man. She needs a man to lean on, one who has convictions and a spine and a direction. If you dissolve yourself into her wishes until there's nobody home, you've robbed her of a husband. Lay down your preferences, your rights, your comfort, your pride. Don't lay down yourself. She needs you.

Here's the test. After you've laid something down, is she stronger or weaker? Christ's sacrifice made the church radiant. If your sacrifice is making your wife stronger, more secure, more herself, more radiant, it's the real thing. If it's making her carry more because you've quietly checked out and called it humility, it's the counterfeit.

## Under a newborn

Tomás, and every man in the first year.

You're about to lay down more than you've ever laid down in your life, and you're going to do it exhausted, and nobody's going to thank you, and it's the most Christlike stretch of your marriage so far. Here's the list.

Sleep. You take a shift. Not "I'll help when she asks." A shift, on the schedule, every night: the 2-to-5, or the first stretch so she can go down at 8 and get five hours in a row for the first time in months. Tomás did the late shift for six months and worked ten-hour days on it. "I was a zombie," he said. "She was a person again. Fair trade."

The bedroom. Chapter 25 will say more, but for now: her body's been through a war and it's still occupied territory, and your claims on it are laid down until she's ready. That's not a season of deprivation. That's a season of giving yourself up for her.

The gym, the lease, the game, the guys. This year. Not forever. This year, the things that were yours get smaller so the house can survive. They'll come back. If you fight for them now, something else won't.

Your expectation that she'll be who she was. She isn't, right now. Lay down the wife you married and love the one who's here, and she'll come back, changed, and better, and she'll remember that you loved her when she was underwater.

And your need for credit. Nobody's clapping. That's chapter 7. Your Father sees the 3 a.m. Let that be enough for this year.

## After forty years

Roy, and every man past sixty, you've got laying down to do that the young men can't imagine.

Lay down the retirement you planned alone. The one where the days were yours. She's been sharing a calendar with your work for forty years and now the calendar's finally open, and it's hers as much as yours. Ask her what she wants the years to be. Then make them that.

Lay down the remote, and the news, and the recliner's claim on the evenings. Roy said the hardest thing he laid down after the badge was the television, because it was the only thing that felt like rest. "Turned out rest was sitting on the porch with her, and I'd never tried it."

Lay down the control. If you've run the money, the decisions, the direction for forty years, it's time to hand her the reins on some of it. Not because you can't. Because she can, and she's waited.

And this one, because some of you are living it. When she gets sick, laying down your life stops being a metaphor. A friend I'll call Mr. Alvarez has bathed his wife every morning for three years since the dementia took most of her. She doesn't always know him. He knows her. He said, "I said 'in sickness' in 1968. I meant it. I just didn't know what it would look like." That's Ephesians 5:25 at seventy-eight. That's a man giving himself up for a bride who can't thank him, and it's the most beautiful thing I've ever watched a man do, and there's a Father who sees it every single morning.

## For the man who's single again

Kenny, you may be reading this and thinking about what you wouldn't lay down when you had the chance. Hear it, grieve it, confess your part, and don't stay there. There's laying down for you to do right now.

Lay down your right to win with your kids' mother. Every exchange, every schedule negotiation, every text about the school thing. You're going to be right sometimes. It doesn't matter. Your kids need two parents who can be in a room together, and that requires at least one of them to lay down the war, and it has to be you, because you're the man and you're reading this. Kenny said the sentence at the soccer field, the one from chapter 6, was a laying down. "I had a whole list of things I could've said. I laid the list down. Best thing I ever did for my kids."

And lay down the narrative. The story where it was all her. Even if most of it was. You don't have to tell it anymore. Let God hold it.

## For the single man

You're not laying down your life for a wife yet. Practice anyway, because a man who's never lost a preference for anyone will not suddenly become good at it on his wedding night.

Lay down where you eat when the guys want somewhere else. Lay down your Saturday for the widower who needs his gutters cleaned. Lay down your first fifteen minutes for a roommate who's had a bad day. Lay down your side of the story when your mother's wrong about something small. Small deaths, daily, on purpose. That's the training ground. Micah told me that Dale made him practice losing arguments with his roommate for a year. "I thought it was ridiculous. Then I got married and realized I'd been in spring training."

## The reward for the man who loses

Jesus said something about laying down your life that sounds like a loss and is a promise.

:::margin
Matthew 16:25 (NIV)
"For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me will find it."
:::

Whoever loses their life will find it. Every husband who's actually done this knows what Jesus meant. You lay down your right to be right and you find a wife who trusts you with the truth. You lay down your Saturday and you find a marriage that's still alive at fifty. You lay down your sleep and you find a woman who remembers, thirty years later, that you got up. You lay down your plans and you find plans you'd never have made that are better than yours.

And the Father sees all of it. The laid-down fight in the kitchen that nobody witnessed. The 2 a.m. shift. The remote set down. The bath at seventy-eight. None of it's wasted. It's stored where the weather can't get to it, and one day the Man who laid down everything is going to look at a husband who laid down a little and say: well done. You did it like I did.

Anthony was right that night. He laid it down anyway. His wife told me, months later, that it was the night she started trusting him with her fears, because a man who'd give up a fight he was winning was a man who wouldn't use what she told him against her.

You'll be right this week. Lay it down.

---

**Name it.** What's the one thing on the list you hold hardest: being right, your preferences, your phone, your Saturday, your first fifteen minutes, your side of the story, your mother's vote, your sleep, your anger, your money, or your plans? Say it plainly.

**Do this week.** Lay that one thing down every day this week, on purpose, without announcing it or keeping score. If you're single, pick the practice version: lose a small preference daily for someone in your life.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him what you're laying down and how it went. Ask him what he sees you gripping that you can't see yourself.

**Carry this.** Headship is a cross. Men want the authority of Ephesians 5 without the giving up of Ephesians 5. You can be right and lay it down. That's the whole marriage.
