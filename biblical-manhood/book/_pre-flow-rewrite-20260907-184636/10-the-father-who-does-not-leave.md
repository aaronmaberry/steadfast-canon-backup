# 10. The Father Who Does Not Leave

*Part 2: What you inherited, and what is yours*

Watch a boy at a Little League game and you'll see him do something between innings, or after a hit, or after a strikeout, especially after a strikeout. He scans the bleachers.

He's not looking for the crowd. He's looking for one face. And you can tell, from the dugout, which boys find it. Their shoulders drop a quarter inch. They stop scanning. They go back to being a kid at a ballgame.

You can tell which boys don't, too. They scan longer. They scan every inning. Some of them are still scanning at thirty-eight, in a doorway between a garage and a kitchen, and they don't know what they're looking for anymore, only that they haven't found it.

There's a Father you can stop scanning for. He's in the bleachers every inning. He was there before you looked. He's not leaving between the fifth and sixth to take a call. For some of you the word "father" is the whole problem, so start there.

## The problem with the word

If I say "God is your Father," what happens in you depends almost entirely on the chair at the end of your table.

If the chair was empty, "Father" means someone who isn't coming. You'll believe God exists the way you believe your dad exists: somewhere, doing something, not here.

If the chair was full and silent, "Father" means someone who's in the room and hasn't got a thing to say to you. You'll pray like a man talking to a newspaper.

If the chair was dangerous, "Father" means someone whose mood you have to read. You'll spend your Christian life trying to figure out what God is mad about today, and you'll be sure it's you.

If the chair was good, you're ahead, but even you have inherited limits. Your father couldn't be everywhere. He got tired. He missed things. So somewhere in you is the quiet assumption that God gets tired and misses things too.

You've been reading God through your dad. Every man does. It's the only picture of a father you had, so you held it up to heaven and assumed that's what was there. And for most men, that picture has been quietly poisoning their walk with God for decades, because they've been relating to a Father they invented out of an absence.

The Bible does the opposite. It tells you to read your dad through God. God is the original; your father was a copy, and copies are always worse than the original, some badly. So put the copy down for a minute. Here's what the original is like, from the only Man who's seen Him.

:::margin
John 14:9 (NIV)
"Jesus answered: 'Don't you know me, Philip, even after I have been among you such a long time? Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father. How can you say, "Show us the Father"?'"
:::

Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father. If you want to know what God the Father is like, you don't look at your dad. You look at Jesus. Everything Jesus did, the Father is like.

## What He's like

**He comes toward you.** The most famous story Jesus told about a father is about a boy who left, blew everything, and came home stinking of pigs with a speech ready. And the father doesn't wait at the door with his arms crossed.

:::margin
Luke 15:20 (NIV)
"So he got up and went to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him."
:::

While he was still a long way off. The father had been watching the road. That's the only way you see someone a long way off. He'd been looking for a face in the bleachers too, and when he saw it, he ran. Grown men in that culture did not run. This one did. That's your Father. Not waiting for you to get it together. Watching the road.

**He tells you who you are before you've done anything.** When Jesus was baptized, before He'd preached a sermon or healed a soul or done one thing you could put on a résumé, the Father spoke from heaven.

:::margin
Matthew 3:17 (NIV)
"And a voice from heaven said, 'This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased.'"
:::

My Son. Whom I love. With him I am well pleased. Three things every boy needs to hear from his father, and Jesus heard them at the start, not the finish. That's the order the Father works in. He blesses first and then sends you to work. Your dad may have had it backward, making you earn what should've been given. The Father doesn't. If you're in Christ, that voice is speaking over you right now, on a Tuesday, before you've done a thing.

**He carries you.** Moses, looking back on forty years of wilderness, described God this way.

:::margin
Deuteronomy 1:31 (NIV)
"and in the wilderness. There you saw how the LORD your God carried you, as a father carries his son, all the way you went until you reached this place."
:::

As a father carries his son. You've seen it, a man with a sleeping boy over his shoulder walking in from the truck, careful on the steps, not waking him. All the way you went. Not part of the way. Not until you got heavy.

**He knows what you need before you ask.** Jesus said so, and He said it to men who were anxious about food and clothes and tomorrow.

:::margin
Matthew 6:8 (NIV)
"Do not be like them, for your Father knows what you need before you ask him."
:::

Your Father knows. The man who learned from the empty chair that needing things is dangerous needs this for a long time. You don't have to hide the need. He already sees it. He's not put off by it. He's a Father.

**He disciplines you, because you're His.** I won't pretend this one's comfortable, but a man with a dangerous father needs to hear it more than anyone, because he's got discipline and rage confused.

:::margin
Hebrews 12:7 (NIV)
"Endure hardship as discipline; God is treating you as his children. For what children are not disciplined by their father?"
:::

God is treating you as his children. The hard season isn't proof He left. It's proof He stayed. A father who doesn't care lets a boy do whatever he wants. Your Father cares enough to shape you, and He never does it in a rage, and He never does it to make you small. He does it to make you like His Son.

**He does not leave.** This is the one the whole chapter hangs on, in His own words.

:::margin
Hebrews 13:5 (NIV)
"Keep your lives free from the love of money and be content with what you have, because God has said, 'Never will I leave you; never will I forsake you.'"
:::

Never. Jesus said it a different way the night before He died, to men who were about to watch Him taken and were about to scatter.

:::margin
John 14:18 (NIV)
"I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you."
:::

Orphans. He knew that's what they'd feel like. He knows that's what some of you have felt like since you were nine. And He says: not you. Not on my watch. I will come to you.

## The hardest thing for a man to do

A man who's fathered himself since he was a boy doesn't know how to be fathered. It's not that he doesn't believe God is a Father. He'd tell you he does. It's that when it comes to actually being carried, actually being told who he is, actually letting somebody else hold the weight, every muscle in him locks up. He's been the corner post so long that leaning on anything feels like falling.

Anthony said it exactly. "I know God's my Father. I just don't know what to do with that. I don't know how to let Him be one. I never learned how to be somebody's son."

That's the honest sentence. Say it. Don't fake something.

Here's what works, in men who learned to be sons late.

**Call Him Father out loud.** Not "God." Not "Lord." Father. In the truck, alone, when it's awkward. Paul says the Spirit inside you is already doing this.

:::margin
Romans 8:15 (NIV)
"The Spirit you received does not make you slaves, so that you live in fear again; rather, the Spirit you received brought about your adoption to sonship. And by him we cry, 'Abba, Father.'"
:::

Abba. That's the word a small boy used. You're allowed to use it. It'll feel strange in your mouth. Say it until it doesn't.

**Bring Him your exit.** Whatever your home exit is, anger, itch, or drift, take it to Him first, before the brother, before the counselor. Not cleaned up. "Father, I'm about to blow up at my kids and I don't know why." "Father, I'm reaching for my phone again and I hate it." "Father, I've been gone and I don't know how to come back." He's not disgusted. He's the father watching the road.

**Let Him tell you who you are.** Every day, read something He said about His sons and say it back to Him as true about you. Not as a mantra. As a boy repeating what his dad just told him. "You said I'm your son. You said you love me. You said you're pleased with me because I'm in Christ." A man who was never told who he was needs to be told a lot, and the Father is patient.

**Let yourself be carried.** This is the one that'll cost you. Somewhere this year, something's going to be too heavy, and your instinct will be to grit it out alone the way you always have. Don't. Say, out loud, "I can't carry this. Carry me." Then let a brother help. Then let your wife see you need help. That's not you failing to be a man. That's you finally learning to be a son, which every real man is first.

**Stay in the house.** A son who's learning to be fathered doesn't wander off. He's in the Word, where the Father talks. He's in the church, where the Father's other kids are. He's in prayer, where the Father listens. A man who only visits the house on holidays never learns what the Father's voice sounds like on a Tuesday.

## What this does to your other father

Here's a thing I didn't expect, walking this road with men. Once a man starts to actually be fathered by God, he starts to see his own father differently.

Not more softly, necessarily. Sometimes more clearly, which is harder. But he stops needing his father to be something the man never was, because he's getting it from Somebody else. The scanning stops. And a man who's stopped scanning can look at his dad, whatever kind of chair he sat in, with a kind of steadiness. He can honor what was good. He can name what wasn't without rage. He can forgive, because he's not waiting for a payment anymore. He might even be able to reach toward the man, which is what chapter 12 is about.

None of that is possible while you're still trying to get from your dad what only your Father can give. The chair at the end of your table can't fill the chair in your chest. It was never big enough. Only He is.

## In your season

If you're young, learn this now. The men who find the Father at twenty-five are spared thirty years of scanning. Don't wait until you've got a family to start being a son.

If you've just had a baby, you're about to understand something about the Father that you couldn't before. You'll look at your kid asleep and feel a love that has nothing to do with anything he's done. He hasn't done anything. He's just yours. That's how the Father looks at you. Let it teach you.

If you're divorced, the Father didn't leave when your marriage did. I know it felt like He did. Some of you stopped praying because you figured He was as disappointed in you as everyone else seemed to be. He was watching the road the whole time. Come home.

If you're older, it's not too late to be somebody's son. Roy learned to call God Father at sixty-eight, in his truck, in a parking lot, because he'd never once done it in sixty-eight years and he decided that was long enough. "It felt like a fool," he said. "Then it felt like coming in out of the wind."

## Stop scanning

Back to the boy in the dugout. He's looking for one face. When he finds it, he can go back to being a kid. He can strike out. He can boot a grounder. He can get a hit and not have to look up to see if anybody noticed, because he already knows Somebody did.

That's what the Father does for a man. He lets you stop scanning. He lets you stop auditioning. He lets you fail on a Tuesday and get up Wednesday, because your standing with Him was never about the Tuesday. It was about the Son, and it's settled.

:::margin
1 John 3:1 (NIV)
"See what great love the Father has lavished on us, that we should be called children of God! And that is what we are! The reason the world does not know us is that it did not know him."
:::

And that is what we are. Not what we're trying to become. What we are. Sons.

He's in the bleachers. He's been there every inning. Drop your shoulders. Go back to the game.

---

**Name it.** Which picture of "father" have you been holding up to heaven: not coming, in the room but silent, dangerous, or good with a gap? Say it. Then say what Jesus shows the Father is actually like.

**Do this week.** Every morning before your feet hit the floor, say out loud: "Father." Just the word. Then one sentence to Him about the day. Do it seven days in a row.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him which false father you've been praying to. Ask him to pray for you, out loud, right there, and let him. Being prayed over is practice at being carried.

**Carry this.** You've been reading God through your dad. Read your dad through God. Anyone who has seen Jesus has seen the Father.
