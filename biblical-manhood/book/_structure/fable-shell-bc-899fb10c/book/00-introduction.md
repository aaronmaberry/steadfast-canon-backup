# Introduction

The first man I ever walked through this material did not want to be there.

His wife had signed him up. He told me that in the parking lot before we ever sat down, like he wanted it on the record. He was in his thirties, doing fine at work, coaching his kid's team on Saturdays, and by every outside measure he was a good guy. He also had not initiated a real conversation with his wife in about a year. He had not prayed out loud in his own house since their wedding. He could tell you the depth chart of his favorite team and could not tell you what his son was afraid of.

He was not a bad man. He was an absent one. Present in the room, absent from the fight.

I knew him because I had been him. That is the only reason I had any business sitting across the table. I was not a pastor. I was not a counselor. I was a husband and a dad who had spent the first stretch of my marriage waiting for someone to hand me a manual, and who finally figured out that the manual was never coming. What did come, eventually, was a handful of older men who kept a chair for me at a table and would not let me stay comfortable in it.

This book is that chair.

## What this book is about

Somewhere in the pages that follow you will run into a single sentence, and I want you to see it now so you can watch for it:

**A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.**

Four marks. Reject passivity. Accept responsibility. Lead courageously. Expect the greater reward.

I did not invent that sentence. Its shape comes from Robert Lewis, who taught a definition of manhood in that form to a generation of men, and I have never found a better frame for what the Bible says a man is for. What I have done with it is live it, fail at it, get back up inside of it, and teach it at kitchen tables and in church classrooms to men who were as skeptical as my friend in the parking lot. This book is what came out of those years. The stories are mine or belong to men who have given me permission to tell them, with the details changed. The teaching is mine. The sentence, I hold with gratitude and an open hand.

Here is what I believe about it. Most of the trouble men bring into their homes is not wickedness. It is drift. It is the slow, reasonable-sounding decision to let things ride. To let her handle it. To stay quiet in the meeting. To scroll instead of speak. To wait for a better moment to have the hard conversation, and then another better moment, until the moment is gone and the damage is done and we cannot even remember deciding anything. That is passivity, and it is the default setting of the male heart since the garden. Everything else in this book flows out of refusing it.

And here is what I believe about God. He is not standing over you with a clipboard waiting for you to fail again. He is a Father, and He is for you. The reward the sentence talks about is not a trophy for good behavior. It is Him. His pleasure, His presence, His "well done." The whole reason a man can reject passivity and accept responsibility and lead with courage when it costs him is that he has decided the only applause that matters is coming from one direction. Get that settled and the rest becomes hard but possible. Leave it unsettled and the rest becomes performance, and performance always quits.

## Who I'm writing to

You do not need to be in a particular decade of life to read this. You need to be in a particular season, and here are the ones I had in mind while writing.

You are newly married, or about to be, and you have realized that the wedding did not come with instructions and that you are now responsible for someone else's flourishing. Good. Start here.

You are in the thick of it. Little kids, a demanding job, a wife who is as tired as you are, and a nagging sense that you are running your household like a well-meaning roommate rather than leading it. This book was mostly written for you, because that is where I was when I needed it.

You are raising teenagers and you have started to hear your own passivity coming back at you in their voices. It is not too late. It is actually the best possible time.

Your kids are grown or nearly grown, the house is getting quiet, and you are asking whether the second half of your life is going to be a long coast to the finish or something that counts. It can count. Men in your season are the ones I most want to hand this book to, because you are the ones who should be keeping chairs for the rest of us.

You are single, and you are tired of being told that manhood starts at the altar. It does not. Every one of the four marks applies to you today, in your job, in your friendships, in your church, in the way you treat the women around you. Read this now and you will be a different kind of husband if God gives you that.

You are starting over. Divorce, a moral failure, a job loss, a season of sin that cost you more than you thought it would. You may be wondering if a book with the word "godly" on the cover is even for a man with your history. It is. Some of the steadiest men I know were built in the rubble of what they tore down. Grace is not a footnote in this book. It is the floor.

Whatever season you are in, if you are a man who suspects that God made you for more than comfort, and you are willing to be uncomfortable to find out, I am writing to you.

## What I am not

I want to be honest about my limits before you invest your time.

I am not a pastor. I love my church and I serve in it, but I do not stand behind a pulpit and I am not trying to replace the men who do. Everything in here should send you toward your local church, not away from it.

I am not a counselor. Some of you are carrying wounds that a book cannot reach. I will say plainly, more than once, that getting professional help is not weakness. It is one of the most responsible things a man can do.

I am not finished. I have been walking this road long enough to teach it and not nearly long enough to stop needing it. My wife could give you a list. So could my kids. I am not writing from the summit. I am writing from a little farther up the trail, turning around to say: keep coming, it is worth it.

## How the book is built

Read it front to back. I know that sounds obvious, but a lot of men treat a book like this as a reference manual and jump straight to the chapter about marriage or money because that is where it hurts today. Resist that. The book is one continuous argument, and it only works in order.

It opens by naming the problem. Not "the culture," not "men these days," but the specific, ordinary shape of passivity in a man's actual life. Where it hides. What it costs. Why good men fall into it without ever choosing it. I spend real time here because you cannot fight an enemy you have not identified, and most of us have never had anyone put a name on the thing that is quietly eating our homes.

Then it hands you the frame. The four marks, one at a time, each with its roots in Scripture and its feet on the ground. You will see the first man, Adam, standing silent in the garden while everything fell apart, and you will see the last Adam, Jesus, holding all four marks perfectly and then handing that life to you. Along the way the book stops to deal with grace and repentance, because a man cannot carry a standard like this unless he knows what to do the moment he drops it.

Finally it goes room by room through a man's life. Your wife. Your kids. Your work. Your money. Your body and your mind. Your friendships, your church, your words, your calendar. In each one the question is the same: what does it look like, right here, to reject passivity, accept responsibility, lead courageously, and expect the greater reward? Those chapters are practical. They end with things to do. And the book closes the way I hope your life closes: steadfast, still standing, still moving, all the way to the end.

That is the whole architecture. Problem, frame, action. Start at the beginning and let it build.

## How to read it

You can read this alone, and it will do you good. Keep a pen in your hand and write in the margins. Argue with me. Underline what stings. Do the things at the ends of the chapters even when they feel small, because small and done beats ambitious and imagined every time.

But I will tell you what I have watched for years now. The men who change are almost never the ones who read alone. They are the ones who read with somebody. One friend on a Thursday morning at a diner. Three guys in a garage. A dozen men in a church classroom. Something happens when a man says out loud, in front of other men, "I have been passive with my wife and I am done with it." The words go from ink to iron. Isolation is where passivity breeds. Brotherhood is where it dies.

So if you can, find one other man before you start. Send him a text tonight. It does not have to be a program. It has to be a chair.

## One more thing before you start

I owe you some straight talk about what this book is and is not, because I do not want you to feel sold to on page 300.

Steadfast Men is also the name of a 24-week training I teach to men in person and online. That is not a secret and I am not going to pretend otherwise. This book grew out of that training, and the training continues to grow out of the men who have gone through it. If you finish the last chapter and want more, the full class guide, the weekly videos, and the group materials are at walksteadfast.com. I would love to have you there.

But hear me clearly: this book stands on its own. You do not need to buy anything else to get everything I have to give you here. The frame is complete. The application is complete. If you read it, mark it up, do what it says, and bring one other man along, you will have gotten what I most wanted you to get.

For the men who want to take it further without spending another dollar, I have put a condensed version of the training in the back of this book. It is a 24-week map that pairs the chapters you are about to read with a single question and a single action each week, so you and a friend or a group can walk through it together at a pace that lets it sink in. It is the bones of the full class. It is enough to start. The full guide at walksteadfast.com is there if and when you want the rest.

Book first. Training if you want it. Either way, you are welcome at the table.

## Before you turn the page

My friend from the parking lot finished the twenty-four weeks. Somewhere around the middle he stopped telling me his wife had signed him up. Near the end he brought a guy from work. Last I heard he was leading a table of his own, and his son knows exactly what his father is afraid of, because his father tells him, and then tells him what he does about it.

That is not a miracle story. That is just what happens when a man stops drifting.

Reject passivity. Accept responsibility. Lead courageously. Expect the greater reward.

Let's go.

Max
