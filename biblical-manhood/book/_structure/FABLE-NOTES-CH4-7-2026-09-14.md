## Chapter shape (applies to this wave and every later one)

Each chapter runs one loop: hook on an ordinary-day picture, name the mark early, explain plainly, one worked example carrying the weight, a teaching beat to close, an optional Before you turn the page, then the locked Your Turn (four lines) and `:::notes`. One job per chapter. Even rhythm. No question banks, no study-guide homework, no teacher-name parade, no hashtags, no color or layout directives in the text.

How the four map to it:

| Chapter | Hook | Mark named | One worked example | Closing teaching beat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stop Waiting | the stair everyone steps over | fourth paragraph | the stair, carried through to "The first screw" | James 4:17, then Before you turn |
| Pick Up the Weight | the wall you can't take out | fourth paragraph | the load-bearing wall and the floor above it | Isaiah 53:4, then Before you turn |
| Go First | the tree across the street | fifth paragraph | the first door open, carried to "the second door" | Hebrews 12:2, then Before you turn |
| Live for a Reward That Lasts | the FREE box of trophies | third paragraph | the yard sale, read back through Matthew 6 | four marks on one man, then Before you turn |

Everything else in each chapter is either Scripture doing the teaching or a sentence-level image (lawn chair, kitchen crossing, engine running, shoveled driveway). After the shape pass, Pick Up the Weight had its three supporting mini-scenes (long march, one-trip grocery man, piano recital) cut to a sentence each so the wall is the only developed illustration.

## Chapter notes

### Stop Waiting

- Picture: the loose third stair the whole household steps over. It does the smoke detector's job and adds the turn the chirp couldn't: the family adapts around what the man won't fix. The stair recurs at Adam's question ("past the stair"), in the seasons pass ("The step is different in every house. The stepping over is the same."), in the how-to ("The first screw"), and in the reward ("fix the step. Then make the call").
- Replaced: smoke detector, tailgate (now lawn chair and a driveway at night with the engine off), Roy the retiree (now second person), doctor's note, starting gun, "fields don't weed themselves" (recast).
- Teaching kept: six disguises (patience, peace, humility, preparation, faith, busyness); four excuses plus comfort underneath; real waiting versus counterfeit with the hands test; five moves; James 4:17 as the shut back door.
- Scripture (NIV, text verified): Genesis 3:9, Proverbs 24:30-31, Psalm 27:14, Luke 9:51, James 4:17.
- Fact check: Abraham's wait is illustrated only with events that precede Isaac (household move, altars, rescuing Lot, dealing with kings). Wells were dropped because the Beersheba dispute falls after Isaac's birth.
- Forward pointer: "I'll give it a whole chapter later in this book" points at the reward chapter.

### Pick Up the Weight

- Picture: the load-bearing wall. Looks like every other wall; the second floor is riding on it; step back from under the weight and the house sags until a door stops latching. One image carries "the weight is where the weight is," fairness ("a fine question for a courtroom, a useless one for a wall"), the floor that doesn't move ("Upstairs, nobody thinks about the beam"), and the reward inside the responsibility ("the beam nobody upstairs ever thanks").
- Sentence-level images only beyond the wall: the soldier's pack for the second Greek word; the man who won't make two trips from the car for the wrong weight; the piano lessons and the empty cafeteria chair for provision. Each is one sentence after the shape pass.
- Teaching kept: Adam's blame, Cain's question answered "Yes," fault versus responsibility, five weights in order, Galatians 6:2 against 6:5 with the two words explained plainly, Moses and the seventy, 1 Timothy 5:8 read on "household," the five how steps, Isaiah 53:4 with "took up" and "bore," fourth mark inside the second via Hebrews 12:2 paraphrase.
- Scripture (NIV, text verified): Genesis 4:9, Galatians 6:2, Galatians 6:5, Numbers 11:14, 1 Timothy 5:8, Isaiah 53:4.
- Decision: Genesis 3:12 is paraphrased in prose rather than quoted in a margin because the NIV text contains an em dash. Cain takes the margin.
- Closer is deliberately settling rather than crushing: "God builds a man for the load he's given and sends seventy for the rest."

### Go First

- Picture: an oak across the street after a night wind, power out, a face in every window, nobody moving. One door opens; for a stretch of seconds he is the only man in the street; then the second door. "Nobody on that street was waiting for permission. They were waiting for a door." The street runs the whole chapter: exposure, the eight seconds, Deuteronomy ("Someone in the street ahead of you"), the pioneer, and the closer ("the sound of the second door").
- Teaching kept: first door open rather than head of the table; John 13 towel as "An example. Not a memo."; Judges 7:17 as leadership in two words; first is exposed; the five places (clean apology with "the and-you is a hook," prayer at your own table, naming the rubble via Nehemiah, turning first, setting direction with "come, then walk"); four fears; 2 Timothy 1:7 read on "not timid" rather than "not afraid"; Deuteronomy 31:8 with "the second door open"; five how steps including the eight seconds and the ordinary day; Hebrews 12:2 on "pioneer" with the fourth mark holding the third.
- Scripture (NIV, text verified): John 13:14-15, Judges 7:17, Nehemiah 2:17, 2 Timothy 1:7, Deuteronomy 31:8, Hebrews 12:2. "Pioneer" is the NIV 2011 rendering.
- Note: Hebrews 12:2 also appears as a paraphrase in Pick Up the Weight. In that chapter it carries "for the joy set before him"; here it carries "pioneer." Different work, same verse. Flagging in case you want one of them moved.

### Live for a Reward That Lasts

- Picture: a cardboard box of trophies marked FREE on a folding table at a yard sale. "Every one of those was once a reward a man lived for, and it worked. For a night, it worked." Jesus's moths, vermin, and thieves are read as a description of the yard sale. Secret good is pictured as the widow's driveway shoveled before her kitchen light comes on. Planting what you won't taste is praying for grandchildren who don't exist yet and teaching a boy who'll teach his own boy.
- Teaching kept: every man already lives for a reward (applause, the number, being wanted, being right, the father's nod, comfort, being remembered) and each has a bottom and is not God; the fourth mark makes the other three hold, shown as three questions the first three marks can't answer alone; Matthew 6:19-21 and 6:3-4; Colossians 3:23-24 on "whatever you do"; not mercenary because the reward is Him (Matthew 25:21 as "a welcome, not a payout"); Moses looking ahead; six things it does to a man; what it doesn't promise with 2 Corinthians 4:17-18; five how steps including "received their reward in full"; close on one steadfast man, the four marks sentence verbatim, "Live for a reward that lasts. Then get back to work."
- Scripture (NIV, text verified): Matthew 6:19-21, Matthew 6:3-4, Colossians 3:23-24, Matthew 25:21, Hebrews 11:26, 2 Corinthians 4:17-18. Hebrews 11:24-25 is paraphrased in prose; only verse 26 is quoted.
- The "One man" section calls back to the three earlier pictures in one sentence each (the step, "that's mine," the first door). If any earlier picture is changed in a later pass, that paragraph needs a matching edit.

## Decisions for the editor to confirm

1. Titles. The H1s keep the source's numbered form (`# 4. Stop Waiting`). Numbers appear nowhere in prose. If the locked chapters run unnumbered titles, strip the numerals from these four H1s.
2. Because leads. All three action items in Your Turn carry a Because lead, matching the source pattern. If the lock intends Because on Tell a brother only, the other two can be trimmed without touching the instructions.
3. Forward pointers. Only Stop Waiting points ahead ("later in this book"). Pick Up the Weight and Go First point at the fourth mark by name rather than by position.
4. Repeated Scripture. Hebrews 12:2 appears in two chapters (see Go First note).
