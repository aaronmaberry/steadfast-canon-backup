# Structure proposal, Steadfast Men

Prepared 2026-10-03 for Aaron, from his Part Two notes. This is a proposal only. The manuscript, the 03d print and Drive are untouched.

Files in `book/structure-review-2026-10-03/`:

## What you said

1. There's too much quoted Scripture, and it feels like we're trying too hard. Tell what Jesus teaches and does in plain words.
2. There are too many breaks, titles and bold. It reads like we're talking at men, or like a workbook, not a book.
3. "Before you turn the page" at the end of each chapter isn't needed. Find another way to land it.

## What the numbers say

- **158 NIV verse boxes**, 4.5 per chapter. About 100 of them are set up by a sentence that points at the verse ("Paul says it in eight words." "Jesus said it like this."). That set-up and then the box is the "trying too hard" feeling. It happens on almost every page.
- **228 subheads**, one about every 230 words. Add the boxes and a 1,500-word chapter breaks about 11 times.
- **The paragraphs aren't the main problem.** They average 50 words. What chops the page is the heads and boxes between them, plus 218 short paragraphs and runs of paragraphs that open the same way ("Some of you... Some of you...").
- **There's almost no bold in the prose (9 spans).** The bold you're seeing is 228 headings, 158 verse labels and 140 Your Turn labels.
- **Every chapter ends twice.** First comes a "Before you turn the page" blessing (33 of 34 are built on "May the..."), then a full Your Turn form page. That's 35 form pages, 15% of the book.

## 1. Scripture

**Option A. Tell it, with no quotes.** Every passage is told in Wes's words ("Jesus told a story about a father whose son...", "watch what He does with Peter on the beach"). No boxes. Nothing is quoted word for word. References go in a back-of-book Scripture notes section, keyed by the phrase on the page, with no superscript numbers.

**Option B. Tell it, with one anchor verse per chapter (recommended).** Same as A, but each chapter carries one NIV verse word for word, set quietly under the chapter title as an opening line. It's the verse the chapter is built on. The story then pays it off in the prose, the way the sample opens on Luke 15:20 and ends inside that same scene. For the four mark chapters, the anchor is the mark's verse.

**Option C. Keep the boxes, but fewer.** Cap them at one or two per chapter and cut the set-up sentences. It's the smallest change. It still reads as a teaching book with callouts.

**Why B:** Men still get one line of the actual Bible, the authority stays plain, and nothing interrupts the prose. The NIV footprint drops from 180 verses to roughly 35 to 40. The Biblica notice on the copyright page still applies.

## 2. Prose form

**Option A. No subheads (recommended).** Chapters run as continuous prose. Two to four line breaks with a small ornament mark the real turns. Transitions come from the story. In ch10, Curtis in the pickup line carries the reader from section to section where the subheads used to.

**Option B. Two or three quiet subheads.** Keep a few only where a chapter truly changes ground, set small and light, with no bold.

**Option C. Keep the structure and tighten it.** Cut a third of the heads and merge short paragraphs. It reads better, but it's still a workbook shape.

Under A or B:
- Paragraphs should average 80 to 110 words. Keep a short paragraph only for a real hit, one or two per chapter.
- Turn list-like runs into connected paragraphs.
- Use no bold in the body. The creed line becomes an italic line set on its own.

## 3. Chapter ending

**Option A. A quiet last paragraph, with Your Turn at the back (recommended).**
- Drop the "Before you turn the page" heading. Its blessing and charge fold into the last paragraph of the story, in the chapter's own words, not a "May the..." formula.
- The protected Carry this line becomes the chapter's last sentence. In ch10 the chapter ends on "The original was in the line before the bell."
- The four Your Turn lines (Name it, Do this week, Tell a brother, Carry this) move to a Your Turn section at the back of this same book, one page per chapter, with the same 03d wording and write-in lines.
- The intro tells the reader once that each chapter's work is waiting at the back. The work stays in this book, so we still honor the "no second workbook" rule.

**Option B. One plain page in the chapter.** Keep the Your Turn page at the end of each chapter, but strip it down to three short questions, with no labels, bold or form lines.

**Option C. A separate study guide.** Move the work into its own group guide. This breaks the locked "no second workbook" rule, and it costs men who only buy one book.
