### Scripture: margin vs block

- PDL puts the day’s verse as a **centered block quote** on the chapter-open page, then sprinkles short quotes in the body. Apparatus at the back explains versions.
- Hughes and most Crossway trade books run verses as **indented blocks** in the text column, not as a true margin column.
- Tripp’s note-taking edition is the rare Christian-living product that **sells the margin** as a feature (write-in, not a running verse column).
- **Steadfast already chose a true outer-margin NIV verse** (`:::margin`). That is a differentiator on this shelf. Do not also dump a second verse block at the opener or the closer (Hughes already has memory verses; PDL has “Verse to Remember”). One verse, in the margin, is enough.

## 3. KDP paperback production

Cited: Amazon KDP Help.

- Create a Paperback Cover — `https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G201953020`
- Set Trim Size, Bleed, and Margins — `https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/GVBQ3CMEQW3W2VL6`
- Print Options — `https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G201834180`
- eBook cover criteria — `https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G200645690`

### Trim: 5.5×8.5 vs 6×9

KDP (Print Options + Trim Size pages): **the most common US paperback trim is 6×9**, and it is the default in the setup form. Both 5.5×8.5 and 6×9 are “regular” trims (not large-trim pricing). Black ink + cream or white: 24–828 pages (cream max 776).

**This category:** men’s *trade paperbacks that stay in a truck* cluster at **5.5×8.5**. Crossway study books and PDL cluster at **6×9**.

**Recommendation for Steadfast: 6×9.** Reasons that are production, not vanity:

1. We need an **outer margin for NIV**. 5.5×8.5 with a verse column either crushes the text or makes the verse unreadably small.
2. 35 short chapters + part dividers + a chapter-34 worksheet wants a spine that can carry type (KDP: **≥79 pages** for spine text; Cover Creator: 80). 6×9 at our word count lands ~200 pages, not a pamphlet.
3. 6×9 sits next to Hughes and PDL, i.e. “a book,” not next to Beeke’s 79-page teal guide or a 365 flexcover.
4. KDP large-trim surcharge starts above 6.12" wide or 9" high. 6×9 stays in the cheap regular band.

Keep 5.5×8.5 as a later “carry edition” only if a reprint is ever wanted. Do not design two interiors now.

### Page count for 50–70k words

Current manuscript (intro + ch. 1–16 on disk, 31 Aug 2026): about **1,310 words a chapter** including front-matter files in the average. Thirty-five chapters plus intro at that pace is about **47k** before part look-backs and the chapter-34 worksheet. If later chapters hold 1,400–1,800, the book lands in the **50–65k** band already targeted.

Industry rule of thumb for 6×9 trade serif at 11/15 with decent margins: **~280–320 words per page**.

| Words | Body pages @ ~300 wpp | + front/back/parts/worksheet | Ballpark book |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50,000 | ~167 | +24–32 | **~190–200** |
| 60,000 | ~200 | +24–32 | **~225–230** |
| 70,000 | ~233 | +24–32 | **~255–265** |

Hughes is 336 because of the study guide. We are not building that appendix. **Plan the cover spine for ~200–230 pages.** Recalc after the last chapter.

### Spine width (official KDP formula)

From G201953020. **Do not add a third-party “+0.06 in cover allowance.”** Official:

- White paper: `page count × 0.002252"`
- Cream paper: `page count × 0.0025"`

Ballpark:

| Pages | White | Cream |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | 0.45 in | 0.50 in |
| 220 | 0.50 in | 0.55 in |
| 240 | 0.54 in | 0.60 in |

Spine text allowed at ≥79 pages, with **≥0.0625" (1.6 mm)** clearance to each spine edge. KDP warns of ~0.0625" fold variance; **no hard vertical rule on the spine fold.**

Full wrap (no interior bleed on the book block):

```
cover width  = 0.125" + trim width + spine + trim width + 0.125"
cover height = 0.125" + trim height + 0.125"
```

For **6×9, 220 pp, cream**:

```
spine  ≈ 0.55"
width  ≈ 12.80"
height ≈ 9.25"
```

Download KDP’s template at final page count. Do not lock the wrap until the interior PDF is numbered.

### Cover wrap vs ebook thumbnail

| | Paperback wrap | Kindle thumbnail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| File | One PDF: back + spine + front, CMYK, 300 DPI, ≤40 MB recommended (hard cap 650 MB) | JPEG or TIFF, **RGB**, ideal **2560×1600 px** (h×w), ratio ≥1.6:1, <50 MB (G200645690) |
| Bleed | 0.125" all outside edges | None |
| Barcode | Leave the KDP box or supply one | n/a |
| Light covers | Fine on a physical book | KDP: add a **3–4 px medium-gray border** so a cream cover does not vanish on a white product page |

Design the **front as a 6×9 crop that still reads at ~80 px wide** (Amazon search). Then extend it to the wrap. Do not put the only important type in the outer 0.25" (trim variance).

### Matte vs gloss

KDP Print Options: covers are 80 lb (220 GSM) stock. **Gloss** = shiny, darker blacks, “typical for textbooks, children’s books, and nonfiction.” **Matte** = low sheen, “typical for novels and other fiction.”

That “typical” note is KDP-generic. **Every manhood trade cover inspected this pull is designed as a matte object** (charcoal grain, cream paper, hunter green, navy swirl, cedar-adjacent textures). Gloss would make Troutt/Hughes/Titus look like a textbook. **Matte.**

Cream interior paper for a text-only Christian-living book. White only if we later add many figures. Chapter 3’s four-marks figure and chapter 34’s worksheet are one-color friendly.

### Interior margins (KDP minima, then our real needs)

KDP minima, 151–300 pages, no interior bleed: inside (gutter) **0.50"**, outside/top/bottom **≥0.25"**. Those minima look cheap.

**Use:**

| Edge | 6×9 recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Inside (gutter) | **0.80"** |
| Outside | **0.85–1.00"** (this is where the NIV verse lives) |
| Top | **0.80"** (running head sits in it) |
| Bottom | **0.80"** (folio) |

If any part divider or figure must kiss the trim, the whole interior has to be built with bleed (page size 6.125×9.25). **Prefer no interior bleed.** Keep figures inside the type page.

## 5. Interior system for print (book, not workbook)

Trim **6×9**. Cream paper, black ink, **no interior bleed**. Matte cover. ~200–230 pages.

### Hierarchy

| Role | Spec |
| --- | --- |
| Body | 11 pt trade serif (Minion, Caslon, or Freight Text), 15 pt leading, rag-right or justified with decent hyphenation. Not 10 pt. Not sans. |
| First line of chapter | No drop cap. Regular indent off after the opener. |
| Subheads (rare) | 11 pt small caps, extra space above. Most chapters should not need them. |
| Display kicker (optional, opener only) | 14–16 pt italic, one line, then body. Do not turn every chapter into PDL’s “It’s not about you.” |
| NIV verse | **Outer margin**, 8.5–9 pt italic serif, hanging reference in small caps. Verso = left margin, recto = right. Never in the gutter. One verse per chapter unless the manuscript already has two; do not add a second “memory verse” at the closer. |
| Block quote | Only for a longer excerpt that cannot live in the margin. 10 pt, indented, no quotation marks, reference under. |
| Running head | Verso: part name in small caps. Recto: chapter title, truncated. 8.5 pt. |
| Folio | Bottom outside, 9 pt. |
| Part divider | Own leaf. Part number, part name, one sentence. No illustration. |
| Closer box | **Your turn** — existing four beats: Name it / Do this week / Tell a brother / Carry this. A ruled rectangle, 0.5 pt rule, 10 pt serif inside, **two short write-in lines under “Do this week” in the print PDF**. Kindle: same questions, no fake lines. |
| Chapter 34 | The one **worksheet**. Full-page table/plan, still 6×9, still black ink. The only chapter that may look like a form. |
| Chapter 3 figure | Existing four-marks figure, one-color, inside the type page. |
| Front matter | Title, copyright, NIV permission, dedication/how to read this, TOC. No author photo. |

### Chapter opener (print)

From the top of the type page:

1. Part name — 8.5 pt small caps, letterspaced.
2. Chapter number — 10 pt.
3. Title — 18–22 pt regular serif, 2–3 lines max, no stacking stunts.
4. Small repeating mark — original, **8–10 mm**, same mark every chapter (not Hughes’s compass, not a cross-as-logo, not a lion). A simple steel rule, a nail head, a limestone square — pick one and freeze it.
5. One line space, then body.

No sword. No cliff. No four-marks icon on every opener (that figure is chapter 3’s).

### What this is not

- Not a 365. Not a journal with a daily box. Not Hughes’s back-of-book study guide (the closer is enough). Not a Field Manual. Not 40 days. Not lined notes on every page.

## 6. Three things this category overuses (from covers actually opened)

1. **The cliff / adventure man.** Eldredge’s inspected cover still puts a man jumping off a cliff in a little photo frame on black. That silhouette is the category’s 25-year memory. A landscape hero on a cover will be read as *Wild at Heart* even if we never use the words. **No posed man, no cliff, no waterfall, no ridge.**
2. **Sword / knight / medieval metal.** *Raising a Modern-Day Knight* (ancestor, inspected): leather, ghosted sword hilt, wax seal, chiseled silver type with a cross built into a letter. The ESV Men’s Study Bible on today’s print list is “Forest/Tan, Trail Design.” **No sword, no camo, no trail map, no wax seal, no armor.**
3. **Distressed stacked shout-type in hunter green / blaze orange, plus the lion.** *Stand Firm and Act Like Men* (inspected) is the 2025–26 version of this: huge distressed orange MEN on forest green. The Kindle list also carries *Bold as a Lion* as a title. Type-only is fine; **that palette and that roar are taken.** Do not distress the letterforms. Do not go blaze orange on green. Do not put a lion, a ram, or an eagle on the cover.

Honorable mention, seen but not in the top three: gold-foil compass-with-cross on navy (Hughes); brick-wall stamp (Titus Ten); torn newspaper (Hansen); L-hand gag (Clary); brown faux-leather 365 (the print shelf’s actual #1). Steal none of those objects.
