# Theology check: "God's pay" is inheritance, by grace (second pass)

**The rule.** Wherever the fourth mark is taught, or wherever pay or reward carries weight, the pay must read as the inheritance the Lord gives His children (Col 3:24). It is grace-given, never wages that earn salvation (Eph 2:8-10; Rom 6:23; Rom 8:17). No sentence may be readable as "work hard and God will save you." That's from the original brief §2. The second pass adds two conditions (Fix 2, Fix 3):
- Use the anchor less.
- No accounting language on God's side.

**Method.** Every prose sentence in `book/` was listed and read if it contains any of these words: pay, paid, paycheck, reward, wage, inheritance, earn, "well done", saved, salvation, heaven. Ordinary uses need no anchor: bills, "pay attention", a man's paycheck, a child earning approval, the false pay of the trophy box, and Christ's atonement ("the price is already paid", ch15). ## Anchors cut (Fix 3), and why no anchor is needed now

- **`04:104`.** The paragraph has no pay language left. It reads "God is glad over that, the way a father is glad over a son."
- **`16:17`.** The hypocrites' line was softened to "that audience is all they've got", which uses no wage word.
- **`20:58`.** Family wording only: "the only Judge whose opinion lasts says well done, a Father's voice to a son who was His before the work began."
- **`21:112`.** "the Father's well done doesn't wait for a second name on the mailbox" uses no pay language.
- **`24:29`.** No pay or reward word is left in the paragraph.

## Result

No sentence in the book presents work as the way to be saved. Every place where God's pay or reward carries weight has an inheritance-and-grace anchor in the same paragraph, or it is one of the full anchors above. Chapter 7 has the anchor on its first page.
