# 10. The Father Who Does Not Leave

Three o'clock at any elementary school, the doors bang open and a few hundred kids pour out hunting for exactly one vehicle. Watch their faces. The ones who spot their ride go loose all over. A backpack swings. They're halfway through a story before they've buckled in. Then there are the few still scanning. Minivan, not theirs. Pickup, not theirs. One of them winds up on the curb next to a teacher who keeps glancing at her watch.

Curtis is in that line every afternoon. Twenty minutes early, engine off, radio off. Years ago he swore none of his kids would ever be the one on the curb, and not once have they been. What he's never had is the other side of it: walking out a door and seeing somebody already waiting for him. Nobody came for Curtis. He learned the way home on foot.

This chapter is about a Father who's already parked out front when the bell rings. But for plenty of men, the word father is where the trouble begins, so we'll begin there too.

## A borrowed picture

Tell a man "God is your Father" and he'll hear it through whoever sat at the end of his childhood table.

If that seat was empty, father means someone who doesn't show. God exists the way your dad exists. Out there somewhere. Occupied. Not here.

If the seat held a silent man, father means someone in the room with nothing to say to you. Prayer starts to feel like leaving voicemails on a line that's been disconnected.

If the seat was dangerous, father means you check his mood before you speak. Faith turns into a daily inspection for whatever God is upset about, and you've got a strong hunch it's you.

If the seat held a good man, you started ahead of most. Still, good dads get worn out and miss things, and somewhere under your prayers sits a guess that God will miss things too.

I've prayed to a copy and called it faith. We hold our fathers up to heaven like a photograph and assume God must look like that, and it's spoiled a lot of prayers. Scripture flips the order. God is the original. Your dad was a copy, sometimes a decent one and sometimes a terrible one. Set the copy face down on the table. If you want to see the original, exactly one Man has, and when Philip asked Him to show it, here's what He said.

:::margin
**John 14:9 NIV**
Don't you know me, Philip, even after I have been among you such a long time? Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father. How can you say, 'Show us the Father'?
:::

Want to know what the Father's like? Watch Jesus.

## Love before the work

Jesus hadn't preached a sermon yet. He hadn't healed anyone or called a single disciple. He came up out of the river, wet from His baptism, and heaven spoke.

:::margin
**Matthew 3:17 NIV**
And a voice from heaven said, "This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased."
:::

Notice the order. No assignment came first, and no audition. The Father claimed His Son out loud, called Him loved, called Him a delight, and the public work came after. That's how it runs in God's house: blessing, then work. Some of you had a dad who made you earn every scrap of approval, and the scraps were thin. This Father doesn't operate that way. If you're in Christ, that same voice of love and delight has already spoken over you. You didn't earn it, and a bad week doesn't cancel it. If you've got a newborn, watch yourself at the crib. You adore that baby, and he hasn't done one useful thing. That's how the Father sees you.

## He carries, and He stays

Moses described God carrying His people the way a dad carries his little boy, mile after mile, through the heavy years too. He knows what you need before you say it, and your needing doesn't annoy Him. If needing anybody used to be dangerous for you, slow down and let that land. When He corrects you, it's because you belong to Him. Hebrews calls hard seasons evidence that you're a son. Correction and rage are not the same thing. If He's correcting you, He hasn't left.

And He won't leave. Scripture promises that more than once. On the last night before the cross, with His friends about to see Him dragged away, Jesus said it like this.

:::margin
**John 14:18 NIV**
I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you.
:::

Orphans. He picked the word on purpose, because that's what they were about to feel. Some of you have felt it since second grade. His answer to orphans is Himself. He comes. If your marriage is over, you may need this more than anybody. The Father didn't walk out when the marriage did. Some of you quit praying because you figured His disappointment had joined the pile. He never stopped watching for you.

## Learning the receiving end

You'd expect this to be the light chapter. For a lot of men it's the heaviest. If you raised yourself, you never got to practice being anyone's son. You can sign off on every doctrine and still lock up the moment the load is supposed to shift onto someone else's shoulders. After enough years alone, leaning feels like falling.

Curtis put it plainly. "I believe God's my Father. I just don't know how to be somebody's son. I never got to be on the receiving end." I'd rather have that than something polished. Men who learn sonship late usually get there through a few simple habits.

Use the name. Keep "God" and "Lord" for your other prayers if you like, but somewhere every day, call Him Father, out loud, alone, while it's still awkward. Paul says the Spirit in you is already calling out "Abba, Father," and Abba is what a small boy calls his dad. You have permission. It'll feel odd. Push through until it doesn't. If you're older and have never once said it, give it a week of feeling silly. After that it starts to feel warm, like your hands thawing when you come in from the cold.

Take Him your escape, whichever one it is. He hears it before any brother or counselor, and He hears it raw. "Father, I'm close to losing it with the kids." "Father, I'm about to pick up that phone." The One listening has been watching for you a long time.

Let Him name you. Each day, read something God says about His children and answer back as though it's true of you, because it is. A man who was never told who he is needs to hear it over and over. The Father doesn't get tired of saying it.

Let yourself be carried. Sometime this year a load will come that's heavier than you are, and every reflex will say grind it out alone. Pray the opposite, out loud: "This is too heavy for me. Carry it, and carry me." Then let a brother take a corner. Every man is a son before he's anything else, and learning that order is the work.

Stay close to home. His Word is where He speaks. His church is where the rest of His kids gather. Prayer is where He listens. Show up only at Christmas and Easter, and His voice will stay a stranger's on an ordinary Wednesday.

## Your dad, seen again

Once God starts fathering you, the man who raised you, or didn't, looks different. Sometimes sharper, and sharper can sting. You quit squeezing him into a shape he never had, and you quit searching his face for it. If you're young and certain you don't need another father, learn this now and skip twenty years of searching faces. You can thank him for what was good, name what hurt, and put down the rage. Forgiveness opens up once you no longer need him to pay you back. You might even reach toward him someday. That road gets its own chapter. But none of it opens while you're still trying to pull out of your dad what only your heavenly Father has. That old chair was always too small for the hollow place in your chest. He's the only one who fits it.

## Before the bell

Curtis started practicing in the pickup line. Alone in a parked car, the awkwardness cost him nothing. "Father." The first few days it felt like talking to the dashboard. He kept going, one sentence each afternoon. Somewhere in the second week it stopped feeling like talking to glass. It felt like there was somebody in the passenger seat.

Then one day his youngest burst through the doors, looked down the line, saw their car, and quit searching. Curtis watched that small face change and realized what was being held out to him. For years he'd been the dad holding a place in line. He had never once been the kid who finds his ride. Now he was being told that part was his too.

Jesus told a story about a father like that, whose son blew everything and came home with an apology rehearsed. Look where the father is.

:::margin
**Luke 15:20 NIV**
But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him.
:::

A long way off. You only catch someone that far down the road if you've been watching it. In that culture, grown men didn't run. This one did. You don't have to clean up before He comes for you.

That's the Father's work in a man. The searching can stop. The tryout can stop. Your place with Him was settled in His Son, and today's performance was never the verdict. If your career is soaring, hear that one slowly: a title is not a Father, and neither is applause. John marveled at how much the Father loves us, enough to call us His children, and said that's exactly what we are. He was in the line before the bell. Let your shoulders drop. You've been found.

## Before you turn the page

He knows sons learn slowly. Say the word clumsily tomorrow morning, and again the morning after. May the Father who kept His eyes on the road for the prodigal keep them on you. And this week, walk out those doors and look for the One who's already waiting.
