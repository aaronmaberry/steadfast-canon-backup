# 18. Before the Fall

A seed packet is stuck to the fridge with a magnet, and it knows more than the backyard does. Tomato. Full sun. Plant after the last frost. Without that packet, the bare stretch in the third row is plain dirt. With it, you know a plant belongs there. A row can't fail until you know what it was meant to grow.

Get men talking and it comes around to their marriages, their tempers, their sons. Underneath, they're nearly always asking one thing: what was I supposed to be before I got bent? If you've never looked at the original, you can't sort damage from personality.

So let's go back to the first week, before anybody hid or blamed. What did God make when He made a man, back when He was still calling everything good?

## Dust, and breath

Start with the dirt.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:7 NIV**
Then the LORD God formed a man from the dust of the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living being.
:::

If you've ever knelt by a fire pit the morning after, found one orange coal under the ash, and put your face down near it to blow until it caught, you've seen a picture of this. Clay in His hands. Then His face close enough to breathe. Nobody assembled you at arm's length. God leaned in.

And what that dust was shaped to look like is the very first thing the Bible says about you. Sinner is coming. It isn't first.

:::margin
**Genesis 1:27 NIV**
So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them.
:::

Before your first trophy or your first wreck, you were made to resemble God. Nobody earns that, and failing doesn't erase it. Women bear it as fully as men. Manhood doesn't lift you a step above the women in your house. It's one of two ways of carrying one likeness, and God dreamed up both.

## Blessed first

God's opening words to the people He'd made weren't instructions. They were a blessing.

:::margin
**Genesis 1:28 NIV**
God blessed them and said to them, "Be fruitful and increase in number; fill the earth and subdue it. Rule over the fish in the sea and the birds in the sky and over every living creature that moves on the ground."
:::

Identity, then assignment. The Father keeps that order at the Jordan, calling Jesus His beloved Son before Jesus has preached a sermon or healed anyone.

And the assignment is no small one. Raw ground was meant to come alive under a man's care. That's why a fence line you set true, a faucet you finally fixed, or a table you built from rough boards makes you feel like you for an evening. That's the original wiring.

Subdue was never permission to bully. God governs by making things grow. Ruling only curdled into grinding people down after the fall. In Eden, ruling looked like tomatoes coming in.

If there's a new baby in your house, you're living inside be fruitful right now. Bone-tired doesn't mean you're off the design. It means the curse got heaped on top of the blessing.

## A place, and a job site

Look where God set him down.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:8 NIV**
Now the LORD God had planted a garden in the east, in Eden; and there he put the man he had formed.
:::

Adam wasn't turned loose to roam until he found himself. He had an address, and the address was part of how he was made.

So do you. Most men treat theirs like an accident, or a waiting room until real life starts. The apartment, the job site, the small town you keep meaning to leave: that's the plot God handed you. A man forever squinting at the next county never tends his own.

And that plot was a workplace from day one.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:15 NIV**
The LORD God took the man and put him in the Garden of Eden to work it and take care of it.
:::

Nobody has sinned yet. No curse has been spoken. And already there's soil under the man's nails.

Thorns came later. Work came first, and it was good. So a man with nothing worth doing isn't really resting. He's idling rough. A layoff, a retirement with no purpose in it, a back that can't do the old job anymore can flatten a man, because he was built to tend a garden and someone took it. Abe went hunting in that verse for a retirement clause once the paychecks quit. He stopped introducing himself as let go. "Same job," he said. "Different garden." Grandkids now, and younger men, and a wife who's carried plenty.

Take care of it means watch over it, guard it. The man who turned the soil also walked the fence. And part of what he guarded was a boundary God Himself set.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:16-17 NIV**
And the LORD God commanded the man, "You are free to eat from any tree in the garden; but you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, for when you eat from it you will certainly die."
:::

Listen to how God opens: free. The whole orchard is open except one tree. Adam got that straight from God before Eve was there, and keeping the garden meant keeping that line too, against any voice that might talk them over it. Something is about to slip through the fence.

## A day to stop

Men tend to skim this part.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:2-3 NIV**
By the seventh day God had finished the work he had been doing; so on the seventh day he rested from all his work. Then God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it he rested from all the work of creating that he had done.
:::

God wasn't tired. Rest is where good work is headed. He put the stopping day into the calendar before the man ever lifted a tool, and He called that day holy.

Picture a shop on Saturday evening. Every tool wiped and hung back on its painted outline, floor swept, lights off. That isn't a lazy shop. It's a shop that knows what it's for. You aren't a machine. Six days on, one day off the clock. Grinding through the seventh isn't a promotion. If the whole outfit calls you on Sunday, hear this: a business that can't spare you one day in seven has started to own you. A man who never stops goes numb, and numb isn't holy.

## Not good

Then, with all that good stacked up, God says something that stops you cold.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:18 NIV**
The LORD God said, "It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make a helper suitable for him."
:::

Every day so far had ended good. Then God looked at one man in a flawless garden, with meaningful work and God's own voice in his ear, and said, not good.

He hadn't sinned. He had God. And God still said solitary wasn't good. Needing people isn't a weakness that crept in after things broke. It was drawn into the plans.

## His first words

So God walked the animals past him.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:19-20 NIV**
Now the LORD God had formed out of the ground all the wild animals and all the birds in the sky. He brought them to the man to see what he would name them; and whatever the man called each living creature, that was its name. So the man gave names to all the livestock, the birds in the sky and all the wild animals. But for Adam no suitable helper was found.
:::

God let the man's words count. What he said, stuck.

Yours stick too. A boy grows toward whatever his father calls him. In a marriage, a husband's words become the walls she lives inside. Even a quiet man's few sentences land like hammer blows.

None of the animals was the answer. A good dog is fine company, but he needed someone like him and not like him. So God put him under, shaped the woman out of his side, and walked her over. Then, for the first time in Scripture, a human being speaks.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:23 NIV**
The man said, "This is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called 'woman,' for she was taken out of man."
:::

The first recorded words of a man were poetry, and the poem was about her. Ask a husband when he last said something to his wife that wasn't logistics, and watch him squint. The design is a man so taken with his bride that words come out of him. He leaves home and clings to her. Marriage is in the plans before anything breaks, and the clinging was his to do.

But hear not good wider than a wedding. Brothers. An older man out ahead of you. A church that knows your name. If you're divorced, I know that sentence can sting. God hasn't torn up the plan, and He isn't pushing you toward the altar either. This is what He's handing you. If you're single, you're inside that sentence, not outside it. Almost everything in this chapter was given to Adam while he was still on his own. A wedding doesn't make you a man. Tend your plot and walk your fence now.

## Nothing to hide

Then, right before it all breaks, one more line.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:25 NIV**
Adam and his wife were both naked, and they felt no shame.
:::

Fully seen, and not afraid. That's a man before the fall.

Think about closing day on a house. You walk through before the movers come. Empty rooms, late light across bare floors, not a scuff anywhere. Then the years happen. Crayon down the hallway, a dent where the dresser hit, a water ring under the window. One day you catch yourself talking as if the stains were the house. They aren't. It was good before the marks, and its Builder remembers exactly how it looked. Every locked room in a man since Eden is a long, sideways try at walking around unashamed again.

## What comes next

Picture him at the end of that first week, walking with God in the cool of the evening, hiding nothing from Him or from her. That's what you were made for. Next, we see what he did with it.

## Before you turn the page

Sit here a minute while the garden is still whole. Shaped by hand. Breathed into. Blessed before you'd done a thing. Given ground to tend and a day to stop. May the God whose likeness you carry remind you before you sleep tonight what He made you for.
