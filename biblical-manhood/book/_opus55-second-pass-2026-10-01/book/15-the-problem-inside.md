# 15. The Problem Inside

Cody's first truck had a bubble on the front fender about the size of his thumbnail. Every spring he sanded it to bright steel, primed it, sprayed on the closest match the parts store sold, and told himself it was handled. By every fall it was back, wider, with a couple of smaller ones gathered around it.

Third year, he gave up and drove it to a body shop. The man didn't say a word about Cody's handiwork. He pushed on the bubble with his thumb and the fender gave like soggy cardboard. Rusty water dribbled out. "Paint's not going to fix that," he said. "It's coming from the inside." Behind the panel, where none of the touch-ups had ever reached, the steel had been rotting all along. Cody had spent three years making the front of the problem look nice.

This is the hinge of the book. We've spent a lot of pages on what got handed to you: fathers who stayed or left, a blessing no one spoke, brothers who weren't there, older men you never went to. All of it shaped you. But I know men who've done every bit of that work. They left home. They asked the older man. They named the empty chair. Then they walked into the next room and wrecked it anyway. If this book ended with your father, I'd be giving you a can of primer and a pat on the back.

The trouble is in you. It's in me. Scripture has a one-syllable word for it, and as long as you won't put that word on yourself, you'll keep sanding.

## Say the word

It's sin. And men find two ways around saying it.

The first way is to trade it for something gentler. Struggle. Baggage. An issue. A struggle is weather; it lands on you. Sin is a choice; you did it. Weather you manage. A choice you repent of.

The second way belongs to men who heard the word used on them like a belt when they were boys. I'm sorry that happened. Those preachers never owned it, though. The word is God's, and when He says it, it's a doctor reading an X-ray, not a man winding up a punch. The body man wasn't insulting Cody. He was the first person to tell him the truth about his truck.

Sin bends a man inward. Left alone, it curls him around himself like a fist.

Before Cain lifted a hand against anybody, God told him sin was crouched at his door like something hungry, and that he had to rule over it. That warning cuts two ways. It's yours to answer for. It also isn't stronger than the God who's telling you to stand against it. Cain shrugged Him off, the crouching thing got him, and Abel never came home from the field. Same animal sits at every man's door.

## Even with a perfect father

Give yourself the dad you always wanted. Home at six. Gentle and strong. Said he loved you and you never doubted it. Put a hand on your shoulder at eighteen and blessed you. Surrounded you with brothers and found you an older man to walk behind.

You'd be steadier for it. You'd still lie to your wife about what the card got used for. You'd still take the second look. You'd still keep that private list where you rank a notch above the man in the next pew.

Adam is the proof. No wound, no bad father, a flawless garden, honest work, a wife who was pure gift. And he reached for the fruit, then hid in the trees and blamed her. What's broken in a man didn't begin with his dad. It passed through his dad, from Eden on down. The best home in the world couldn't have filtered it out. Jeremiah called the heart crooked beyond a man's own repair, and Jesus told men who polished their outsides daily where the real trouble lived.

:::margin
**Mark 7:23 NIV**
All these evils come from inside and defile a person.
:::

Your father, the culture, the wound: those may have opened doors. They didn't build what came through them. A newborn's sleepless months work the same way: they strip a man down to what was already there, but they don't put it there.

Here's why that's mercy and not just a hard word. If every bit of it was done to you, you're stuck, because there's no getting a new father. And you've got an excuse that never runs out. You can't repent of your dad's sin. You can only stew in it, repeating Adam's line about whoever God gave him. But when you own what's yours, God has something to forgive and something to rebuild. He isn't offering you a tidier account of your childhood. He's offering you a new heart.

If you're divorced, I'm not going to hand you the whole blame. God has the whole picture, and so do the two of you. You do have a share, and the healing starts when "I did this" can stand without "but she" stapled to the back.

:::margin
**Romans 5:8 NIV**
But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us.
:::

He didn't wait for us to get our story straight. He saw what was there and went to the cross for it. Use His word, then. The cure travels with that word.

## Wade

Wade's turn came in a twenty-minute check-in. Another man asked him what was weighing on him. He opened his mouth to say the job, then didn't. A long quiet. The evening before, his little girl had gone small in front of him. He hadn't hit her. He hadn't needed to. His voice climbed, she shrank, and he saw his own boyhood in her face. For years he'd filed that under temper. Sitting there, he called it sin.

The man across from him didn't look away. He said thank you. He asked what Wade was going to do about it. Then he prayed. Wade had expected to feel stripped. He felt washed, and he couldn't remember the last time.

:::margin
**James 5:16 NIV**
Therefore confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person is powerful and effective.
:::

Notice the word healed. Forgiven is a verdict. Healed is what happens to a man after the verdict, when somebody who loves him has heard the worst and stayed in his chair.

## What actually holds

Paul wrote down every man's week. I want the good thing and don't do it. I hate the bad thing and do it again. You promised yourself patience with the kids and you barked. Then he asks, who will rescue me? A who. You don't get rescued by a method.

I've watched a lot of men run programs, and most of them land on try harder, which is just primer with a new label. A capable man can run his life on skill for years, and a polished résumé still isn't a pure heart. What holds is plainer.

Say it to God, out loud, specifically. Not "I've had a rough stretch." I lied to her about the money. Reading the Sunday confession along with everyone isn't the same as naming your own sin alone in the cab, and a man who's kept the paint fresh for fifty years can forget there's steel underneath. David said that keeping quiet dried him out to the bone, and that when he finally spoke, forgiveness met him. One sentence stood between the two.

Tell guilt and shame apart. Guilt points at the deed: that was wrong, and it was me. Shame points at you: you are the wrong thing. Guilt is the rusty water on the fender, ugly and accurate, and it settles once you've said it. Shame never settles. It keeps up its lies and herds a man back to his temper, his bottle, his screen, or his long silence, the very things he leans on to dull it.

Then change direction. Feeling rotten can go on for years without the feet moving. Repenting moves them. Confess. Repair what you can. Put something in the way of tomorrow's door. Get back to your work. Do it again tomorrow if tomorrow needs it. From the inside, following Jesus looks a lot like a son who keeps drifting and a Father who keeps opening the door.

And ask for a new interior. Fresh off the worst thing he ever did, David didn't ask God to fix his situation.

:::margin
**Psalm 51:10 NIV**
Create in me a pure heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me.
:::

Rot coming from behind the steel shrugs off another coat. The panel has to be replaced, and only God does that kind of bodywork, at any age.

## New steel

The body man cut the rotten section out and welded in a new one. That fender stayed smooth. Cody stood in that shop feeling like a fool for three springs of wasted paint, and lighter than he'd felt in a while, because somebody had finally said the true thing, and it had a fix.

Letting go of the case against your dad and naming your own sin goes about the same way. A short sting of foolishness. Then a deep relief. The trouble inside has a name, the name has a cure, and the One who saw behind your panel all along has already covered the cost.

Everything this book asks of you from here on starts with the paint can put away and the word said out loud.

## Before you turn the page

I've asked you to aim the hardest word in the language at yourself. Under it is very good news: God does His work from the inside, and the price is already paid. May the Father who forgave David the instant he confessed meet you the instant you do, and once the foolish feeling passes, may you know the lightness of a man who is finally fixing what was really wrong.
