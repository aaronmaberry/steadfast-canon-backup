# 26. Your Daughter Is Watching

From the second pew at a wedding, you catch a moment most of the room misses. The bride gets to the end of the aisle. Her dad's hand is still on her arm. And before she turns to the groom, she glances at her father. It lasts maybe half a second. She's been giving him that glance since before she could talk.

Most dads think she's asking permission. She's doing something bigger. She's checking the face she learned the world from. That face told her what a man is, what a woman ought to be able to count on, and what she herself is worth. The groom standing two steps away got picked with that face as the reference.

Your daughter is watching you. She's already learning three things from you that she'll carry the rest of her life, and none of them waited for you to sit her down.

## A face in the stands

Wade, the man we met earlier whose little girl shrank when his voice climbed, found this out on an aluminum bleacher at his daughter Nora's softball game. She struck out to end the inning, and before she made it back to the dugout she turned, found him in the stands, and studied his face. He'd watched her do that a hundred times. He'd never asked himself what she was looking for.

She was checking what the strikeout cost her. Hits made Wade light up and get loud. Misses made his face sink a little, nothing cruel, just a dad who wanted it for her. But she'd learned to watch for the sink. As far as she could tell, her value went up and down with the box score.

On the ride home he said, "I don't come to watch you hit. I come to watch you." Then he went to work on his own face, which was harder than any speech, because kids can spot gladness that's put on. Before every pitch, Wade decided what his face would do next. Whatever she saw after a strikeout had to match what she saw after a double. It took a season. She quit checking after the misses. These days she looks up after the hits, because she wants somebody to celebrate with. Wade says that's the gap between a girl who's still checking and a girl who already knows.

## What she's learning

She's learning what she's worth. Nora read it off a face in the stands, and your daughter reads it off yours. Attention she has to earn with grades or trophies or looks teaches one thing. Attention she gets for being yours teaches another. A father's delight settles the question so she doesn't have to go hunting for the answer. Hold it back and she'll go find a guy who's eager to say what you never did. The ones who volunteer for that are seldom men you'd have chosen.

She's learning what a man is. You're the first definition she gets, and every man after you is measured against it. Gentle at home, and a harsh man will shock her. Stormy at home, and she'll either marry the weather or spend her life bracing for it. When she watches you walk first into the hard apology, the awkward phone call, the conversation everybody else is avoiding, that's Go First, and she's the one taking notes.

She's learning what a woman can expect, from how her mother gets treated under your roof. Thanked or ignored. Defended or corrected in front of company. Heard out, or met with the eye roll and the three-day freeze. She's keeping notes, and the man she marries will line up with them. Want a preview of the guy who'll come for your daughter someday? Watch how you treat her mom between now and Sunday.

## What she needs from you

Delight comes first, and it has to be visible. Approval is something a kid earns by performing. Delight is something she receives for being herself. That's how God is with His own. He takes joy in His children and sings over them.

:::margin
**Zephaniah 3:17 NIV**
The LORD your God is with you, the Mighty Warrior who saves. He will take great delight in you; in his love he will no longer rebuke you, but will rejoice over you with singing.
:::

Let your face change when she walks into the kitchen. Gladness that never leaves your chest gives her nothing to read, and she's reading anyway.

She needs time with you, or the delight stays a mood you were in once. A standing breakfast. A drive with the radio off. A walk after supper that survives the years. And she needs a father with backbone when she pushes him away. Plenty of daughters go through a stretch of wanting nothing to do with Dad, and wounded men take the hint and drift off. She's testing whether you'll stay. Keep the breakfast through the silent months. Say "I love you" through a shut door. When that season ends, she'll know who didn't go anywhere.

She needs words. Girls grow toward the names their fathers call them. Beautiful, meaning all of her. Strong. Mine. "I love you," out loud, every day. One day some boy is going to use that sentence as a key, and it opens fastest on a girl who's been starved for it. Job did something unheard of in his day: he gave his daughters an inheritance right beside their brothers. His name and his weight backed up what they were worth.

She needs to see you honor her mother. Thank her at the table. Take her side. When you blew it, apologize where the kids can hear. Few things will count for more with a girl. Divorce doesn't end that lesson; she's listening to how you talk about her mom from across town. When she tries to recruit you to a side, have one answer and keep giving it: "Your mom loves you. She's doing the best she can. And I won't talk about her like that." You'll see her shoulders drop. She'd been scared she would have to choose.

She needs a guard. God told the first man to keep the garden, and nothing in your keeping matters more than she does. Lock her in a tower and she learns she's breakable and men are the bars. Better is an open yard with a gate where you stand watch. You know what's on her phone and you've told her why. Any boy who wants an evening with her gets an hour with you first. Long before she needs it, she knows the plan if a date's hands wander: say no, get out, call you, and you'll come. And you guard her from yourself, too: the temper, the sarcasm, the bad day that lands on her because she's nearby. Paul has a word for fathers about exactly this.

:::margin
**Ephesians 6:4 NIV**
Fathers, do not exasperate your children; instead, bring them up in the training and instruction of the Lord.
:::

She needs to see you pray, and to see where your eyes go. Bring her name to God and let her know you did; a girl whose dad kneels learns to recognize that in a man. She notices when your gaze drifts at the pool or the restaurant. Roving eyes tell her women are scenery. Faithful eyes tell her it's sane to hope for a faithful man.

## The Man who took her hand

Jesus was tender with daughters. A synagogue leader's little girl had died, and Jesus went into the room where she lay.

:::margin
**Luke 8:54 NIV**
But he took her by the hand and said, "My child, get up!"
:::

Then He handed her back to her father. Follow that Man. He was gentle with a girl who wasn't even His.

## If your season looks different

If you're divorced, you'll miss some breakfasts. Don't run down her mother, whether she baits you, whether the complaint is true, whether you're worn out. Half of that girl comes from her mom, so every shot you take hits your daughter. Show up for every hour the custody order gives you, and fight for a few more. Call on the same night each week, even through the long run of "fine" and "nothing." The night she finally opens up was bought by all the nights she didn't. Give her a real place at your house, her own room, her toothbrush in the cup. The divorce shook the ground under her. A dad who's present, clean, and still growing gives her somewhere solid to stand.

If she's grown, she's still watching. Did Dad change? Will he show up for my kids where he didn't for me? Your blessing doesn't expire when she moves out. If you failed her, tell her so plainly, with no excuse stapled to the end. If you've never told her you're proud of her, the call is overdue. Make it anyway. It'll mend a crack she stopped noticing years ago.

If there's no daughter at your house, you're still teaching. Some of you are nineteen with a little sister who reads every move you make. Girls at your church are short on this, so let them see a good man up close. Honor women where they can see it. Coach a team. Be the uncle who shows up and stays in the light. Never alone behind a closed door with a girl who isn't yours. Stay visible, stay above reproach. One of the things they need to learn is that a good man can be safe.

## Worth looking for

She's been studying your face since before she had words. The end of an aisle may be the last time she checks it, and she'll find there what she's always found. Make it worth finding.

## Before you turn the page

A face that's been teaching the wrong thing can learn the right one, and beating yourself up won't speed that along; she reads guilt as easily as gladness. Wade rebuilt his the way you rebuild a weak muscle, rep by rep, on ordinary afternoons. May the Father who lights up when His children come home light up your face this week, and may your daughter see how glad you are she's alive.
