# 31. Feelings Are the Dash, Not the Wheel

My friend Clay learned to drive a tractor from Vernon the summer he got big enough to be worth something out there. Vernon rode the fender and Clay got the seat. Before Vernon would let him steer, Clay had to call out every gauge, oil, water, amps, like a pilot running a checklist. "Those don't lie about the machine," Vernon told him. "Read 'em every pass." Then he pointed at the line he'd cut the trip before. "That's your road. Put your right wheel in that and leave it there."

So of course Clay stared at the gauges like a ballgame was on and put a curve in the whole field. Vernon didn't yell. One knuckle on the glass of the water gauge, then a finger aimed back at the furrow.

Weeks later they were dragging a disc up the long side and the engine started heating. Clay's row looked perfect, so he watched the needle climb and kept going. Vernon heard it in the motor. "You looking at that?" Yes sir. "Then why haven't you done anything about it?" Clay had nothing. He says he's still chewing on that question.

Here's what Clay eventually figured out. The gauge was honest about the motor. It had no clue where the fence was. Ignore it and you cook the engine. Steer by it and you're all over the pasture. Feelings work the same way. They're the dash. They report. Somebody else has to drive.

## The One who felt everything

This chapter isn't about shutting feelings off. Look at Jesus. He cried at a friend's grave. In the garden He told His closest friends the sorrow was about to kill Him. Hebrews remembers loud cries and tears. Every needle on His panel moved.

None of them took the wheel. He asked the Father for another way, laid His will under His Father's, and walked toward the cross, the exact thing every reading in His body wanted to avoid. Felt, spoken, obeyed. That's the pattern for this whole chapter.

## Two ways into the ditch

Nearly every man I know ends up in one ditch or the other.

One man tapes over the dash. Ask him and he's "good." He says frustrated when he's enraged, worn out when he's grieving, busy when he hasn't had a real conversation since spring. The tape doesn't stop the readings. They leak out sideways, as temper, as appetite late at night, as a man who's home but not really there. He swears he's not emotional. His emotions have had the keys for years. Often he's the man who's best at work. He overrides every feeling from eight to six, and his family gets the leaks after the garage door closes.

Plenty of older men were raised with electrical tape over the whole cluster. It isn't too late to learn the names: grief as friends die off, fear of what your body's doing, a widower's loneliness. Go be with the people you still have.

The second guy gives the keys to the needle on purpose. "I don't love her like I used to." "God feels far, so maybe this faith thing isn't for me." Honest words, disastrous turn. Marriages end because a mood moved. Pews empty because a warm feeling cooled. He gets praised for his honesty and ends up nowhere.

Scripture gives us another way through. Say every reading out loud, to God and to a brother who knows you. Then take your direction from what stays put when the forecast changes.

## What the readings mean

Nobody explained the panel to most of us, so here goes.

Fear: something you love feels exposed, and you're not sure your arms are long enough. Ask what you're scared to lose. Anger: a wrong, real or perceived. Dig under it, because anger is usually fear or shame with its jaw set. Sadness: a loss. Grieve it on purpose. Men skip this reading most, and unattended grief turns into a slow drift. Loneliness: God looked at a man alone before sin ever entered the world and said that wasn't good, so this one is as trustworthy as they come. It's an instruction to go find men, not a verdict on what you're worth. Single men, let it point you at brothers, and turn down whatever promises the fastest relief. Numbness: you've redlined so long the sensor fried. Quiet isn't always peace. Shame: not "I did wrong," but "I am wrong." Hold it to Scripture, because it's the biggest liar on the panel. Guilt is working fine when it points to a thing you did. Joy: you've come close to the work God built you for. Notice what your hands were doing.

Open the Psalms and you'll find strong men reading their gauges out loud. David said things to the Lord most of us wouldn't say to our wives.

:::margin
**Psalm 42:5 NIV**
Why, my soul, are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me? Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise him, my Savior and my God.
:::

First breath, he reads the gauge. Second breath, he grabs the wheel and turns toward God. That's the manual.

## What steers instead

Four things don't swing with the weather.

God's Word. If your heart says one thing and Scripture says another, Scripture is describing what's real. Your heart is describing your morning.

Your vows. You made a promise in front of God and people. Promises exist for the days the feeling goes missing.

Your brothers. They ride in the passenger seat, so they read your dash from an angle you never get.

The marks. They don't drift with your mood either. When you can't tell what to do next, one of the four is usually lying at your feet. Pick that one up.

Back to Vernon's field. The water temp rises on a long pull. You can't pretend you didn't see it, and you can't drive toward it, because a gauge isn't a direction. So you name it: she's running hot. You ease out of the row, let her cool, check the radiator, and slide back into the furrow you'd already started. Your driving adjusted. Your destination didn't move an inch.

## What a mood can't settle

Your forgiveness. Christ settled that on a cross. "I don't feel forgiven" is a reading, and readings don't reopen closed cases.

Your marriage. Every couple married long enough hits a stretch where that needle sits on E. Drive by it and you'll end up in a lawyer's office. Keep your grip on the covenant, and a decade on it can lift again over a marriage your feelings never could have built. Married men, your wife is not your dash. You don't steer by her mood, and she shouldn't have to steer by yours. Young and single, the same goes for the wife you hope for. She's not your thermostat. Freshly divorced? For a year or two you'll see readings you didn't know the panel could show. Take every one to God and your brother, and leave the big controls alone: no move, no quick remarriage, no quitting the job or the church. Broken gauges mean slow and straight until they're fixed.

God's will. "I feel led" can be a holy sentence, and it can be a mood wearing a robe. Put it next to Scripture and in front of the men who know you best. Ask whether it lines up with the marks or just points at the exit.

:::margin
**Proverbs 28:26 NIV**
Those who trust in themselves are fools, but those who walk in wisdom are kept safe.
:::

Hunger, exhaustion, and a gray week bend every needle. Midnight decisions belong to the weather. Sleep, then talk it over in daylight with a brother. A new dad lives in that weather, every gauge pegged and none reliable. No marriage talks at 3 a.m. Pick a phrase together now: "That's the tired talking. We'll pick this up after a nap."

## The broom bush

This is the heaviest end of the panel, and men die there. For some of you this isn't a blue week. It's been dark for months. Elijah was a day removed from calling down fire on Carmel when he ran into the wilderness alone and prayed this.

:::margin
**1 Kings 19:4 NIV**
while he himself went a day's journey into the wilderness. He came to a broom bush, sat down under it and prayed that he might die. "I have had enough, LORD," he said. "Take my life; I am no better than my ancestors."
:::

Look how God answered. No rebuke. An angel woke him with bread and water, let him sleep, then fed him again. Only after the meals and the rest came God's voice, a new assignment, and a companion.

So tell God what you're reading, and then tell a person. If your reading is "I'm done," a human being needs to hear it from your mouth this week. And look after your body. God's opening move with a suicidal prophet was a meal and a nap.

:::margin
**Psalm 103:14 NIV**
for he knows how we are formed, he remembers that we are dust.
:::

Then get real help. Doctors and counselors aren't outside of faith. They're how God still delivers bread and water. If the dark has lasted weeks, if you've gone numb and stayed there, if you've thought about ending your life, see a doctor and tell them all of it. If they write a prescription, fill it like insulin. You're repairing a gauge so it works. Scripture still holds the wheel.

If you're thinking about killing yourself tonight, put this book down and call somebody. A brother. Your pastor. If there's truly no one, dial 988. Someone will answer whose whole job is sitting with men in the dark. Elijah prayed to die and God served him breakfast. Let someone find you under your bush. Elijah didn't wait to feel better before going back to work. He went with a friend, in small steps, and his heart caught up later. Food. Rest. A brother. One small assigned thing. Stay there until things come back into range.

## Before you turn the page

If your gauge has read dark for a long stretch, you have one job today: tell a man. If it hasn't, tell God the truth about your dash in a single sentence tomorrow morning, then take hold of the wheel. He knows how you were formed. He remembers you are dust. May He give your body rest and keep your tire in the furrow.
