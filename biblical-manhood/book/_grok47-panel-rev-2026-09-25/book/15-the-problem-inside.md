# 15. The Problem Inside

My first truck grew a blister in the fender paint, about the width of a thumb. Spring after spring I took the spot to bare metal, primed it, and covered it again. Fall after fall it returned, larger, and brought two smaller blisters with it.

Year three, a body man got the truck. His thumb went into the blister and the metal yielded. A little brown water seeped. "Paint's not going to fix that," he said. "It's coming from the inside." On the back of that panel, where no paint had ever reached, rust had been feeding the whole time I was prettying the face of it.

The turn in the book is here. Inheritance has been the subject. Fathers who stayed and fathers who did not. Blessings that were never spoken. Missing brothers. Older men never asked. Those things shaped you. End the book on them and I have handed you primer and a driveway. Something in you will not yield to a better theory of your father.

You are the trouble. I am. So is every man. Scripture keeps a plain word for it. Coat the blister forever if you will not say the word.

## Even with a perfect father

Imagine the good version. Father home each night. Kind, present, strong. Love spoken. A blessing given as you entered manhood. Brothers around, and a man ahead of you on top of that.

Are you then a good man? Improved, probably. Good? Are the lies to your wife gone? The look you were not free to take? The private ranking that puts you above the man one pew over?

The first man answers you. Nothing around Adam was damaged. Father, place, work, and wife were whole, and no wound had touched him. He took the fruit anyway, or he stood mute while it was taken. He hid. He blamed. A man's inward trouble did not begin as a complaint against his dad. It came down through that dad from the garden. Saintly raising would not have kept it out of you. Jeremiah called the heart crooked beyond a man's own cure. Jesus said as much to religious men who were managing the exterior with great care.

:::margin
Mark 7:23 (NIV)
"All these evils come from inside and defile a person."
:::

Inside, He says. Father, culture, and wound opened doors. They did not create what came through.

It sounds like a hard page. The door is in the hardness. Blame the whole thing on what was done to you and you have no exit, because a father cannot be un-had. File the entire trouble under inheritance and you have also filed a permanent excuse. Another man's act is not a thing you can repent. You will spend your years on the excuse Adam used. The father I was given did this. Own the trouble and God has something to deal with, since He deals in what is yours. The offer on the table is a rebuilt you.

:::margin
Romans 5:8 (NIV)
"But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us."
:::

Still sinners. That is when Christ died. Not after we had sorted our wounds into a cleaner story about absent fathers. He named the trouble and died for the trouble. Name it with Him. The cure is attached to the name.

## The word

Sin is the word. Men avoid it along two paths.

One path calls the word too harsh and reaches for softer ones. Struggle. Issue. Baggage. A struggle lands on you. Sin is done by you. Management fits the first. Repentance fits the second.

The other path remembers churches that swung the word like a weapon at boys. Those men do not own the word. God does. In His mouth it diagnoses. The man at the shop was not swinging at me. He was first to tell the truth.

Sin bends a man in toward himself. Stand up, and he hides. Own it, and he blames. Give, and he takes. Speak, and he goes quiet. Adam, in the garden. You, last night. God had already warned Cain, before any hand was lifted on his brother. Sin crouched at the door. It wanted him. He was told to rule it. An animal, then, and a command. The command means you answer for it, and the command means you are not stranded. Cain refused. The animal took him. His brother died in a field. Same crouching thing. Same doorway. The reader is not exempt.

## What to do with it

The sentence Paul wrote covers the room. I fail at the good I intend. I repeat the very thing I hate. Patience with the children is the aim, and patience is what you miss. Presence with your wife is the aim, and the phone is where the hand goes. His question is the one that leads somewhere. Who will rescue me? He is asking for a person. No system answers a who.

Programs, in my experience, collapse into try harder. Try harder is fresh paint. What I have seen hold is smaller than a program.

Say the word about yourself. Not a vague report of stuff you have. I sinned. Aloud. To God. Particular. The lie I told her about the money. David said the silence went to work on his bones. Confession came, and forgiveness with it. Those two conditions were one honest sentence apart.

Separate guilt from shame, or shame will steal the confession. Guilt says the deed was wrong and the deed was mine. Shame says I am the wrong. Guilt is the blister, unpleasant and accurate. After you have said the true thing, guilt can sit down. Shame keeps lying. It drives a man back to anger, or appetite, or absence, because those were built to numb the feeling shame keeps feeding.

Turning is the next act. A sour mood can last decades with the feet still aimed the old direction. Repentance changes the direction. Confess. Repair what can be repaired. Make the door harder to use tomorrow. Return to your work. Turn again when tomorrow comes. From the inside, that is the Christian life. A man keeps returning to the Father he keeps leaving. The Father keeps receiving what returns.

And a brother has to hear it. Dark is where sin lives. A man who loves you and remains in the room takes the dark away when the sin is spoken.

:::margin
James 5:16 (NIV)
"Therefore confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person is powerful and effective."
:::

Wade said it about himself to another man for the first time inside the twenty minutes. The question had been what was heavy. Work was the word he started, and then he stopped. Quiet held. He had scared his daughter the night before. He never touched her. Touch was unnecessary. His voice rose, she got small, the way he once got small, and temper had been his name for it across the years. He called it sin. The other man waited. He thanked him. He asked what came next. He prayed. Wade had expected exposure. Clean is what arrived, the first time in years he could remember the feeling. James is talking about healing there. Healing is more than a verdict of forgiven.

Then ask for a steadfast spirit. David's worst sin was behind him, and the prayer he prayed was not for better circumstances. He wanted a new inside.

:::margin
Psalm 51:10 (NIV)
"Create in me a pure heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me."
:::

Rust coming through from behind the steel will laugh at another coat. The metal has to be made over. God does that, and God keeps doing it. A man who has stopped decorating the front is the man who can pray it.

## The same rust in every season

Learn this while you are single and you will enter a marriage holding what a lot of husbands only find after damage. A new baby will introduce a man to parts of himself the year will not let him hide. Exhaustion peels paint. The thing underneath was already there. It only became visible. If you are divorced, you may be braced for me to call the whole ending yours. I will not. The whole is known to God and to the two of you. Your portion exists. Healing begins when the sentence "I did this" is allowed to end, with her portion not glued to the period. Midlife competence can run the exterior of a life for decades. Competence is a different country from righteousness. Old age can run that exterior so long the interior is forgotten. A confession spoken with the congregation is not the same as your own sin, named, in the truck. New metal is offered at every age.

## The right thing

He cut the ruined panel out. New steel went in. The blister did not return. Two things hit me in that shop at once. Three springs and good money, spent on the wrong repair, and the foolishness of it. Relief, because the truth had finally been said by somebody, and the truth included a fix.

Laying down the case against your father and using the word on yourself feels the same. A short spell of foolishness. Then relief, deep. There is a name for the inward trouble. A cure sits with the name. The Man who knew your contents already paid it.

The marks begin when the paint can is set down and the word is spoken.

## Before you turn the page

I have asked you to turn the hardest word in the language on your own chest. Under it is good news. God deals with the inside, and the price is paid. May the Father who met David with forgiveness the moment the words were out meet you in that same moment, and may the far side of the foolishness be the relief of a man repairing the thing that was actually wrong.
