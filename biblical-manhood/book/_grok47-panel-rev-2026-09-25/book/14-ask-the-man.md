# 14. Ask the Man

Young as I was, I thought a long marriage could be explained if you caught the right man before he left the lot. After church I put the theory to Harlan. He had been married longer than I had been alive. We were at the driver's door. I still cannot tell you where the question came from.

He looked at me long enough to decide. The words he chose were, "Ride with me Saturday."

That Saturday I took the right-hand seat in the truck he hauled feed with and rode the route. Around noon he stopped for fuel and used the pay phone. His wife got the town and the hour he would reach the driveway. I learned later he had made that call on every working day since the wedding. Two more Saturdays followed. The cab outtaught the sermons I had stored on marriage.

Somebody sits about two decades farther down the road under your boots. He has already been through what you are entering. He is near. The asking is yours.

## Two men, both waiting

Silence on the young end has its reasons. The older man seems to have no spare hour. Or manhood, the young one tells himself, means sorting your own mess. Or his father owed him the lesson, never paid it, and borrowing the lesson from a second man would advertise the lack. Silence on the old end has reasons too. An old opinion, he figures, is the last thing a young man wants. Or the mistakes piled so high that nothing in them is fit to hand down. So they wait each other out. Boys become men with nobody twenty years on to describe the next mile.

A chain is the picture Scripture actually draws. Joshua had Moses ahead of him. Elisha had Elijah. Timothy had Paul, and Paul would not let the line die in Timothy's hands.

:::margin
2 Timothy 2:2 (NIV)
"And the things you have heard me say in the presence of many witnesses entrust to reliable people who will also be qualified to teach others."
:::

Four generations sit in that charge. Paul, Timothy, reliable men, and the people those men will teach. In plenty of churches and plenty of houses, both ends are empty. A brother knows your trouble because the same weather is on him. The man ahead knows it because he has come out the far side and can tell you what is over there. You need the two of them. One cannot stand in for the other.

## What to ask for

Mentor is the wrong opener. It sounds like an office. An older man hears a promise he may not keep and a title he may not have earned, so he refuses, or he agrees and then cannot be found.

Ask something narrow. What has held the marriage all these years? He can answer in words, or point at the empty seat the way Harlan did. How did you know it was time to leave that job? What would you redo with your children if those years came back? What did the year after the divorce actually take out of you? May I watch you work?

One question is a finished ask. A good hour earns another question next week. If he offers the truck, go ready to be useful. If you want a fence on it, use twenty minutes. Could we take twenty minutes a week, by phone, for two months? A few things are still cloudy from where I stand. I doubt they are cloudy from where you stand.

## How to pick him

Find a man roughly two decades ahead who is living the four marks. Leave the impressive man to the people who need impressing. You need a man who took a road like yours and is still upright at the far end you can see.

Watch his wife's face while he talks. Decades with a real man and decades with a performance put different looks on a woman, and she is not confused about which she has. Distrust the man who only reports wins. He is hiding the losses, or he never saw them. Either way he cannot take you where you need to go. You came for scars, and for the noon call he still makes.

He should be a man you would accept as your own life at his age, in what will still matter, and a man you can actually reach. No name means you are looking too high. He may be the deacon people thank and never question, or the man on the last pew, married longer than you have been breathing, who has not had a straight talk with anyone half as old in years. He may be the retired man who lingers at the hardware counter. More of them are around you than the excuse allows, and the silence from your side is what they have been living with.

## Once you've asked

Bring a real question every time. An older man will keep opening his life to that. Come only to hang around and he will drift. The question was the honor.

Then do what he said, at least once, and bring back what happened. Older men have emptied counsel on young men who nodded and changed nothing, and they learned to stop. Act, report, and he will hand you what he has.

Do not hire him to be your father. That weight will break him. He can give you a great deal. He cannot fill the particular hole your father left. Demand it and you will wear him out, then leave disappointed.

Thank him out loud, and name the help. I did what you said about my dad, and something moved. Men his age go a long time without a sentence that specific.

## To the man who's ahead

Half of you are the older man.

The man he lacks is you. Unfinished is the normal condition. Nobody graduates. Paul, in the week he died, was still on about a race he had not finished. A younger man is just now entering country you have already crossed, washouts included. A spotless record is not the requirement. He needs the truth about the places you failed, and he needs the ordinary practices, on ordinary days, that held the house together anyway.

:::margin
Titus 2:6-7 (NIV)
"Similarly, encourage the young men to be self-controlled. In everything set them an example by doing what is good."
:::

An example, and a word next to it, on an ordinary week. That is the plan. No program required.

He will not start. You will. Busy is the story he has about you, and you leave him uneasy. Give him an easy yes. Saturday I am hanging a gate, and I need a second man if you want the skill. Skip the speech once he is there. Ask what work is doing to him, how it is with her, and what is heavy. Asked, and then given quiet, a young man will tell an older man what he would never tell a peer.

Say where you failed. Let him hear a man he respects admit he nearly lost her, and that the fault was his. Something in the young man opens, and he can stop performing. The scar is the class.

Stand on the same side of a job. Shared work teaches more than a table does. Keep it small. Twenty minutes in a week, or a job together every other Saturday. A young man who never had anyone reliable will learn more from eleven straight weeks of you showing up than from your best talk on any one of those weeks.

You will receive more than you give. The older men who have stayed with this land on one report. I thought I was the help. Mostly he was keeping me alive. If the badge or the hard hat is hung up and the mornings are quiet, this is a use for them. God puts lonely people into families, young and old, in the same season.

## The man ahead, in every season

A single man should ask a husband how a marriage is kept, and he should ask before he has a wife. One year of questions beats three years of learning by wreck. If a baby is in your house, find a father whose children are already raised. Ask what he would change about the little years, while the answer can still be spent. Shame will tell a divorced man to skip this. He needs a man ahead as much as anyone here. Find someone who has lived through a marriage ending, or a long-married man who will not make you his exhibit, and ask him to walk the next stretch. Midlife stands in both directions. There is a man ahead he has never asked about a quiet house, and a man behind, watching a marriage fray, he has never spoken to. The chain needs him looking forward and back. A man whose own dad never offered this is not ruled out. The life he lived is what he can give.

## Ride with me

Years of Saturdays followed that first summer in Harlan's truck. Hundreds of questions, by my count. Often the answer was a gear. He has been gone awhile. My wedding day, he was seated in the last pew. Afterward he had my hand. "Well. You paid attention."

I had asked. He had told me to get in. We had not stood around assigning the first move to each other.

Ask him. Later, be him. Time alone will not mend the chain.

## Before you turn the page

The broken links are not a private guilt you have to solve before you can move. Repair belongs to the men who are reading. Younger, you bring one honest question. The foolishness lasts about eight seconds. You come back the week after. Older, you offer the seat. May the Father who gathers men into companies set a Harlan near you, make you that man for someone coming up, and show both of you how thin the busyness was that you had been pleading.
