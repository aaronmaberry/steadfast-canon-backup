# 26. Your Daughter Is Watching

Weddings put me up front, so I catch a look the guests miss. She reaches the end of the aisle. Her father's hand is still on her arm. She looks at him before she looks at the groom. Half a second. She has been doing that look since before she could say what it was for.

He hears a request for permission. She is reading the only face she has ever used as a dictionary. It taught her what a man is, what a woman may expect from one, and what she herself is worth. The man waiting six feet away was chosen with that dictionary open.

Your daughter is watching. Three lessons are already in her, headed for her grave, and none of them waited on a speech from you.

## The three lessons

First, what a man is. You are the entry she will use for the rest of her life. Every man after you gets measured on it. Gentleness in you teaches her to expect it, so a harsh man will startle her. A storm in you teaches her to marry weather, or to spend her years flinching at it. Choose the definition. The four marks, as she sees you live them, become the word.

Second, what a woman may expect. She takes that from the way her mother is treated in your house. Defense, thanks, a gentle hand, a listened-to sentence. Or the eye roll, or three days of ice. She is writing it down, and the husband she picks will match the notes. Want to know the man coming for your daughter? Look at this week's treatment of her mother. That is the forecast.

Third, what the girl is worth. She reads it off your face when you look at her. Attention she has to purchase, with grades or goals or pretty, teaches one lesson. Attention given because she is yours teaches another. Delight from a father settles a girl's worth so she does not have to shop for it. Withhold the delight and she will go find a man willing to say the sentence you never said. The volunteers for that job are rarely the men you would have picked.

Three lessons, already in place before breakfast, whether you meant to teach or not.

## The face in the bleachers

Wade met the third lesson in the bleachers at his daughter Nora's softball game. She struck out to end an inning. Before the dugout, she turned, found him, and read the face. He had watched her do it a hundred times. He had never asked what the look was hunting.

It was hunting the strikeout. Hits made him loud and proud. Misses dropped his face a notch, a father wanting the hit for her, nothing mean in it. She had learned to check for the drop. By her math, her worth traveled with the count.

In the car he told her, "I don't come to watch you hit. I come to watch you." Then he took his own face on as the work, and the work is harder than a speech. Children spot a gladness that was put on for them. Before each pitch he decided what his face would do after it. The face she found on a strikeout had to be the face she found on a double. A season of that, and she quit checking after the misses. She looks up after the hits now, wanting somebody to share them. Wade calls that the difference between a girl still checking and a girl who already knows.

## What she needs from you

Start with delight, and put it where she can see it. Approval pays for a performance. Delight pays for a person. God does this. He delights in His child and sings over her. A face that changes when she comes through the door. Yours can do that. Look up. Let the gladness be visible. If it is only inside you, she has nothing to read, and she is reading anyway.

:::margin
Zephaniah 3:17 (NIV)
"The LORD your God is with you, the Mighty Warrior who saves. He will take great delight in you; in his love he will no longer rebuke you, but will rejoice over you with singing."
:::

Honor her mother in her line of sight. Thanks at the table. Her side taken. An apology the children hear when you were the one who was wrong. For a daughter, little else you do will outweigh that. Divorce does not retire the lesson. She is studying the way you speak of her mother from the other house. When she baits you into a side, keep one answer. Your mom loves you. She is doing her best. I will not talk about her in that tone. The girl loosens. The fear had been that she would have to pick a parent.

Then guard her. God's second verb to the first man was keep. She is the dearest thing inside your keeping. A locked tower teaches her she is breakable and that men are the lock. A fence with a gate, and you at the gate, is the other picture. You know the phone. You have said why. A boy who wants to take her out meets you before he meets the evening. Before she needs the sentence, she has heard what to do if his hands go where they have no right. No. Leave. Call. You come. Guard her from yourself in the same breath. Temper, sarcasm, a bad day that uses her as the landing. Paul told fathers to stop exasperating their children. A girl worn thin by her father's moods files men under exhausting.

:::margin
Ephesians 6:4 (NIV)
"Fathers, do not exasperate your children; instead, bring them up in the training and instruction of the Lord."
:::

Words next. She will grow in the direction of the names you use. Beautiful, and mean the whole of her. Strong. Yours. I love you, out loud, every day. A boy is on the way who will spend that sentence to get something, and it lands hard on a girl who has been hungry for it. Job handed his daughters an inheritance next to their brothers, in a world that did not do such things. His name, and his weight, stood behind their worth.

Time under the delight, or the delight stays a mood you had on the drive. Breakfast that does not move. A drive, radio off. A walk after supper that survives the years. And a spine when she shoves you off. Daughters often pass through a season of wanting no father at all. Wounded men take the hint and leave. She is running a test on whether you remain. Keep the breakfast through the silence. Say the love to a shut door. On the far side of that season she will know the name of the one who stayed.

Faith she can watch, and eyes that come home. Pray her name and tell her you did. A girl who has seen her father on his knees knows the shape, and she will look for it later. She also tracks where your eyes travel. Pool, restaurant, screen. Wandering eyes teach her that women are scenery. Eyes that stay home teach her a faithful man is a sane thing to hope for.

## In every season

Grown, she is still watching. Did he change. Will he show up for her children in the places he missed her. A father's blessing does not expire at the curb when she moves out. If you failed the job, say the failure in plain words, and leave the excuse off the end. If pride has never been spoken, the call is late. It will repair a crack she had quit noticing.

From the divorced seat you will miss some breakfasts. Speak no evil of her mother. The bait does not license it. The truth of a complaint does not license it. Tired does not license it. Half of that girl is her mother, so the insult lands on the child. Show up for every hour the order allows, and contend for a few it does not. Call the same night, every week, including the stretches of fine and nothing. The hour she finally talks is purchased by the hours she would not. Give her a house, her room, her toothbrush in the cup. The divorce shook the floor. A father present, clean, and still growing is a place to stand while it shakes.

No daughter of your own still leaves you a teacher. Girls at church are short of this. Let them watch a man. Honor women where those girls can see the honoring. Coach. Teach. An uncle who arrives, and arrives in the light. Never a closed room with a girl who is not yours. Seen. Above reproach. One of the lessons is that a good man can be safe.

## The face she checks

Daughters got tenderness from Jesus. A ruler's little girl had died. He entered the room, took the hand, and said, my child, get up. Then the father had her back. Follow that Man. Gentle with a girl who was not even His.

:::margin
Luke 8:54 (NIV)
"But he took her by the hand and said, 'My child, get up!'"
:::

She has been reading you since before she owned words for the reading. An aisle may be the last time she does it. She will find then whatever she has been finding. Make the face worth the search.

## Before you turn the page

A wrong lesson on your face is repairable, and shame is a poor tool for the repair. She can read shame too. Wade got his gladness back the way a man gets a muscle back, by using it on days that felt like nothing. May the Father whose face lifts when His children enter lift yours this week, and may she catch the gladness that she is alive.
