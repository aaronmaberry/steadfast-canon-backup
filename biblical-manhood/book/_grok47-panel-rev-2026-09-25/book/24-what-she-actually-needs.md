# 24. What She Actually Needs

Dale heard it from the mudroom, one boot still in his hand. Renee was on the phone with her sister. "Oh, he's a good provider," in the voice a person uses for a furnace. Nothing in it was false. He had worked the long weeks so she could say it, and he heard two things at once. It was the kindest true thing she had. Leave it there and it becomes the eulogy.

"That's what you say about a man at his funeral," he told me later, "when you can't think of the next thing." I know the pause that follows, standing at the front. The rest of the sentence was supposed to be there.

## The measurable thing

I will give you the whole chapter in two sentences first. She needs you. More of what you make will not cover the gap.

Good provider is a furnace sentence. The house stayed warm. Men get turned around here because provision can be counted and presence cannot. The check, the truck, the faucet. Scripture says those count. Pour yourself into the part you can measure and you will build her a fine house and leave her alone in it.

## Steadiness

She needs to know you are staying. A wife can feel an unless written in the back of a vow. She will not rest in a marriage with an exit sketched in pencil. Strike it. Say it out loud, more than once. I am not going anywhere, and whatever this is, we are in it together. Then keep the oath on the weeks it costs, and let her watch.

:::margin
Malachi 2:15 (NIV)
"Has not the one God made you? You belong to him in body and spirit. And what does the one God seek? Godly offspring. So be on your guard, and do not be unfaithful to the wife of your youth."
:::

## Presence

She needs you present when you are home. A man can sleep there every night and be with her on none of them, and she cannot even name why she is alone. Attention has to come in with you. Phone down. A question, then the whole answer. She can tell a person from a body in the chair.

## Providing without disappearing

This is the trap that catches the best men in the room.

A good man provides, the work asks for more, and he gives it, because the work feels like love for the family. You have started to vanish when she stops asking what time you will be home, when you hear about your own house secondhand, when you are too tired at night to be anyone, when you have decided she wants the life and you have never asked.

Dale asked. One question at the kitchen table. What do you need from me that you are not getting? Renee did not have to search for it. Look up when I come into a room. He had provided the whole house, and he had stopped lifting his eyes when she walked into it.

He did not quit the job. Most men cannot. Here is what he changed.

He set a floor, a few things that do not move no matter what the job does. Supper at the table four nights. Sunday morning. Prayer with the kids at bedtime.

He named the hour he would be home, and he was home at that hour. Six forty, and then six forty. If it moved, he called before the miss, and he called her. A reliable hour is a kind of presence from down the road.

He gave her the good part of himself. The phone spends the first hour on top of the refrigerator. She gets the first fifteen minutes, while he still has a self to give.

And he quit playing for the scoreboard. Work claps. Home mostly does not. He stopped working for the clap. He worked for a Father who sees a supper table.

This spring Dale heard Renee on the phone with the same sister, from the same spot in the mudroom. She did not say provider. She said, "He's here," and went on to something about their mother, as if it were ordinary.

## Safety

She needs to be unafraid of you, and I mean more than fists. Most of you would never use those. I mean the voice that drops, the three-day silence, the sarcasm that draws blood, the truck that leaves fast. A wife who reads your weather before she speaks is on alert in her own kitchen. A gentle answer comes from strength that does not need volume. When she knows your anger will not land on her, she has a freedom many wives never get.

:::margin
Proverbs 15:1 (NIV)
"A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger."
:::

## Pursued, still

The man who asked her out ought to still be asking. Pursuit that was only a tool for getting her stops at the wedding, and she feels shelved. Rejoice in the wife of your youth. Plan a Thursday. Name the evening. Iron the shirt. Let her catch you looking. That is how a husband says he would choose her again.

## Leadership she can rest in

She wants a direction she can rest in, so she can set the map down. Say where the house is going. Go first into the hard call and own it. Leave her as the only adult and she will lead because somebody has to, tired and unsure she may say so. Pray with her out loud, once a week, her hand in yours. It does something to a woman to hear her husband talk to God about her.

## Heard and known

The chapter behind this one already did the long work, so one line here. She needs a man who wants to know her, and who has quit pretending the early years were enough.

## Words

She needs to hear it out loud, in particular words, on a regular week, where the children can hear. You surpass them all tells her she is the best there is, and it says so where people can hear. Say it this week.

:::margin
Proverbs 31:28-29 (NIV)
"Her children arise and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praises her: 'Many women do noble things, but you surpass them all.'"
:::

## Help that isn't helping

She needs the load shared, and she needs you to retire the word help. The dishes are yours. The kids' weeks are yours. A husband who "helps out" has announced that the house is her job and he is a volunteer who showed up. Eve was called a helper, and the title is a strong one. Christ came to serve. Neither of those truths gives a man permission to sit while she runs the place around him.

## Tenderness

She needs touch that is going nowhere near the bed. A hand on her back at the counter. Her feet after a long day. Arms around her while she cries, the fix left in your pocket. Hallway traffic and a start toward the bedroom are the two touches she already knows. Give her the third. The bedroom will go better for it.

## A man who's growing

She needs to see you moving, toward God and toward the four marks. A husband who is stuck, the same man he was when the kids were small, only heavier and shorter on patience, is a weight she carries on top of the rest. A husband who is growing, even slow, even with failures on the record, is a man she can walk with. Hope in the house has a lot to do with whether he is becoming someone.

## Her seasons

The seasons change how the list sounds. The meaning holds. Under a newborn she needs sleep more than anything you can buy, and she needs you in the room at three in the morning even when you cannot feed the baby. Decades in, she needs to be seen as a woman with a name and a want, long after the kitchen knows her habits. Make the plans for the years left together. Go to your checkups. Her quiet fear is finding you on the floor. If you are in the divorced seat, hold this list up to the marriage that ended. Do it so the next chapter of you is a different man. The mother of your children still needs your reliability, your civility, and your backing when the kids are listening. Cutting her down across two houses wins a point and wounds them. If you are single, this is the man you are building now. Your mother could use a few of these from a grown son. Show up. Say the words. Call her and let the call be about her.

## What He gives His bride

Christ already gives the church everything on this list. Steadiness. He will not leave. Presence. He is with you. Safety. Gentle and humble in heart. Pursuit. He came looking. Leadership. Follow me. Being known. I know my sheep. Words. This is my body, given for you. Help. A towel and a basin. Tenderness. He wept at a grave. Growth. The Son grew in wisdom and stature and in favor.

She is asking for a small copy of that, in a house, on an ordinary weeknight. It costs you. That is what she wanted.

## Before you turn the page

If the floor dropped, hear this. Few things in the book repair this fast. She is asking the man she married to look up. May the Father who gives His bride steadiness, presence, words, and tenderness give you nerve to ask one question this week and grace to hear the whole answer.
