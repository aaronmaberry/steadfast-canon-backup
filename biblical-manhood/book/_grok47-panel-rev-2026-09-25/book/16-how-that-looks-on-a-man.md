# 16. How That Looks on a Man

The foyer receives one face. It asks about the sermon. It crosses the room to the man sitting alone, and the handshake is meant. Home is another climate. The voice changes countries between the door and the kitchen. Shirt unchanged. Ring unchanged. Weather, not the same.

That is sin as a man wears it. Wreckage is optional. A finished surface will do the job. So will competence. It dresses us differently than it dresses anyone else. Learn the shapes or you will greet the man in the mirror each morning and tell him he is fine.

## The shapes it takes

I see these most. Yours is in the list.

The locked drawer. The pull is a second ledger. Money she has never seen. A screen. A friendship under the wrong name. Or fear, ruled off limits. A boy's private corner becomes a man's shut room, and the damage finds him there.

The fortress. How he is doing gets answered with fine. What he needs gets answered with nothing. Everyone leans on him. He leans on no one. Pride has put on a work shirt and named itself strength. It impersonates the second mark, accepting responsibility, which is why it hides. A responsible man can still be helped. The fortress was raised to refuse the help.

The scoreboard. He quietly ranks the room by truck, job, wife, children, body, and pay. Another man's win cannot make him glad, and his own life cannot let him rest. Saul lived on that ranking. It sent him after the young man who had just won a fight for him.

The podium. Sin dressed as righteousness. Jesus hated this shape with a particular heat. Generous when watched. Patient beside the church, and a storm in the truck before he gets home. He said men like that have already taken their whole payment from people who do not really know them. Nothing further is coming.

The throne. Every conversation in the house has to be won. Loud will win it. Silence will. So will sarcasm that leaves a welt. Leadership is his name for the habit. Ask her and the work she will describe is managing him. The children learned the truck in the driveway the way other children learn weather. One sentence in Scripture fences the whole thing.

:::margin
Colossians 3:19 (NIV)
"Husbands, love your wives and do not be harsh with them."
:::

He saw the harsh man coming and fenced him in a single line.

Contempt is the throne with the volume down. A sneer. Eyes rolled while his wife tells a story. Disdain aimed at a softer man on Sunday, or at a son in tears. Look down on people and the same neck cannot look up at God.

The recliner. Sloth, costumed as rest. Laziness is not the usual look on a man. The usual look is a fellow collecting an evening he believes he banked. Rafters are what the house pays.

## The man on the roof

One spring, a single man in Scripture wore about half of these at the same time. His generation had nobody better. David could be taken. So can you. The entry is passivity.

:::margin
2 Samuel 11:1 (NIV)
"In the spring, at the time when kings go off to war, David sent Joab out with the king's men and the whole Israelite army. They destroyed the Ammonites and besieged Rabbah. But David remained in Jerusalem."
:::

Going out was the king's work, and it had been David's work. Joab went. David stayed in the city. Sin is not what the court would have called it. A rest, earned. Empty hands. An evening walk on the roof. You know what an empty roof and an empty evening are capable of.

He sees her. He asks. The report comes back. She is married. He sends anyway. Put the marks on that afternoon and watch them invert. Passivity had been in charge since the army marched off. Responsibility was reassigned. The war went to Joab. The conscience went to appetite. Power stood in for courage. A coward uses power when the office hands it to him. She had no safe way to refuse a king, and he took her. He was living for the hour in front of him.

The drawer came next, because she was pregnant. Home came the husband. Drink was applied. The hope was that the child would pass as his. Uriah refused the part. Bed was dishonorable while his brothers remained in the field. A sealed order went back with him. Worst of the fighting. Then pull the others off. Uriah was killed. The widow became David's wife. Covered, to every eye that mattered in the court.

:::margin
2 Samuel 11:27 (NIV)
"After the time of mourning was over, David had her brought to his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing David had done displeased the LORD."
:::

Israel kept moving. Wars kept being won. Almost nobody knew. For most of a year David governed with a murdered man shut away and another man's wife in his house, and he governed well. Administration and righteousness part company there. Church, kingdom, or home, the work can look sound while the man decays.

Psalm 32 is that year, described from inside the body. Bones wasting. God's hand heavy. A working lock does not protect a man from what the drawer does to him.

## What exposed him

Conscience did not bring him in. No morning arrived when David chose, on his own, to come clean. God sent Nathan.

:::margin
2 Samuel 12:7 (NIV)
"Then Nathan said to David, 'You are the man! This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you from the hand of Saul.'"
:::

You are the man. Four words. The story Nathan brought was a rich man and a poor man's only lamb, stolen. Fury was David's response. Then the story was turned. You. A brother had entered the throne room and named the sin to the face of the king. Exposure of a man's sin rarely begins with the man. A wife reaches the end of her silence. A brother will not take fine for an answer. The mail brings a statement. God's hand gets heavy past the point of standing. That is the usual path, and it was this path.

So you need a Nathan. And you need the willingness to be one. A table of brothers exists for the moment somebody has to say four words where you sit like a king.

The sentence that turned it is the one to learn.

:::margin
2 Samuel 12:13 (NIV)
"Then David said to Nathan, 'I have sinned against the LORD.' Nathan replied, 'The LORD has taken away your sin. You are not going to die.'"
:::

I have sinned against the LORD. Five words, and the sentence stops. The roof was not her fault in his mouth. Uriah was not blamed for remaining with the army. The weight of being king was not offered as a balance. Confession ends at the period. Forgiveness was in the next breath. Consequences followed, and they were terrible. Restoration of his standing did not wait on the consequences. The drawer opened, and the standing returned.

## Checking the glass

Sin lies to the man wearing it. Use tests.

Drawer. What would shame you if a brother saw it? Phone, history, accounts, calendar, that friendship. Contents aside, a drawer is already the problem. Nothing hidden means nothing you are required to defend.

No. Name the last time someone told you no and you received it. If the memory will not come, you are seated on a throne.

Wife. Married men, choose a quiet hour and ask where she sees the hiding, and where the harshness lives. Argument stays down. You listen. You thank her. Single men, ask the brother who knows you best, or ask your mother.

Podium. What do you perform only under eyes? Church lot one man, cab another. Sunday morning one man, an unseen night another. The gap is the performance.

Throne. Fear is the question, separate from respect. Who in the house is afraid of you? Any name but none means you get off the seat. Knees, in front of the people who have had to read your weather.

## The shapes in different houses

Single, watch the drawer and the podium first. Unwatched hours. A reward for looking solid among the saints. A brother should hold the passwords and the habit of asking. Close the drawer while it is still nearly empty.

A baby in the house puts the throne on notice. Used-up men get harsh, and the reasons arrive quickly when sleep is gone. She is carrying more fatigue than you. Apologize the same night, every time.

Divorce feeds the scoreboard. Her house, your house. The life she is building, the life you are building. Holiday tables compared. Peace leaves in small thefts. Drop the board. The Father sees what is secret. Her column is not the score He keeps.

Older men should stare at the fortress. Distance down the road produces men who need nothing with great skill, and the skill has cost them. At the brothers' table the expensive question is what you need from the rest of us. Plenty of those men have gone unasked. An answer, when one finally comes, changes the table.

## The Man with no drawer

No drawer was in Jesus. The Father had the whole of Him. When they came to arrest Him, He could say the speaking had been public. They had to invent a charge. He was known to the bottom, and unashamed.

That is the man being formed in you. The lock comes off. Inside and outside agree. Wife, brothers, and God can look without a flinch, because the hidden thing has been named and pardoned.

Let a Nathan see it. Say the five words.

## Before you turn

You have probably found your shape. David needed four words from another man. Five words back restored his standing in a breath. May the Father set beside you a man who can speak those four words, and give you the plainness to answer I have sinned and then stop.
