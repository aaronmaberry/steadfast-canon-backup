# 9. Anger, the Itch, and the Drift

One week, Owen used every way out he had. He would have named the problem a temper and stopped the conversation there.

His daughter stacked the dishwasher wrong, and the door came shut hard enough that a glass left the top rack. The kitchen went still, the way it had been trained. Two nights on, a locked bathroom held him with a phone for forty minutes. Nobody in the house required him there. That was the draw. Weekend put him on the couch under a game. She wanted his mind on July, her sister's offer to keep the kids. He answered "whatever you think," and he meant it from another room.

Three nights, three exits. He would have logged three moods. Under them sat a fall contract, already spent in his head, awarded to another man. The figures were finished. He was afraid, and he had told no one.

## Where pain goes

You have already heard that a chair refuses to stay vacant. Pain behaves like that tenant. It will move. The doors it uses are three.

Anger takes it out. Appetite takes it down. Absence takes it away. In a heavy week a man trusts one door and calls that door his personality. A boy found a way to stop feeling something. The way hardened. It answers when his name is called. Shut the preferred door and the pain finds another. Owen used all three before the week was out.

I know the tug of each door. None of them will be softened here. None of them will be used to shame you. Shame is gasoline for the whole set.

## Anger

The door men prefer is anger, because anger wears a strong face.

Lift the anger and you usually find fear, shame, or helplessness underneath. The month's money will not stretch. A specific shame is sitting on him. Something he loves is breaking and he cannot get his hands on the break. Weakness is how each of those lands in the body. Power is how anger lands, so he seizes it the way a freezing man seizes the jacket closest to the door. Owen's hand was not moved by a poorly loaded machine. The figures moved it.

God's word expects anger to show up. The instruction is about the next move.

:::margin
Ephesians 4:26-27 (NIV)
"'In your anger do not sin': Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry, and do not give the devil a foothold."
:::

The sentence starts inside the anger. Then a clock. Sundown. Then the foothold. Sleep on it, wake still holding it, sleep on it again, and the enemy has a foot in the house. Keep on, and the anger becomes the weather your family lives under.

Anger as your door comes with assigned work. Memorize the ninety seconds. Leave the feeling unfed, and the surge in your body peaks, then begins to drop, before two minutes are gone. Go out as far as the mailbox and return. The sentence you have cocked stays unsaid. Indoors, say the layer beneath. "I'm scared about the money." A wife can plan with a man who admits fear. Finish while the sun is up. The man who learns this is the steadiest man in the house.

## The itch

Appetite is the downward door. Sex has the hardest grip, so I will stay with sex, and I will tell you the itch is the same one that runs through a bottle, a cart, a sports book, and a scroll with no last page. Comfort on a short clock. No demand. No witness.

In that locked room Owen was chasing relief ahead of pleasure. Relief from the figures. Relief from a kitchen gone still. The screen required nothing, least of all a man. The pull is that vacancy. The lie sits beside it, because being required as a man is what would start to heal him.

:::margin
James 1:14-15 (NIV)
"but each person is tempted when they are dragged away by their own evil desire and enticed. Then, after desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, gives birth to death."
:::

Dragged names the feeling. Nobody schedules two in the morning. The road ends in death, seldom a spectacle. What dies by degrees is presence, desire for an actual woman, and the power to meet his wife's eyes. She senses the wrongness before she can name it.

Flee is the verb Paul gave the Corinthians. Joseph left a house at a run because a woman inside it wanted him. Match that speed. Your work, if this is your door, starts with one man and a complete account. What you do, how often, how long it has been, what it is costing. Set down the sentence "I struggle with purity." This appetite feeds on being unknown. A brother who knows it begins the dying. Then remove the supply line. A filter belongs on each device. The password belongs in his keeping. Through the night the phone belongs on a kitchen charger. You would set that limit for a child. Set it for the man acting like one while the house sleeps. Then face the loneliness. That is the real subject. Brothers have a later chapter. The line belongs here. Men who are known find the itch smaller.

## The drift

Absence is the quiet door. I watch it hardest in men whose working years are behind them, because the rooms still look fine and nobody calls a crisis.

A drifting man leaves with his body still in the building. He is home. He reaches work. Nothing has exploded and nothing has been exposed. He is elsewhere. His wife stopped asking, once the answers shrank to "fine" and "whatever you think." Children learn that a father can weight a couch and still be gone. That is how a man builds the chair that looks full and holds no one, the chair some of you watched through childhood.

Usually a hurt sits under it. He decided, minus any announcement, that care charged too much. Or fatigue was honest, and rest kept sliding until withdrawal was the name for it.

:::margin
Ecclesiastes 10:18 (NIV)
"Through laziness, the rafters sag; because of idle hands, the house leaks."
:::

A boot did not punch that roof. Leak followed from an empty ladder and an occupied couch. Wait until someone finally names the drift, and the ceiling already carries the stain.

If this door is yours, say it to your wife, or to the people in the house. "I've been in the house and I've been gone, and I'm sorry." She already knows. Hearing it from you changes the room. No wife means you tell a brother. Drift with no witness keeps drifting. Then reclaim one practice. Once a week, an evening with every screen off and your mind in the room. Once a day, a question, and you stay until the answer finishes. Go find the boy who quit caring because the price looked too high. A brother can go. A counselor can. That climb is the ladder.

## The same root

Fear had injured the angry man when he was a boy, so he exchanged it for a sensation that passed as power. The man with the itch had learned he would go unseen, so he bought comfort that asked no introduction. The drifting man had learned care's invoice and resigned the account. Refusing to feel the chair is the shared purpose. Leaving the house is the shared result.

Jesus used none of these doors. Anger came without sin riding it. Temptation met Him across the whole field, and He refused the downward door. Another road appealed to everything in Him. He said "not as I will," and He stayed. If you have already left by an exit, He can meet you there and walk you home without shame.

Stopping by force is the attempt you have already made. A harder clench leaves a man in place. Carry the pain where it can be healed. Stop carrying it where it can be numbed. A Father remains. Your anger does not alarm Him. Your itch does not revolt Him. He did not drift when you did.

## The exits in every season

Season changes the clothing. The doors stay available.

Single and young, appetite usually shouts loudest. Keep a watch on absence too. Work you have no heart for. Evenings surrendered to a screen. At your age, numbness belongs on the warning side of the gauge. Peace is a different reading.

A baby in the house will ambush you with anger. The clock says three. The crying has no end in sight. A voice exits you, and you last met that voice in your father's mouth. Use the ninety seconds. Follow them with the true line. "I'm so tired I'm scared of myself."

The first year or two after a marriage ends can swing all three doors open together. Anger with her name on it. Appetite, fed by a solitude you did not used to live in. Absence, because caring is the choice that delivered you here. Recovery, when it comes, often wears the face of one older man on a weekly call who will not take "fine" as an answer.

Older men should watch anger and absence travel as companions. The hours in front of the television are where the pair often sits down. Rise before she does. Have coffee waiting. Stay in the chair while she drinks, ask one question, and let the answer arrive.

## Owen's mailbox

On an empty night Owen put the figures in front of his wife. Three weeks of fear, he said, had gone to the dishwasher, the bathroom, and the couch. She had sensed the wrongness already. She could stand with him in the fear. What had damaged the house was his being gone.

She did not say any of that gently the first night. She was angry, and not about the contract. She had spent three weeks in a house with a man who was not in it. He took it without a defense. It was most of a week before the kitchen felt like theirs again.

Quiet started in that kitchen again, and he made the walk to the mailbox and the walk back. The true sentence got said. Autumn's contract remained lost. Hiding from it in the kitchen was over.

## Before you turn the page

A name now belongs to your exit. Named things can be carried to the Father. May He be at the mailbox when you arrive, and at the screen, and at the couch. May one small refusal this week be the start of a habit.
