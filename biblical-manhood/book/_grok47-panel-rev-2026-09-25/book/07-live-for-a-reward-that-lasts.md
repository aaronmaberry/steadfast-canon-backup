# 7. Live for a Reward That Lasts

I know the drive home with the radio high and the prize on the seat beside me. The night feels permanent. Years later the prize is in a cardboard box on a folding table at a yard sale. FREE, in marker, down the side. Inside, a gold batter missing an arm. A bowling pin mounted on marble. A salesman plaque from a firm that folded. Saturdays a man gave away are in the box. Nights he was sure would define him are in it. By noon nobody has claimed them. The garage takes the box back. Spring puts it at the curb.

Holdable rewards behave like that. I have organized months around them. They perform for a night. Then the hand is empty and the hunt resumes.

Expecting the greater reward, God's reward, is the fourth mark. The first three depend on it. Passivity rejected, weight accepted, the first step taken, each one sends an invoice today. Payment continues only if the man trusts the return. Lose the fourth mark and the other three are courage spent on a losing trade. The step stays unfixed if nobody is keeping the account. That's mine stays unsaid if blame carries no later cost. The first door stays shut if the street is all there is. Questions, until this mark answers.

## What you're already living for

You are aimed at a reward already. So is every man, from the moment he wakes. The choice still open is which prize.

I have run this list. Rearranged, it is still the same list.

A father's approval, living or dead. More men are on that payroll than will confess it. Comfort, the prize of a new father by week six and of a retired man on too many afternoons. Being right, worth a ruined evening to a young husband. The number on a screen, checked before the parking lot. Applause, including the small nod from across a room. Being wanted, the quiet prize of single men and the forgotten prize of married ones. Memory. Trophies in a box. A name attached to an object. A hope that the work outlives you by a little.

In their place, several are good. Each one runs out, and God is a different reward from all of them. The nod dies in the parking lot. A higher number arrives without the feeling that was supposed to ride along. A father's approval, finally given, is smaller than the version you rehearsed. Comfort has no refusal in it and no filling either. Collect prizes that last a night, and a lifetime of them ends on that folding table.

## Treasure that keeps

Quitting the desire for reward was never Jesus' order. He redirected the desire, and He named the storehouse.

:::margin
Matthew 6:19-21 (NIV)
"Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moths and vermin destroy, and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where moths and vermin do not destroy, and where thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."
:::

Moths. Vermin. Thieves. The yard sale, described in advance. Earlier in that sermon He explains how the lasting kind gets stored.

:::margin
Matthew 6:3-4 (NIV)
"But when you give to the needy, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, so that your giving may be in secret. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you."
:::

Audience is required for every prize on the old list. This prize requires you to dismiss the audience. A widow's drive cleared of snow while her kitchen is still dark. A check that carries no signature story. Praise for a man who will never learn you spoke it. A single man's Saturday spent carrying a young family's couch up to a second-floor apartment, and nobody to tell about it that night. Hidden work still has a witness. He runs the books.

Church hours are too small a container for it.

:::margin
Colossians 3:23-24 (NIV)
"Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving."
:::

Whatever you do covers the invoice, the diaper, the spreadsheet, the graveyard shift. Stages do not own this reward. Ladders do, and sinks with no spectator, when the work is offered to the Lord. The size of the job is beside the point. Who you are working for is what turns the work into treasure.

## The reward is Him

Reward can sound like a bonus plan. Read the offer before you decide.

:::margin
Matthew 25:21 (NIV)
"His master replied, 'Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you in charge of many things. Come and share your master's happiness!'"
:::

Well done. A larger trust. An open door into His happiness. Welcome is the content. Nearer to Him, with more in your care, His joy shared. A son wants a Father who is glad at the homecoming.

Moses priced the exchange correctly. Palace childhood. The great treasure room a hallway away. He left it and took his stand among slaves.

:::margin
Hebrews 11:26 (NIV)
"He regarded disgrace for the sake of Christ as of greater value than the treasures of Egypt, because he was looking ahead to his reward."
:::

Ahead. He looked past the treasure room at a higher price, and the look is what let him choose. Almost any leaving is possible while a better thing is in view. Almost no leaving is possible when the frame holds only the thing being left.

## What it does to a man

No interview is required. The man aimed at God's reward shows it. Unseen good stays unspoken afterward. Winnable arguments get released once being right no longer covers the damage. Staying put, in marriage, church, and town, continues on the day a justified exit is available. Low seat, low task, and no scan of the room for credit. Forgiveness, with the other man still undeserving, because the ledger that matters is the Father's. The side tally can go. He sows crops his mouth will miss. Grandchildren not yet alive get his prayers. A boy learns work from him and will pass work to another boy when he is gone. Mr. Pruitt keeps pecan trees on those terms. The nuts are for other people. The watering is his.

Kept inside this life, the pattern is nonsense. Set past this life, the pattern is the only math that works.

## What it doesn't promise

The loud version is a counterfeit, so name the limits in plain words. God's reward includes no guarantee of health, a larger house, an easier year, or children who land where you aimed them. Staying married comes with no promise of warmth by spring. Forgiveness comes with no promise that an apology will come back. Men who preached those deals left damage behind them. Paul wrote the true scale after beatings and after a shipwreck.

:::margin
2 Corinthians 4:17-18 (NIV)
"For our light and momentary troubles are achieving for us an eternal glory that far outweighs them all. So we fix our eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen, since what is seen is temporary, but what is unseen is eternal."
:::

Light and momentary, said by a scarred man. His arithmetic used the long numbers. Erasing the trouble was never the claim. The trouble was producing something. Hard years remain on the record. They are the material the reward is made from.

## How to live for it

Daily, fix the eyes. Fix is Paul's verb. Glancing will miss. One line before your feet find the floor. Today is seen. Today counts.

Weekly, one kindness with no witnesses at all. Wife or roommate excluded. Brother excluded. The men you meet excluded. A leaked story means you chose the wrong kindness.

On the ride home from an unthanked day, practice the words well done in His mouth. Memory, aimed at your actual employer.

Catch the performance while it is starting. Jesus was plain. Good done for a crowd has already been paid, in full. The moment you look around for who noticed, the payment has already cleared, and it cleared small.

Pray past Thursday. Thursday can stay in the prayer. Then look further. An apartment is enough for a single man. A porch is enough for a grandfather. Same audience being lost. Same Father doing the seeing.

## One man

Stand the four marks on a single life and look. The needed task does not get walked around. That's mine gets said, and the period holds. Door one opens under the eyes of the street. The reward in view is out of reach of any yard sale. An old name fits him. Steadfast.

**A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.**

Live for a reward that lasts. Then go back to the work.

## Before you turn the page

Nearest prizes pay the same night, which is why small rewards get chosen. Move your aim up. Secret seeing, in the Father, includes a perfect memory of the account. May your eyes be fixed where moths cannot finish the treasure, and may the sentence you want be kept for you until the day it is spoken.
