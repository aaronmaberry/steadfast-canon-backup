# 33. What You Are Building When Nobody Claps

High summer, and the foundation crews pour at four in the morning. Concrete must set before the heat, so the work is under portable lights. By sunrise the slab is down and the crew is gone.

Cameras skip slabs. Thirty years a family lives on one and never thinks of what is underneath. Walking gets learned in those rooms. Fights happen there, and so does making up. The pour was done in the dark. Most of what a man builds, nobody watches. The unseen part carries the rest.

## Most of your life is unwitnessed

Count your own week. The feeding at two. The hotel room where you turned from the temptation. Eleven years of coffee made for your wife. The lie you refused at work when the true answer cost you. No applause came. None is coming.

Poison is the culture's reply. Unseen, so it did not count. Scoreboards teach that. It is why men perform, and why they drift into the room that claps. One sermon, again and again, Jesus said otherwise.

:::margin
Matthew 6:6 (NIV)
"But when you pray, go into your room, close the door and pray to your Father, who is unseen. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you."
:::

Who sees what is done in secret. The return is highest on the thing no crowd watched, because you can be sure the crowd was not the reason. That is the fourth mark at its cleanest. Expect God's reward and a life of hidden good will not leave you cheated. You know who stood in the room.

## What you're actually building

A floor under his children. A thousand suppers no one can recall become, in a child, the settled knowledge that somebody is in charge. They will not remember one ordinary night. Their bones will know whether those nights happened.

A marriage still holding at seventy. Roy's has run forty-four years on coffee, not on the anniversaries. It is better now from the coffee he began making once he retired. A couple in their seventies who still like each other are standing on a slab poured in the dark across fifty years.

A name that walks in ahead of him. What people learned later, the things done when they were looking elsewhere, that is a reputation. A son's first weight is his father's last name. Credit at a feed store two counties over comes from forty years of bills paid on time, bills nobody else knew about.

A church. The men no one thanks are why it functions. Chairs stacked. An old man sat with in a hospital. Fourth-grade boys taught for twenty-two years. The pastor has the platform. The men who laid the floor beneath it are in the lot afterward, hands in their pockets, and they are at peace.

A self, the build men forget. Trustworthy in very little, Jesus said, and you can be trusted with much. The little is what nobody witnessed. This year Eli's hidden work was the truth told to a customer when a lie would have been free, and the phone left in the kitchen when keeping it would have gone unknown.

Treasure in heaven. That names where the labor went. The account is kept by a Father who does not misplace a thing.

## The fire

Paul pictured the inspection.

:::margin
1 Corinthians 3:13 (NIV)
"It will be revealed with fire, and the fire will test the quality of each person's work."
:::

Every man builds with something. Applause as the reason is straw. From the road, straw can outshine gold, because straw shows off and gold weighs. Fire, and the crowd's work is gone in a second. What remains was done for the Father where no one looked. A four-in-the-morning slab is gold. A two o'clock feeding is gold. Straw is the sermon preached so the preacher would be admired. Straw is the gift that bought a name on a wall. Straw is fatherhood acted at the ballpark and dropped in the truck on the way home.

## The day after the marker

You have had markers. A wedding. A baptism. A morning at a fence when you gave your son his name. A plaque at retirement and a handshake. The stone on the bank is the marker. The land is the Tuesdays. Decision lives in the day after, and that day has no clapping in it.

After the marker a man needs a rhythm, hidden structure under the moments people see. Roy built his after the hardest marker he has lived.

His alarm does not migrate. He is still up at 5:30. Hour one is the plumb line, a chapter, and a prayer that opens with Father, before the phone is touched. Then the floor, family things that stay put, written where he can see them. Twenty minutes with his brother, the same slot each week. One good thing done in secret during the week. By noon, a task someone is counting on. A blessing, hand on a head and three sentences if the children are home, a call if they are grown. A notebook, one line a day of what she said. Sundown, and nothing is carried into the bed. The apology has been made. The anger has been given to God. Sunday he is in the Father's house as a man with a job there.

The same rhythm shrinks. Tomás has a four-month-old and a shift of ten hours. He gives the first fifteen minutes of the two-to-five feeding to a Psalm on his phone. His floor is twenty minutes after the boots are off, plus the prayer over the crib. At nine, dishes running, his brother has the phone. The secret good is an extra hour of his wife's sleep on Saturday, never mentioned. Sundown sounds like this. "I'm so tired I'm scared of myself, and I'm sorry, and I'm not going anywhere." No spare room in that life. The rhythm lives in the cracks. Gold.

An every-other-weekend life has its own card. The hour before the children wake. The call in the middle of the week. The weekend that does not get traded.

Build the card. Ten lines, or five. That card is your slab. Visible things sit on it.

## When you're weary

Unseen work has a stretch where nothing shows. Slab poured, no house, only flat gray in the dirt for months. Coffee for a year, and she is still far. Your son, called every week for eight months, answers "nothing." The thought arrives that the hours were wasted.

:::margin
Isaiah 49:4 (NIV)
"But I said, 'I have labored in vain; I have spent my strength for nothing at all. Yet what is due me is in the LORD's hand, and my reward is with my God.'"
:::

What is due me is in the LORD's hand. The servant carried that weight and did not argue it off. He moved the account. My reward is with my God. God holds it. The results do not. The boy who said nothing does not. He saw all thirty-four calls before the one when the boy talked. Keep at it, Paul says, and the harvest arrives at its proper time. Pecan time, and not yours. Mr. Pruitt's trees are at his knee. He will never see a crop. He is not worn out by that, because the pecans were never what he thought the reward was.

## The barn nobody remembers

Roy's grandson carries a plumb bob of the kind his great-grandfather used to raise a barn. The barn stands. The county cannot produce the builder's name.

Legacy means a thing still standing. Towns forget. Churches hire a new pastor. Grandchildren move. Build in order to be remembered and disappointment is certain. Build in order that something stands, and you will have the barn.

After the badge, Roy told me, the hard part was seeing that thirty-one years had already been forgotten by the people the work was for. "And then I realized that was fine. I didn't do it for them. I thought I did. I didn't. I did it because it was mine to do and God was watching." He found the fourth mark at seventy. The Thursday table will be forgotten as well. The two men who sit there will still be standing.

## Thirty years in a shop

Jesus had thirty years with no witness on them. Luke spends one sentence on the whole stretch.

:::margin
Luke 2:51 (NIV)
"Then he went down to Nazareth with them and was obedient to them. But his mother treasured all these things in her heart."
:::

Boards got planed by the Son of God in a town people thought little of. He obeyed a carpenter and the carpenter's wife. Clapping did not happen. Later work stood on those years. At thirty He entered the Jordan and the Father spoke aloud. This is my Son, whom I love. A single sentence of praise, then three years of doubt, and death at the end of them. He did not go looking for the crowd's substitute. Thirty years had been seen. Enough.

Enough for you. The slab will not be applauded. You poured it in the dark, and the Father watched.

:::margin
Hebrews 6:10 (NIV)
"God is not unjust; he will not forget your work and the love you have shown him as you have helped his people and continue to help them."
:::

Your work will not be forgotten. Pour. Make the coffee. Make the call. The only One whose count matters is already looking.

## Before you turn the page

Silence wears on a man. Hear the turn in the servant's line again. What is due me is in the LORD's hand. May the Father who saw His Son plane boards in Nazareth see your pour this week. Keep the rhythm seven days with your mouth shut about it. He will have kept each day.
