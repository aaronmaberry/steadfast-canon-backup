# 17. No Fixed Star

The dryer holds his two-foot level. A shelf in the laundry room does not get to trust a wall that only looks right. He sets the level on the board. The bubble moves, stops between the marks, and the shelf he trusted is off.

The wall gets no argument. The bubble keeps no opinions. It reports down.

Masons use the cousin of that tool, a plumb line, string with a weight. Hang it beside the work. The weight settles. The string aims down every time, because gravity is not polling the crew. A wall that has drifted gets told.

Most of us are checking a life against references that wander.

## Everyone did as they saw fit

Judges ends near a sentence I cannot find a sadder summary for.

:::margin
Judges 21:25 (NIV)
"In those days Israel had no king; everyone did as they saw fit."
:::

As they saw fit. The grief is the measuring, even where some of the work was decent. Each man let his own judgment be the star, and the star moved when he moved. Drift stays invisible while the instrument rides in the boat.

Most men I know are in that condition. They are drifting, and a moving reference hides how far. A marriage, a work, or a faith can be badly off before they can tell you the route.

Men keep trying to freeze a point that will not freeze.

A father was the first fixed point for most of us. What he did defined a man. Then you grew up and found he had been moving too. If he was gone, or never in the house, you never had a point to sight. Learn from him. Do not navigate by him.

Other men come next. I clear more than the husband of my wife's sister. I am home more evenings than the men on the crew. I never did what the deacon did, and I have used that gap as proof. One leaning wall checked against another leaning wall. A wall can beat every other wall on the street and still fail the string.

Culture will not hold still. What made a man for your grandfather and what the screens in front of your son are selling are different stars. You are right to notice.

Feelings offer themselves next. I do not feel this is right anymore. I feel God leading me there. Feelings are real. They also move with sleep, with blood sugar, and with supper. A man who steers by them has chosen clouds.

Success offers itself last. If it works, it must be right. The shop grew, so the life must be sound. The marriage has not come apart, so he calls the marriage healthy. For a year David governed with real skill while a murdered man lay shut in a drawer. Success is a wall that has not fallen yet. It will not do a plumb line's work.

:::margin
Proverbs 14:12 (NIV)
"There is a way that appears to be right, but in the end it leads to death."
:::

The appearance of right is what drift feels like while you are in it. Reasonable is the costume. The end is where the road gives its name.

## The one that doesn't move

There is a plumb line, and Amos was shown the Owner of it.

:::margin
Amos 7:7-8 (NIV)
"This is what he showed me: The Lord was standing by a wall that had been built true to plumb, with a plumb line in his hand. And the LORD asked me, 'What do you see, Amos?' 'A plumb line,' I replied. Then the Lord said, 'Look, I am setting a plumb line among my people Israel; I will spare them no longer.'"
:::

God told Amos He was setting that line in the midst of His people. String and weight, held on a man's life. The hang is true. The wall does not get to edit it. Scripture is that string. Commands live in it, and so does the reference that lets a moving life be seen.

:::margin
Psalm 119:105 (NIV)
"Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path."
:::

A lamp at the feet gives the next step. That is what an ordinary morning needs. Paul named the same help another way.

:::margin
2 Timothy 3:16-17 (NIV)
"All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness, so that the servant of God may be thoroughly equipped for every good work."
:::

Teaching tells you the truth. Rebuking is the string on a wall that has left true. Correcting shows the way back to plumb. Training stays until the hands themselves build true. Thoroughly equipped means the man is no longer borrowing a moving star. He has a point that stays when he shifts his weight.

Hang the four marks on that string and you will see which wall of yours has drifted.

## Why men don't use it

Nobody trained them. They started Genesis in January, hit Leviticus in February, and quit. That is a training gap, and about a month of a different practice will close it.

They say it feels like assigned schoolwork. Read it as a man hearing his Father. Read it aloud in the truck. Carry one question. What is a man being told to do?

They say there is no time. Ten minutes exist. A man who cannot find ten minutes for the only thing in his life that holds still has named his real star.

They say they feel nothing. Feeling was never the errand. You came to learn which way is down. A level does not stir you. It tells the truth about the board.

## How a man reads

If you want the short Gospel, take Mark. If you want the deep one, take John. Read a portion daily. A few weeks of that and you have been watching the Man you claim.

Then Proverbs, a chapter a morning, for a month. Hugh began at midlife, the morning he saw that his father's opinion had steered his whole grown life, and that his father had been dead for years. One chapter, aloud, in the truck on the way to work. The book is worn through. A younger man asked how Hugh knew what to do about money, women, anger, and friends. A chapter a day, Hugh said. Those pages are a father speaking to a son, and a man can stay with them for a lifetime.

When you cannot find a prayer, open a Psalm and read it aloud to God as your own words. David left them for that.

Every time, one question. What does an unwatched morning require of a man, and will you do it before noon? Read with a brother. One of you brings the verse that has had hold of you and says why. Say it where you can hear yourself. The cab. The porch. Supper, with the children in the room.

## A story bigger than your story

God knows your story, the shapes and the shut room included. Your stretch is not the master story. Read only that stretch and you will misread it. What was done to you did not open the story. Your sin will not close it. A rule list is the small use of the fixed point.

A garden opens the master story, and a man in it built for work. Two figures stand at the hinge, and two manners of manhood. Hold the line on the whole account. Adam is at the beginning. The Man who completes it is at the end.

Lose the fixed point and you are lost inside the story, past the trouble of a single week. The work you were built for goes blank.

## The star that moves under you

Other men his own age usually supply the measure a young man uses. How he treats a woman may be whatever got a laugh in the truck. A chapter of Mark in that truck before the day starts will not move when the laughter does.

A new father often lets his feelings set the measure. On almost no sleep he decides the marriage is failing, when the marriage is tired. Ten minutes with Scripture before anyone picks up the phone will outwork an hour of trying the marriage's case after dark.

If divorce is your chair, other men and the look of success will both call. Her house against yours. A life that seems to work against the one that did not. Put the other walls down and hold the string on yours. Your Father sees what is hidden. Her results are not the mark He writes on you.

Through the working years a man often lets success set the measure, that wall still upright. The job kept telling him he was fine. His wife was building a life that left him out. A balance sheet cannot do what a plumb line does.

An older man often steered by the world that formed him, and now he hears that world laughed at. Scripture will mend what a generation got wrong and keep what that generation got right. The string ignores the decade that built the wall.

## The house that held

Jesus closed His longest sermon with a picture a man already understands.

:::margin
Matthew 7:24-25 (NIV)
"Therefore everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice is like a wise man who built his house on the rock. The rain came down, the streams rose, and the winds blew and beat against that house; yet it did not fall, because it had its foundation on the rock."
:::

Hears, and puts into practice. The wise man is the hearer who also does the thing. Under him is ground that stays. Rain arrives. The house holds.

Sand held the other build. It looked all right until the rain. He had been doing whatever seemed fit, sighting along walls that stood on sand too.

Explanation is not required before a fixed thing can save you. Hold the string to the wall. Let the weight quit moving.

## Before you turn

February can arrive with Leviticus still ahead of you. Put the string on your own wall. May the Father light your feet this week and stay patient while you learn the tool.
