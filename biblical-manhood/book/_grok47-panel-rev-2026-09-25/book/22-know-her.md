# 22. Know Her

He keeps a notebook on the sill. The first morning the wrens came back, he wrote the date, the weather, and which box they took. One note is enough for him to split a chickadee from a nuthatch. He has been at that book since he was a boy.

Ask him what his wife is afraid of this year and the notebook does him no good.

They have shared a table for years. He knows her chair. He thought the chair was the woman.

Before a man lays his life down, and before he sorts out what she needs, he owes her this. He has to know who he married.

## Live with her according to knowledge

Peter gave husbands one sentence, and it has carried more weight than almost anything else written straight to a married man.

:::margin
1 Peter 3:7 (NIV)
"Husbands, in the same way be considerate as you live with your wives, and treat them with respect as the weaker partner and as heirs with you of the gracious gift of life, so that nothing will hinder your prayers."
:::

Be considerate as you live with your wives. Older translations sit closer to the Greek. Live with her according to knowledge. Dwell there as a man who is learning her. Make her a study you stay in.

Men skip the last clause. So that nothing will hinder your prayers. A husband who shares a house and will not understand the woman in it has a problem that reaches past her. The Father is in it. The way you know your wife and the way you are heard belong in the same conversation.

We study what we love for years without being asked. A lot of husbands learned enough to get along, closed the book, and have lived on those first notes ever since. She changed. They missed it. She did not. A shared roof will not do the studying. She can share your bed for decades and still be a stranger in it. Rejecting passivity in a marriage looks like study. Knowing her is that work.

## What there is to know

"Know your wife" is a line men nod at and then leave on the table.

Learn her story. How she was fathered, and what that taught her about men. What happened before you that she has never told all the way. Ask her to walk it slowly. You will hear why she flinches at a thing you filed under nothing.

Learn the fears she actually has. Ask what frightens her most right now, then stay quiet. Given room, most wives name something the husband did not have on his list. Money is rarely the center. Being left. The children. Becoming her mother. Whether he sees her.

Learn what fills her and what empties her. A full room brings some women alive and wrings others out. Plan the wrong days often enough and you will call her difficult when you were only careless.

Learn how love lands on her. Men usually give the kind they would want back. Doing, fixing, providing, handling it. Some wives receive that. Some need words, or an hour with no agenda, or a touch that is going nowhere near the bedroom, or the job done before they had to ask. Ask when she has felt most loved by you. Repeat that.

Learn her body without getting cute about it. Her cycle, so you carry more in the week she has less. Pregnancy. The stretch after a birth. The later changes a lot of husbands sail past. She lives in that body every hour.

Learn her walk with God. How she prays, what she is wrestling, which verse she is standing on. Then you can pray for a real need instead of a foggy bless-her.

Learn the ordinary day, hour by hour. Midafternoon with children. The hard stretch at work. A man who has never asked will say things that land like a slap and wonder why she went quiet.

Learn her people, her friends and her mother, and what those ties cost. Learn what she still wants, what she set down, what she quit saying because nobody asked. Honor those things. Look for a way to hand a few back. She will trust a man who does.

:::margin
Proverbs 20:5 (NIV)
"The purposes of a person's heart are deep waters, but one who has insight draws them out."
:::

Deep waters. She is not going to float this to you on the surface. Drawing it out takes attention, and attention takes time, and time takes a decision that she is worth the study.

## How a man learns his wife

Ask, then wait. Most men ask and start building their reply before she has finished the first sentence. When she goes quiet, count a slow ten. She is usually not done. The true thing comes in after the gap.

:::margin
James 1:19 (NIV)
"My dear brothers and sisters, take note of this: Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to become angry,"
:::

Hear her before you repair anything. Your body will want a solution. Hold it. Tell her you are still in it, and ask for the rest. Fix her every time and she will quit talking. Hear her and she will hand you the house.

Watch what she does when she is tired, what makes her laugh when she is not performing, what she reaches for when she is sad.

Write the small things. Sizes. Friends' names. A want she mentioned once. The day her father died. The fear she finally named. She notices when a one-time sentence comes back to her later.

Ask what you misunderstand about her. Skip the defense. Take notes. That half hour will teach you more than a year of assuming.

Bring three questions home on the same night each week. What happened. What is heavy. What is coming. Answer them yourself before you ask her to.

## The man who opened a clean page

The man with the bird book asked her. Nothing clever. What are you afraid of this year. He waited past the answer she offered first. She kept talking. He wrote. He turned to a clean page and put her name at the top.

He is still filling it. He said it felt like meeting her again, in the house where they had been standing side by side the whole time.

The years next to her were never the study. The study is the page you leave open.

## This stretch

If a newborn is in the house, the woman across from you is under a weight you cannot see. She may not have slept a full night in months. She may be grieving who she was, and feeling guilty for the grief. Ask what she needs in the next hour. At three in the morning the bigger question is too large. Notice if she has not eaten, showered, been outside, or laughed. If she does not want to be touched, believe the exhaustion. The load is the reason. It will pass.

Watch for a darkness that is more than tired. It has a name. It is common. A doctor can help. The husband is usually the first to see it. If she is not herself for weeks, if she cannot sleep even when the baby does, if she says things about herself that scare you, take her in and go with her. That is a man who knows his wife and keeps her.

A wife in that stretch said the line that would change a house if a husband could hear it. "I do not need you to fix it. I need you to know how hard this is."

A long marriage has its own risk. You know a version of her from years back, and the file went stale. Body, fears, stacked grief. Ask what she wants with the years left, and expect a new answer.

In the middle years you are likely still on the early notes. The question you are afraid to ask is often the one the house needs.

If the marriage ended, and the truth is that you did not know her, put that inside "I did this." You still have to know their mother well enough to parent without a war. What she fears for the kids. What sets her off. What calms it. If God gives you another wife, open the page on day one.

If you are single, practice on the women who are already your people, as sisters. Ask a real question and wait for the whole answer. Practice with your mother. Practice with your sister. Learn to listen without fixing and without flirting. A man who can hear a sister will know how to hear a wife.

## How He knows His bride

Christ knows His church by name. Fear, wound, hidden thing. What He knows does not send Him out of the room. He keeps drawing the deep water up.

:::margin
John 10:14 (NIV)
"I am the good shepherd; I know my sheep and my sheep know me"
:::

I know my sheep. That is the pattern. A shepherd who stays at it. Let her know that she is known.

## Before you turn

You may feel behind. Most husbands are, and behind still leaves you time. She is waiting to see whether you will ask. May the Father who knows His sheep by name give you patience to draw the deep water up, one question at a time.
