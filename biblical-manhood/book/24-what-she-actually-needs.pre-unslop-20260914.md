# 24. What She Actually Needs

Dale heard it from the mudroom, standing on one foot with a boot in his hand. Renee was on the phone with her sister, answering some question about how things were: "Oh, he's a good provider," in the voice you'd use for a furnace. Nothing in it was false. He'd worked long weeks for most of their marriage so she could say that sentence, and he understood two things at once: it was the kindest true thing she could find, and it would be his eulogy if he let it.

"That's what you say about a man at his funeral," he told me later, "when you can't think of the next thing." I've stood at the front of enough of those services to know the pause that comes after it, where the rest of the sentence should be.

## The measurable thing

Here's the principle, early, so nothing else gets mistaken for it. She doesn't need more of what you make. She needs you.

"Good provider" is a furnace word. It means the house stayed warm, not that anyone was home. Men get this backward not because they don't love their wives but because provision is measurable and presence isn't. The paycheck, the truck, the fixed faucet: he can see those, and Scripture says they count. But they aren't what she needs most, and a man who pours himself into the part he can see will build her a beautiful house and leave her alone in it.

## Steadiness

She needs to know you aren't going anywhere. A wife can tell when there's fine print on the vow, an "unless" in the back of his head for when it gets bad enough. She'll never fully rest in a marriage with fine print, so strike it. Say it out loud, more than once: "I'm not going anywhere, ever, and whatever this is, we're doing it together." Then keep the oath when it hurts, and let her watch you keep it.

:::margin
Malachi 2:15 (NIV)
"Has not the one God made you? You belong to him in body and spirit. And what does the one God seek? Godly offspring. So be on your guard, and do not be unfaithful to the wife of your youth."
:::

## Presence

She needs you to be here when you're here. A man can be home every night and present on none of them, and that wife is lonelier than one whose husband travels, because at least she knows why she's alone. Presence means your attention is in the room. The phone is down. You ask a question and wait for the whole answer. You're a person she's with, not a body she's near.

## Providing without disappearing

This is the trap that catches the best men.

A good man provides, and the work asks for more, and he gives it, because giving to the work feels like giving to the family. You know you've started to disappear when she stops asking when you'll be home, when you find out about your own house secondhand, when you're too tired at night to be anybody, and when you've told yourself she wants the lifestyle without ever asking her.

Dale asked. One question at the kitchen table: "What do you need from me that you're not getting?" Renee didn't have to think. "Look up when I come into a room." He'd provided an entire house and had stopped lifting his eyes when she walked into it.

He didn't quit his job. Most men can't. Here's what he did.

He set a floor, the few things that don't move no matter what the job does: supper at the table four nights, Sunday morning, bedtime prayer with the kids.

He said when he'd be home and was home then. Not "I'll try for six," but "6:40," and then 6:40, and if it changed he called before, not after. Reliability is presence at a distance.

He gave her his good hours. His phone spends the first hour at home on top of the refrigerator, and she gets the first fifteen minutes while he's still a person.

And he quit playing for the scoreboard. Work claps and home mostly doesn't, and he wasn't working for the clapping anymore, but for a Father who sees the supper table.

This spring Dale heard Renee on the phone with the same sister, from the same spot in the mudroom. She didn't say provider. She said, "He's here," and moved on to something about their mother, like it wasn't news.

## Safety

She needs to not be afraid of you, and I don't mean fists; most of you never would. I mean the voice that drops a register, the three-day silence, the sarcasm that draws blood, the truck that leaves fast. A wife who has learned to read her husband's weather before she speaks isn't safe in her own house. A gentle answer isn't a weak one; it's what a man gives when he's strong enough not to need volume. A wife who knows his anger will never land on her has a freedom most wives never get.

:::margin
Proverbs 15:1 (NIV)
"A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger."
:::

## Pursued, still

The man who asked her out should still be asking her out. The pursuit that got her tends to stop at the wedding, because it was for getting her, and she feels it as being acquired and shelved. Rejoice in the wife of your youth, the proverb says, not tolerate or manage. Plan a Thursday. Ask her out with a specific evening and an ironed shirt. Look at her across a room the way you did the first year and let her catch you. Pursuit is how a husband says, every year, that he'd choose her again.

## Leadership she can rest in

She isn't asking to be ruled. She's asking not to be the only adult in the house, for a man who'll say "here's where we're going and how we'll get there," so she can set the map down. A woman married to a man who won't lead ends up leading by default, exhausted and resentful and unsure she's allowed to say so. Leading means going first into the hard decision and owning it. And it means praying with her, out loud, once a week, holding her hand. It does something to a woman to hear her husband talk to God about her.

## Heard and known

There's a whole chapter behind us on this, so one line here: she needs a man who wants to know her, not one who assumes he already does.

## Words

She needs to hear it. Men think it's understood, and it isn't; it's needed out loud, in specific words, regularly, in front of the children. "You surpass them all" is not "you're a good wife." It's a man telling his wife she's the best one there is, where people can hear. Say it this week.

:::margin
Proverbs 31:28-29 (NIV)
"Her children arise and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praises her: 'Many women do noble things, but you surpass them all.'"
:::

## Help that isn't helping

She needs the load shared, and she needs you to stop calling it helping. The dishes are your dishes; the kids' schedules are your schedules. A husband who "helps out" has told his wife the whole thing is hers and he's a volunteer. Eve was called a helper, and it's a title of strength. Christ came to serve. Neither truth lets a man sit while his wife runs the house around him.

## Tenderness

She needs to be touched without it being about the bedroom. A hand on her back in the kitchen. Her feet after a long day. Being held while she cries without you trying to fix the crying. Most men touch their wives for two reasons: to get past them in a hallway and to initiate. She needs a third kind more than the second, and if she gets the third, the second will go better than it ever has.

## A man who's growing

She needs to see you moving, toward God and toward the four marks. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. A husband who's stuck, the same man he was when the kids were small with more weight and less patience, is a weight she carries. A husband who's growing, even slowly, even with failures, is a man she can follow. A wife married to a man who's becoming something has hope in the house.

## Her seasons

The seasons change what the list sounds like, not what it means. Under a newborn, she needs sleep more than anything you can buy, and you in the room at three in the morning even when you can't feed him. Decades in, she needs to be seen as a woman and not a fixture, plans for the years left made together, and you at your checkups, because her quiet fear is finding you on the floor. If you're in the divorced seat, read this list honestly against the marriage that ended, not to bury yourself but so that whatever comes next, you aren't the same man. Your children's mother still needs your reliability, your civility, and your backing in front of the kids; undermining her across two houses hurts them to win a point. If you're single, this is the man you're building now, and your mother could use a few of these from her grown son: presence, words, a call that's about her.

## What He gives His bride

Everything on this list, Christ gives the church. Steadiness: never will I leave you. Presence: I am with you always. Safety: gentle and humble in heart. Pursuit: He came looking for us. Leadership: follow me. Known: I know my sheep. Words: this is my body, given for you. Help: a towel and a basin. Tenderness: He wept at a friend's grave. Growth: even the Son of God grew in wisdom and stature and in favor.

She isn't asking for anything He doesn't already give you. She's asking for a small copy of it, in a house, on an ordinary weeknight. It doesn't cost money. It costs you, which is the only thing she ever wanted.

## Before you turn the page

If you read this and felt the floor drop, hear this: it's one of the most fixable things in the book. She isn't asking for a different man. She's asking for the one she married to look up. May the Father who gives His bride steadiness and presence and words and tenderness give you the courage to ask her one question this week and the grace to hear the whole answer. Presence costs less and gives more than you fear.
