# 23. Lay It Down

He was right, and I want that on the table first, because it matters. They were standing in an inch of water on the bathroom floor. She had taken the cheap quote. He had said the job would fail. The job had failed. The estimate he had wanted was still folded in his pocket, and he could feel the sentence loading, the one that would end the night and prove the point.

He put the paper in the trash.

"I'm sorry I got loud," he said. "We'll figure it out."

She looked at him as if the man in the doorway were a stranger, and then she started to cry. It took him a while to understand the tears. In all their years he had never once set down a fight he was winning.

You have been learning her. Lay your life down for her now. The altar in a good suit was the easy hour. The ruined room, on a night you are right, is where the vow gets a body.

## The harder job

Men love one line out of Ephesians 5, and they quote a different line less.

The loved line is wives, submit. Men underline it. Paul wrote it, and it is Scripture. The next thing he writes is addressed to us. It runs longer. It weighs more. I have never heard a man underline it in the same voice.

:::margin
Ephesians 5:25 (NIV)
"Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her"
:::

Gave himself up for her, the way Christ did. Love her the way Jesus loved the people He died for, all the way, on purpose, while they were still failing Him. Men want the authority in that chapter and hope the cross stays on the page. Headship is a cross. The man out front takes what is coming. The whole gift aims at her good.

:::margin
Philippians 2:5-7 (NIV)
"In your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus: Who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage; rather, he made himself nothing by taking the very nature of a servant, being made in human likeness."
:::

He had every right. He held higher ground than any husband will ever stand on. He set it down and took a towel. That is the mind behind the words just as Christ. That is the mind you need when you are standing in water with the proof still in your pocket.

## What a husband lays down

Lay down your life sounds like one dramatic hour. Most of it happens in pieces, on days nobody will remember but her.

He sets down the right to be right. You will be right often. Most of those wins are worth less than what the winning costs her. You do not have to pretend the facts moved. You decide the point is smaller than the two of you. We'll figure it out. The back half of that sentence is the marriage.

He sets down his preferences. The meal, the screen, the way a Saturday goes. Gladly, with no scoreboard. A husband who fights for every small thing has told her his comfort outranks her.

He sets down the phone. Face down when you come through the door. Out of your hand while she is talking. I have watched a man look at a screen through a whole conversation and swear he was listening. She knew.

He sets down his day off. Golf, the shop, the game. None of those is a sin. When the house needs you, the day loses, and it loses without a sulk.

He sets down the first minutes after work. Those belong to her, ahead of the chair.

He sets down his version when other people are listening. In front of the kids, your parents, your friends, take her side. A wife defended in public can survive a hard kitchen. A wife used as the joke learns she is alone in a friendly room.

He sets down his mother's vote. When your mother and your wife disagree, your wife wins, and your mother hears you say it. You left that house. Live like it.

He sets down sleep. When she is sick, when a child is up, you get up. Guarding your rest while she runs on almost nothing is a way of spending her so you can keep yourself.

He sets down the anger where she can see it. Take the heat to the truck, to God, and to a brother. Bring her what was underneath it.

He sets down the money as his private kingdom. It is the house's money. She has full standing, full sight, full say. A husband who keeps her in the dark is ruling a subject, and he should stop calling that leadership.

And he sets down the future he drew with her out of the room. If the next years exist only in your head, put them down and draw them again where she can hold the pencil.

## What laying down isn't

There is a counterfeit, and it has wrecked marriages from the other direction. It is the first mark turned over.

Abdication borrows the language of a laid-down life. A husband who quits leading, quits deciding, quits going first, and calls it letting her have her way, has disappeared. She will feel the absence even on the days she gets what she asked for. Christ gave Himself up and kept leading. He led with the towel on. He led from the cross. A strong man chooses her good over his comfort. A weak man chooses quiet over the job.

A doormat borrows it too. Jesus said hard true things to the people He loved. A husband who never tells his wife the truth has set down the assignment. Love speaks. It speaks gently, at the right hour, after it has listened. Then it speaks.

And a man can erase himself and call it holy. She married someone with a spine and a direction. If you dissolve until nobody is home, you have taken her husband away. Set down preferences, rights, comfort, pride. Keep a self. She needs one.

Here is a test you can run after you have set something down. Is she stronger, or is she carrying more because you left? If she is more secure, more herself, more alive, you did the real thing. If the house got heavier because you checked out and named it humility, you ran the counterfeit.

## This stretch

If a newborn is in the house, take a shift before she has to beg. Kill the silent tally of sleep and showers. Do one unpaid thing and skip the announcement. Leave the bedroom alone until she is ready. Set the gym and the guys down for this year. Love the wife who is here. Your Father sees three in the morning.

If the marriage has years on it, set down the retirement you planned by yourself. Ask her what she wants those years to be, and then build that. Set down the remote. Hand her some of the say, because she can hold it, and she has been waiting. When illness comes, the metaphor ends. I have watched a man bathe his wife every morning for years after the sickness took most of her. She does not always know him. He knows her. He said in sickness. He simply had not seen the room yet. There is a Father who sees that morning.

If you are in the divorced seat, you may be staring at what you would not set down while you had the chance. Grieve it. Confess your part. Then do the next thing. Set down your right to win against the mother of your children. You will be right sometimes, and the kids still need two parents who can stand in one room. That has to be you. Set down the story where it was all her. Let God hold the rest.

If you are single, practice while nobody is handing you a ring. A man who has never lost a preference will not become good at it between the vows and the cake. Let somebody else pick the meal. Give a Saturday to an older man who needs the help. Small deaths, on ordinary days, on purpose. That is the training.

## The reward for the man who loses

Jesus said a line about laying a life down that sounds like a loss and turns out to be a promise.

:::margin
Matthew 16:25 (NIV)
"For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me will find it."
:::

The man who loses his life finds it. You set down the right to be right, and you find a wife who will trust you with the truth. You set down the Saturday, and you find a marriage that is still breathing years from now. You set down the sleep, and you find a woman who remembers that you got up.

The Father sees the fight you lost on purpose, the night shift, the bath she may not remember. None of it is wasted. The Man who laid everything down will look at a husband who laid a little down and say well done. You did it the way I did.

The man on the wet floor was right that night. He set the proof in the trash anyway. His wife told me later that the night was when she started trusting him with her fears. A man who will give up a fight he is winning is a man who will not use her honesty against her.

You will be right this week, more than once. Set it down.

## Before you turn

The man who loses his life finds it. None of the quiet losses are wasted. May the Father who watched His Son lay everything down give you strength to set one thing down this week, and may you find the house different because you did.
