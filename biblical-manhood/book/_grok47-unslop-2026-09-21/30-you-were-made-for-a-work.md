# 30. You Were Made for a Work

Hard rain, and Dale is on the county grader before daylight. That blade has been his as long as his youngest has been alive. He pulls the washboard out of forty miles of gravel. School buses run it. So does the ambulance. Nobody on those roads can give you his name.

After church he stopped me. Did the work count for God. Calling sermons had split his week. The building was the real part. Fifty hours on the grader only paid the way in.

"I'm just running a blade," he said.

Kids get to school on what that blade leaves, and the sick get to a hospital. God saw the road first. Does the job belong inside the life, or is it only a fee until real life starts? Blank the work and most of a life goes with it. I will not allow that. Scripture will not either.

## Work came before the curse

Genesis puts a man in a garden to work the ground and guard it, before sin, and before a curse mentions sweat. Thorns came later. Read only the fall and a paycheck sounds like something God resents.

God works. Religious men caught Jesus doing it on a day they had forbidden. He answered them where everyone could hear.

:::margin
John 5:17 (NIV)
"In his defense Jesus said to them, 'My Father is always at his work to this very day, and I too am working.'"
:::

You serve a God who stays at a task. He made you in that image, so work was included. A layoff undoes a man in places a check never reaches. A retirement does it. A body that quits does it. He was shaped for a garden. The garden is gone.

Most of Jesus' life was a trade. People preach the three years. The long part was a shop, making things for people who paid. The plain word for the man on the grader is yes. It counts. That blade is the life, or most of what the life is.

## What work is for

Provision. Paul did not soften it. Will not work, and you do not eat. He meant the refusal. The man who cannot is under grace. The man who refuses is under a rule.

Service. Buses trust the road Dale leaves. Your job is forty hours of loving a neighbor. Shabby work is shabby love. Paul told thieves to quit taking and start making, and he said why.

:::margin
Ephesians 4:28 (NIV)
"Anyone who has been stealing must steal no longer, but must work, doing something useful with their own hands, that they may have something to share with those in need."
:::

Work puts something in a taker's hand for somebody else.

You leave the world more than you found it, a small plot of what God did across six days. The finished job feels right because the image is showing, and the work is forming you as you do it.

Worship. Whatever you do, do it with your whole heart, as for the Lord. On that blade Dale is working for the Lord, name known or not. The four marks The last of those four turns a time card into an offering.

Son, brother, husband, father, keeper of a garden. That is the name. The job is one garden in the row. Tie your name to the job and you die when the job dies. The shop will clap for you. The kitchen mostly will not. Stay because the shop is easier than home, and a good gift has become the door out. Standing with God is already yours in Christ. You work because you are His.

## When you hate the job

Some of you cannot stand the job, and "work is worship" lands like a slap. Thorns are real. Foolish bosses. Customers who lie. Systems that grind a man down. Solomon had every luxury and every kind of labor, and he still came here.

:::margin
Ecclesiastes 2:24 (NIV)
"A person can do nothing better than to eat and drink and find satisfaction in their own toil. This too, I see, is from the hand of God,"
:::

God gives that satisfaction on three paths. Skill, a whole craft on hated work, keeps what the job cannot take. The men beside you. An apprentice watches how a bad day gets carried. A coworker watches his marriage come apart. Stay for them even if you hate the task. The audience. Aim at the boss and his mood runs the job. Aim at the Lord and the Lord does.

Leave like a man if leaving is right. A feeling makes a poor resignation letter. Bring a plan, your wife in the room, the next thing or savings to bridge the gap, and a brother to test the reasons. Men quit in a huff. Or they stay, and call the staying a life, when it is a coffin.

## When you lose the job

Thursday they call you in. Friday the garden is empty. Money is rarely the first hit. A sentence has introduced you for years, and now it stops at "I'm."

Your wife hears it that day, before any plan. Men bury a layoff for weeks. She learns the hiding. Your brother hears it that week. "What's heavy" finally has an answer. Treat the search as the job. Same hour up. Shaved. Table by eight. Looking until five. Take bridge work. Nothing that feeds the house is beneath you. Then kill the shame. Guilt says you did a wrong thing. Shame says you are a wrong thing. The lost job is neither. It is a thorn your Father already saw.

## When your work is the house

Some work here does not look like work, even to you. The money put you home with the children. Or you bathe your wife every morning because she cannot, which is the second verb of the garden, keeping what you were given. It may be the hardest work in the book. A house and a body, if that is the plot, tend it and guard it. The Father sees what is hidden. He does not score you by the traffic going by.

## After the last day

Alton, at the creek. You know the morning after his retirement dinner. Scripture owns no recliner with your name on it. It owns a shift.

:::margin
Numbers 8:25-26 (NIV)
"but at the age of fifty, they must retire from their regular service and work no longer. They may assist their fellow Levites in performing their duties at the tent of meeting, but they themselves must not do the work. This, then, is how you are to assign the responsibilities of the Levites."
:::

Then assist. They set the heavy pieces down, remained Levites, and stood beside the men who still carried the poles. The garden changes.

Any man past the last paycheck can use what Alton built. The alarm stayed put. Rise when you feel like it and the drift already has half of you. Coffee he made, and he remained while his wife finished her cup, after a marriage of mornings spent elsewhere. A chapter, out loud, from the porch. He needed a plumb line a badge could not move. Every day, one job a person was counting on. A footing. A widow's gutters. The church van. "A man with no job by noon," he said, "is a man in a chair by two." Two younger men who needed someone ahead of them got his time. His grandson got hills.

The body can quit before the years. Assist is still the word. One man I know cannot be away from his wife beyond an hour. Widowers get the call in that hour. He asks what is heavy. Drift wants the older man, and a fishing hat is how it dresses. Anger comes in beside it, through the television. Carry less. Assist more. Tell the next men what you know until you are done.

## Work in the other seasons

Single, this job is the main garden. Tend it as ground worth handing a wife, and stay unwed to the work itself. Getting loose takes years.

A baby makes one more shop hour look sane. The shop is the quiet room. Say so. Provision is holy. Slipping away from the house is not.

Ended marriages often carry the tightest money here. Two households, a lawyer, and maybe more hours. Take the hours and stay findable by the children. A cab call on the way home is free. Their world shook. A father who leaves for work and returns is ground under them.

## He finished the work

The night before He died, Jesus said it.

:::margin
John 17:4 (NIV)
"I have brought you glory on earth by finishing the work you gave me to do."
:::

A man wants to say it at the end. Given a work, he did it, and he finished. Shop, preaching, and cross were one assignment. He closed it. Blade, classroom, crew, a body that needs bathing. The handling of it will be most of the life.

Dale has not climbed down. Last spring the habit started. Crown of the road over the hill, and he prays for each house he comes to. Morning drivers use his gravel. None of them have met the man who asked God to keep them while he kept the road.

Go do the work.

## Before you turn the page

If the hours have seemed useless to God, carry another picture in tomorrow. The Son of God at a bench for most of His life. Layoff, a body that quit, or a birthday with a plaque, you were moved. Same man, other garden. May the Father who stays at His work put one needed task in your hands by noon tomorrow.
