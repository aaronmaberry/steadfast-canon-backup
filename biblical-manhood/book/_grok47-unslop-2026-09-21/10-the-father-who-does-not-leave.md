# 10. The Father Who Does Not Leave

Stand at a grade school when the bell lets out at three and watch where the eyes go. The children are hunting one car, and the sidewalk tells you who has found it. A backpack loosens. The search quits. They are talking before the door is fully open. A few keep hunting. Car after car fails them, and one child ends on the curb beside a teacher who checks her phone while the line thins to nothing.

Curtis has held a spot in that line on every school day for years. Twenty minutes early. Radio off. He swore no child of his would be the one left with the teacher, and he has kept the oath. He has also never been the child who comes through the doors and sees his own car. The line never contained anyone for him. Home was a walk he learned.

This chapter is about a Father who ends the hunt. He is already in the line when the bell rings. Some of you hear the word father and the trouble starts there, so that is where I will start. I have aimed prayers at a copy and called the aiming faith.

## The trouble with the word

"God is your Father" lands according to the chair at the end of his childhood table.

Vacancy taught you that a father is someone who fails to arrive. You grant God the same kind of existence you grant your dad. Out there. Busy with something. Absent from here.

Silence in a full chair taught you that a father can share a room and still have no sentence for you. Prayer takes on the temperature of a man speaking at a page that never answers.

Danger taught you to read the weather before you risk a word. The Christian life becomes a daily investigation into what God is angry about, and you are fairly sure the finding is you.

A good chair put you ahead of most of the room. Even that father tired out and missed things, and under your prayers sits the guess that God will miss them too.

You have been reading heaven through your dad. His picture was the only father you owned, so you held it up and assumed the original matched. Years of prayers have been soured by that assumption. Scripture reverses the order of the two pictures. God is the original. Your father was the copy. Set the copy down. Look at the original through the only Man who has seen Him.

:::margin
John 14:9 (NIV)
"Jesus answered: 'Don't you know me, Philip, even after I have been among you such a long time? Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father. How can you say, "Show us the Father"?'"
:::

Meet Jesus, and you have met the Father's character.

## What He is like

He moves in your direction. The story of a father that Jesus made famous concerns a son who left, spent the whole inheritance, and came back smelling of pigs with a speech rehearsed. Watch the father. He is out on the road.

:::margin
Luke 15:20 (NIV)
"So he got up and went to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him."
:::

A long way off. You see a figure at that distance only when the road has had your eyes for a while. Grown men in that culture stayed off a run. This father ran. Getting yourself together is not the toll he charges for the approach.

He tells you your name before you have produced a result. At the baptism, ahead of any sermon and ahead of any healing, the Father spoke.

:::margin
Matthew 3:17 (NIV)
"And a voice from heaven said, 'This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased.'"
:::

Three sentences a boy needs from his father. You are mine. I love you. I am pleased with you. Jesus heard them at the beginning. That is the Father's order. The blessing comes, and the work is handed out after. Your own father may have made you earn a sentence that ought to have been a gift. This Father gives it. In Christ, the sentence is already over your head.

He carries. Moses pictured a father carrying his son the whole distance, heavy years included. The need is known before you ask, and it does not put Him off. Stay there if needing once proved dangerous. Discipline comes because you are His. Hebrews reads a hard season as sonship. Discipline and rage were never the same act. A hard season means He stayed.

He also remains. Never will I leave you. Never will I forsake you. The night before the cross, to men about to watch Him taken, Jesus set the promise in another form.

:::margin
John 14:18 (NIV)
"I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you."
:::

He chose orphans because that feeling was about to land on those men, and some of you have carried it since boyhood. The promise is His arrival.

## The hardest thing for a man to do

This ought to be the light chapter. For many men it is the heavy one. Fathering yourself from boyhood leaves no practice at being a son. You can affirm the doctrine and still lock up when the weight is supposed to move to other shoulders. Alone long enough, a lean feels like a fall.

Curtis said the honest version. "I know God's my Father. I don't know how to be anybody's son. Nobody ever showed me the receiving end." Give me that sentence before you give me a polished one. Men who learn sonship late tend to be helped by the same few practices.

Say Father where you can hear it. Other prayers can keep God and Lord. This one gets the name. Say it alone, while it is awkward. Paul writes that the Spirit already in you cries "Abba, Father," and Abba belongs in a small boy's mouth. The word is permitted. It will feel strange. Continue until the strangeness thins.

Bring Him the exit that is yours, anger or the itch or the drift. He hears it before the brother does and before the counselor does, and He hears it uncleaned. "Father, I'm about to blow up at my kids." "Father, I'm reaching for the phone again." The one you meet has been watching the road.

Let Him say who you are. Read, each day, something He has spoken about His sons, and answer it as true of you. A man never told his name will need the telling repeated. Patience for that repetition lives in the Father.

Let the carrying be real. This year something will outweigh you, and the reflex will be to grind alone. Say the other sentence out loud. "I can't carry this. Carry me." Then let a brother help. A real man is a son before he is anything further. Learning the order is the work.

Remain inside the house. The Word is where He talks. The church is where His other children gather. Prayer is where He hears. Show up only on holidays and an ordinary afternoon will never teach you His voice.

## What this does to your other father

Fathering from God changes the view of the man who raised you, or failed to. Sometimes the view gets clearer, and clearer costs more. The project of forcing that man into a shape he never had comes to an end, and the scanning with it. Good can be honored. Harm can be named. Rage can be set down. Forgiveness becomes possible once you have stopped waiting to be paid. You may even move toward him. That road has its own chapter. None of those moves open while you are still trying to draw from your dad the one supply that belongs to your Father alone. The chair at your old table was always too small for the chair in your chest. He is the one who fills it.

## Learning it in your season

Learn it while you are young and least convinced you need another father, and decades of scanning get spared. A new baby teaches it from the crib. Love for a sleeping child ignores achievement. That look is aimed at you. A marriage that ended needs this chapter most. The Father stayed when the marriage ended. Some of you quit praying, sure His disappointment had joined the room's. He was watching the road the whole time. If you are older and have never said Father aloud, the word will feel foolish for about a week, then warm, the way heat feels after a long cold. You have waited long enough.

## Before the bell

The pickup line became the place Curtis practiced the word. Alone, and the awkwardness cost him nothing there. Father. At the start he might as well have been addressing the windshield. One sentence a day, and he kept the practice. Somewhere in the second week the glass stopped being the audience. What replaced it was the sense of a person in the seat next to him.

An afternoon came when his youngest pushed through the doors, searched the line, found their car, and ended the search. Curtis watched and understood the offer. Years of being the man who held a place in the line. Never once the child who finds the car. He was being told the second role was open to him.

This is the Father's work in a man. The scanning can cease. The audition can cease. Standing with Him was settled in the Son, and today was never the verdict. John writes, See what great love the Father has lavished on us, that we should be called children of God. And that is what we are. Sons, as a fact already spoken. He stood in the line before the bell. Drop your shoulders. You have been found.

## Before you turn the page

He knows sons are slow. Say the word badly in the morning, and again the next. May the Father who watched for the prodigal watch for you. This week, come out those doors and meet the One who was already in the line.
