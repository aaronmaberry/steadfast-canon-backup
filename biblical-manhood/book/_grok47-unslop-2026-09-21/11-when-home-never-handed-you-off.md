# 11. When Home Never Handed You Off

A creek can slip across a county line with no marker on the bank and no witness to announce the crossing. Most men became men that way. They woke holding a mortgage, or a badge, or a baby, and a part of them still wondered whether anyone had ever counted them in.

Nate belongs to that number. High school kids. Two vans with his name on the door. Saturday morning still finds him at his father's place, passing the old man a wrench. A disposal dies, or a gutter lets go, and Nate does the labor while his father stands close enough to narrate. Good has never been the old man's word. The word is, "should've used a longer screw." Every finished job at Nate's own house goes to his father as a photo. Correction comes back, every time. The next photo goes anyway.

The older brother took the other road out of the same kitchen. Summer after high school, he left inside a fight, and the years since have been a proof offered to a man he barely speaks with. One son slammed a door. One son, in every way that counts, remained. Neither received a handoff. Ask either of them for the morning he became a man and the morning will not exist.

## What a handoff is

For most of history, manhood was conferred. Men took a boy, put him through something, told him what a man was, laid a man's weight on him, and brought him into their company. He woke a man because they had said so and meant it.

Watch David, near death, place a kingdom into Solomon's hands.

:::margin
1 Kings 2:2-3 (NIV)
"'I am about to go the way of all the earth,' he said. 'So be strong, act like a man, and observe what the LORD your God requires: Walk in obedience to him, and keep his decrees and commands, his laws and regulations, as written in the Law of Moses. Do this so that you may prosper in all you do and wherever you go'"
:::

An older man, close to leaving. A word with content. Act like a man, and here is the meaning of the command. A weight. A kingdom, God's law set inside it. Paul did the same work with Timothy, and the work included his hands.

:::margin
2 Timothy 1:6 (NIV)
"For this reason I remind you to fan into flame the gift of God, which is in you through the laying on of my hands."
:::

Hands on the younger man, and the meaning was plain. You belong with us. Here is your work. Years later Timothy was tired and afraid, and Paul sent him back to that hour. Remember the handoff. Fan it into flame again.

Four pieces live in the pattern. A man. A word. A weight. A welcome. Most of you were given none. Some were given weight with the word withheld, a father stacking duty on you before a razor was any use, never once saying that the duty was a man's. Some were given the word with the weight withheld, a dad announcing "you're the man of the house now" on his way through the door and out.

## The counterfeits

Missing the handoff does not cancel the want. A boy goes looking. Substitutes are ready.

Sex fails first. No older man, no word, no weight, only a girl who never signed on to make anyone a man. A paycheck fails next. Pay a boy and he remains a boy. The slam is third. The fight, the drunk night, the birthday that was supposed to change his rank, the walkout mid-argument. A boy is trying to hand himself across. He cannot. Men give a handoff. He receives it.

Orbit is the quiet fourth. Adult years can circle the childhood house, waiting for a voice inside to say the circling may stop. Unsure he is a man, he will also refuse a man's weight, still waiting to be told the weight is his. Everything else this book asks sits on that telling.

## Jacob's night

Genesis records a man who spent half a life trying to steal what should have been given. Jacob wanted the blessing badly enough to wear his brother's clothes, deceive a blind father, and exit with the words. Theft did not finish the job. Twenty years followed, made of running and schemes. What the stolen blessing produced was a fugitive.

Then a night alone, on the road toward home. A Man wrestled him until daylight. Jacob finally did the thing missing from every earlier year. The scheme stopped. He held on.

:::margin
Genesis 32:26 (NIV)
"Then the man said, 'Let me go, for it is daybreak.' But Jacob replied, 'I will not let you go unless you bless me.'"
:::

Asking replaced stealing. A new name ended the grabber. A limp ended a boy's walk. In the morning he faced his brother as a man. God supplied, in the dirt and the dark, what a father had withheld, and half a life was already gone. Darkness where you stand does not mean the hour has closed.

## How a man receives it now

Hear the Father first. He speaks identity before performance, and in Christ He has already said it. This is my son, whom I love. Your Father gave the handoff your dad withheld. Another starting place will not hold.

Then ask an older man to say it aloud. The ask will feel awkward. Make it. Find a man older than you who is living these pages, and tell him the truth. Nobody ever told me I was a man. Would you? Let his hand rest on your shoulder for the saying. More older men than you imagine have waited their whole lives for a real request.

Let brothers receive you. A few who know you and count you as theirs. If none ever did, find a circle that will.

Pick up a man's weight before you feel ready. "Act like a man" was David's command. Solomon felt young and afraid and told God both. David told him to act anyway, and the acting was part of becoming. Carry it for a year. The men near you will treat you as one. Feeling catches up to a life already underway.

Write the date. Decision day, or the day the older man spoke, or the day you asked in the dark. Jacob got a limp. A date inside your Bible will do. No date, and you will half-wonder for the rest of your life whether you crossed.

## When the man in the chair won't say it

Certain fathers will never say it. Earning is not the missing piece. They never received a handoff of their own, or keeping you a boy is the only method they know for staying a man in their own eyes. Honor and the end of the orbit can share a week. Honor has a road ahead of us. Ending the orbit means the drive is no longer a collection run for the sentence. You go because he is your father. Release is no longer what you are waiting on. Leaving, in that form, breaks nothing on the way out. Nobody remains posted in the old kitchen for a word that will not come. The word is taken from the Father, and from men willing to give it.

Nate's came from an older man at church who had asked for help putting in a water heater. Afterward, at the utility sink, hands being washed, the man said, "I've watched you a long time. You're a good man. I'd trust you with anything I own." Nate produced no answer. He dried his hands twice. A handoff had occurred, and his father's chair had not performed it. A man willing to stand in the chair's place had. God still arranges men of that kind for the sons who ask.

## Handed off in every season

The young man is who this was built for, and he is least likely to receive it. It can arrive in pieces from any attentive man, so find men who pay attention. A young father doubts he can hand a boy across, because nobody handed him. He can. Giving heals a place receiving never reached. Divorce can feel like the title was removed. Wedding and role came apart, and he is unsure what remains. A courtroom did not cancel the Father's word. What you carry now is a man's weight. Let an older voice say so. If you are the older man, you are the other half of the repair. You have seen it in a younger man and never told him. Telling is the job. Most of Scripture's handoffs needed no stage, only a man who spoke, set a hand on a shoulder, and sent him. A utility sink is enough this week.

## Saturday, a year on

Saturdays still take Nate to his father. The old man still explains the work. The explaining lands in a different place, because the sentence is no longer the reason for the drive. The man is. A date sits inside Nate's Bible. The following week he called his brother. Their father had never been discussed between them without the fight still in the room. This time the fight stayed out.

You are permitted in the company of men. Permission was spoken by the Father before anyone else spoke. Find a man who will say your name as a man's name, where you can hear it. Then become a man who does the same.

## Before you turn the page

If no voice has ever told you that you are a man, let mine be one that does. The Father who spoke over His Son at the Jordan has spoken that kind of word over you. May God give you awkward courage for the asking, and plain courage for the telling.
