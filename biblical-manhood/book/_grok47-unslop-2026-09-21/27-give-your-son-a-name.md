# 27. Give Your Son a Name

A new father meets a pen before he meets much else. A form, a box, block letters. He thinks the naming ended when the ink dried. That was the small part. He will name the boy thousands of times before the front door closes behind him, and he will miss most of the times he does it.

Boys answer the name they are given. Who gives it is the only question left open. God put naming in a man's mouth, and a son makes the power large. Speak a name and he grows toward it. Leave it unspoken and he will hunt another mouth. The world is generous with names for boys. Good ones are scarce.

## The names he's getting anyway

School is already at work, and a few of those labels last a lifetime. Lazy. Weird. Too small. Too much. A culture scared of boys names them as a problem to be handled. Failure names him too. Every boy fails at nearly everything, and with no man to interrupt the verdict he will wear the failure as identity. I can't. I'm no good at this. I wreck it every time.

Home may be naming him in a voice the father does not hear. Ray heard his. A birthday video, his wife hitting play, singing over the top, and under it, off to the side, Ray. "What is wrong with you, bud?" He had no memory of the line. Memory's absence was the proof he had used it a hundred times. The boy's face on the screen did a small collapse and then put itself back, which is what a face does when it has been issued a name it intends to keep. Ray said he replayed those four seconds past the point of watching the party.

Grown men still sit with me and produce the exact word. The voice changes when they say it. A sigh names a boy. An eye roll names him. Why can't you be more like your sister names him.

## The three names

Three names, from his father, repeated, from the week he arrives until the week you are gone.

Mine comes first. Belonging. You are my son. I am staying. He has earned nothing yet, and he is already yours, and no failure evicts him. Jesus heard this one before the others, in the Jordan. You are my Son, whom I love, with you I am well pleased. Possession leads the sentence. A boy sure of the belonging can fail anywhere and still find the porch. A boy unsure will audition for the rest of his life.

:::margin
Mark 1:11 (NIV)
"And a voice came from heaven: 'You are my Son, whom I love; with you I am well pleased.'"
:::

Second, what I see in you. Character, the person ahead of the product. The window is broken and he tells the truth. You hand him the word. That is honesty. You are an honest man. You caught him being the thing God made, and you said the name a half step before he could have said it himself, so growth has a direction. Call him honest a hundred times and a lie will feel wrong in the mouth. Call him only a liar and he has no floor. Paul did this fathering with three verbs. Encourage. Comfort. Urge. I see it in you. Fail, and you are still mine. Now live up to the name. Urge alone and the boy cannot rest. Comfort alone and the boy will not grow.

Third, man. The name of what he is for, and the one most of us missed. Somewhere he can remember, he needs his father to say that he is becoming a man, and to say what the word contains. The four marks The question is already in him. Am I one yet. A girl, a fight, a check, or a truck will answer if you do not. You answer. First. Aloud.

## How to give a name

A habit does this. A speech cannot carry it.

Daily, catch one act a man would recognize and say the name. That is what a man does. Five seconds, and they may be the richest five seconds you spend on him.

Weekly, a blessing on a night you protect. Hand on his head. Three sentences. Identity. You are my son, I love you, I am staying. Sight. This week I watched the truth cost you something. That is courage. God. He made you on purpose, work is waiting, I will help you find it. Moses told fathers to impress the words, lying down and getting up. Impress means pressure across time, a mark pressed into leather until it stays.

:::margin
Deuteronomy 6:6-7 (NIV)
"These commandments that I give you today are to be on your hearts. Impress them on your children. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up."
:::

Yearly, a letter on the birthday. One page. Who he is now. What you watched him become. What you see ahead. Into a box, for the day you tell him he is a man. Letters in a father's hand, a year of life on each page, and a man can enter any room there is.

Praise where people hear it. Correct where they do not. This is my son. Saturday he and I finished the fence, and he did not quit. Wrongdoing gets a private room. Public shame sticks as a name. Public praise lifts as one.

Failure gets the act pulled off the boy. A lie happened. Say so. You are my son. A lie came out of you. Here is the next move my son makes. He tells the truth to the person he lied to. You are a liar gives him a name to fight or to fulfill. You are my honest son, and this was a dishonest act, gives him a road back.

Under your name, tell him the larger one. Made on purpose. The Father who stays is his Father too. Yours over him is a good covering, and it is meant to point past itself. You will fail him. You will die. God's name remains. Aim him there. That is the best use of yours.

Ray started badly and did the whole practice anyway. Once he hunted for man-shaped acts, the boy was full of them. Then a little sister broke a thing and cried, and Ray heard the brother say, "You're not bad. You did a bad thing. Go tell Mom." A name, once it is in a boy, gets handed on.

## Naming in every season

A baby boy can start this week. He cannot use the words yet. He knows the voice. Phone in the other room. You are my son, said over him daily. New fathers feel useless with an infant. The name is the work.

Divorced, he may collect other names across town. A stepfather. A silence where you belong. You cannot run the other house. The weeknight call is yours. Never turn him into a courier or a spy. That name helps no one. He does not belong in the middle. He is yours, and the bed he sleeps in does not change it. Close every call the same. You are my son. I love you. I will call next week.

Older men still have grandsons, and grown sons, and both still need a name from you. A grandfather's that is what a man does can land where a father's voice has gone stale. If the third name never reached your grown son, say the pride now. The man he became is worth the sentence.

No son at home still leaves boys on the street with nobody to do this. See one do a man's thing. Say it. Let the other boys hear.

## The Name that holds

What you speak over him is a small copy of what God speaks over sons. Mine. What I see. Man. All three, over every man in Christ, and over you, with or without a father who ever managed it.

:::margin
Psalm 127:3 (NIV)
"Children are a heritage from the LORD, offspring a reward from him."
:::

The psalm calls him a reward. God's reward, sitting in your house, and the return gift is a name. He is asking who he is. Daily. You hold the answer. Give him one he can plant his feet on until he is old.

## Before you turn the page

The son who never received a name is still allowed to give one. Ray did not wait to feel qualified before the hand went on the head. The hand was the way he became the man. Sighs and eye rolls can be replaced tonight, three sentences. May the Father who called His Son beloved before any deed put the same words in you, and may the boy grow up standing on a floor nothing can kick out.
