# 13. A Man With No Brothers

Two names. That is what the clipboard required.

Dale met that requirement a little after two, under hospital lights, when a pressure in his chest had felt like everything for three hours and then turned out to be nothing. A nurse set the intake form in his hand and went looking for a gown. His wife's name took the first line. The second line wanted a contact who was not a spouse. The pen hovered. No name came.

Hundreds of names lived in the phone. Customers. Suppliers. Men he hunted beside. Men from church. A brother-in-law two states off. His pastor. Couch-moving was available from any of them. "I'm scared," at that hour, was available from none.

He told me years afterward, the color drained out of the telling. "I didn't know I didn't have anybody until I needed somebody."

## Most men have no brothers

The man who needs this is usually certain someone else does. Hear it straight. Brothers, in the sense that matters, are missing from most men's lives. Buddies are what they have, and a buddy is a different post. He is the man beside you while something gets done. A hunt, a river, a round of golf, a game on a screen. Marriage often means one wife is carrying the entire weight of being the person who knows him, a weight she was never built to carry alone.

Put two questions to a man. Who is allowed to phone him at two in the morning, and who is he allowed to phone. Who may tell him he is wrong, face to face. Who knows the actual fear. Then watch the quiet spread through good men whose phones are full.

Solitude was never the design. Page two of the Bible records the first thing God called not good, and it was a man by himself. A wife was the remedy given first. Company of men runs through the rest of the story at her side. Jonathan belonged to David. Twelve belonged to Jesus, and three of the twelve sat closer in.

:::margin
Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 (NIV)
"Two are better than one, because they have a good return for their labor: If either of them falls down, one can help the other up. But pity anyone who falls and has no one to help them up."
:::

Pity the one who falls with nobody to get him up. Dale was that man, a pen in his hand, on a gurney.

## Why we don't have them

I have asked a lot of men about the blank. Fine, they say. That is pride. Nobody is fine. No time, they say. The time is there. Nobody has asked them for a piece small enough. They will not be a burden. Under that, a boy usually learned that need was dangerous, and grew up willing to go under before he would reach. Men do not do this, they say. Men did it for most of history. Quiet dying followed the quitting, and we named the quiet independence. They were burned, some of them truly. One ruined supper is a thin case against eating. They have a wife. Keep her. Her calling is real, and it was never to be the only one who knows you. That weight bends a woman over years. Ask her if it has.

## What a brother is

He knows your reach when a day spoils. Anger, a screen, or silence. He asks. The truth is what you give him. Closed rooms are absent from his questions. Money. The marriage. The bedroom. What crossed your eyes this week. Whether prayer happened. Two in the morning, a hospital, and he comes, because brother is a word with a body in his life. Truth is what he returns. Love is in it. Once is enough. The softened edition stays unsaid.

:::margin
Proverbs 27:17 (NIV)
"As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another."
:::

Iron on iron throws sparks. A sentence from him will sting. Sharpness is what you leave with.

He also helps you locate God on the days your own locating fails. David is in flight, a king is hunting him, weariness has him hidden in the woods, and a friend comes looking.

:::margin
1 Samuel 23:16 (NIV)
"And Saul's son Jonathan went to David at Horesh and helped him find strength in God."
:::

Jonathan helped him find strength in God. He brought no spare strength of his own, and he could not stop the hunt. His father was the hunter. He went into the woods anyway. That is the job. That is what you are for.

You need one man, two at most. He might tell you to see a counselor and remain your brother. The man you enjoy most can still be the wrong man to trust with the truth.

## Twenty minutes

Men usually have the want. What they lack is a picture small enough for the week they actually live. The pictures on offer are too large. A six o'clock breakfast every week. A group with homework. A weekend gone. Newborn at home, or a business failing, and he sees those, knows he cannot, and does nothing.

Take a smaller picture. This is the one that finally worked for me. Twenty minutes. One man. Once each week. The same time on the clock. Sameness is required, because a time that floats is a time that dies.

Three questions, then a prayer. That is the spending of it.

What happened this week? Leave the briefing. Name two or three things that actually counted.

What is heavy? The question that earns the time. Name the load, and name the fear under the week. Truth from both of you. The listener's work is listening. Repair is a different work, and tonight it is not his.

What is next? Each man names one move before the next meeting and says it so a human ear caught it. Sit down again and the opening question is whether the move got made.

Prayer takes the last two minutes. Aloud. Each for the other.

Some weeks one of you is a wreck, and his answer about the weight consumes the whole twenty. Weeks of that kind are why a floor exists. Twenty minutes, weekly, same hour. The floor does not shift.

Twenty minutes survives tax season, a twelve-hour shift, chemotherapy, and grief. I have never met a man who could not find twenty minutes. I have met many who could not find two hours and so found none. The questions steer. Without them, sports and trucks take over inside eleven minutes. Twenty is the seed.

## How to find him

Look for a man living the four marks, mostly. The four marks Church already holds him, or the crew does, and a real question has never been put to him. Your age is acceptable. Older is often better. Boss, employee, and brother-in-law are the wrong chairs. The whole truth has to be tellable with no job hanging on the hour and no Thanksgiving hanging on it.

Ask in ordinary language. "I'm trying to be a better man, and I've realized I don't have a brother. Would you be willing to talk with me for twenty minutes a week for the next three months and see how it goes?" Three months makes the yes easy. It also makes an ending easy if the thing fails. Most of the time it holds, and neither man remembers that stopping was permitted.

It starts awkward and gets easier. He has been waiting too. Men are lonely, and they will not go first. You will.

## Brothers in every season

Shape changes with the season. Need does not.

Roommates can mean a single man already has a brother and only needs the twenty minutes added. They can also mean a house of men and still no one who knows him. A father with a newborn owns twenty minutes and does not own thirty, which is why the number is twenty. Dishwater can be running while you make the call. Friends often leave when a marriage does. They were mostly hers, or they had no idea what to do with him. Of everyone reading this, he needs one man the most. The large group can wait. Get one voice on the phone. Retirement, or the death of a wife, removes the built-in company that came with the job or with her. One man is still required.

## The garden

Three men went with Jesus into a garden the night before He died. Sleep would take them. He knew. Denial would come from one of them before sunrise. He knew that too. He took them, and He asked for a thing.

:::margin
Mark 14:34 (NIV)
"'My soul is overwhelmed with sorrow to the point of death,' he said to them. 'Stay here and keep watch.'"
:::

The Son of God, who lacked nothing, asked three men to stay through His worst hour. He refused to take that hour alone. You do not get a pass He turned down.

A brother belongs to Dale now. Most of a year passed before he asked. Church was where the man sat. Two decades of acquaintance had produced zero real conversations. Thursdays, on the drive, they talk. Three questions, a prayer. Month one convinced Dale he had agreed to the dumbest arrangement of his life. Last spring a scheduled surgery put another clipboard in front of him. The form asked for the second name. His pen kept moving.

Find the man. Give him twenty minutes at the same hour, every week. Open with the sentence. You are the one who goes first.

## Before you turn the page

The sentence may be the most awkward one you know how to say. Nearly every man who has said it received the same reply. "I've been wanting somebody to ask me that." May the Father supply the nerve for a twenty-minute ask. May the man who hears it be a brother born for a time of trouble.
