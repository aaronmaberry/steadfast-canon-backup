# 25. The Bed Is a Covenant

Mitch sat down and said the sentence I hear when a marriage bed has gone quiet. "I'm holding up my end. She's not holding up hers."

I asked what he had actually promised. He could recite it. For better, for worse, in sickness and in health, as long as we both shall live. I asked him to point at the exits. He looked. He could not find them. One ending exists, and a man reaches it standing beside a grave.

Contracts are full of endings. Two parties, a term, each one checking whether the other kept up. Most of us learned to run a bed that way. Nobody had to teach us the vocabulary.

## A sign, not a term sheet

A contract is a plan for getting out clean if the other person fails. A covenant is a plan for staying. You swap goods under a contract. You hand yourselves over under a covenant, which is why there is always a sign you can see. A ring. A cup. A body.

The old English service had the groom say it as the ring went on her hand. With this ring I thee wed, with my body I thee worship. Worship, there, meant honor. Worth. His body was going to spend the years telling his wife what she was worth. That is what the bed is. The sign, renewed whenever the two of them are able.

## Recreation, dirt, or covenant

One loud story calls sex play. Consent and eagerness are the whole rule, and the other person is equipment. Another story, told in churches for a long time, called it something slightly dirty that decent men finished fast and felt strange about. Scripture told its story before the fall. One flesh. Naked. No shame. And He called it good.

:::margin
Genesis 2:24-25 (NIV)
"That is why a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh. Adam and his wife were both naked, and they felt no shame."
:::

Keep the bed pure. Kept for its purpose, between the two it belongs to, and honored. The One who made it is not ashamed of it. He guards it.

:::margin
Hebrews 13:4 (NIV)
"Marriage should be honored by all, and the marriage bed kept pure, for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral."
:::

## What the covenant means in the bed

The well is yours alone. Proverbs says drink from your own cistern, running water from your own well. Jesus carried the line into the chest. A lustful look is adultery already underway in the heart. A husband can keep his body at home and still leak through his eyes. His wife feels the level drop. She may never locate the crack.

Generosity sits in the same place Paul wrote the line people quote only halfway. Her body belongs to him. Then, in the same way, his body belongs to her. That second sentence turned an old world over. You are for her delight. Your own finish is the smaller aim. Ask how she is made. Ask what would bless her. The man who asks receives more than the man who takes.

:::margin
1 Corinthians 7:4 (NIV)
"The wife does not have authority over her own body but yields it to her husband. In the same way, the husband does not have authority over his own body but yields it to his wife."
:::

Stay. Her clock runs on a different time than yours. She can tell when she is with you and when she is being used to get you somewhere. Hurry takes what the vow said it would give. A slow tenderness is that vow with skin on.

Refuse to spend the bed on winning. Absence as punishment. A demand treated like a debt. A private score. The hour the bed becomes a lever, the sign has left and you are bargaining. A quiet bed will not heal because you made her pay. Ask what broke. The break is usually older than the bed, and wider than it.

Speak. Most couples never do. Pick an hour that is neither the bed nor the sting right after a disappointment. Ask, gently, what she wants more of and what she wants less of.

Guard it in the ordinary touch. Neglect will quiet a bed long before age does. If your hand shows up only when you want something, one day she will flinch. You were the teacher.

Mitch was keeping a count, and Dana could feel the number in his hands. I asked him to set the ledger down for a season. No tally. No beginning that expected a return. Touch that wanted nothing. One calm talk in the truck, on the drive home from her mother's. She said, "I want to stop feeling like a bill that's overdue." Months later he told me she comes to find him. He had the debt facing the wrong direction. The bed was where he owed her a true word about her worth.

## The screen, in the covenant frame

The tools are already in your hands. One brother who has the whole truth. A filter whose password he keeps. The phone charging in the kitchen. The reason you fight is the change. A living woman holds your covenant, and every image tears it a little. She is asleep beside the tearing. Those pictures train a hunger for whatever is new. They retune what your body answers. They whisper that she is the shortage. The shortage is that you are somewhere else.

The four marks do not clock out when the house is dark. The four marks Own the eyes. Trust the Father who sees the hidden thing and writes the faithful night in the book.

## In her seasons, and in yours

After a baby, her body has been through a war. Set your claims down. She is spent, and she is done with hands. You stop counting. You touch her in the way that asks for nothing. When she is ready, you go slow, you ask, and you stop if she needs the stop. Trust you keep at her weakest becomes trust when she is strong.

Illness leaves the covenant standing and changes the shape of it. Tenderness that never reaches the bed is still the vow.

In the middle years her body will do something it has not done before. Refuse to read that as rejection. Ask. Go to the appointment. Men who keep the well drink from it when they are old. Slower. Still sweet. When the act itself can no longer happen, you are still keeping faith. A man bathing a wife who has forgotten his name is keeping that bed pure. The bed is still. He is still one flesh with her.

## If there's no wife yet, and if there was

Single, and you mean to marry, your body is already casting a vote about a covenant you have not made. You are building it, or you are spending it ahead of time. Paul told the Thessalonians to learn control of their own bodies. Learn is the word, so it takes practice. Years of dating with no movement toward a wedding is where good men go down. Alone with her in a bedroom at midnight is a test nobody passes. If you can carry the weight, and she is the woman, marry her.

Divorced, you are single again. Memory makes that harder. The culture will tell you a man who has already been married has earned his freedom. He has earned no such thing. Former husbands get no footnote. No sex outside a new covenant. No moving in on the excuse that you are basically married. You are unmarried. Same brother. Same filter. Same phone in the kitchen. The stakes rose, because children are watching what a man does with a body when wanting hurts.

A widower hears the same standard, and I am sorry for the weight of it. You kept faith for a lifetime, and the empty bed still caught you off guard. God is not asking you to call the loneliness small. He is asking for a pure bed through the empty years. The Father who counted every faithful night is counting these.

## What the bed preaches

Paul sets the one-flesh line down and then calls the whole thing a mystery, and the mystery is Christ and the church.

:::margin
Ephesians 5:31 (NIV)
"'For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and the two will become one flesh.'"
:::

The bed is a sermon. Exclusive. Generous. Present. Patient. No weapon. No slow silence. In a small true way a wife can see how Christ loves His people. Faithful. Handed over. Glad. For good. Break the covenant with your body or your eyes and the sermon lies about God.

His name for it is a vow a body keeps, for as long as both of them live. Mitch went looking for endings. There are none. There is all of you, already promised, and a bed where you say it again.

## Before you turn the page

Good men have failed in this room and told nobody. If that is you, bring it into the light. David did, and his standing returned in a breath. Tomorrow is soon enough to keep the vow. May the Father who honors the marriage bed give you a well of your own, keep you from every other, and let that bed tell the truth about Him.
