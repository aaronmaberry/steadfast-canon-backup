# 19. Two Men, Two Ways

After the kids are down he stands in the dirt strip beside the garage. Hose. Tomato cage. Boots. From the street it looks like nothing that could settle a life. Eden looked like nothing from the road too. So did the hillside where He put His face in the dirt.

Two gardens. Two men. Two nights with everything on the line.

The first man had the whole place and lost it in an afternoon by saying nothing. The second Man had a choice left and almost nothing else. He made the choice face-down in the dirt, and the world was saved in that choice.

Silence. Blame. Hiding. Grabbing. First garden. The reverse of those, on a plain morning, when it costs you. Second Man. Scripture hangs the human story on those two.

:::margin
Romans 5:19 (NIV)
"For just as through the disobedience of the one man the many were made sinners, so also through the obedience of the one man the many will be made righteous."
:::

Which man are you in, and whose way will you walk this week?

## The first garden

The serpent enters. He skips the man. He starts with the woman, questions about the word, a twist, a hint that God is holding out. And the man?

:::margin
Genesis 3:6 (NIV)
"When the woman saw that the fruit of the tree was good for food and pleasing to the eye, and also desirable for gaining wisdom, she took some and ate it. She also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it."
:::

Who was with her. Three words, and they have been on men ever since. Adam had heard the command alone, from God's mouth, before she existed. Work it and keep it was already his. The thing he was posted to stop came through the fence, and he stayed on his feet and watched, and then he ate. The first sin was the quiet while the word got bent. Passivity. The first mark flipped.

God comes walking. They hide.

:::margin
Genesis 3:10 (NIV)
"He answered, 'I heard you in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked; so I hid.'"
:::

Afraid, so he hid. The third mark flipped. Courage would have stepped out. He took the voice he knew and went into the trees. Asked what he had done, he answers with the woman you put here. Wife blamed. God blamed. Himself omitted. The second mark flipped. The weight refused.

Under those three sits the fourth. You will be like God, the serpent said, a prize available this afternoon. He swapped the greater reward for the one in his hand. Fruit instead of the Father.

One afternoon. Four marks, all backward. Passive, evasive, hidden, grabbing. That groove is the one a man finds without trying.

Then thorns, death, exile. Inside the curse, two mercies. God clothes them Himself, from an animal that died. The first covering cost a life. Keep that. And He speaks to the serpent a line that is really a promise.

:::margin
Genesis 3:15 (NIV)
"And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel."
:::

Someone is coming. A son of the woman. He will stand in a garden when the enemy shows, and he will not stay quiet. It will cost him. He will crush what slipped the fence. The second Man is promised while the first garden is still smoking.

## The second garden

Night. A hillside He knew. His men are with Him. Three are told to watch. He goes farther, alone, and drops onto His face.

:::margin
Matthew 26:39 (NIV)
"Going a little farther, he fell with his face to the ground and prayed, 'My Father, if it is possible, may this cup be taken from me. Yet not as I will, but as you will.'"
:::

Fruit that would make a man like God was the first offer. A cup that will make this Man look like a criminal is the second, and He does not want it. Sweat like blood. Then the line. Not as I will, but as you will. Second mark. A cup He did not fill, a sin He did not commit, and He takes it as His.

Three prayers. The watchers sleep. He is alone, and the trees do not get Him.

:::margin
Matthew 26:46 (NIV)
"Rise! Let us go! Here comes my betrayer!"
:::

He hears them coming and walks at them.

:::margin
John 18:4 (NIV)
"Jesus, knowing all that was going to happen to him, went out and asked them, 'Who is it you want?'"
:::

He went out. Nobody had to search the trees. Third mark. First into the worst of it. Afraid enough to sweat blood, and still out the gate. While the arrest closes, He covers the men.

:::margin
John 18:8 (NIV)
"'I told you that I am he,' Jesus answered. 'If you are looking for me, then let these men go.'"
:::

Let these men go. Caught, He aims the finger at Himself. Responsibility, finished. His body between the danger and the people given to him. Me. Not them.

Hebrews says the joy set before Him is why He endured it. At the end He files the work.

:::margin
John 19:30 (NIV)
"When he had received the drink, Jesus said, 'It is finished.' With that, he bowed his head and gave up his spirit."
:::

It is finished. A completion report, spoken before He died. Fourth mark. He lived for the Father's well done, and three days later He had it.

:::margin
Philippians 2:9-11 (NIV)
"Therefore God exalted him to the highest place and gave him the name that is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue acknowledge that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father."
:::

Therefore. The reward arrived for a Man the crowd had already filed under loser.

## Two ways, laid side by side

Twisted word, and the first man said nothing. The second Man asked, who is it you want?

She did it, said the first. Take me, let them go, said the second.

Trees, for the first, when God spoke. Dirt, then his feet, for the second.

A reward grabbed in the hand. A reward trusted past the cross.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. If those four have sounded like traits, hear them as a body in a garden. Rejecting passivity is Rise, let us go. Accepting responsibility is not as I will, but as you will. Leading courageously is went out and asked them. Expecting the greater reward is for the joy set before him. You are looking at Jesus in the worst hour He had. Live one of the marks and you are wearing His face.

## Which man you're in

Scripture does not stop at imitate the second man. It locates you inside one of them.

:::margin
1 Corinthians 15:22 (NIV)
"For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive."
:::

Birth put you in the first man. Passivity feels easy. Blame is ready. Hiding feels like sense. You were in him when he did it, the way a household is in a father.

Trying harder is not the offer. The offer is a move, out of the first man and into the second, so His garden counts for yours. His obedience becomes the record. It is finished becomes the last word on you. That is the gospel.

:::margin
Galatians 2:20 (NIV)
"I have been crucified with Christ and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the body, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me."
:::

Christ lives in me. Location, changed. The Spirit that got up off that ground lives in a man who is in Christ. That is how he gets up off his own.

If you have never said it, say it. Tell God the first man's garden is the only one you have known, and you are done with it. Tell Him you want the second Man, His garden covering yours. The way in is open.

If you have said it, hear the rest of the page. The four marks are not a ladder into His acceptance. They are the family face, showing, because you are already His.

## This week's garden

A kitchen. She says something that is not quite fair, and quiet or a shot back both feel like relief. That room is the garden. A low, clear sentence is the second Man. That landed hard. Can we talk after the kids are down.

A phone, late. Charger in the kitchen. Bed. Two in the morning, the baby, her body already rising. Not as I will. A mess at work with your fingerprints on it. Own your piece. Let these men go.

Adam's afternoon was one piece of fruit.

## The two men in your house

The second Man had no wife, and He was the fullest man who ever stood up. If you are single, stand in the plot you are actually on.

A baby makes the garden a 2 a.m. story. He prayed for the cup to pass, then got up. So can you.

Divorced, stare at let these men go. Pointing is the first man's reflex. Her. The lawyers. The God who handed her to you. The second Man stands between the wreck and the people he was given. Me, not them. Children do not get to carry your war. Cover them. Say your part. Leave hers with God.

Older, think about final sentences. An excuse, from the first man. It is finished, from the second. Cal began to pray, let me finish. Whatever remains, let me finish it. A man farther down the road still has a garden.

## The last Adam

:::margin
1 Corinthians 15:45 (NIV)
"So it is written: 'The first man Adam became a living being'; the last Adam, a life-giving spirit."
:::

Last Adam. Not next in a line. The one who did the assignment the first man dropped. He finished it. He is gathering men into Himself, then walking them through small gardens with His Spirit in them and His record over them. Born in one. Moved into the other. Stand in your garden like the Man you are in. If you have never asked Him to move you, ask tonight.
