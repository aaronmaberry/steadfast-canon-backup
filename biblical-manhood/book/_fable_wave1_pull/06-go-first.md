# 6. Go First

The wind came through after midnight and by morning there was a tree across the street. A whole oak, roots up, crown in a neighbor's yard. Power's out. And in every window up and down the block there's a face, looking at the tree, looking at the other windows, doing the math about who's going to move.

Nobody is. Not because nobody cares. Every man on that street is thinking the same three things: somebody should get out there, somebody who knows what he's doing should get out there, and it probably shouldn't be me. So the faces stay in the windows and the tree stays in the street.

Then one door opens. A man in a coat over whatever he slept in, work gloves, a saw. He doesn't call a meeting. He walks to the tree and starts looking at where to cut. For a stretch of seconds that feels longer than it is, he's the only man in the street.

Then the second door opens. Within a quarter hour there are six men, two saws, a truck backed up to the curb, and somebody's wife bringing out coffee she made on a camp stove. Nobody on that street was waiting for permission. They were waiting for a door.

The third mark of a man is that he leads courageously. Most men hear leadership and picture the head of the table, the title, the biggest voice in the room. That isn't it. Leadership is the first door open. It's the man who moves while everyone's still looking, and takes the stretch alone, so the second man has somewhere to go.

## Watch me

Jesus led the twelve for three years, and the clearest lesson He gave them about it came with a towel around His waist. Nobody in that upper room was going to wash feet. It was the lowest job in the house, and every one of them was hoping the silence would pass. He got up, poured the water, and knelt.

:::margin
John 13:14-15 (NIV)
"Now that I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also should wash one another's feet. I have set you an example that you should do as I have done for you."
:::

An example. Not a memo. He didn't explain how to serve. He went first at the thing nobody wanted to do, and then He said, now you. Gideon led the same way, with three hundred men in the dark above an enemy camp, and his whole strategy came down to a sentence.

:::margin
Judges 7:17 (NIV)
"'Watch me,' he told them. 'Follow my lead. When I get to the edge of the camp, do exactly as I do.'"
:::

Watch me. That's leadership in two words, and it tells you why so few men want it. The man at the head of the table is safe; the table's between him and everyone else. The man who goes first is exposed. If the water's cold, he finds out. If the saw's the wrong tool, everyone sees. If nobody follows, he's alone in the street holding a plan. First doesn't get to be sure. First gets to be first.

## Where first happens

There may never be a tree across your street. Here's where going first actually happens.

The apology. Go first, and go clean. I'm sorry I raised my voice. Stop. Not I'm sorry I raised my voice, and you know how you get. The and-you is a hook. An apology with a hook in it isn't an apology; it's an opening statement, and she can hear the difference before you finish the sentence.

The prayer at your own table. There's a silence at a lot of tables when the food's set down. Everyone knows what's supposed to happen and everyone's looking at the same man. Plenty of men have prayed in front of a room full of strangers and never once in front of the four people who share their last name.

The rubble. Nehemiah walked the broken wall at night, alone, before he said a word. Then he stood up in front of the people and said what all of them could see and none of them were saying.

:::margin
Nehemiah 2:17 (NIV)
"Then I said to them, 'You see the trouble we are in: Jerusalem lies in ruins, and its gates have been burned with fire. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, and we will no longer be in disgrace.'"
:::

We haven't been to church since spring. We're behind on the truck. Our son has been drinking and we've been pretending we don't smell it. Rubble that gets named is rubble you can rebuild from. Rubble nobody names becomes scenery.

The turn. Every marriage has cold spells where both people are waiting for the other one to turn. Turn first. Not because you're more wrong, but because you're the man. The distance across a kitchen is the longest walk a lot of husbands never take.

The direction. Nehemiah didn't say go rebuild the wall. He said come, and then he walked to the wall. Leadership says come and moves. Management says go and watches from the porch. The man who decides his family will be in church Sunday is the man in the car with the engine running while everyone else is still finding shoes.

The door has a different handle in every season. The single man goes first when he's the one in the group who says let's actually go Sunday, and drives. The new father goes first when he prays out loud over a crib, feels ridiculous, and does it again the next night. The divorced man goes first with the cleanest apology of his life to his children's mother, whatever she does with it. The father of a silent teenager goes first with one question in the truck, and the older man goes first across the church lobby toward a younger one, because the younger one is never going to cross it.

## What we're afraid of

Four things keep a man's hand off the door.

He's afraid of looking foolish: the prayer comes out wrong, the apology sounds rehearsed, he gets to the tree and doesn't know where to cut. He's afraid of being turned down: he turns first in the kitchen and she doesn't turn back; he says son, I've noticed, and gets a shrug and a closed door. He's afraid of being wrong, so he sets no direction at all and calls it humility. And he's afraid of the cost, because there is one. The first man in the street loses his morning. The first man to apologize gives up the argument he was winning. First pays.

Scripture is honest about this. It never once tells a man to stop being afraid before he moves.

:::margin
2 Timothy 1:7 (NIV)
"For the Spirit God gave us does not make us timid, but gives us power, love and self-discipline."
:::

Not timid. It doesn't say not afraid. Timid is afraid and standing still. Courageous is afraid and moving. The man walking toward the tree with his heart going hard isn't a lesser version of the brave man. He's the definition of one.

## You're never actually first

Here's the part that changes the street. When Moses was handing a nation to Joshua, who had to lead it across a river into a country full of walled cities, he didn't tell him to be brave. He told him who was already out there.

:::margin
Deuteronomy 31:8 (NIV)
"The LORD himself goes before you and will be with you; he will never leave you nor forsake you. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged."
:::

Goes before you. Every door you open, His is already open. Every hard sentence you start at your own table, He's in the room ahead of it. You're never actually first. You're the second door open, and the second door is a very different thing to be. It still costs. It's still exposed. But there's Someone in the street ahead of you, and He isn't leaving.

## How to go first

You need the first sentence, not the whole speech. Men stall trying to write the entire conversation before they open their mouths. I've been wrong about something. Let me pray. Son, I've noticed. After the first sentence, the room does most of the rest.

Small rooms before big ones. The man who wants to lead at church but goes quiet at his own table has it backwards. Lead in your truck, your kitchen, the group text with three friends. The big rooms are made of small ones.

Lead with a question. What's it been like being married to me this year? What are you worried about that you haven't said? A question is a door you open and then hold.

Expect the eight seconds. Between your door opening and the second door, there's a stretch when you're alone in the street. Most men quit at four. That stretch isn't failure. It's what first feels like from the inside.

And do it on an ordinary day. Trees across the street are rare. Dinner is tonight.

## The pioneer

:::margin
Hebrews 12:2 (NIV)
"fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith. For the joy set before him he endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God."
:::

Pioneer. The one who goes first through the worst of it and cuts the trail so the rest can walk. He was alone in the street a lot longer than eight seconds. His friends slept, then scattered. And the writer tells you how He did it: for the joy set before Him. He went first with His eyes on a reward.

That's the fourth mark holding the third. A man can't lead on nerve for long; nerve runs out around the second door. What doesn't run out is a reward you're sure of. You go first because you're certain the One who went ahead of you will make the going worth it.

## Before you turn the page

You don't have to be the bravest man on your street. You have to be the one who opens the door while your heart's still going, and even that isn't quite right, because He's already out there, and He's been waiting on you longer than the tree has. May He give you the first sentence, the eight seconds, and the sound of the second door.
