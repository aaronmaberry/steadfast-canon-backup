# 7. Live for a Reward That Lasts

There's a folding table at a yard sale with a cardboard box on it, and somebody has written FREE on the side in marker. Trophies. A little gold man swinging a bat with one arm chipped off. A bowling pin on a marble base. A plaque for top salesman from a company that doesn't exist anymore. Somebody's whole life of Saturdays is in that box, somebody's proudest nights, the ones he drove home from with the radio up. Nobody's taking them. By noon the box goes back in the garage, and next spring it goes to the curb.

I don't tell you that to make you sad. I tell you because every one of those was once a reward a man lived for, and it worked. For a night, it worked. That's the thing about rewards you can hold. They all work, for a while, and then you need another one.

The fourth mark of a man is that he expects the greater reward, God's reward. It's the one that makes the other three hold, because rejecting passivity, picking up weight, and going first all cost something now, and a man will only keep paying if he's sure the payout is real. Take this mark away and the other three turn into a brave face on a bad deal. Why get up off the step if no one's keeping score? Why say that's mine when blame costs nothing later? Why open the door first if this street is all there is? The other three marks are questions until this one answers them.

## What you're already living for

You don't have to decide whether to live for a reward. You already do. Every man does, all day, and the only question is which one. Here are the usual ones.

Applause, even if it's only a nod from a room. Money, or more exactly the number, the one you check on the drive in. Being wanted, which is the reward the single man is quietest about and the married man forgets he's still chasing. Being right, which is the reward a young husband will burn a whole evening to collect. The father's nod, whether he's alive to give it or not; more men are still working for that one than will say so. Comfort, which is what the new father is living for around week six and the retired man most afternoons. And being remembered, the box of trophies, the name on something, the hope that what you built outlasts you by a little.

None of these are evil. Most are good in their place. Here's what they have in common: every one of them has a bottom, and every one of them is not God. Applause fades before you reach the parking lot. The number goes up and the feeling doesn't come with it. The father's nod, if it finally comes, turns out to be smaller than you'd made it. Comfort is the one that never says no and never says enough. You can live your whole life collecting rewards that work for a night and end up with a box on a folding table.

## Treasure that keeps

Jesus never told men to stop wanting a reward. He told them to want a better one, and He was specific about where to keep it.

:::margin
Matthew 6:19-21 (NIV)
"Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moths and vermin destroy, and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where moths and vermin do not destroy, and where thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."
:::

Moths, vermin, thieves. He's describing the yard sale. A few lines earlier in the same sermon, He tells you how the other kind gets stored.

:::margin
Matthew 6:3-4 (NIV)
"But when you give to the needy, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, so that your giving may be in secret. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you."
:::

Every other reward on the list needs an audience. This one requires you to lose the audience on purpose. The widow's driveway shoveled before her kitchen light comes on. The check with no name on it. The good word about a man who'll never hear that you said it. When nobody sees, one Person still does, and He's the one who keeps the account.

And it isn't only the religious hours.

:::margin
Colossians 3:23-24 (NIV)
"Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving."
:::

Whatever you do. The invoice, the diaper, the spreadsheet. The reward isn't reserved for men on a stage. It's for the man on the ladder and the man on the night shift and the man doing dishes with nobody watching, if he's doing it for the Lord. That's the phrase that turns a job into treasure. Not the job. Who it's for.

## The reward is Him

Is that mercenary, working for God the way you'd work for a bonus? Look at what the reward is.

:::margin
Matthew 25:21 (NIV)
"His master replied, 'Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you in charge of many things. Come and share your master's happiness!'"
:::

Well done. More to do. Come and share my happiness. That isn't a payout. That's a welcome. The reward for serving Him is more of Him, closer, with more entrusted and His joy thrown open to you. A man who wants that isn't a mercenary. He's a son who wants his Father to be glad he came home.

Moses understood the trade. He grew up in the palace, with the biggest treasure room in the world down the hall, and he walked out of it to stand with slaves.

:::margin
Hebrews 11:26 (NIV)
"He regarded disgrace for the sake of Christ as of greater value than the treasures of Egypt, because he was looking ahead to his reward."
:::

Looking ahead. That's the whole posture. Not gritting his teeth. Looking past the treasure room to something worth more, and choosing accordingly. A man can walk away from almost anything if he's looking at something better. He can't walk away from much of anything if all he can see is what he's leaving.

## What it does to a man

You can tell a man who's living for God's reward without asking him. He does good that nobody sees and doesn't mention it later. He loses arguments he could win, because being right stopped paying enough to be worth the damage. He stays, in the marriage and the church and the town, when leaving would be easier and he could justify it. He takes the low place at the table and the low job on the crew without checking who noticed. He forgives, not because the other man deserves it but because the account he cares about isn't the one between him and that man. And he plants what he won't taste. He prays for grandchildren who don't exist yet. He teaches a boy to work who'll teach his own boy after he's gone.

None of that makes sense if the payout is here. All of it makes sense if it's there.

## What it doesn't promise

Be clear about this, because the counterfeit is loud. God's reward isn't a health plan. It isn't a bigger house or a smoother year or a guarantee that your kids turn out. It doesn't promise the man who stays a warmer marriage by spring, or the man who forgives an apology back. Men who preached that have left wreckage. Paul, who'd been beaten and shipwrecked, put it this way.

:::margin
2 Corinthians 4:17-18 (NIV)
"For our light and momentary troubles are achieving for us an eternal glory that far outweighs them all. So we fix our eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen, since what is seen is temporary, but what is unseen is eternal."
:::

Light and momentary. From a man with scars. He could say it because he'd done the math with the right numbers, and the troubles weren't being subtracted. They were achieving something. The reward doesn't cancel the hard years. It's what the hard years are building.

## How to live for it

Fix your eyes daily. Paul's word is fix, not glance. Before your feet hit the floor, one sentence: what I do today is seen, and it counts.

Do one good thing a week that no one will ever know about. Not your wife, not your brother, not the men you meet with. If it leaks, it wasn't the one.

Rehearse well done. Say the words to yourself, in His voice, on the way home from a day nobody thanked you for. That isn't vanity. That's remembering who you work for.

Notice when you're performing. Jesus said men who do good for the crowd have received their reward in full. The moment you're checking who saw, you've been paid, and it was a small check.

And pray with heaven in view. Most men pray for Thursday. Pray for Thursday, and then lift your eyes past it.

## One man

Put the four marks on one man and stand back. He doesn't step around the thing that needs doing. He says, that's mine, and stops at the period. He opens the first door while the street is still watching. And he does all of it with his eyes on a reward the yard sale can't touch. There's an old word for that kind of man. Steadfast.

**A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.**

Live for a reward that lasts. Then get back to work.

## Before you turn the page

If you've been living for a small reward, you took the nearest one, because it paid tonight. Trade up. The Father who sees in secret doesn't lose the account. May He fix your eyes where the treasure is, and may you hear one day the words that make every unseen thing worth it.

## YOUR TURN

**Name it.** Because you can't trade in a reward you haven't admitted you're chasing: write down which small one has been steering you, applause, the number, being wanted, being right, the nod, comfort, being remembered, and where it's been steering you.

**Do this week.** Because the Father who sees in secret only rewards what stayed secret: do one good thing that no one will ever know about. Not your wife. Not your brother. Not the men you meet with. Then don't mention it, ever.

**Tell a brother.** Because a man who's named his small reward to another man has already loosened its grip: tell him which one it is and what it's cost you. Don't tell him the secret thing. That one's between you and the Father.

**Carry this.** Every man lives for a reward. The only question is which one.
