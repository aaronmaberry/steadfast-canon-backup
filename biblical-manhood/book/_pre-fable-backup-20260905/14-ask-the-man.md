# 14. Ask the man

Nobody picks you off a wall.

You have seen the wall. The lobby after the last song, coffee in a paper cup, standing close enough to be seen and far enough to leave. Same third row for two years. A job site where the older man walks past with a list and you nod like the nod is a relationship. You are not hiding. You are standing where the wise men walk. You are useful. You are not a problem. You assume a good older man will notice and tap you.

He will not.

He is already full. He has a job, a house, three men he is already walking with, and a limited number of mornings. He is not scanning the wall for talent. He is walking. If you want to walk with him, you ask.

Do not wait to be chosen. Ask a man. That is this page. Not the aloneness. Not the problem inside. The ask.

I have waited. I told myself I did not want to be a burden. I told myself if it was supposed to happen it would happen. That sounded humble. It was me, hoping a man would do the wanting for both of us.

I have stood ten feet from him and talked about the weather. I have said "we should grab coffee sometime" and counted that as courage. It was a hint. He smiled. Nothing got a day on a calendar. He was not cruel. He was busy. I stayed undrafted and called it timing.

Some of you already know his name. You have been calling the delay timing too.

The older man you are staring at already has a life. That is why you want him. It is also why he will not come find you. He is not failing you by being full. You are stalling by treating his fullness like a closed door you must not knock. Full men still answer a clean ask. They do not answer a hint. Hints are how we stay proud and unchosen.

A hint protects you. If he never answers, you can tell yourself it was never supposed to happen. An ask can be refused. That is why we hate it. Refusal is a real risk. So is another year on the wall.

Walk with the wise and become wise, for a companion of fools suffers harm. That's Proverbs 13:20.

:::margin
**Proverbs 13:20 NIV**
Walk with the wise and become wise, for a companion of fools suffers harm.
:::

Walk is a verb that needs a person. You do not walk with the wise by admiring them from a pew. You do not become wise by collecting their clips. You do not walk by sitting two tables over at the same coffee shop and hoping he looks up. You walk. Walking starts with a sentence in your mouth that feels too small and too large at the same time. "Will you walk with me?" Awkward is allowed. Awkward is not a reason to stay on the wall.

After he says yes, you still have to talk. Some men get the breakfast on the calendar and then spend six mornings on scores and weather and call it a walk. It is not a walk. A walk is a man who will let another man interrupt him. You tell the truth. You let him ask the next question. If you will not open your mouth after the ask, you asked for a mascot.

A companion of fools suffers harm. That is the other half, and it is not a joke. If you will not ask a wise man, you will still walk with someone. The feed. The guys who never ask you anything true. The version of yourself that always agrees with you. Harm does not always look like a crash. Sometimes it looks like a decade of being unformed and well liked. The fools in that proverb are not always loud. Sometimes they are just the men who will never ask anything of you, and you chose them because they felt safe. Safe is not wise. Wise will cost you a sentence you do not want to say.

The man you ask is a man. Not a program. Not a church department. Not a wife. She can cheer the ask. She cannot be the wise man. If you make her that, you will resent her when she is only a wife, and you will still be undrafted.

He does not have to be famous in the building. Famous is how you pick the man everyone already wants, and then you wait in line. Pick the man who is wise. He is also not a man who reports to you. A report will manage you. He will not risk the sentence you need. Ask a man who does not need your favor. That is why it feels like a fall. It is supposed to.

I have asked the wrong man because he was convenient. The funny one. The one who would not make me feel twelve. I have also not asked the right one because I wanted the conversation to already be easy. Easy is how you stay unchosen. The right sentence is short. "I want to walk with you. Can we start with breakfast once a month." If he says no, you honor it and you ask someone else. A no is not a verdict on your soul. It is a full man telling the truth. Thank him.

Do not ask him to fix your whole life. Do not hand him a list of your father. Do not make the first sit-down a deposition. Ask him to walk. If he is wise, he will know what to do with a man who showed up. If you unload the truck on day one, you are not asking. You are hiring a counselor he never agreed to be.

Some of you will try to make this a curriculum. Twelve sessions. A binder. A name for the group. Do not. Mentoring is initiation, not a brand. You ask a man to walk. You show up. You tell the truth. You let him interrupt you. That is the whole machine.

If you're nineteen, or twenty-two, the ask will feel like you are twelve. Do it anyway. Awkward is allowed. A young man who asks a wise man is already walking. A young man who waits to be discovered is practicing a long stay on the wall. You do not need a speech about your potential. You need one sentence and a calendar. He does not need your whole story on the sidewalk. He needs a time.

If you're single, do not wait for a wedding to have a man in your life who is not trying to date the same people you are. Ask now. The wise man is not a stand-in wife. He is the man you walk with so you do not become a fool with a nicer apartment.

If you're married, ask a man. Tell her you did. Do not ask her to be the one who forms you. Do not make the breakfast a secret she finds out when you are late. Do not ask a man on your team so the office can absorb it. The ask belongs outside the house and outside the org chart, or it will collapse into both.

If you got hurt on the way here, asking can feel like groveling. It is not. Groveling is how a boy begs to be wanted. Asking is how a man takes a step toward wisdom. You can limp and still ask. You can be slow and still ask. Kind is not the same as waiting until you feel safe enough to never be refused. Refusal is part of a real life. A man who once used you is not this man. Do not make the last wound the reason you never knock.

If you're older, you can still ask. A 62-year-old who thinks he is done being formed will become a companion of his own habits. Ask a man. Also be askable. The younger men are watching whether you have room, and whether a no from you can be kind. Full is honest. Invisible is how they stay on the wall. If you have room, say so when they come. If you do not, point them to a man who might. Do not give them a speech about how busy wisdom is and send them back to the lobby.

If the job is going well, you will want to ask the man everyone already wants. You might also want to skip the ask and hire a coach so it stays professional. A coach can be useful. He is not this page. This page is a wise man, asked, who does not need your invoice and does not report to you. Name him. Then ask him. One sentence. This week.

I know the fear that he will think you are needy. He might. Let him. Need is not the enemy. Fools are. A man who will not risk looking needy will walk with whoever is easiest, and easiest is how harm stays quiet.

Name the man. Then ask him. Not a program. A sentence. If you cannot name him, you are still waiting to be drafted. Look at the men you already respect and pick one. The one whose life you keep studying from the third row. Start there. A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that.

Tell one other man that you asked, or that you are asking on Thursday. If you only confess the wait, you will get a nod and stay on the wall. If you tell him the name and the sentence, he can ask you whether you sent it. Let him ask.

Do not wait to be chosen. Ask a man. The wall will still be there tomorrow. It is comfortable. It is also how a capable man stays unwise.

Name it. Who is the man you will ask?

Do this week. Ask him. One sentence. Not a program.
