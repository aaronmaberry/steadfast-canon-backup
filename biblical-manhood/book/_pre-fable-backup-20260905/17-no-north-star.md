# 17. No north star

He can tell you 4:30.

Ask him what the week holds and he does not hesitate. The meeting. The shift. The class. Pickup. A gym if that's his. The truck has gas. The texts get answered, most of them. He is not the man on the couch. He is the man in motion.

Ask him what he is for, and watch the fog come in. Not because he is slow. Because nobody handed him a star that sits still. He has been steering by nearby lights. Office windows. A field on Friday. The next invoice. The next problem a capable man can solve. Those lights are real. They move. A north star does not.

A capable man whose week is full and whose direction is fog. That is this page. A man with no north star. Missing vision. Last page was how it looked. This page is the missing heading.

I have been that man. I could pack a week until it looked like a life. If you asked me what I was becoming, I would have handed you a list and felt responsible for having one. The list was not a heading. It was ink.

Where there is no revelation, people cast off restraint; but blessed is the one who heeds wisdom's instruction. That's Proverbs 29:18.

:::margin
**Proverbs 29:18 NIV**
Where there is no revelation, people cast off restraint; but blessed is the one who heeds wisdom's instruction.
:::

Revelation is a word from God, not a mood you wait on. Without that word over a man, he does not freeze. He unbuckles. I used to hear "cast off restraint" as a warning for wild men. It is a warning for full men too. On a capable man it often looks like energy with nothing that can judge it. He will still pay the bills. He will still be liked. He just has no star that can tell a packed week, this is not north.

Restraint is what a heading does to a life. A star that sits still can tell you which lights to pass. Without it, every nearby glow gets a yes. The gym. The cart. The feed. The next win. The next good thing that keeps you from deciding what you are for. You do not need a longer list. You need a word over the list.

You can name the problem inside and still have no star. You can even name how a week told on you and still drive by the nearest glow. Those pages stand. This one is the heading you still do not have.

This is not the wait. Hear that. A man can move all day and still be in the fog. Motion is not a heading. Passivity sits still. This wound fills the squares. That is why it hides on men other people trust.

We already have a line that sits still. It can judge a week. If it is only a sentence you can recite, you will keep steering by whatever is lit. A poster is not a north star. A north star is a word you heed.

Blessed is the one who heeds wisdom's instruction. Heed. Not brainstorm a personal brand of manhood and call it vision. Not wait for a feeling that will lift the fog. The fog does not have to clear for you to stop treating nearby lights like north. You heed a word that was already spoken.

I have treated a good week like a compass. It is not. A week tells you where the hours went. It does not tell you what you are for. I have also treated a hard week like a verdict. Hard is not the same as lost. Lost is when nothing sits still long enough to aim you.

Nearby lights are not evil. Work is good. A game is good. A paycheck is good. A body you can still use is good. God is not embarrassed by a man who can pack a week. He is after a heading, and a packed week is a great way not to have one.

A heading will cost you a yes you like. That is how you know it is a star and not a nearby light. Nearby lights never cost you the glow. They only ask for more of it.

Some of you were never told. Dad did not. Church taught you to be useful and nice. The feed handed you other men's weeks. So you borrowed. A job. A sport. A version of your father, or the opposite of him. It got you this far. Competence is not a star. It is a skill. A skill can take you a long way in the fog. You can cover miles and never choose north.

Farm-to-market in the morning, fog on the hood. You can still do forty-five. You can still hit every appointment. Capable hands. No horizon. That is a lot of us. We are not in a ditch. We are moving. We just cannot see past the paint.

The job can be a nearby light without being your name. You can know you are not your work and still not know where the life is pointed.

At 11 the nearby lights are still on. The star is the same as it was at 6. If your heading changes with the last text, it was not a star. It was weather.

If you're young, a full week can impress your friends and still have no sentence for what a man is. Nineteen. Twenty-two. Classes, a job, the gym, a game, a group that never asks. Do not wait for a career or a lease to have a heading. Delayed manhood loves a packed calendar. It hates a star, because a star would tell him he is already in the life. A 22-year-old can write a heading this week. Waiting for a more official life is how a young man stays busy and still lost.

If you're single, a wife is not the north star. Do not park a heading in a woman who is not in the room. Marriage is good. It is not the finish line and it is not the compass. Name the light you steer by now. Write a sentence that would still be true if you stay single five more years. If the only future you have is "once I meet her," you are already in the fog.

If you're married, a full house calendar is not a heading. She can feel the fog even when the bills are paid and the kids got where they needed to go. Providing is a load you pick up. It is not the star. If the only sentence you have is the family's schedule, you have nearby lights with car seats. Tell her the heading. Then live it when the week is loud. She does not need a speech about the man you might become. She needs to see you heed one.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. The wound can become the whole map. Hurt is real. It is not north. Steering by what was done to you is still a nearby light. Kind is not the same as letting the old injury aim every year. You can be wounded and still heed a word that sits still. The star does not mock the wound. It keeps the wound from driving.

If you're older, years of competence can look like vision. They are not. A 62-year-old can pack a week with useful things and still be in the fog. You still have a next move. Name the light. Write the heading. Retirement is not a star either. The younger men need a man who knows north, not only how to fill a day.

If the job is going well, the calendar is camouflage. The office will pay you to never need a star. You can hit every target and still have no heading. A title is a nearby light with better parking. Name it. Then write a sentence that would still be true if the title came off on a Saturday.

Name the nearby light you have been steering by. One. Then write one heading that sits still. Not a task. Not a season. A sentence you could still use when the week is full and the road is fog. The four marks can be that sentence if you mean them as a star, not as a poster. A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. You do not have to finish a life map by Friday. You have to stop calling a packed week a direction.

Tell one man the light, and the heading. If he only hears that you have been busy, he will nod and you will stay in the fog. If he hears the sentence, he can ask you next week which one you drove by. Let him ask.

A full week is not a north star. Nearby lights move. Heed the word that does not.

Name it. What nearby light have you been steering by?

Do this week. Write one heading that sits still. Not a task. Not a season.
