# 34. Write the next move

He can talk the year in the truck.

Twenty minutes. Engine on. He can name the job, the house, the son, the thing he will stop, the man he will be by Christmas. It sounds like a life. Then he comes inside. The page at the kitchen table is still blank. January he bought the notebook. September it is still page one.

I have been that man. I have given a good year to a windshield. I have not sat down. A speech in the truck can be revised while you drive. Ink cannot. That is why the page stays empty.

I have pulled into the drive still talking. Kids already inside. Wife already inside. I sat in the cab one more minute to finish the year out loud, then I locked the truck and left the year in there with the heat.

This page is the ink. Write the next move. Not a binder. Not a three-year grid. A course on a page, then the step this week. God establishes the steps. You still have to put the course down.

I have treated a blank page like humility. It was not humility. It was me, keeping the year in a place I could still lie about. Passivity loves a spoken plan. Spoken plans never fail in the air. They fail on Monday, when nothing was written and nobody can ask.

I have also written too much. A stack of aims so large I could hide in it. That is the same dodge as the blank page, with better stationery. If you cannot say the next move in one sentence, you do not have a course. You have a pile. Cut the pile. Put one course down. Then a step with a day on it. The rest can wait until this one has actually happened.

In their hearts humans plan their course, but the Lord establishes their steps. That's Proverbs 16:9.

:::margin
**Proverbs 16:9 NIV**
In their hearts humans plan their course, but the Lord establishes their steps.
:::

Plan the course. That is not playing God. That is being a man with a heart. The second half is not a reason to skip the first. The Lord establishes the steps. He does not fill in a page you refused to touch. If you will not put a course down, you will still walk. You will just walk a year you never chose, and call the drift guidance.

I have waited to write until I was sure. Sure is a trap. You do not need a finished map of sixty. You need one true course that can sit still on a page tonight, and one step this week that costs you something. If it does not cost, it is still the truck.

I have called a feeling of resolve the same as a plan. Resolve is cheap in a parked cab. It costs when you have to look at a sentence you wrote, with a day on it, after the feeling left.

A blank notebook is still a dodge. Sitting down is still the first move. A prettier speech is still not a life. Write it so a man can see it. If only you can see it, you will edit it back into the windshield.

A course is not a personality transplant. It is a direction you will still have to walk when you are tired. I have written sentences that made me look decided, then lived the old week anyway. The page is not magic. The page is a witness. If you will not let it witness, do not pretend the truck was the work.

I have filled notebooks with wishes. That is not this. A wish has no date and no cost. A next move has both. "Be a better man" is a wish. "Tell him on Thursday" is a move. "Get healthy" is a wish. "Walk before the office, three days, and text one man when I skip" is a move. The Lord can establish a step. He will not do that for a wish you never dated.

If you are twenty-two, the year you are in is already the plan. Delayed manhood wants a later house, a later title, a later calm, then a first page. You can put ink down tonight without a child in the house. Write one course and one step. You do not wait for a better decade to start counting.

If you are single, a later house is not the first page. Marriage is good. It is not the start of having a course. Write the move that belongs to the life you actually have. The job. The body. The younger man. The money. Do not leave page one blank as a gift for a woman who is not in the room.

If you are married, the family calendar is not the move. Soccer, travel, a shared month on the fridge, those can be love. They can also be a way to never sit down. She already knows if you have a course or only a schedule. Honor what she has carried. Then write. This is not her homework. It is yours, in ink, where she could read it if you let her.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. A plan is not pretending the wound did not happen. Some of you will not write because a plan feels like a dare to a year that already broke you. I am not going to joke that. Kind is not the same as a blank page. Write a small true course that includes the wound, not one that erases it. One step this week. Not a rebuilt childhood.

If you are older, a finished story is a cheap plan. A 62-year-old can talk the years like they already closed. They have not. Gray hair is not a memoir. It is still a course. Write the next move, not the eulogy. A grandson, a younger man, a repair you have been meaning to make. Late ink is still ink.

If the job is going well, the Q3 deck is not this page. You already know how to put a course on slides. Then you come home and the notebook is still page one. Use the same spine at the table. The office will not write this for you. It will take every hour you will give it and call you planned.

Sit down tonight. Not in the truck. At a table. One course that sits still. Then the next true step this week, with a day on it. Show the page to one man. If he cannot see it, you still have a speech. You are not writing a whole life by Friday. You are putting the year where it can be asked about.

A heading that sits still is not a speech. It is a sentence you would still own if a brother read it out loud. Write that. Then the step.

Tell one man what you wrote. If he only hears that you have thoughts, he will nod and you will drive. If he sees the page, he can ask you next week whether the step happened. Let him ask.

The truck will still take a speech tomorrow. The page will still take ink. Write the next move.

Name it. What have you been giving to the windshield? What is still blank on the table?

Do this week. Sit down. One course. One step with a day on it. Ink.
