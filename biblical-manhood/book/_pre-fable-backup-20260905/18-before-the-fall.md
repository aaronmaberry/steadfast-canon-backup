# 18. Before the fall

Some of us skip to the fruit.

We know the hiding. We know who got blamed. We can walk a man through what went wrong in him, including this one, and never stop for the morning before it. Ask what was given. Watch him go quiet. We start the story at the wreck.

I have done that. I can give you a tour of my stains like a man proud of a bad inspection. I get slower when you ask what was handed to me first.

Closing day. July. The seller's furniture is gone and yours is still in the truck. You walk every room because that is the only hour they will ever be this empty. The AC has not caught up. Light on the floor. Your feet are too loud. A leftover paint can in the garage. A closet that still smells like someone else's detergent. You can tell what each room was for. Nothing is leaking yet.

Then you live there. A mark shows up on the ceiling. Another on the baseboard. You start talking like the marks were the house.

That is this page. Before the fall. Design. Goodness. What was given. Last page was a full week with no heading. This one is older than your week. The first morning.

The LORD God took the man and put him in the Garden of Eden to work it and take care of it. That's Genesis 2:15.

**Genesis 2:15 NIV**

Took. Put. He did not find a man wandering and decide to use him. He placed him. A garden already made. Then a work, and a charge to keep it. This is before the fruit. Before the hiding. Before anybody had a wound to explain himself with. God is not handing a man a punishment and calling it a life. He is giving him a good place and telling him to stay with it.

I used to hear "work it" and "take care of it" as the grind. The thing you do after the world goes wrong. That is not when they show up. They show up while the world is still called good. The thorns come later. They are not the origin of the job. If you only know work as a curse, you will treat every place you are put as a sentence. It was a gift first.

A garden is not a mood. It has edges. It can be neglected. It can be kept. God did not drop a man in the dark and tell him to invent the point. He put him in a place that was already made, and He told him what the place was for.

You were not invented by what was taken from you. Hear that. The empty chair was real. The problem inside is real. Those pages stand. They are not the first page. The first page is a God who makes a world, looks at it, calls it good, and puts a man in it with something to do that is not yet stained.

I have treated my life like a used place I inherited from a bad tenant. I could point at every hole. I have stood in a kitchen I paid for and talked only about the leak. I stopped asking what the rooms were built for. That can sound like honesty. It is often a man who will only tell the wreck.

Design is not a repair kit. Design is what was true before the leak. Manhood is not a workaround we invented after everything broke. It is what a man was given while it was still morning. A place. A work. A charge to take care of it.

Manhood is not a patch after the leak. It is how a man lives a given life. He does not sit the garden out. He works what was put in his hands. He stays with a place that can be lost if he drifts. He does it for the God who put him there, not for a scoreboard he can hold up on Friday.

A place can be a lease. A shop. A desk nobody else wants. A body you did not grow. A name. A morning you did not earn. You do not have to own land to have been put somewhere. Put is the word. You are not floating. You are placed.

Work it. That is not a career sermon. It is a man who treats what he was given as worth his hands. The dishes in a sink you actually live with. The shift you already said yes to. The nephew who is in reach. The patch of grass that is yours for now. Take care of it. Keep it. The garden could be lost. That is why it needed a man and not only a view.

Some of you think goodness is naive. You have seen too much. I am not asking you to pretend the wreck did not happen. I am asking you not to baptize the wreck as the design. God is not embarrassed by a man. He made him. He put him in something good and told him to keep it. If that feels like someone else's story, that is how far the stains have talked.

In the story, the man is given a place and a work before a wife is even in the room. The gift does not wait on a wedding. The charge does not wait on a title. You can be given a life and still live as if the real thing has not started. It has. You are already in a place. The question is whether you will work it and take care of it, or only narrate what went wrong.

I have waited on a bigger garden so I would not have to keep the one I had. I told myself the real keeping would start when the place looked like it mattered. The verse does not wait with you. The small place is not a rehearsal. It is the thing.

If you're young, you already have a place. A room. A job that does not look like much yet. A body. Do not wait for a better garden to treat this one as given. Nineteen. Twenty-two. You imagine the life you will keep once it is official. Official is a stall. A 22-year-old can work a small place and take care of it this week. Delayed manhood wants a bigger stage so he never has to keep the one under his feet.

If you're single, a wife is not the start of design. Marriage is good. It is not the moment you become placed. Your apartment is already a garden you can neglect. So is your word. So is the actual work in front of you. Do not park "what was given" in a woman who is not here. Name what you already have. Keep it.

If you're married, she is not the garden. She is not the origin of your manhood. You were given a charge before the story had her in it. Do not make her the proof that you have a life, and do not make her the one who keeps the place while you visit. Tell her what you were given. Then keep it when the house is loud. She can feel when you only narrate the stains.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. I am not erasing the chair. I am not calling the wound a story you made up. What was taken is real. What was given is older. You can be wounded and still name a place God put you. The design does not mock the wound. It keeps the wound from claiming it was there first.

If you're older, years of wreck-talk can sound like wisdom. They are not always. A 62-year-old can give a long tour of the damage and almost nothing about the gift. You still have a place. It may be quieter. It may be a younger man who needs to see that the rooms were built for something. Retirement is not the end of being put. Name what is still given. Keep it.

If the job is going well, you can treat the garden like a scoreboard. The office will pay you to. You will work it and forget it was given. You will take care of results and not the place. A title can make a man talk as if he planted the thing himself. You did not. You were put. Gift first. Then hands. A high performer can name what was given without the badge in the sentence, then keep it on a day the badge does not need him.

Name one thing that was given. Not taken. Not earned as a trophy. A place, a body, a morning, a job you did not invent, a person already in reach. Then work it and take care of it once this week, on purpose, as gift. Not as a way to catch up. Once.

A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. You are not rebuilding Eden before Monday. Stop talking as if the stain was the floor.

Tell one man the given thing, and that you kept it. If he only hears a talk about Eden, he will nod and you will skip to the fruit again. If he hears the actual place, he can ask you next week whether you worked it. Let him ask.

The rooms were built for something. God put a man in a good place and told him to keep it. That was first.

Name it. What was given to you that you have been treating like a stain, or like nothing?

Do this week. Name one given thing. Work it and take care of it once, as gift.
