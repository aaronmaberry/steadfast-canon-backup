# 7. Live for a reward that lasts

I have seen it on a man they just celebrated.

They put a plaque in his hands, or an envelope with a number in it, and he smiled like it had worked. He sat down with the thing still in his lap. Ten minutes later he was already hungry for the next one. It had not made him feel finished. He told people it was a good night. He meant it in the hallway. Later the thing sat on a dresser and he was still the same man.

I have seen it on a younger man refreshing a screen for a count that would not love him back. I have done it. Not always a stage. A little more respect in a room that would forget my name by Monday. A toy I could post. I have photographed a win before I sat down, as if the photo were the point, then never looked at the photo again. I have chased a thing, got it, and felt the air go out of the room. The prize was real. It was just too small to live in.

You can work yourself hollow for a prize that cannot hold you.

A real man expects the greater reward, God's reward. That is this page. Not the wait. Not the load. Not going first. The prize. The one that lasts. The cheap ones do not.

This is not a scold against work. I like a man who will stay on a job until the job is done. A paycheck is good. So is a body you can still use, and a name people trust. God is not embarrassed by a man who builds something. He is after the thing you are using those goods to worship. If the goods are the god, they will not hold you. They were not built to.

Jesus ran toward a cost most of us would run from. For the joy set before him he endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God. That's Hebrews 12:2.

He did not endure because pain is holy. He endured because a joy was out in front of him that was bigger than the shame. He wanted the Father, and he wanted us, and that reward did not evaporate when the week turned over.

That is the mark. Not a man who hates a good thing. A man who will not sell his life to a prize that cannot keep him.

I have sold pieces of a week to prizes that could not keep me. I knew they couldn't. I ran at them anyway, because they were close and God felt far. The sin was treating them like they could hold a soul.

A prize that cannot hold you still asks for your best hours. That is the trick. It looks like a grown life: extra nights, a title, a body you are managing, the weekend thing you bought to feel finished. You can be exhausted and still be empty, which is a strange combination until you name the prize. Then it makes sense. You gave a whole man to a thing that could only pay in the currency of this week.

You can tell which prize is running you without a speech. Watch the first hour. Watch what you will not miss. The cheap one is loud about the calendar. It pays this week. God's reward will often make you wait through a year that does not clap, and a practical man can call that delay a reason to switch gods. It is not practical. It is a short memory.

Some of you are not lazy. That is not the problem. You are ambitious in the wrong direction. You would run through a wall for a scoreboard. You go quiet when the scoreboard is God's. That quiet is not humility. It is unbelief with a calendar.

The greater reward is not a bribe. It is not God paying you to ignore your life. It is God telling the truth about what will still be standing when the close ones have gone quiet. Some fruit takes years. A boy you tell the truth to at four may not thank you until he is thirty. A hard no at work may cost you a quarter and save you a name. A marriage you stay in on a gray night may not feel like winning. If the only prize you have is the feeling in the hour, you will quit the things that matter. If God's reward is real, you can stay. That staying is already a better life for the people near you. The rest of the reward is later, and it does not evaporate.

Do not hear prosperity in this. God's reward is not a truck, or a better parking spot, or your enemies watching you win. It is the Father, pleased, and a life that was actually His. Jesus sat down at the right hand. That is not a raise. That is arrival.

If you're young, the cheap prize is often speed. Followers, a title you can say at a table, a body other people notice. None of that is evil. All of it is thin. A nineteen-year-old can already spend a whole self on a count that will not love him back. A 22-year-old who lives for a prize that lasts will look slower than his friends for a while. He will also still have a self when the count drops. Do not trade a life for a weekend that photographs well.

If you're single, you can live for a reward that lasts without a wedding in the story. The cheap prize here is often a future that is always about to start. Or a present that is only appetite. God's reward is already on the table: a life hidden with Christ, a brother, a neighbor, work done clean. A wife is a gift. She is not the greater reward. Do not make her the prize that holds you, and do not wait for her before you start wanting the one that lasts.

If you're married, the cheap prize can hide in the house. Being right. Being left alone. Being the man she used to brag about at dinner. You can win those and still lose her. God's reward will send you into the small costly thing that does not photograph: the apology, the budget, the hour you did not feel like giving. She is not the prize either. She is a person. The prize is God, and a marriage lived under Him will look different than a marriage lived for applause.

If you got hurt on the way here, hear me kind. I am not offering heaven as a gag for a wound. Some of you were promised a prize by a person who did not keep it. A man, a woman, a church, a coach. That lie does not make God's reward fake. It makes you tired. You can be wounded and still need a prize that is actually God, not another person who might leave. The greater reward is not a bribe to swallow pain. It is a reason you do not have to sell yourself to the next small god that smiles at you.

If you're older, the cheap prize can be a finished story. I already did my years. Let the younger men want things. You still have a next move that only makes sense if God is the reward. A 62-year-old who lives for the Father's yes is not retired from manhood. He is free of the need to keep proving he won. The younger men need to see that freedom on a face, not just hear it in a talk.

If the job is going well, this one will find your calendar. The office prize is loud. It pays in public. It has a board and a bonus and a room that claps on cue. God's reward often does not. You can close a year and still have spent the year on a prize that cannot hold you. Competence is not the greater reward. Neither is the house it bought. Honor the work. Do not let it baptize you. The people who love you already know which prize is running you. They can hear it in what you will not miss, and what you will.

I know the pull to keep both. To say God is the prize and still organize the week around the one that claps. I have done that and called it balance. It was not balance. It was two masters, and one of them always got the first hour.

Name the cheap prize you have been living for. Say it plain. Then take one move this week that only makes sense if God's reward is real. Give the hour you were going to spend proving yourself. Tell the truth that will not get you liked. Stay in a hard good thing one more week. A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. The move will look small from the outside. That is fine. The greater reward is not trying to impress the outside.

Tell one man the prize, and the move. If he cannot ask you whether you went back to the small one, you told the wrong man.

The other marks will starve without this one. A man can do hard right things and still go empty if what he is running at cannot hold him. God's reward is how a man stays.

Expect the greater reward, God's reward. Live for the one that lasts. The small ones will still be for sale tomorrow. They always are. They cannot hold you.

Name it. What prize have you been working for that cannot hold you?

Do this week. One move that only makes sense if God's reward is real.
