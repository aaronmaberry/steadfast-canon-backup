# 3. The four marks

You are still here. That matters.

The last page asked you for a sentence your week already preaches. Some of you wrote it. Some of you wouldn't, and the week wrote it anyway. Either way, you already know the line that has been running the house. Now you need one that can hold.

"A man is the last one to leave the office." "A man doesn't need anybody." Those pay. They'll get you promoted. They'll get you left alone. They will not hold a life.

I have lived on lines like that. They feel like wisdom because they hurt a little. Hurt is not the same thing as true.

You needed a sentence. Hunger is not a definition. Here is a line that can hold. You can write it on a napkin. You can say it in a truck.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Four marks. One line. Not a sticker. A way a man actually lives.

A false sentence pays. That's why it's hard to drop. Last-one-out looks like loyalty. Doesn't-need-anybody looks like strength. Both will starve a house and still get you complimented in a parking lot. The four do not pay like that at first. They cost. Then they hold.

I'm not going to unpack each mark here. We'll take them one at a time. Not today. This page is the whole thing in your mouth. If you can't say the four, you will keep living the ugly one, because a man will live some sentence. The only question is which.

A man can collect talks. He can collect clips. He can stack almost-true things and still have nothing in his mouth when a younger man is waiting, or when a decision is already happening in the room. The four are not a stack. They are one sentence. If you have to look them up, you do not have them yet. That is not shame. That is just the truth about a line that hasn't made it past the page.

I have tried to look like I already had a definition. I used enough words that a man might think I did. He would have been wrong. Extra words are how a man hides a missing sentence.

Passivity. Responsibility. Courage. Reward. That's the shape. You hang a week on it now. Not as four sermons. As one line you can actually say.

It is not a title. You can be vice president of something and still be a boy in the four. Quiet men and loud men both get these four, or they don't. Personality is not a loophole. You don't receive them because you felt like a man that morning. You receive them because this is the line, and you put it in your mouth.

Paul told a church to have the same mindset as Christ Jesus. That's Philippians 2:5.

:::margin
**Philippians 2:5 NIV**
In your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus:
:::

He said it about how they treated one another, which is the right place to start. A definition that only works in a deer stand is not a definition. It has to work on a person. A roommate. A wife. A son. A coworker who is about to lie and is watching whether you will. The man you keep meaning to call. If it cannot survive those rooms, it is a hobby.

The mind of Christ on a man is not vague. It has a shape. He does not sit the day out. He takes what is his to take. He goes first when going first will cost him. He does not live for the cheap prize that is gone by Thursday. That is the four, said without the four. We use the four because you can remember them when the week gets loud.

You will not get a medal for memorizing them. You will get a way to tell, in the middle of a decision, whether you are about to live the ugly sentence again. That is the whole use of a definition. Not so you can win an argument. So you can catch yourself.

You will be in a conversation, or a truck, or halfway through a yes you should not give, and the old sentence will offer itself. Last one out. Don't need anybody. Whatever paid last time. If the four are only on this page, the old one will win, because the old one is already in your mouth. A definition you cannot say is a definition you do not have.

I have needed that catch more than once. I have also skipped it and called the skip freedom. It was not freedom. It was the ugly sentence still running things. I have nodded at this line like I was agreeing with a weather report. Then I walked back into the week with the old one still in charge.

I have said the four out loud and lost the third mark. Started over. Lost it again. That is not a failure of manhood. That is a man getting a sentence into a mouth that has been full of other things. Keep going. The ugly one did not get there in a day either.

You already heard the line once, up front. Hearing it is not having it. I can hear a street name and still miss the turn. This chapter is the turn. Say it out loud. Not as a performance. As the sentence that replaces the ugly one.

If you're young, this is the first decent line some of you have been handed. Don't add a fifth mark you saw in a clip. Four is enough, and they are already a lot. Learn these before you go hunting for extra. A 22-year-old with the four in his mouth is already ahead of a lot of men who have stories and no line. That is not a slam. It is a warning not to wait.

If you're single, this is already your life. You don't wait on a wife to start this. You don't wait on a ring to have a line. The four are not a married man's creed with a bachelor's exemption. An apartment can hold this sentence. So can a rented room. The line does not need a wedding to begin.

If you're married, she will not be the audience for a speech about the four. She will watch whether they show up. The ugly sentence she already knows. Give her a different one, then keep it. Don't make her clap for the recitation. Keep it in your mouth so the week has something to obey.

If you got hurt on the way here, the four are not a scold. They are not a stick. They are a floor. You can be wounded and still need a sentence that isn't the one that wounded you. I will not start with man up. Kind is not the same as empty. A floor is a mercy.

If you're older, you can still get this in your mouth. You can still give it. A 62-year-old who can say the four, and mean them, is worth more to a younger man than stories with no line. You do not age out of needing a sentence. You also do not age out of being able to hand one over.

If the job is going well, hear me. The title is not the four. You can close a quarter and still be a boy in these marks. A name on a door will not reject anything for you. The four will cost you the sentence that got you praised. Last-one-out. Doesn't-need-anybody. Those look like leadership from the parking lot. They are still the ugly ones. Trade them.

I know how a line like this can sit on a page and never leave it. The danger is nodding. You will agree. You will even like it. Then you will walk back into the week with the old sentence still running the house, because the old sentence is familiar and the four are not. Familiar is not the same as faithful.

Some of you will treat this like a password. Enough to get into a room of men. Not enough to live. Don't. The four are not a ticket. They are a way.

So write the four. Longhand. Not a screenshot. Your thumbs can copy anything. Your hand has to mean it. Write it on a receipt if that's what you've got. Write it again tomorrow. Keep writing them until you can say them out loud without looking down. Then say them to one man. Let him say them back. If both of you fumble, that's honest. Fumbling a true sentence is better than reciting a false one from memory.

A 22-year-old can do that this week. A 62-year-old can do that this week. Tell one man. A crowd will not help you here, and a post is a performance.

Don't make it a speech. Don't add your own poetry. The four are already the poetry, and they're not trying to be. They're trying to be true.

Some of you will want to improve them. Add a fifth. Soften a word. Make it rhyme. Don't. I have tried to make a better version that sounded more like me. It sounded like me. That was the problem. The line is old enough and plain enough to carry. Your job this week is not to improve the line. Your job is to get the four in your mouth so the ugly one has some competition.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

That is the sentence. The ugly one can go. Not because we shamed it off the page. Because you finally have something that can hold. You will still feel the tug of the old line. Feeling a tug is not the same as obeying it. Catch it. Say the four. Then do the next right small thing. The marks will get their own pages. This page is the line.

Name it. Which ugly sentence have you been living instead of the four?

Do this week. Write the four longhand until you can say them without looking down.
