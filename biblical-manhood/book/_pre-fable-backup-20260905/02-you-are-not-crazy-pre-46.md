# 2. You are not crazy

Somebody asked you what a man is.

A younger guy at work, trying to make it a joke so he doesn't look hungry for the answer. Or your son, and he wasn't joking. Or nobody said it out loud. It was just you, late, trying to finish a sentence you never got handed.

You can run a job. You can pay what's due. You still don't have a sentence.

It feels like a test. Nobody wrote the questions. The answers on the feed fight each other. One crowd treats manhood like a punchline. The other treats it like a weapon. You are not crazy for wanting a third thing. A sentence you could actually live.

The world went quiet on this. That's not in your head.

You've been living off leftover lines. Something you saw win a room. Something that kept you out of trouble. A clip. A coach. A boss you didn't want to become, so you became the opposite and called it a personality. None of it was a sentence. It got you through a week. It will not tell a younger man who he is.

Fathers used to hand a trade and a way across a day spent in the same weather. Then the work left the land and left the house, and a lot of boys got their hours from women who loved them and from men who came home already spent. I'm not telling the father story here. I'm naming the weather. You can feel a cold front without writing a report on it.

Churches got polite. They would talk about people, and families, and being kind, which are good words. They got shy about saying God made men on purpose. Not every church. Enough of them that a lot of us sat in rows for years and walked out with a bulletin and no sentence. If you asked, you got a look. Like you'd brought a chainsaw to a potluck.

Then the culture decided male and female were outfits. Try one. Trade if it pinches. The old words got treated like they were mean on purpose. If you still wanted a definition, you were the problem. Wanting a sentence started to sound like a defect.

That's a lot of quiet to grow up in. A man in that weather usually does one of two things. He makes a mess. Or he shrinks.

The mess is loud. Everybody can see it. The shrink is harder to catch, including in the mirror. He provides. He keeps his head down. He becomes the guy whose one job is the job. He looks responsible on paper. He has no sentence either. He just found a respectable way not to need one.

The mess and the shrink are cousins. One burns the house. One never quite moves in. Both are what you get when a people go quiet about what a man is for, then act shocked when the men come out hungry.

I've used the shrug that says nobody knows anymore, so why try. It sounds wise. It's just tired. A shrug is how a man stays lost and still looks calm doing it.

God was not guessing. That's Genesis 1:27.

:::margin
**Genesis 1:27 NIV**
So God created mankind in his own image,
in the image of God he created them;
male and female he created them.
:::

Not a costume you put on when the room requires it. Not a personality type. A making. He speaks a world, then puts image-bearers in it, male and female, on purpose. That is not a footnote. That is the start of the story. If that sounds too simple for the pile of advice you've been living under, good. Simple is what you were starved of. Complexity made a lot of us feel intelligent while we stayed lost.

You can argue with a verse. A lot of people have. You cannot honestly say the Book is silent. The quiet you grew up in was not God's idea. It was ours. We got embarrassed, or busy, or clever, and we stopped handing boys a sentence. Then we wondered why the men came out hungry and mean, or hungry and small.

This is not a science fight. I'm not here to win a comment section. I'm here to tell you the gap you've been carrying has a source outside your week. Some of it is you. We'll tell the truth about that. Some of it was the air. If you only ever hear the first half, you will despise yourself and call it humility. If you only ever hear the second half, you will blame the weather and never move. Both are a dodge.

You're not broken for wanting a sentence. Wanting one is a sane response to a culture that stopped giving them.

If you're young, the guessing is not proof you're behind. It's proof nobody handed you the thing. Stop collecting other men's leftover lines like they're yours. You need one you can say without looking at your phone.

If you're single, this talk is already about you. A wife is a good gift. She is not the start of manhood. Don't wait for a wedding to admit you're already in it.

If you're married, she can feel the missing sentence even when you can't hear it. Provider is a real weight. It is not the whole of you. If the only true thing you can say about being a man is that the bills clear, that's a start, and it's too small. She already knows.

If you got hurt on the way here, I'm not going to joke you through it, and I'm not going to make this chapter the place we sit in that house. You can be wounded and still not crazy. The fog was real. Your pain is real. Those can both be true without one eating the other.

If you're older, this did not pass you. A 62-year-old can still lack a sentence. He can also still give one. The years don't retire you from this. They make the next younger man in your life more expensive to shrug at.

If the job is going well, hear me. A title is not a definition. You can be competent and still empty of a sentence. The younger guys already know. They're waiting to see if you'll say something that costs you, or if you'll keep being vague.

So write the sentence you have actually been living. Not the one you wish. The one your week already preaches. It might be ugly. "A man is the last one to leave the office." Or, "A man doesn't need anybody." Put it on paper anyway. A false sentence you can see is better than a hole you keep falling into.

Then tell one man. Don't make it a speech. Read him what you wrote. Let him tell you his. If both of you stare at the page and hate it, that's a start. You can't repent of a sentence you won't admit.

A better sentence can wait one night. Tonight's job is to stop pretending the hunger is a personal defect. It isn't. The world went quiet. God did not.

Name it. What sentence has your week been preaching, even if you'd never say it out loud?

Do this week. Write that sentence down. The real one, not the cleaned-up one.
