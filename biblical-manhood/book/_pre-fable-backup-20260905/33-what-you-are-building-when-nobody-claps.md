# 33. What you are building when nobody claps

He mowed the strip that faces the street twice.

Saturday. The sidewalk looks finished. The backyard fence is still leaning, two posts in dirt, a gate that will not latch. Nobody driving by can see the gate. They can see the strip. He did the strip twice. He told himself he ran out of daylight. He had daylight. He did not have an audience for the fence.

A neighbor slowed and nodded at the lawn. He waved. Then he put the mower away and left the posts in the dirt. I have been that man. I have built the part that photographs. I have left the part that actually holds a life. Then I have wondered why the life leaned.

This page is the unseen hour. The clap is a liar. The gate is the truth.

I have stayed late for a visible win and skipped an hour that would not make a slide. I have said yes to the thing that would get mentioned and no to the thing that would only get done. Passivity loves an audience. An audience lets you feel like a man without doing the work that stays after they leave.

Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters. That's Colossians 3:23.

:::margin
**Colossians 3:23 NIV**
Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters.
:::

Paul is talking to men whose work would never trend. Household work. Hidden work. The temptation was to look busy when the master was in the room, and coast when he was not. The Lord is in the room. That does not make the task glamorous. It makes the unseen hour count. You are not working for the clap. You are working for Christ. If that sounds like less than a career, you have been shopping for a different lord.

The street strip is not evil. A job well done in public can be love. The tell is what you do with the gate. A man who only builds what can be seen is not a craftsman. He is a performer. Performers need a room. Fathers, brothers, workers under God need a week with no one in the stands.

I have confused being busy with building. Busy can be a strip. Twelve visible tasks, none of them the lean. A man can exhaust himself and still leave the gate. The question is not how tired you got. The question is whether the thing that actually holds got an hour. If it did not, you were not building. You were performing tired.

I can tell you what my front looks like from the road. I cannot always tell you if the latch still works. That is not a mowing problem. That is a man who built for the drive-by.

I have used "nobody noticed" as a reason to stop. Of course they did not notice. That was the point of the hour. A son who sleeps in a house that latches will not write you a review. A coworker you told the truth to, off the clock, will not put it in the newsletter. A prayer you actually prayed in the lot will not get you a title. Those are the hours that hold a house after the strip grows back.

A clap is a hit. It works. Then you need another one. I have rearranged a whole Saturday around being seen, and called it serving. It was me, hungry. The Lord does not starve you of being known. He already knows. The hunger for a room is how we skip the gate. Do the hour. Let the hunger sit there unanswered.

God's reward is not the mention. This page is the method. Build the part that will still be standing when the mention is gone. Do it with your whole heart, even if the only One keeping score is the One who already sees.

If you are twenty-two, you are already building. The feed is an audience. The group chat is an audience. You can do one true hour this week that will not be posted. Waiting for a later stage so the work can look finished is how the gate stays leaning. You do not wait for a clap to start a life. You latch a gate nobody is grading.

If you are single, the unseen hour is already the life. No wife is coming to make hidden faithfulness count, and no future house is the first place you get to be real. The apartment. The job nobody thanks you for. The younger man who will never know you stayed. Do that. Do not save your first unposted hour for a family you do not have yet.

If you are married, she already knows which strip you mow twice. She lives with the gate. Honor what she has carried in rooms that do not clap. Do the hour that serves her and the people in the house, not the hour that makes you look like a man from the street. This is not her page. It is whether you will build the part she actually walks through.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. Some of you built and nobody came. Some of you had a father who only performed. I am not going to joke that. Unseen work is not a consolation prize for men who failed to get famous. It is the real job. Kind is not the same as hiding so you never have to be seen. Do the true hour. Then let a brother know you did it, not so he claps, so you do not disappear.

If you are older, a highlight reel will not raise a grandson. A 62-year-old can still chase the strip. He can also spend the years he has left on work that will not be announced. A younger man in reach. A quiet repair. A word that costs you and will not trend. Gray hair is not a brand. It is a chance to build without the noise.

If the job is going well, the clap is already on a schedule. Reviews. Wins. A room that tells you you are the work. Then you come home and the gate still leans. Use the same whole heart on the hour that will not promote you. The office will not ask you to. Christ already did.

You already know how to finish something when a room is watching. Put that finish on the hour that will never make the minutes. That is the build. Do it once this week and see what it costs you not to be seen.

The hour will feel small while you are in it. A latch. A conversation. An apology nobody else will hear. It will not feel like a life. It is how a life is built.

Name the strip you keep mowing, and the gate you keep passing. Then one unseen true hour this week. Not a personality. An hour. You are not installing a whole legacy by Friday. You are building the part that holds when nobody is driving by.

Tell one man the gate, and the hour. If he only hears that you work hard, he will nod and you will mow the strip again. If he hears the actual lean, he can ask you whether you fixed it. Let him ask.

The street will still be there tomorrow. The gate will too. Build the part that latches.

Name it. What do you build when someone is watching? What leans when they are not?

Do this week. One unseen true hour. For the Lord, not for the clap.
