# 4. Stop waiting

He knows.

Not in a vague, someday way. He knows the conversation. He knows the name. He knows the good he ought to do, and he can feel the hour it would take. Then he waits. He gives the wait a decent name. Timing. Prudence. Not wanting to be rash. Not wanting to make it worse. He calls it wisdom. It is not wisdom. It is sitting the day out with a clean shirt on.

I have done this. I have sat in a truck after a meeting and told myself I would say the true thing next time. Next time was a coward's favorite day. I have kept a stack of next times. It never came, and I still felt thoughtful. Thoughtful is a great costume for a man who will not move.

I have opened a name on a phone and watched the screen go dark. I have written the first line of a true sentence and then decided the hour was wrong. I have seen a man I needed to stop, and taken the long way around him, and called that not making a scene. It was a scene. I just wasn't in it.

Some of us wait in a chair. Some of us wait on our feet, which is more respectable and just as still. You can pace a warehouse and never make the call. You can mow the same strip of grass three times because the conversation in the house would require you to go inside. You can fill a Saturday until there is no room left for the one thing you already know. Motion is not the same as moving.

A real man rejects passivity. That is the first mark, and this page is only that mark. We are not doing the whole line again. We are doing the wait.

Passivity is not always lazy. Lazy is honest. Passivity can look busy. It can look like a full calendar and an empty will. It can look like research. One more article. One more podcast. One more year of getting ready for a life you already have. The feed will help you wait forever and call it growth. Growth that never costs you a known good is not growth. It is a library you hide in.

Waiting is not peace.

Peace can sit still. Passivity sits still and calls the stillness holy. You can tell them apart by what they do to the people near you. Peace makes a room safer. Passivity makes a room guess.

A wife starts reading your face for a verdict you will not give. A son learns that later means never. A roommate stops asking if you're coming. A friend stops bringing the thing, because you never pick it up. You call that not being dramatic. They call it being alone in the same house, the same shop, the same group text you never answer. The guessing is the tax they pay for your delay.

James did not dress it up. If a man knows the good he ought to do and doesn't do it, it is sin for him. That's James 4:17.

:::margin
**James 4:17 NIV**
If anyone, then, knows the good they ought to do and doesn't do it, it is sin for them.
:::

Not a personality. Not a season. Sin. The wait has a moral name, which is why we hate to give it one. We would rather call it a process. Process sounds like we are doing something. Often we are only circling the thing we already know.

I have called it prayer when I meant stall. Prayer is real. I was not praying. I was buying time and hoping the known good would expire. I have called it letting the thing breathe when the air had already gone stale. I have called it kindness, as if the people near me were safer because I would not speak. They were not safer. They were guessing.

You already know the good in at least one place. You don't need a new list. You need to stop renaming the delay.

Some waits are real. A door that is actually shut. A diagnosis. A job that has not come because it does not exist yet. A wound still open enough that a slogan would be cruel. I am not mocking those. I am after the other kind. The wait you chose because moving would cost you a version of yourself you like. The wait that lets you keep your options and lose your name.

If you're young, you are being trained to treat your life like a draft. Everything is provisional. The job, the church, the city, the man you said you'd call. You tell yourself you will lock in when it feels like the one. Feelings do not lock anything. Starting does. The good you already know is probably smaller than the life you are imagining. Call him. Show up. Tell the truth you already have. Do the small known good. The imagined life can wait. The known good in front of you cannot.

If you're single, do not baptize delay as purity of intention. You can honor a future wife and still refuse to sit this year out. The neighbor, the work, the brother: none of that is on hold. Waiting for a wedding to start this is still waiting. A woman is not going to arrive and switch you on. The life is already yours.

If you're married, the wait often hides in the gap between what you know and what you will say. You know the question. You know the apology. You know the budget conversation you keep sliding to next month. You tell yourself you are keeping the peace. You are keeping the fog. She can feel the difference. She has been feeling it. She does not need a speech about the man you are becoming. She needs you to stop sitting the known thing out.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will not scold you into motion. Some men wait because moving used to get them wrecked. That is not nothing. It is also not a life. You can be wounded and still take one small known good this week. Kind is not the same as frozen. Frozen will start to feel like identity if you let it.

If you're older, you can wait in a quieter way. You have enough years to call it perspective. Sometimes it is. Sometimes it is a man who has earned the right to sit down and is using that right on the one thing he still knows he should do. A 62-year-old can still stop waiting. The younger men are watching whether you will. They do not need your stories about the years you already moved. They need to see you move in the one thing you have been renaming.

If the job is going well, hear me. Busyness is not the same as refusing passivity. You can close a quarter and still not make the call. You can run a team and still let the truth die in a meeting because speaking would have been inconvenient. The office will praise the hours. The hours are not the mark. The mark is whether you sat the real thing out. A packed week can be a hiding place with better lighting.

I know the pull to wait until you feel ready. Ready is a rumor. I have waited on it and watched a week leave without me. I have also moved clumsy and found that clumsy obedience beats elegant delay. Not because clumsy is a virtue. Because delay was the sin with a better vocabulary.

You do not have to finish the whole thing this week. You have to stop pretending you don't know where it starts.

Stop sitting the day out.

You already know if this is you. That little heat in the chest when someone names the delay. Don't talk yourself out of the heat. Don't turn it into a joke. That's the known good, knocking.

Name the wait. Not ten waits. The one you have been calling wisdom. Say it plain. "I have not apologized." "I have not asked him." "I have not told the truth in that room." Then take one small move this week. Not a personality overhaul. One move you cannot rename as research. A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. The size of the move is not the test. Whether you stopped waiting is.

Tell one man what the wait was, and what you did. If you only tell him the wait, you will get sympathy and stay seated. If you tell him the move, he can ask you next week whether you did it. That question is a gift. Take it. Let him be a little bit annoying about it.

You will not finish becoming a man by waiting to feel like one. You will not get peace from a delay you have dressed up as discernment. Reject passivity. Stop waiting.

Name it. What wait have you been calling wisdom?

Do this week. One known good. Do it, don't rename it.
