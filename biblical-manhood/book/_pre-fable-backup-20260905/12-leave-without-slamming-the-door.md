# 12. Leave without slamming the door

Most of us still know the sound of the door at the house we came from.

The storm door if you let it go. The one with the glass beside it. You have gone through it with a plate, a bag, a bad mood, a dish you were supposed to return in March. For a long time that door did not mean anything except you were home, or you were leaving for the night. Then you were supposed to be a man, and the same door started asking a different question.

How do you go. And do you still have to make a sound.

Some men never take their hand off it. One foot in the old kitchen. One foot in a life that is supposed to be theirs. They call it being a good son. They call it not wanting to hurt anybody. The door stays open. Their weather still sets the week.

Some men go so hard the glass in the sidelight cracks. They want a sound that matches the years. They draft the message. They send it. Then they sit in the truck with the radio off, waiting to see if the old house felt it.

I have done both. I have stayed because leaving felt like betrayal. I have also left with a speech in my mouth that was really a score. The speech felt like a spine. It was a boy with a louder door. A slammed door still has you in it. You can hear it from the street.

Leave. Honor them. Do not slam the door to prove you are a man.

This page is the leaving. Not the tie. Not a full marriage chapter. A wife can be in the picture. She is not the whole of it. Single men leave too. You do not wait on a wedding to belong to your own life.

That is why a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh. That's Genesis 2:24.

Leave is the first verb. United is a real second. If you skip leave, the union will have extra people in it who were never invited. If you slam the door on your way, you will carry broken glass into the next room. The verse does not give you a right to be cruel. It is a man going, with honor, into a life that is his.

The verse names father and mother. Some of us slammed one and stayed inside the other. That is not leaving. That is picking a side in a house that was supposed to become a visit.

Leaving is not a city. You can move two states over and still need a yes from the old house before you change jobs, churches, or the way you spend a Saturday. You can live three miles away and have actually left. Miles are not the test. Who runs your life is the test. If their weather still sets your week, you have not left. If you cannot say a kind sentence about them without a hook, you have not left either. You have only changed the volume.

Honor is not asking permission for a life God already called you into. Honor is telling the truth without a trial. Honor is showing up when it costs, and not showing up when showing up would put you back in a role that is not yours.

I have confused honor with access. I thought if they could still reach me at every hour, I was a good son. Access is not honor. I have also confused leaving with a coldness I could brag about. Coldness is not leaving. Coldness is a slammed door with better manners.

I have made the Sunday call into a report. How work is going. What I decided. Waiting to hear if I did it right. They did not ask for a report. I offered one because I had not left. A man who has left can still call. He just does not need the grade.

I have sent money that was really a vote. I have also held money back as a verdict. Both were me, still in the door. A check can be honor. A check can be a leash. You know which one yours is by whether you could send it without a speech attached, or withhold it without keeping score.

The man who will not go keeps explaining. He has a reason every month. Next year. After this season. After they are less fragile. After the holiday. The calendar will not do the leaving for you. You go, or you do not. A real man rejects passivity. Standing in a doorway and calling it love is still sitting the life out.

Some of you will not go because you are afraid they will not survive it. Hear me. You are not their husband. You are not their pastor. You are not the extra adult they never got. You can love them and still go. Staying as the extra adult in a house that already had its years is not kindness. It is a refusal to become a man, and it will not save them. Needed is not the same as called. I have used needed like a purpose. It felt holy. It was me, remaining useful in a room that was supposed to release me. Taking over their house is not the job. Taking yours is.

The man who breaks the glass wants a scene that matches his pain. He drafts the message. He tells three friends first. He wants them to feel the years. They might. They will also remember the shatter longer than the years. You do not owe them a fake peace. You also do not owe the room a demolition.

I have sat in a truck after a hard leave and felt taller for about twenty minutes. Then I checked the phone to see if they had read it. A slammed door is not freedom. It is a conversation you are still having, just louder.

Some of you will go like a storm because you are angry, and the anger is not nothing. You were held too long, or used, or compared, or never blessed. Leaving as revenge will not heal that. It will give you a story you can tell, and a relationship you cannot return to when you are ready to be a son who is also a man. Kind is not the same as staying. Kind is also not a wrecking ball.

A slammed door is a prize you can hold. Being proven right. Making them feel it. That is a small prize. A real man expects the greater reward, God's reward. God's reward is not a crater. It is a life that is yours, under Him, with honor still in your mouth. The glass is loud. It is not a spine. Courage here looks like a calm no, and a door you close with your hand on it.

If you're young, leaving might be the apartment, the church, the job you already know is yours. It might be one conversation where you do not ask them to vote. You can do that at 19, at 22, without a speech about how they failed you. Go. Call on Sunday. Do not make the call a report card they have to grade.

If you're single, this verse still belongs to you. Leave is not a married man's verb. United to a wife is a gift, later, if God gives it. Leave is now. A future marriage will not extract you. If you cannot leave with honor as a single man, you will not suddenly know how when someone says yes.

If you're married, you leave toward her. That does not make her the enemy of your parents, and it does not make them her project. You are the man who has to actually leave. If you have not, she is living with extra people. If you slammed the door, she is living with the glass. Honor them. Belong to her. Those can sit in the same week. Do not send her in to do the leaving for you. Do not make her the speech. A call from you, without a trial, is the move.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will not tell you to pretend the house was gentle. Some doors should not be stood in front of every holiday like a test of your manhood. Distance can be wisdom. Distance with a curse in your mouth is still a slam. You can put miles between you and still send a true, short, clean sentence. You can also refuse a room that would undo you, without a manifesto. Leaving is not revenge. Revenge is how the old house keeps you. Kind can include a no.

If you're older, you can still leave a pattern. A 62-year-old can still be waiting for a blessing that is not coming, or still punishing a slight from a kitchen that no longer exists. You can still make the call. You can still stop making the call that is really a leash. You do not age out of this. The younger men need to see a man go without making a scene, and stay gone without making a religion of it. Honor from a man with years lands different. So does a grudge with years on it. Do not give them the grudge and call it wisdom.

If the job is going well, moving for work is not the same as leaving. The company will relocate you. It will not cut the cord. You can be on a stage in another city and still need the old house to tell you you did well. A title is not a departure. Do not use a boarding pass as the proof.

Name how you leave. Or how you will not. Then take one move this week that is actually leaving, with honor. A conversation without a speech. A no that is calm. A yes to your own house that does not require their applause. A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that.

Tell one man which way you lean: stuck, or slamming. Tell him the move. If he only hears a rant about your parents, you have not left. If he hears a man going, and honoring, he can ask you whether you went back through the glass. Let him ask.

Leave. Honor them. Do not slam the door to prove you are a man.

Name it. Do you refuse to go, or do you go so hard the glass breaks?

Do this week. One leave-with-honor move. No speech. No crater.
