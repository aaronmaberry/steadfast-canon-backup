# Introduction

You picked this up for a reason. I won't pretend I know which one.

Nobody sat some of you down. Some of you got sat down, and what they told you still doesn't match the man you're trying to be. Nineteen and already tired of guessing. Forty-two, the bills get paid, and you still feel unfinished. Sixty, and you figured this talk had passed you by.

It hasn't. Sit down.

I mean it. Not after you skim for the parts that apply to younger men, or older men, or somebody else. This applies. You are in it.

This room is mixed on purpose. There's a kid still sleeping in a childhood bedroom, and a grandfather who is not done. There's a single man with a job and no script for the hours after it. There's a husband who provides and has gone quiet at the table. Some of you blew a marriage, or watched one blow up, and you are not sure you get another honest page. Some of you are winning at work and cannot tell what the winning is for. I like this room. I am not disappointed you came.

You can be late to a lot of things. A conversation you should have had years ago. A church you kept meaning to try. A call sitting in your phone that you still haven't made. You are not late to this. God is not at the door checking IDs for age, a ring, a clean record, a title. If you are on this page, you are on time.

Some of you stand at the back on purpose. Church. A shop. A circle of men you only half joined. Sitting would mean this is yours. Standing leaves you an aisle. I know that move. I have used the aisle. Not here.

Some of you picked this up for a son, a friend, a guy at work. Good. Read it first. If it is true, you will not hand it over untouched. The first seat is yours. Some of you were handed this by a wife, a pastor, a friend who got tired of watching you guess. You can roll your eyes. You can also sit down. Being handed a book is not the same as being handled.

Most of us learned manhood the way some of us learned to drive. Somebody tossed us the keys, pointed at the road, and hoped we wouldn't hit anything. We borrowed what was close. Work. Sports. A version of our dad, or the opposite of him just to prove a point. That got a lot of us here. It's a weak way to finish.

Sometimes the man who tossed the keys loved you and still didn't know what to say. Sometimes nobody tossed them, and the silence did the teaching. Sometimes it was a coach, a boss, a brother one year less lost than you were. You took what was close. That is not a crime. It is still a weak way to finish.

The borrow can look like a decent year. It can look like a name on a door. It can look like a young man building himself out of locker rooms and whatever the feed spat up. It can look like an older man who did his hours and never got a line of his own. I am not asking you to despise what you were handed. Some of it kept you alive. Alive is not the same as finished.

God isn't confused about you. He made you on purpose. He isn't embarrassed by manhood and He isn't impressed by the knockoffs. He is not waiting for you to get more impressive, or less messy, before He means it. You can spin. The people who raised you can have spun. None of that reaches Him. He has not changed His mind about you.

There's no book in the Bible called Manhood. There's a story. A garden. A fall. A second Adam. A people. An ending. We're going to walk that, and we're going to tell the truth about the leftover stuff that still runs a regular week. Not as a lecture you survive. As the life you actually have.

This is the line. Say it until you don't have to look down.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

It's a lot. That's alright. It's also good news. You're not stuck as the boy you were, and you don't have to wait around for a feeling to show up.

I'm not above this. I know how easy it is to check out and call it peace. I know how easy it is to run a job and still lose the table. I know how easy it is to fill a single man's nights until there is no room left to be called. Same pull. Different rooms. We're not doing that here.

Five parts. The man you were built to be. What you inherited, and what is still yours. The story that names you. The house. Then the long road. We take them one at a time.

The first is a design with a spine, not a mood. The second tells the truth about what still talks in you, and what you keep choosing. The third is older than your family name. The house is the people in reach. Marriage is in this book. It is not the ticket in. The long road is the rest of your years, which are not leftover.

You do not have to see the last mile from here. You have to stay. We will not dump the whole road on you in one sitting. We also will not do this for you. A man can walk a road. He should not have to invent it while he walks.

I'm not here to roast you. The guy next to you isn't the joke. Getting married will not finish you. If you're single, your life already has a call on it. If you blew it, hope is real. If you're older, you still have a next move.

If you showed up with a wound, I will not start with man up. If you showed up with a title, I will not clap you into staying passive. If you showed up at nineteen, you are not crashing a meeting that belongs to your dad. You are on time. So is the man who thought he aged out. So is the man carrying a house and starving in it. Nobody is a guest here.

Get a pen. Find one man who will tell you the truth and not flinch. When we get to the plan, write it down. A warm hour is not a life. The plan is how a next move gets a name. We will get there. Do not skip the sitting to get to the homework.

One brother. Not a crowd. If you cannot think of him yet, stay anyway. Do not make this a private project you intend to finish alone. That is how a man reads something sharp and stays the same.

Paul told a room of ordinary men, in 1 Corinthians 16:13-14, to get on their guard, stand firm, be courageous, be strong, and do the whole thing in love.

:::margin
**1 Corinthians 16:13-14 NIV**
Be on your guard; stand firm in the faith; be courageous; be strong. Do everything in love.
:::

Not a slogan. A way to live.

Anyway. Here we go.
