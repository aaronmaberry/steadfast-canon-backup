# 32. Every season still counts

He is twenty-two in a grocery line, buying whatever will get him through the next three days, and he is already waiting for his life to start.

Two carts back, a man with gray at the temples is buying the same coffee he has bought since the kids left. He is waiting for his life to be over. Same lie. Different decade. Both of them think manhood is a season they are not in.

I have been both men. I have treated twenty-two like a dress rehearsal nobody was filming. I have treated sixty like a refund. Neither year asked my permission. Both still wanted a man.

This page is the season you are actually in. Every season still counts. You do not wait for a later one to begin. You do not get to retire from the four marks because a birthday showed up.

The glory of young men is their strength, gray hair the splendor of the old. That's Proverbs 20:29.

:::margin
**Proverbs 20:29 NIV**
The glory of young men is their strength, gray hair the splendor of the old.
:::

Solomon did not pick a winner. Strength is a glory. Gray hair is a splendor. The young man is not a guest until he marries. The old man is not a mascot until he dies. God put honor on both, which means both have a job. Envy is how we skip ours. The twenty-two-year-old wants the older man's settled house. The sixty-two-year-old wants the younger man's body back. Meanwhile the week in front of each of them goes unlived.

I have skipped seasons while I was in them. I told myself I would be a man when the job got serious, when the ring was on, when the kids were older, when the kids were gone, when I felt like it, when I had more time. The season did not pause for the speech. It just passed, and I carried the skipped thing into the next one. That is the honest map. You can fail a stretch and still have to walk. You do not get a do-over of twenty-four. You get fifty-one with twenty-four still in the bag. Stack enough skipped fights and a life craters. One missed stretch is already heavy. Name it. Then take the next step in the year you actually have.

I have watched a man spend a whole decade as a tourist in his own house. He was there. He was also waiting. The people in the house were not waiting. They were growing up without him, in the same rooms. He called it a phase. It was a season he refused to count, and it counted him anyway.

Passivity loves a later season. A later season is a clean story. This one is messy. This one has a lease, a boss, a wife who already knows, a son who will not stay nine, a body that does not recover like it did, a church that keeps asking younger men to serve and older men to sit. You can sit in any of those and call it timing. It is usually you.

The young glory is strength. Use it. Earn. Tell the truth. Master what will master you if you don't. Do not spend it proving you can stay a boy with better equipment. The old splendor is a gray head that has actually learned something. Spend it. A younger man in reach will take a sage. He will not take a man who only talks about what he used to lift.

You are not supposed to stay in one glory. Strength is not a personality. Gray is not a personality. They are weights for the years they belong to. A fifty-year-old still living only as a fighter will bruise people who needed a father. A twenty-five-year-old trying to be a sage so he can skip the fight will stay soft and call it wisdom. Take the year you are in.

If you are twenty-two, this is not the lobby. This is the book. Delayed manhood wants a son, a title, a house, a calm, then a start. You already have a body, a name, a week, and a God who is not waiting. Strength is your glory. Spend it on a true thing, not on a later fantasy. A 22-year-old can name the season he is actually in this week and take one step in it. You do not graduate into counting.

If you are single, your season is already the life. Marriage is good. It is not the on-switch. Do not draft a wife as the start of being responsible. The grocery line still counts. The job still counts. The younger man who copies you still counts. Do not sit this out because there is no car seat in the truck.

If you are married, do not wait for a quieter house. The loud years are a season, not an excuse. She already knows if you are in this decade or waiting for a better one. Honor what she has carried. Take the year you have with her, with the kids if you have them, with the table that is actually set tonight. If the kids are gone, that is not a refund. That is winter with a job in it.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. Some of you had a season stolen. A father who never handed you off. A marriage that broke in the summer. A body that quit early. I am not going to joke that, and I am not going to tell you it made you interesting. You still have a year. Carrying a skipped stretch is not the same as being finished. Kind is not the same as camping in the old one. Name what you are carrying. Then take a step in the season you are standing in. A brother, not a feed.

If you are older, gray hair is only splendor if it has a next move. A 62-year-old can elevate a younger man or disappear into a chair and a remote. Youth culture will help you disappear. Do not let it. You are not a mascot. You are not done. If you never had a son, you are not off this page. There is a man in reach who still needs a gray head that will tell the truth.

If the job is going well, a title can freeze you in last decade's fight. You already know how to win a summer. Fall and winter will ask if that is all there is. Do not buy a new toy to avoid the question. Do not blame her for a season you will not enter. Take the year. The office will not.

Name the season you have been waiting for, and the one you are actually in. Then one next move in this one. Not a rebuilt twenties. Not a speech about retirement. One step that belongs to the year on the calendar. A 22-year-old can live this Tuesday without waiting for a title. A 62-year-old can live this Tuesday without waiting for a quieter chair. You are not solving a whole life by Friday. You are stopping the skip long enough to live the year you have.

Tell one man the season, and the step. If he only hears that time is flying, he will nod and you will wait again. If he hears the actual year, he can ask you whether you took the step. Let him ask.

The grocery line will still be there tomorrow. Both carts can still be a man. This year counts.

Name it. What season have you been waiting for? What season are you actually in?

Do this week. One next move that belongs to this year. Not a later one.
