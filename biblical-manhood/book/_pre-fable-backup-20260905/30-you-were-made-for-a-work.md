# 30. You were made for a work

The badge still works. The week still fills. You still cannot say what it is for.

I have been that man. Good at the tasks. Early in the lot. Tired in a way that felt adult. Then somebody asked what I was made to do, and I gave them a title. A title is a slot. It is not a work. A slot can pay you and still leave you rented.

This page is the work. Last page was a day for a son. This is Part 5. The long road. Not a ceremony. Not a feeling. Not a written plan yet. You were made for a work. That is this page. God did not save you only from something. He made you toward something, in Christ, on purpose.

I have treated work like a hallway. Get through it. Get to Friday. Get to a better hallway. Passivity loves a hallway. A hallway does not ask you to be a man. It asks you to keep moving. I have also treated work like a name, as if the badge were a soul. Both misses are cheap. One checks out. One worships a slot.

For we are God's handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do. That's Ephesians 2:10.

:::margin
**Ephesians 2:10 NIV**
For we are God’s handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do.
:::

Handiwork. Not a spare part. Not a man who lucked into a team. Made. In Christ Jesus, not in a brand. Toward good works that were already waiting. You do not invent a purpose in a journal so you can feel deep. You receive a life that already has work in it, and you enter it. Some of that work is the job you already have. Some of it is the people in reach. Some of it is a gift you keep leaving in the truck.

Prepared in advance does not mean the path will feel obvious. It means you are not a guest in your own week. God is not waiting to see if you turn out interesting. He prepared work. Your job is to stop treating the week like leftover time.

I have waited for a calling that would arrive with a letterhead. I wanted a lane that announced itself. Most of the good work I have actually done looked like an ordinary hour that needed a man. A hard conversation. A task nobody wanted. A younger man who needed time I had already promised to a screen. The letterhead never came. The work was already there.

Work is not the enemy. Genesis already put a man in a garden with a job before the fall. This page is not anti-job. It is anti-rental. You can hate your hours and still be made for a work. You can love your hours and still be missing it. The tell is not whether you like Mondays. The tell is whether the week is aimed at the good God prepared, or only at staying employed and unbothered.

If the job is a grind, do it as a man anyway. Earn. Keep your word. Do not steal the hours. That can be holy. It is not the whole of the prepared work. A paycheck can feed a house and still leave a gift unused. If the job is a win, do not let the win eat the works. The office will take every hour you will give it and call you faithful. Faithful to a slot is not the same as entering what was prepared.

I have been faithful to a slot. I have also been lazy in a slot and called it waiting on God. Both were me, not a mystery. God prepared works. I wanted a feeling that would make them easy. The works did not get easier. They got clearer when I stopped asking the week to entertain me.

You were not saved to look finished. You were created toward good works. Some of those works are spoken. Some are served. A lot of us only count the kind that can go on a review. The man who sets up chairs, who stays after, who carries a quieter gift, is not a lesser handiwork. Do not despise the work that does not photograph.

I have used "I don't know what I'm called to" as a way to do nothing. Mystery can be honest. It can also be a costume. You know the next true work in reach. You are dodging it. Name that. Then do the next piece. Calling gets clearer in motion, not in a mood.

If you're young, the have-to job still counts. Getting off a parent's payroll is not unspiritual. Trying a lane is not failure. A 22-year-old can name one work God has already put in his hands this week without waiting for a dream with a soundtrack. Delayed manhood wants a later vocation to make him a man. You are already made. Enter the work in front of you.

If you're single, the work is already the life. No wife is coming to make your days matter, and no wife is coming to be the excuse they don't. Do not draft a future house as the start of being useful. The prepared works include today. A 22-year-old and a 40-year-old without a ring can both stop treating the job as a waiting room.

If you're married, she is not the project that replaces your work, and your work is not allowed to replace her. You already have pages for her. This page is whether you are a rental at the office and a guest in your own design. Honor what she has carried while you hunted a better hallway. Then enter the works, including the ones at home that never make a slide deck.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. A job that used you is a wound. A father who only loved a slot is a wound. Getting fired can feel like getting erased. I am not going to joke that, and I am not going to tell you it made you stronger so we can move on. You are still handiwork. A lost title is not a lost making. You can still do the next good work in reach. If you cannot work right now, the prepared works are not only a W-2. People. Prayer. A younger man. A honest day.

If you're older, late is not never. A 62-year-old can stop renting the years he has left. The lane can change. The making does not. Invest what you know. Do not spend the last stretch proving you can still win a slot. A grandson, a younger man at work, a quiet gift you parked in the '90s. Enter it.

If the job is going well, hear this without flattery. You already know how to finish. The danger is finishing the wrong pile with excellence. Ask what was prepared, not only what will promote. The office will not ask. A brother will, if you let him.

Name the work you have been treating as a hallway. The slot. The hunt. The nothing. Then do one piece of the prepared work this week. One. The task you have been circling. The person you have been meaning to help. The gift you leave in the truck. Not a new personality. One piece.

A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. You are not solving a whole vocation by Friday. You are stopping the rental long enough to enter a work that was already yours.

Tell one man the hallway, and the piece. If he only hears that you want purpose, he will nod and you will go back to the badge. If he hears the actual piece, he can ask you whether you did it. Let him ask.

The badge will still work tomorrow. A slot is easy to keep. Enter the work.

Name it. What have you been treating as a hallway? The slot. The hunt. The nothing.

Do this week. One piece of the work that was prepared. Do it on purpose.
