# 6. Go first

You already know the next true thing.

It is hanging in the room. Not hidden. Not complicated. The apology. The honest number. The boy who needs you to start, not to referee. The meeting where a half-true sentence is about to become the plan if nobody opens his mouth. You can feel it before anyone names it.

Then you let someone else step into it. Sometimes you even hold the door. After you. You call that being collaborative. It is being second on purpose.

I have done this. I have had the true sentence fully formed, even tasted the first word, and still waited to see if a braver fool would say it. When they did, I nodded like I had been with them all along. I was not with them. I was behind them, where it was cheaper. I have sent a text that said you should tell him. I was the one who should have told him. I still hit send.

A real man leads courageously. That is this page. Not the wait. Not the load. Going first. When going first will cost you.

Courage is not volume. A loud man can still send someone else through the door. Courage is not a title. You can run a team and never go first in the one conversation that would actually lead them. Managing is keeping the machine running. Going first is stepping into the thing the machine is avoiding.

Jesus put it without a speech. When he has brought out all his own, he goes on ahead of them, and his sheep follow him because they know his voice. That's John 10:4.

:::margin
**John 10:4 NIV**
When he has brought out all his own, he goes on ahead of them, and his sheep follow him because they know his voice.
:::

He does not drive them from behind. He does not take a poll and then appear to have led. He goes on ahead. They follow because they know the voice, which means the voice has already been in front of them long enough to be trusted. Ahead is how a voice becomes known.

Ahead is a costly place. You can get it wrong in public. You can be the first one blamed. You can be the first one tired. That is why a lot of us prefer the second row. Second row has opinions. First has skin.

I got good at second row. I could sit close enough to look involved and far enough to stay clean. Notes out. Nodding. If the true thing landed, I agreed. If it blew up, I had not been the one who said it. People even called that wisdom. It was not wisdom. It was a talent for never being first when first would have cost me.

The people near you learn it. A boy learns that the hard room is his to enter. She learns she has to start. A team learns the truth is optional until someone with less to lose is willing to say it. You call that developing people. They call it being sent in first.

Going first is not bullying. A bully goes first to make the room smaller. A man goes first to make the room truer. If your going first requires everyone else to shrink, that is not this mark. That is you needing a stage. We will not baptize that.

It is also not a personality. Quiet men go first. Loud men hide. Some of the bravest rooms I have been in were started by a man who did not like talking, who said one true sentence and then sat there while the air changed. Some of the loudest rooms I have been in were a man filling the silence so nobody would notice he had not gone first in the one thing that mattered.

The names we give it are decent. Collaborative. Coaching. Letting her lead. Giving the boy independence. Humility. After you. Sometimes those are true. A lot of the time they are you, standing behind a true thing you already knew, hoping a smaller person will take the hit. The boy walks into a room you should have entered first. She starts the hard talk. The intern names the problem in the meeting.

Feelings will try to veto this. Not always the big dramatic ones. The small ones. It will be awkward. You will look intense. You are tired. You would rather keep the room pleasant. Those are real. They are also not the boss. A pleasant room is a great place to hide. A man who lets the awkward feeling run the week will spend years being liked and still not being followed. People follow a voice that has been ahead of them. They do not follow a man who only moves when it feels easy.

I have let my wife walk into a hard talk I should have started. I have let a younger man tell the truth I was already holding. I have called that humility. It was not humility. Humility goes first in the low thing. Cowardice lets someone else bleed for the true thing and then praises them for being so brave.

If you're young, going first might be the text, the church, the job, the conversation with a roommate you have already had in your head. You do not need a following. You need to stop hoping someone slightly older will make the move so you can copy it and still look original. A 19-year-old who goes first in one small true thing is already leading. A 22-year-old who only comments on other men's courage from a group chat is practicing for a long second row.

If you're single, you can go first without a wife in the story. Tell the truth in the group. Be the one who starts the table instead of waiting to be invited to a life. A future marriage will not install a spine you would not use now. The neighbor, the brother, the church that needs a man to open his mouth: those rooms already exist. Go first in one of them this week.

If you're married, she already knows whether you go first. Not in the big crisis. In the small true thing. Who names the tension. Who apologizes without a preamble about how busy the week was. Who starts the hard talk at 8pm instead of hoping sleep will erase it. You can be kind and still late. Late is not courage. She is not asking you to be a general. She is asking whether the true thing in the house has a man in front of it.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will not scold you into a charge. Some of you went first once and paid in a way that taught you never to do it again. That lesson is real. It is also incomplete. Courage is not a personality you lost. It is a next true thing, smaller than the old wound, that still needs a man in front of it. Kind is not the same as letting the room go leaderless. You can be wounded and still go first in one small true thing. That is not a dare. It is still yours.

If you're older, going first can look quieter and still be first. You do not have to sprint. You do have to stop sending the younger men into rooms you will not enter. A 62-year-old who goes first in repentance, in a phone call, in telling the truth about his own life, is leading. A 62-year-old who only advises is a commentator. Commentators are cheap. Shepherds go on ahead. The younger men do not need your highlights. They need to see you step into something now.

If the job is going well, this will find you in the meeting. You already know the true thing. You can feel the cost of saying it. The room would have to change. Someone would be unhappy. So you manage. You summarize. You wait for consensus to do the courageous part so you can sign it. That is not leading. That is administration with a better chair. Managing is not the same as going first. The people who work for you already know the difference. They do not follow a memo.

Go first this week in one small true thing. Not a campaign. Not a personality overhaul. One. Name the room where you should have gone first and didn't. Then go first in the next one that is actually in front of you. A sentence. A call. A confession. The start of a conversation you have been sending other people through. A 19-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. The size of the room is not the test. Whether you went first is.

Tell one man the room, and that you went. Not the pattern as a lifestyle. The room, then the step. If he cannot ask you next week whether you hid again, you told the wrong man, or you told him the wrong thing.

Lead courageously. Go first when it will cost you. The second row will still be there tomorrow, close and comfortable. It is how a man never gets followed in rooms that were his to lead.

Name it. Where did you know the next true thing and let someone else step into it?

Do this week. One small true thing. You go first.
