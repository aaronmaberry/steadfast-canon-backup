# 24. What she actually needs

The bulb over the sink is new.

He walked the house with a four-pack. Closet. Hall. The one that had been out so long they had learned the dark. He got it. He flipped the switch twice. He wanted her to notice she could see. She could see. She said thanks. Then she stood in that new light like the week had not moved.

I have been that man. I have fixed the dark and missed the woman.

This page is what she actually needs. Last page was the dying. You can give yourself up and still hand her the wrong life. The page before that was the study. You can know her and still give her what would have worked on you. Some of you do not have her in the house. Stay. You are not a guest. You are also not the whole page.

She does not need the help that would have worked on you.

Paul wrote it to a church, not a couple: not looking to your own interests but each of you to the interests of the others. That's Philippians 2:4.

**Philippians 2:4 NIV**

Your own interests. That is the four-pack. The gift you would have wanted. The three-sentence repair, because you would have wanted the problem closed. The interests of the others. Hers. Not the ones you projected onto her because they are the ones you know how to give.

I have paid a bill and called it closeness. I have bought the thing she mentioned once in a store and set it on the counter like a trophy. I have given her a plan when she was not asking for a plan. She was asking me to stay in the room with the feeling. I called the plan love. It was me, getting the feeling off the table.

I have asked what she needed and then argued with the answer. That is not looking at her interests. That is looking at mine, with a question mark in front so I could feel fair.

I have come home with a story about the day and thought that was talking. It was a report. She can get a report from a calendar. She needed me.

Passivity loves a gift that does not require him to look at her. Her actual good is not a hobby you visit when it resembles yours. You can go first and still give her the version that protects you. She may not clap for a quiet Wednesday, and a man who needs the clap will go back to the four-pack.

Face is the one that costs me. I can sit on the same couch and live in another county. I can do a whole evening next to her and never actually be with her. A full tank is not her. A new bulb is not her. Logistics can be love. They can also be a man who will not turn his head. She does not need a roommate who shares a remote. She needs a man who will look at her and talk like she is in the room.

A house that is not a surprise is not the same as a paid bill. Provision is good. A paycheck can also be a substitute man. I have paid every bill and still left her as the only adult who knew what we would do if something broke. I have moved a life in my head and told her later, like the house was a unit that would ship. She needed a man at the wheel she could trust, not a decision she had to catch. Direction. A plan if a kid gets sick or a parent needs her, or if you are gone. I have not written that down. I said we would get to it. We have not gotten to it. She is not morbid for wanting a man who will not leave her a scavenger hunt.

Honor on a Wednesday is the one I skip because nobody is watching. I have treated her like furniture I liked. I have called her strong as a way of never having to protect her rest. I have praised her in a room of men and then gone quiet at our own table. Honor is not a holiday. It is a man who talks about her as if she is a person with a soul in the room, including when the room is only the two of you.

I am good at projects. I can close a loop. She was not a loop. When she brought me a feeling I gave her three sentences and a next step. When I rushed the repair I taught her to stop bringing me the true thing. Then I complained that she never talked. I had trained that. She needed a man who could sit in it without turning it into a job.

That is not a seminar. That is not four boxes you check by Friday. It is her interests. Look at them. Not yours in a nicer wrapper.

Some of you think she needs what you needed from a dad, or from a boss, or from a friend who talks like you. You are not her. If the gift would have landed on you, that is a clue, not a confirmation.

Some of you will only give the need that comes with a receipt. Money will do it. So will a tool, or a trip you can post. Those can be love. They can also be a man who will not sit down. If the only gifts you know how to give can be photographed, you are still looking at your own interests. Hers may not photograph.

If you're young, they will talk as if this page is later. Nineteen. Twenty-two. A girlfriend can start to look like a person you impress, or a problem you solve so the life can start. She is not a customer. Delayed manhood wants the need to be obvious and a little heroic. Face is not heroic. A plan someone can trust is not a quest. A 22-year-old can look at one actual person in reach this week and give what they needed, not what would have worked on him. If you will not look at someone else's good now, a wedding will not install it.

If you're single, this is not a brochure for a wife you do not have, and it is not a lecture you sit out. Marriage is good. You may marry. You may not. Either way, a man who only meets his own interests will not suddenly become generous at a wedding. The people in reach already have a good that is not yours. A brother. A parent. The people you sit near on Sunday and never carry. If you are walking toward a woman, do not treat her like a project you are servicing so she will complete you. Look at her interests. If you are not, stay in the room for your brothers. Ask the married man what he has been giving that would have worked on him. He needs that more than he needs another joke.

If you're married, this is your page. She already knows if the gifts are for her or for you. The new bulb is not the house. She can feel a man who fixed the dark and then went back to himself. Do not make her clap. Do not wait for her to assign the need. That is still your interests, with her doing the work. Give the thing that is actually hers this week, on purpose. Do not announce it.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. If she left, this page is not a strategy for getting her back dressed as finally understanding women. Look at the week. If you gave her your interests and called it love, name it without using her exit as cover. If you looked at her interests and the marriage still died, I will not steal that from you. You still have people in reach whose good is not yours. Do not use the wreck as a reason to go back to yourself. Meeting a need is not staying under sin because someone used the word love. If she died, I will not cheer you into a later. Honor is not pretending you got the gift right if you did not. It is not picking a fight with a year that cannot receive a new one. If she never came, you are not a guest on a page about wives. You still do not get to meet only your own interests and call it freedom.

If you're older, thirty years of the wrong gift is not a tradition. A 62-year-old can be loyal and still be handing her the thing that would have worked on him in the house they got when the kids were small. Loyalty is good. A four-pack is not loyalty. She kept living. Empty nest is not a refund where you finally get your interests back. Name one gift that has been yours. Then give hers. The younger men need to see a man who did not retire from looking. A widower still has people in reach. A 62-year-old who never married is not a mascot. He still looks at the interests of the people God actually put in front of him.

If the job is going well, you already know how to get a need right. You would be embarrassed to bring a client the wrong deliverable and call it close enough. You walk into your own kitchen with a four-pack. Honor the work. Do not let the title do the looking. A high performer can read a market and still give a wife the expensive version of what he needed. Money does not convert a wrong gift. Title does not. Would you still look at her interests if nobody at work would ever hear about it? Name the gift that was yours. Then give hers once this week in the kitchen, not in a story about the kitchen.

Name the gift you have been giving that would have worked on you. One. Then give the actual need once this week, on purpose, and do not announce it. Not the one that makes you feel useful. Hers. Once.

A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. You are not finishing a marriage class. You are stopping the four-pack long enough to look at her interests and give those.

Tell one man the wrong gift, and the true one, and that you gave it. If he only hears that you want a better marriage, he will nod and you will go back to the new bulb. If he hears the actual need, he can ask you next week whether you gave it or whether you bought another four-pack. Let him ask.

She does not need the help that would have worked on you. The sink is bright. The week had not moved.

Name it. What have you been giving that would have worked on you?

Do this week. Give the actual need once, on purpose. Do not announce it.
