# 25. The bed is a covenant

The towels are on the bed again.

Wednesday. They came out of the dryer hot, and that was the only empty flat place, so they went down the middle. A little range of terry cloth. Everybody said they would move them in a minute. Then a show. Then a shoe nobody could find. He sits on the edge to take his boots off, looks at the stack, and sleeps on his half like a man checking into a room he already paid for.

I have been that man. I treated the marriage bed like a table that happens to have pillows.

This page is the bed. Last page was what she actually needs. You can meet a need and still leave this room empty. Studying her is not this page. Dying for her is not this page. This is what you do with the room God put in the house on purpose.

The bed is a covenant.

Marriage should be honored by all, and the marriage bed kept pure, for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral. That's Hebrews 13:4.

:::margin
**Hebrews 13:4 NIV**
Marriage should be honored by all, and the marriage bed kept pure, for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral.
:::

Honored by all. Not honored by husbands and skipped by everybody else. All. The unmarried man is in this sentence. The widower is in it. The kid in a first apartment is in it. Kept pure. God is not embarrassed that He made a bed. He is also not casual about what you do with it. The last clause is not a mood. God will judge.

I have wanted this chapter to be a technique. How to want her more. How to get her to want me. That is a manual. This is a vow you keep on a tired Wednesday, including the night you would rather have something cheaper.

The bed does not keep itself. Moods will not walk in and do the vow for you. I have waited to want it enough, as if wanting were the same as keeping. Passivity loves a spare room. A spare room cannot ask you to be a man. I have made one without renting a second house. Laundry down the middle. A laptop on my stomach. A phone that would not stay on the nightstand. I have been proud of how quiet I can be coming in, as if the highest form of love were not waking her. Kind can be a fence.

I have also wanted the bed to fix a day I would not live with her. A covenant is not a tap you turn on at 10:40. She can feel when she is a tap. Keeping the bed is not a bill she owes you. It is not a speech about your rights. It is being a man in the dark who belongs to her and to God, and who will not take the hunger somewhere cheaper because the night was ordinary.

Some of you would never put towels there because you are not in the bed at all. A late game. A snore fight. A back you named so nobody had to say the rest. You make the upstairs bed in the morning so the house still looks married. You have a second address. You did not file papers. You still moved out.

Some of you are in the bed and still not in it. Two cliffs on a king. A little wall of pillows you blame on a shoulder. You share a mattress the way two strangers share a row on a plane.

God put this in the house to bind two people. He is not blushing about it. You do not have to pretend you are above the thing He invented. Shame will not make you holy. It will make you hide. Hidden men take the bed somewhere cheaper and call the cheaper thing private.

A screen is a cheaper bed. It asks nothing. It keeps nothing. I have told myself it did not count because nobody else was in the room. That is a boy sentence. The verse does not ask who else saw. It says kept pure.

Other faces. Other rooms. A file in your head. You cannot keep a bed and keep that file. One of them will win. If that is you, stop managing it. Tell a man who will not flinch. Do not baptize it as stress. The cheap night is easy to find. It does not pay. God's reward is not a lesser night. It is a kept one. A morning you do not have to hide.

I will not draw you a map of the sheets. I will not give you a quota and call it obedience. If the bed is injured, if there is pain, if something in the body or the story is broken, you do not white-knuckle that with a book chapter. A doctor. A pastor. A man who can stand it. Starting over is allowed. Pretending is not.

Honored by all also means you do not treat other people's beds as content. A coworker's marriage is not a story you get to finish. A feed is full of other men's wives. You can steal a covenant without leaving your chair.

If you're young, they will talk as if this page is later, or a joke you grow out of. Nineteen. Twenty-two. You already have a bed. A mattress in a room you pay for, or one you don't. Delayed manhood wants that room to have no Lord, and a girlfriend's apartment to work like a trial membership. It does not. You do not get a practice covenant. A 22-year-old can honor a bed this week by what he refuses, and by how he talks about a woman who is not his wife. The room you have is already under a Lord.

If you're single, you are not a guest. Marriage should be honored by all. That is your sentence as much as anyone's. Marriage is good. You may be given a wife. You may not. Either way, your body is not a private country. The bed you do not have yet is already being decided by what you keep, what you watch, what you joke about, whose ring you treat as scenery. Do not sit this page out because there is no one on the other pillow. Name the cheaper bed you have been keeping. Then honor the real one, including the ones that are not yours.

If you're married, this is your page. She already knows if the bed is a covenant or a storage unit. Move the towels. Come in from the other room. Put the phone on the dresser and leave it there. Go toward her without a speech about being better at this. Do not make her a tap. Do not make her the enemy of a hunger you will not tell the truth about. If you have taken the bed somewhere else, stop managing it. Confess. Keep the vow in the dark, not only in the photos.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. If she left, I am not going to use the empty side as a punchline, and I am not going to hand you a technique for filling it. If she died, that room may be the hardest one in the house. Honor is not pretending. It is not a later wife on a schedule. If the bed was a war, tell the truth to a brother, not a feed. If you were unfaithful, this page is not a speech to save your name. God will judge is not a metaphor you get to sand down. Confess. Get help. Do not call an affair a complicated season. If you kept the vow and the marriage still died, I will not steal that from you. You still have a body under a Lord. Other people's covenants are still not yours. A vow is not a license for someone to crush you and call it holy. If the house is cruel, tell a brother. Do not call the crushing holy.

If you're older, a long marriage can turn into two polite cliffs and a good thermostat. Empty nest, more bed, less covenant. You are not done. A 62-year-old can still come in from the chair. He can still tell the truth about a screen he thought age had retired. The younger men need to see a man who kept the bed after the house got quiet. A widower still honors marriage, including the one that ended in a funeral, including the ones around him. A 62-year-old who never married is not a mascot. Honored by all still includes him.

If the job is going well, you notice rooms that are not yours. Tight corners in a city you will leave at 6 a.m. A view you text. Then you come home and treat the bed with a vow on it like leftover space. Provision is good. A paycheck cannot keep a bed. The hotel cannot keep it either. A high performer can buy a better mattress and still sleep like a guest. He can also take the bed on the road in his pocket. Name the cheaper room you have been keeping. Then be in this one, on a night nobody is grading you.

Name the way you have been treating the bed. The towels. The other room. The screen. The quiet entry. The girlfriend's apartment. Someone else's covenant on a phone. One. Then keep it once this week as a covenant. Married: go to it. Stay. No speech. No trophy. Single: honor it by a refusal you can tell a man about. Not a personality transplant. Once.

A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. You are not repairing a whole house by Sunday. You are stopping the spare room long enough to keep what God called a bed.

Tell one man the cheaper thing, and the keep. If he only hears that you want a better marriage, he will nod and you will put the towels back. If he hears the actual room, he can ask you next week whether you kept it. Let him ask.

God put a bed in the house. Honor it. Keep it pure. The cheap thing will still be on offer tomorrow. The covenant still has a Lord.

Name it. How have you been treating the bed, the one you have or the one you don't?

Do this week. Keep it once as a covenant. Go to it, or honor it by a refusal you can name.
