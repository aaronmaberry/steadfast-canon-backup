# 15. The problem inside

You can get out of the old house, get a man walking with you, and still wreck the next room.

The chair was real. The old house was real. The missing brother was real. You named them. You even made some of the moves. Then you walked into a new kitchen, a new job, a new friendship, and the same wreck walked in with you. You reached for the old story. If they had been better, you would be better. Some of that is true. It is not the whole wound.

The problem is in you. Not only what was done to you. That is this page. Nature, not just nurture. Not a missing friend. Not a catalog of how it plays out in a week. The inside.

I have blamed well. I can tell you what was missing. I can tell you who left. I can give you twenty minutes on a chair and twenty seconds on what I did Tuesday. I have also watched my own mouth do damage in a room that had not done that to me. Boxes still in the hall. A small thing, the kind nobody will remember by Thursday, and I snapped. After, I had a speech ready. If they had been better, I would have been better. The speech was smooth. It was also a lie with a true wound inside it. I wanted to be good. I was not. The wanting did not carry it out. That is not a childhood. That is me.

Paul said it without decorating it. For I know that good itself does not dwell in me, that is, in my sinful nature. For I have the desire to do what is good, but I cannot carry it out. That's Romans 7:18.

He is not performing despair. He is telling the truth about a man who loves God and still finds a law in himself that will not obey. Desire is not delivery. A clean intention is not a clean heart. If Paul needed to say this, you are not deep for skipping it. The desire does not make you the exception. A man who only reports his desire will think he is already good. Paul will not let him.

I have wanted to be patient and then been sharp in the first five minutes. I have wanted to be honest and then trimmed the story so I still looked injured. The wanting was real. The nature was also real. I have used a kind week as proof I was fine, and a hard history as proof I was excused. Both were the same move: managing what lives in me so I would not have to name it.

This does not make the wound fake. Hear that. I am not taking back the empty years. I am not telling you your dad was enough. I am not telling you the tie was in your head. Those pages stand. This page says they are not a full alibi. A man can be hurt and still be the one who lies. Hurt explains a lot. It does not author every hour.

You can change zip codes. You cannot drop yourself off at the last apartment. The truck will take the furniture. It will also take you. I have named the old things and then treated the next wreck as a surprise. It is not a surprise if the same man walked in.

The world likes a simpler math. People are basically good. The mess is what happened to them. Fix the environment and the boy comes out right. Some environments are cruel and should be named. The Book will not let you stop there. Something in us leans the wrong way even in a kind house. You have watched a small child take what was not his with nobody coaching the grab. You have watched yourself do it with a full beard and a reason.

That lean is not a glitch in an otherwise sound machine. It is the machine. You do not need a worse father to explain every lie you told this year. You need a true word for what lives in you. Sinful nature is Paul's word. I will not swap it for a softer one.

I have used "that is just how I was raised" as a hall pass. It kept me from repenting. It also kept me from being loved, because a man who will only be the victim cannot be forgiven for what he actually did. Forgiveness is for sinners. Wounds get tending. Sin gets a cross. You will need both. If you only take the tending, you will stay proud of your damage. If you only take the cross as a slogan, you will skip the hurt. We will not do either.

A real man accepts responsibility. That includes what lives in you. You can tell the truth about what was done to you and still repent for what you did. Using the old story as the only author of your week is how a man never has to repent.

The gospel belongs here. It is the only way a sentence like Paul's does not end in despair. Good does not dwell in me, that is, in my sinful nature. Christ does. A better childhood would have helped. It would not have made you good. A new heart is a gift. You cannot coach it out of a résumé. You receive it, or you keep managing a nature that will not carry out the good you want.

If you're nineteen, or twenty-two, you are not only your dad. You are also you. You do not have to have left home to have this problem. A 19-year-old can treat every bad hour as proof of the house he came from. Some of it is. Some of it is a lie he told this week, a sharp mouth in a car with friends who did not raise him. You do not need a worse story to have a nature. Waiting for more evidence of innocence is how a young man stays innocent in his own mind.

If you're single, the inside is already the life. No wife is coming to be the reason you are like this, and no wife is coming to be the cure. Do not draft a future woman as the problem or the priest. The apartment is already a room you can wreck. Name what you keep blaming. Then tell the truth about you.

If you're married, she is not the problem. She may have her own. This page is yours. You can wreck a kind woman and still have a speech about your past. The speech will not wash the room. Say the true sentence to God first. Then, if you say it to her, mean it. That is not groveling. That is a man entering the actual house he lives in.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. Naming the inside is not calling the wound a lie. You can hold both in one week. You were sinned against. You also sin. The second fact does not cancel the first. The first does not cancel the second. Kind is not the same as letting the old story run every confession. If a marriage ended, I will not turn this page into a trial, and I will not let the ending be the only name you have. The inside is still yours to tell the truth about.

If you're older, you can still be hiding in a long explanation. A 62-year-old can have a polished account of what was done to him, the kind he can give at any table, and a thin account of what he did on Tuesday. The years do not clean a heart. They can bury it under competence. You can still say the sentence. The younger men need a man who will not hide behind his history.

If the job is going well, a clean résumé is not a clean heart. The office will reward the part of you that performs. It will not tell you about the nature that cannot carry out the good you intended on the drive home. You can be trusted with money and still not be well. Name what you keep blaming: the market, the team, the father, the hour. Then tell the truth. The problem is also in me.

Name the thing you keep blaming. Not ten things. The one that has been doing the most work. Then say the true sentence this week, out loud, to God and to one man: the problem is also in me. A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. You do not have to be finished by Friday. You have to stop using the old wound as the only author of your week.

Tell one man the blame, and the sentence. If he only hears the wound, you will get sympathy and keep the alibi. If he hears "also in me," he can ask you whether you meant it when the next room goes wrong. Let him ask.

The problem is in you. Not only what was done to you. The next room will tell you if you believed it.

Name it. What do you keep blaming so you do not have to say the problem is also in you?

Do this week. Say the true sentence. The problem is also in me.
