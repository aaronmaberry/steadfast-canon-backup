# 31. Feelings are the dash, not the wheel

He cancelled it because he did not feel it yet.

The apology. The hard talk. The work he already said he would enter. He sat in the truck with the key in and waited for a mood to arrive that would make him a man. The mood did not come. He went back inside and called it honesty. It was a dash light running the trip.

I have done this. I have treated a feeling like a command. If I felt it, it was true. If I did not feel it, I was allowed to park. I have also treated a feeling like an enemy I had to kill before I could obey. Both are a boy at the wheel. The feeling is information. It is not the driver.

This page is the dash. Last page was the work. This is not a vocational hunt. This is who is steering. Feelings are the dash, not the wheel. That is this page.

The dash is not fake. Heat is real. A warning is real. A man who ignores every light is not spiritual. He is a fool with a dead engine. A man who lets the needle choose the destination is a different fool. He will not go where he said he would go. He will go where the needle points, and then he will have a story about authenticity.

Why, my soul, are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me? Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise him, my Savior and my God. That's Psalm 42:5.

:::margin
**Psalm 42:5 NIV**
Why, my soul, are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me? Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise him, my Savior and my God.
:::

David talks to the feeling. He does not pretend it is not there. He does not hand it the keys. Downcast is named. Disturbed is named. Then a command: put your hope in God. The soul gets addressed. It does not get to drive. If a king had to do that out loud, you are not deep for skipping it.

I have waited to feel like a father before I fathered. I have waited to feel like praying before I prayed. I have waited to feel like a hard conversation before I opened my mouth, and by then the room had already learned I would not. Passivity loves a later mood. A later mood is how a man stays parked and still thinks he is being true to himself.

Feelings can lie. They can also tell the truth. Hunger is a feeling. Grief is a feeling. The pull to quit a promise because it got costly is a feeling too. You have to read the light, then decide by the Word and the work in front of you, not by whether your chest cooperated. Wisdom is not numbness. Wisdom is refusing to let a weather system be a creed.

I have used "I just wasn't feeling it" as a hall pass in a house that needed a man. I have used a good feeling as permission to skip a true thing because the good feeling wanted a different room. The dash will always have a suggestion. The wheel is still yours under God.

A man can be fluent about his inner weather and still be a coward in his week. Naming the feeling is not the same as obeying God. The psalm names the downcast, then hopes. If you only name, you are still parked. If you only hope as a slogan, you skipped the naming. Do both. Then go.

This is not a lecture against being sad. Hear that. A downcast soul is in the psalm. Depression can be a medical fact. Panic can be a medical fact. I am not going to joke that, and I am not going to tell a man in a hole to cheer up so we can move on. Read the dash. Get help if the lights will not quit. Then still do not hand the trip to the needle. A brother. A doctor if you need one. The next true thing that is actually in reach. Kind is not the same as letting a mood keep the house.

Some of you need more than a pep talk. Say that to a man who can actually help. This page is not your whole treatment. It is the refusal to make a feeling into a god while you wait.

If you're young, the crush and the slight will feel like the whole map. A 22-year-old can name the feeling that ran him this week and still do the next true thing. Delayed manhood wants a later calm to make him obedient. You do not wait for a mood to become a man. You steer, including when the dash is loud.

If you're single, do not wait to feel ready to be useful, or to stay, or to tell the truth. A future wife is not coming to regulate your weather. The feeling that says park until someone else makes life real is already driving. Take the wheel this week on an actual promise.

If you're married, she is not the feeling, and she is not required to manage yours. A mood can run a whole house if you let it. You already know how to go to work on a day you did not feel like it. Do that toward her, toward the table, toward the true sentence. Do not make her the thermostat.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. Some of you learned that feelings were either everything or nothing. A house that exploded when someone felt something. A house that mocked anyone who did. I will not dress that up. You can still read a dash without becoming your father. The wound is not fake because the feeling is not allowed to drive. Both can be true in one man. Only one of them is this page.

If you're older, you can call a long habit wisdom. A 62-year-old can have a polished reason for parking that is still a mood with better vocabulary. The younger men need a man who will name the feeling and steer anyway. Late is not never. A grandson is already watching whether a mood gets the last word.

If the job is going well, you already override feelings to close. Then you come home and the needle gets the house. That split is not professionalism. It is a man who will suffer for a close and not for a promise. Use the same spine toward the true thing that does not come with a review. Do not spend your courage only where someone is grading you.

Name the feeling that drove this week. Not ten. The one that parked you, or sent you into the wrong room. Then do the true thing once anyway. The apology. The work. The table. The sentence. Not a personality transplant. Once, on purpose, while the dash is still talking.

A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. You are not fixing a whole inner life by Friday. You are taking the wheel for one true thing.

Tell one man the feeling, and the thing. If he only hears that you have been stressed, he will nod and you will park again. If he hears the actual veto, he can ask you whether you did the thing. Let him ask.

The key will still be in the ignition tomorrow. A mood is easy to obey. Take the wheel.

Name it. What feeling drove you this week? The veto. The rush. The park.

Do this week. One true thing anyway. While the dash is still talking.
