# 27. Give your son a name

The jersey is still in the bag from the league office.

Saturday. Rec complex. Last name heat-pressed a little high on the back. Number 14. He paid the fee. He found the folding chair. The coffee went cold in the cupholder. He knows the useful yells. Mark him. Hands. Get back. The boy stands at the chain-link with his back to the field, twisting the collar, trying to read his own name upside down, like he is checking whether it is really his.

The jersey has a name. The father has not given one.

I have been that man. I have paid. I have sat. I have used the name when the milk hit the floor, and called him bud the rest of the week so I did not have to mean anything.

This page is the name. Last page was a daughter watching. She needs a father in the room. This one is a son, and what you call him. Not a program. Not a speech at a banquet. A sentence he can carry when you are not in the chair.

Give your son a name.

I have summoned you by name; you are mine. That's Isaiah 43:1.

:::margin
**Isaiah 43:1 NIV**
But now, this is what the LORD says—
he who created you, Jacob,
he who formed you, Israel:
“Do not fear, for I have redeemed you;
I have summoned you by name; you are mine.”
:::

You are not the LORD. You do not redeem a people. You are the man in the house who either agrees with that claim out loud, or leaves a boy to collect names from whoever talks louder.

A name here is not the letters on the birth certificate. Those are already filed. A name is a claim. You are my son. I am glad you are. You belong here. You are not the grade. You are not the thing you broke. You are not the joke I make when I am tired. God has a name for you, and I will not let the cheap ones win the week.

I have used the first and middle as a weather warning. That is how he knew he was in it. The rest of the week he was bud, dude, hey, as if a real sentence would cost me something I could not spare. It does. It costs you being a man with a mouth.

I have let a sport name him because a sport is easier than a blessing. I have called him what he did. Lazy. Dramatic. Just like me. That last one is a confession dressed as a joke, and a boy will wear it to school.

Silence is a name. You do not get a pass because you never said the cruel thing. You said nothing, and the room filled with other voices. The world has names ready. Content. Brand. Problem. Delayed. A project. If you do not give him a name, someone else will, and it will not be mine.

Some of you only bless a boy when he performs. Good game. Proud of you, after the score. That is a contract, not a name. Isaiah is not waiting on a stat line. Summoned. Mine. Before the week gets hard. You already heard a Father do that with a Son. This page is whether a boy near you hears a human voice agree.

Some of you will give him a name that is really your unfinished thing. My athlete. My mini-me. The one who will not waste what I wasted. That is not summoning him. That is hiring him. He can feel the difference, even if he cannot spell it.

Passivity loves a nickname that means nothing. I know that pull. A joke is cheaper than a claim. Opening your mouth is the weight. Saying the true thing while he is still at the fence, not later in a text you can edit. God's reward is not a boy who claps for the speech. It is a son who knows whose he is when you are not in the folding chair.

You do not have to make it a speech. Speeches are how we hide. His name, then what he is. My son. I love you. I am not going anywhere. I saw you. You are not that failure. You are mine, and you are God's. Say the true one. Then do not rush the quiet after as if quiet were a problem. The quiet is often the first time he has believed you.

If you're young, you may still be waiting for a man to say it to you. Nineteen. Twenty-two. I will not mock that. Delayed manhood wants a later son to be the first person he blesses, as if a birth certificate were the start of having a mouth. You already name people. A roommate. A little brother. The joke that stuck in a group chat. A 22-year-old can say a true name this week without a child in the house. He can also refuse the cheap name he has been answering to, and go get the Father's. You do not graduate into being called. You receive it, and then you give it.

If you're single, you are not a guest on a dads' page. Marriage is good. A son is a gift. Neither one is the start of this. A nephew. The boy you coach. The kid who stays after because nobody is coming. Call him what he is, not what the room made him. Do not sit this out because there is no car seat in your truck. And do not make a future boy the first human you ever bless. Practice now, on actual people, with a sentence you would not be ashamed for a brother to overhear.

If you're married and you have a son, this is your page. She already knows if you use the name as a siren. She has been writing it on hats and lunch bags so the thing does not get lost. Honor that. Do not make her the only mouth in the house. Do not make her the enemy of a sentence you will not say. Walk in. Use his name as a claim. Not after he scores. Not only when the milk hits the floor. If you have daughters and no son, you still have a mouth, and there are boys in reach who will take a true name if a man will give one. This page is not her page. It is the name.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will go slow. Some of you were named by a wound. Accident. Problem. The quiet one. Too much. Not enough. I am not going to joke that, and I am not going to tell you it made you stronger so we can move on. You can still give a different name than the one that raised you. You do not have to become a siren because you were raised under one. If you have no son because the house broke, or because he will not take your call, I will not hand you a speech to win him by Friday. You can still tell the truth to God about the name you never got, and you can still bless a younger man who is actually in the room. If you were the cruel mouth, stop managing it. Confess. Start using the name as a claim. A brother, not a feed.

If you're older, a 28-year-old still has ears. He may roll his eyes. Say it anyway. A voicemail. A walk to the truck. You are not done because he shaves. A grandson will take a name if you will stop being the funny one long enough to mean it. Empty nest is not a refund on your mouth. A 62-year-old who never had a son is not a mascot. There is a younger man in reach. The Father is not embarrassed by a late sentence. Neither should you be.

If the job is going well, your name is on a door, a badge, an email that other men wait on. Then you come home and the boy gets hey from the hallway. Provision is good. A paycheck cannot name a son. The office will give you a title. It will not sit in the folding chair and say mine. Do not make the scoreboard do a father's job. Summon him by name on a day the office does not need you.

Name the name you have been using. Bud. The siren. The sport. The shrug. The joke that stuck. Then say the true one once this week, out loud, not as a weather warning. His name, then what he is. If there is no son in the house, the younger man in reach. Not a personality transplant. Once.

A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that. You are not repairing a whole childhood by Sunday. You are stopping the cheap name long enough to give the real one.

Tell one man the name you have been using, and the one you spoke. If he only hears that you love your people, he will nod and you will go back to bud. If he hears the actual sentence, he can ask you next week whether you used it as a siren again. Let him ask.

The jersey will still have letters on it. Letters are not a claim. Open your mouth.

Name it. What have you been calling him? The son, the younger man, the boy you still answer to.

Do this week. Say the true name once, out loud, not as a weather warning. His name, then what he is.
