# Before the Fall

A seed packet is taped to the fridge. Tomato. Full sun. Before anything comes up, the packet says what the thing is for. You cannot tell if the row failed if you never read the packet. A bare patch is just dirt until you know it was supposed to be fruit.

Every man I've sat with has wanted to know the same thing under the other things. Not how to fix the marriage, or the anger, or the son, though he asked about those. Underneath: what was I made for? What is the shape before I bent it. If you don't know what a thing was made for, you can't tell whether it's broken.

So we're going back to the beginning. Adam as a blueprint: what God built when He built a man, before the fall. I have never met a man whose deepest ache didn't trace back to this list.

## He was made, on purpose, in an image

The first thing Scripture says about you is not that you're a sinner. That comes later. The first thing it says is this.

:::margin
Genesis 1:27 (NIV)
"So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them."
:::

In his own image. Before you did anything, and before you failed at anything, you were made to look like God. That isn't a status you earn. Male and female both carry it, fully. Your manhood isn't a step above your wife or your daughter. It's a distinct way of carrying the same image, and it was God's idea.

:::margin
Genesis 2:7 (NIV)
"Then the LORD God formed a man from the dust of the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living being."
:::

Formed, and then breathed. A craftsman working with his hands, then getting close enough to breathe into what he'd made. You weren't an accident. God got close.

## He was blessed and given a job

The first words God speaks to them are a blessing and a commission, in that order.

:::margin
Genesis 1:28 (NIV)
"God blessed them and said to them, 'Be fruitful and increase in number; fill the earth and subdue it. Rule over the fish in the sea and the birds in the sky and over every living creature that moves on the ground.'"
:::

He blesses them first. Identity before performance. The Father will speak the same way over Jesus at the Jordan before He'd done a thing. Then He hands the man something enormous to do.

Be fruitful, fill, subdue, rule. A man was made to take raw creation and make more of it. That's why you feel most alive when you've built something or planted something or fixed something. That isn't ambition. It's the blueprint.

Subdue and rule don't mean bully. God rules by making things flourish. Domination is what rule looks like after the fall. Before the fall, it looked like gardening.

## He was placed

Don't skip this.

:::margin
Genesis 2:8 (NIV)
"Now the LORD God had planted a garden in the east, in Eden; and there he put the man he had formed."
:::

There he put the man. God didn't make Adam and let him wander. He put him somewhere, on purpose. Place was part of the design.

You're somewhere too. Most men treat where they are as an accident or a waiting room. God puts men in places. The place you're standing, with its particular dirt, is a garden He put you in. A man who's always looking toward the next place never tends the one under his boots.

## He was given work, and told to guard it

:::margin
Genesis 2:15 (NIV)
"The LORD God took the man and put him in the Garden of Eden to work it and take care of it."
:::

To work it and take care of it. Two verbs, before the fall, before the curse. A man had a job with dirt in it.

Work is not the curse. The curse made work hard. Work itself is in the design, and a man who doesn't have real work to do isn't resting; he's malfunctioning. That's why a man's soul goes sideways when he's laid off, or retires with nothing, or gets hurt and can't. He was made to work a garden and somebody took the garden away.

The second verb carries the sense of keeping, guarding. A man isn't only a worker; he's a keeper. Provider and protector are the same job, and the job came before the fall. Hold onto that. Soon something is going to come through the fence.

## He was given a word, and a line

God speaks to him alone. There is no woman yet.

:::margin
Genesis 2:16-17 (NIV)
"And the LORD God commanded the man, 'You are free to eat from any tree in the garden; but you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, for when you eat from it you will certainly die.'"
:::

You are free, God says, and that comes first. The garden was almost all yes. One tree was no. A wide field of freedom with one clear line.

A man is a creature under a word. He doesn't invent his own standard. He receives a word from Someone above him and lives inside it. That isn't a limitation on manhood. It's the thing that makes manhood possible.

The man heard the command from God's own mouth, alone, before Eve existed. He was responsible to carry it and guard the garden against anything that would talk them across the line. That's going to matter when the line gets crossed and he's standing right there.

## He was given words of his own

God let him name things.

:::margin
Genesis 2:19-20 (NIV)
"Now the LORD God had formed out of the ground all the wild animals and all the birds in the sky. He brought them to the man to see what he would name them; and whatever the man called each living creature, that was its name. So the man gave names to all the livestock, the birds in the sky and all the wild animals. But for Adam no suitable helper was found."
:::

Whatever the man called each living creature, that was its name. God handed the naming over to the man.

A man's words carry weight. What you call your son, he'll become. What you call your wife shapes what she becomes in your house. The few words a man does say land hard, because God built the world to listen when a man speaks.

## He was told it was not good to be alone

:::margin
Genesis 2:18 (NIV)
"The LORD God said, 'It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make a helper suitable for him.'"
:::

Not good, He says, when He had said good about everything so far. Then He looks at a man by himself, in a perfect garden, with work and God's own voice, and He says it is not good.

The man wasn't sinning. He had everything, including God, and God Himself said a man alone is not good. Your need for other people isn't weakness. It's the blueprint. God built you needing others, on purpose, before anything broke.

He didn't bring the animals and say, here, one of these will do. The man's need wasn't for company; dogs are company. His need was for a counterpart, someone like him and not him.

Most men read this as a marriage verse, and it is one. Hear it wider. Not good for the man to be alone is why you need brothers and a man ahead of you and a church, and not only a wife. A single man isn't exempt.

## He was given a bride, and he spoke

God put the man to sleep and built the woman from his side. Then He brought her, and the man spoke. What he said is the first recorded human speech in the Bible.

:::margin
Genesis 2:23 (NIV)
"The man said, 'This is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called \"woman,\" for she was taken out of man.'"
:::

The first thing a man ever said was a poem about his wife. Most husbands I know can't remember the last time they said anything to their wife that wasn't logistics. The blueprint has a man looking at his bride and being moved to speech.

Then he leaves his father and mother and is united to her. Marriage is in the blueprint before the fall. The man was given the job of holding fast. And then this, the last sentence before everything breaks.

:::margin
Genesis 2:25 (NIV)
"Adam and his wife were both naked, and they felt no shame."
:::

No shame, and nothing hidden. Fully seen and unafraid. That's what a man looked like before the fall. Every hidden room since is a man trying to get back to the garden the long way.

## He was given rest

Men skip this one.

:::margin
Genesis 2:2-3 (NIV)
"By the seventh day God had finished the work he had been doing; so on the seventh day he rested from all his work. Then God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it he rested from all the work of creating that he had done."
:::

God rested, and not because He was tired. Rest is part of how good work goes. He built it into the week before the man ever picked up a tool, and He called that day holy.

A man isn't a machine. The blueprint has him working six days and stopping for one. Men who work seven days aren't more manly. A man who never stops eventually goes numb, and numb isn't rest.

## The shape of a man

So here's the man before the fall. Made in God's image, blessed before he'd done anything, given a real job. Put somewhere specific. Given work with dirt in it, and told to guard it. Given a word and words of his own. Told he wasn't built to be alone. Given a bride. Seen and unashamed. Given rest.

That's the shape, and that's what you're for. Every ache traces back to something on this list. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Rejecting passivity is working it and keeping it. Accepting responsibility is carrying the word. Leading courageously is speaking, naming, going first. Expecting the greater reward is remembering that the garden was built around walking with God in the cool of the day.

## The blueprint in your house

If you're single, almost every item on the list was given to Adam while he was alone. He was a full man before there was a bride. You don't become a man when you get married. You become a man by living the blueprint. Work your garden and guard it and carry the word.

If there's a baby in your house, you're standing in be fruitful. That's the curse sitting on top of the blessing. The exhaustion doesn't mean you've left the blueprint. It means you're working it.

If you sit the divorced seat, it is not good for the man to be alone is still true about you, and it hurts to read, and I'm sorry. God hasn't changed His mind about the design because a marriage broke. He isn't asking you to rush into another one. The not-alone is still in your blueprint: brothers first, then a man ahead of you, then a church that knows your name. You can be a whole man on this list while you wait.

If you're older, look at Genesis 2:15 and try to find the retirement clause. There isn't one. Abe read that verse after the paycheck stopped, and it was the day he stopped feeling let go and started feeling reassigned. Same job, he said. Different garden. Grandchildren. Younger men. A wife who's carried a lot. A church that needs a man who has time.

## What's coming

The man in this garden was told to work it and keep it. He has God's word in his ear, the one about the tree. He has a bride he sang over. He has nothing hidden.

And something is about to come through the fence. Soon we'll look at what he did when it came, and at another Man who stood in a different garden and did the opposite. Two men, two ways.

Sit here a minute. You were made for this. For a garden, a word, a work, a bride, others, a rest, and a walk with God in the cool of the day. That's the shape you were bent from, and the shape you're being bent back toward.

## Before you turn

You were made for this. Sit with that before the garden gets broken. Formed by hand and breathed on. Blessed before you'd done anything. Placed, given a work with dirt in it, told to guard it, given a word and a rest. May the Father who made you in His image remind you tonight what you're for, and may you wake in your garden knowing it's yours.
