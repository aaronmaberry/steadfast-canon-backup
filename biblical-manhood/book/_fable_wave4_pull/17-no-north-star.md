# No North Star

He keeps a two-foot level on the dryer. When he hangs a shelf in the laundry room, he doesn't trust the wall. The wall looks straight. Then he sets the level on the board and waits. The bubble crawls, then settles between the lines, and the shelf he was sure of is off.

The wall doesn't get to argue with the bubble. The bubble doesn't have opinions. It only tells you which way is down.

A mason will teach you the same thing with a plumb line: a string with a weight on the end. You hang it next to a wall and the weight settles and the string points straight down, every time, because gravity doesn't have opinions. If the wall is off, the string tells you.

Two things that don't move. Most men measure their lives against things that do.

## Everyone did as they saw fit

There's a sentence near the end of Judges that is the saddest summary of a people I know.

:::margin
Judges 21:25 (NIV)
"In those days Israel had no king; everyone did as they saw fit."
:::

Everyone did as they saw fit. Not everyone did evil. That's what makes it sad. Some of what they did was probably decent. But nobody was measuring by anything outside himself. Each man's own judgment was the star he steered by, and each man's star moved with him, so nobody could tell they were drifting.

That describes most men I know. Not bad men. Drifting men. They're measuring themselves against a reference that moves, so they don't feel the drift, and one day they're a long way off in a marriage or a work or a faith and they can't tell you how they got there.

Here is what men use as a fixed point, and why every one of them moves.

Most men's first fixed point was their father. What he did was what a man does. Then you grew up and found out he was moving too, and if he was gone, or if he was never there, you never even had a point to fix on. A father is a good thing to learn from and a terrible thing to steer by.

Then there are other men. I make more than my brother-in-law. I'm home more than the guys on the crew. I've never cheated, like the deacon did. Every one of those is a man holding his wall up against another crooked wall. You can be the straightest wall in a crooked town and still be off plumb.

There's the culture. What everybody knows a man should be keeps jumping. What made a man in your grandfather's day and what makes one on the screens your son watches are not the same star. You aren't crazy for noticing.

There are feelings. I just don't feel like this is right anymore. I feel like God is leading me to. Feelings are real and they matter. But a feeling is the most moving star there is. It changes with sleep and blood sugar and what you ate. A man who steers by his feelings has decided to navigate by the clouds.

And there's success. If it's working, it must be right. The business grew, so I must be doing something right. The marriage hasn't blown up, so it must be fine. David ran a kingdom very well for a year with a murdered man in his drawer. Success isn't a plumb line. It's a wall that hasn't fallen yet.

:::margin
Proverbs 14:12 (NIV)
"There is a way that appears to be right, but in the end it leads to death."
:::

Appears to be right. That's what drift feels like from inside. It never feels like drifting. It feels like a reasonable way to go, and you only find out at the end.

## The one that doesn't move

God has a plumb line, and He told Amos about it.

:::margin
Amos 7:7-8 (NIV)
"This is what he showed me: The Lord was standing by a wall that had been built true to plumb, with a plumb line in his hand. And the LORD asked me, 'What do you see, Amos?' 'A plumb line,' I replied. Then the Lord said, 'Look, I am setting a plumb line among my people Israel; I will spare them no longer.'"
:::

I am setting a plumb line among my people. God holds a string with a weight on it against the wall of a man's life, and the string points true, and the wall doesn't get to argue. That's what His Word is. Not a set of rules, though it has rules in it. It's the fixed reference, the thing that doesn't move so that you can tell what does.

:::margin
Psalm 119:105 (NIV)
"Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path."
:::

A lamp for my feet, not a floodlight for the whole valley. Enough light for the next step, which is what a man on an ordinary morning actually needs. Paul said it this way.

:::margin
2 Timothy 3:16-17 (NIV)
"All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness, so that the servant of God may be thoroughly equipped for every good work."
:::

Teaching, rebuking, correcting, and training. It teaches you what's true. It rebukes you when you're off, which is the string against the wall. It corrects you, which means it shows you how to get back to plumb. And it trains you until your hands begin to build true. Thoroughly equipped. That's a man with a fixed point.

## Why men don't use it

I've asked, and here's what men say. They don't know how, because nobody taught them. They tried starting at Genesis in January and were in Leviticus by February and that was that. That isn't a moral failure. It's a training failure, and it can be fixed in about a month.

They say it feels like homework. Read it like a man listening to his Father talk. Read it out loud in the truck. Read it with one question: what does this say a man does?

They say they don't have time. You have ten minutes. A man who says he doesn't have ten minutes for the only thing in his life that doesn't move has just told you what he's steering by.

They say they don't feel anything when they read it. That's fine. You aren't reading it to feel something. You're reading it to find out which way is down. The bubble doesn't make you feel anything either. It only tells the truth.

## How a man reads

Start with one Gospel. Mark is the shortest and John is the deepest. Read a stretch a day, and in a few weeks you'll have watched the Man you're following.

Then read Proverbs, a chapter a day, for a month. Hugh started doing that in the middle of his life, on the morning he realized he'd spent his whole adult life steering by his father's opinion of him and his father had been gone for years. He read a chapter of Proverbs out loud in his truck on the way to work, every morning, and he's done it long enough now that the book is worn through. When a younger man asked him how he seemed to know what to do about money and women and anger and friends, Hugh said, chapter a day. It's a father talking to his son, and you can read it for a lifetime and it will still tell you where the wall is off.

Pray the Psalms. When you don't know how to pray, open to a Psalm and read it out loud to God as your own. David left you the words.

Ask one question every time: what does this say a man does, today, on an unwatched morning? Then go and do it. Read it with your brother. One of you reads the verse you've been sitting on that week and says why. And read it out loud when you can. In the truck, on the porch, at the table with your kids. Hearing it in your own voice is what makes it stop being homework and start being a Father talking.

## A story bigger than your story

The fixed point is more than a rule book. If you take it as only that you'll miss most of what it's for.

We've been inside your story. The shapes on you. The hidden room. All of that is real, and God knows every line of it. But your story is not the master story. If all you know is your own stretch, you'll read it wrong. You'll think what was done to you is the beginning, and it isn't. You'll think your sin is the end, and it isn't.

The master story starts in a garden, with a man who was built for something. It turns on two men and two ways of being a man. Soon I'll hold the plumb line up against the whole story of manhood, from the first man to the last.

A man with no fixed point isn't only lost this week. He's lost in the story. He doesn't know what he was made for.

## The star that moves under you

A young man is usually steering by other young men. His standard for how to treat a woman has often been whatever the guys in the truck laughed at. Mark in the truck each morning doesn't move.

A young father is usually steering by his feelings. A man on almost no sleep thinks the marriage is in trouble when the marriage is only tired. Ten minutes with the Word before the phone is worth more than an hour of evaluating the marriage in the dark.

If you sit the divorced seat, other men and success will both call to you. Her new house against yours. The life that looks like it's working against the one that didn't. Every comparison is a moving star. Put the other walls down. Hold the string against your own. You've got a Father who sees in secret, and He isn't scoring you against her.

A man in the middle years is usually steering by his success, the wall that hasn't fallen yet. The work told him he was doing fine while his wife built a life without him. Success is not a plumb line.

An older man has often steered by the culture he grew up in, and he's watching that culture get mocked. The Word will correct a generation's picture and confirm it. The string doesn't care which decade built the wall.

## The house that held

Jesus ended the longest sermon He ever gave with a picture every man understands.

:::margin
Matthew 7:24-25 (NIV)
"Therefore everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice is like a wise man who built his house on the rock. The rain came down, the streams rose, and the winds blew and beat against that house; yet it did not fall, because it had its foundation on the rock."
:::

Hears these words and puts them into practice. Not hears them. Hears them and does them. That's the wise man. He's building on something that doesn't move, and when the weather comes, his house holds.

The other man built on sand. He probably built a fine-looking house. It looked fine right up until the rain. He was doing as he saw fit, measuring by walls that were also on sand.

You don't have to understand why a thing doesn't move to be saved by it. You only have to hold the string against the wall and let it settle.

## Before you turn

I've told you about a bubble and a string. You don't have to finish Leviticus in February. You only have to hold the string against the wall and let it settle. May the Father give you a lamp for your feet this week, and may you find He is patient with the ones who are learning to.
