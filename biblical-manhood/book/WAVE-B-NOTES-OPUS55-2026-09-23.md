## Craft changes, every file

- Plain speech with contractions. The source ran on inversions, dropped articles, and objects doing the verbs ("a locked bathroom held him," "Home has been left by the third man"). Those are now whole sentences with a named actor. Average sentence length moved from 7 to 11 words to 10 to 12, close to Wave A.
- Antithesis kept to about one per chapter. Fragment strings became sentences unless they were a list the reader needs. Repeated sentence frames varied.
- One word held per idea. Exit for anger, the itch, and the drift. Office in the honor section of Leave Without Slamming the Door. Drawer for the hidden life in How That Looks on a Man.
- Every chapter has a self-own in the Wave A manner, and none of them invents biography.
- Chapter titles referenced in prose sit in straight quotes. Title callbacks point back to "The Empty Chair" and "Go First," and ahead to "A Man With No Brothers" and "Leave Without Slamming the Door."
- Lint is clean for em and en dashes, curly quotes, mid-sentence colons, semicolons, the UNSLOP word and phrase lists, the persona's never-import lists, chapter-number references, "pile," "Tuesday," Texas, and any byline or author name.
- Kept exactly as written. All 29 NIV Scripture blocks match the source byte for byte, in the same order. The four marks sentence is verbatim everywhere it appears. Every chapter keeps the ch1 closer box, with Because leads and notes lines of 2, 2, 2, and 1. Robert Lewis is not named in any Wave B chapter, which keeps the Wave A rule (introduction and copyright page only). The source chapters did not name him either.
- The last heading in How That Looks on a Man is now "Before you turn the page," like every other chapter.

## Craft changes, by file

- Anger, the Itch, and the Drift. The drift section dropped an age tag it then contradicted. "Take back one practice" listed two and now says two. Jesus's anger, His temptation (Hebrews 4:15), and Gethsemane are stated accurately. The phone-rule line no longer calls the reader a child, since the chapter promises not to shame him. Owen's walk to the mailbox now happens inside the talk, so the reader sees the ninety seconds used. Tomás now says "I'm so tired I'm scared of myself," which gives later callbacks a speaker.
- The Father Who Does Not Leave. The Carry this line no longer calls Jesus "the original." Jesus shows the Father, and the Father is the original. Hebrews 13:5 is paraphrased, and 1 John 3:1 is quoted with its NIV punctuation.
- When Home Never Handed You Off. Isaac did bless Jacob (Genesis 27 and 28), so Peniel now turns on Jacob giving his own name after once giving his brother's (Genesis 27:19 and 32:27). David's charge now comes before Solomon's "little child" prayer (1 Kings 3:7), and 1 Chronicles 22:5 carries "young and inexperienced." Timothy is losing his nerve (2 Timothy 1:7) instead of "tired."
- Leave Without Slamming the Door. The unmailed letter from "The Empty Chair" returns at the knock, because what it holds is the first two parts of the four-part talk. The divorced son's mother is no longer written as the cause of his hiding.
- A Man With No Brothers. Dale's phone holds his county crew instead of customers and suppliers, to match the grader operator in "You Were Made for a Work." He and his brother talk Thursdays at noon by phone, to match "Write the Next Move." The closing prayer uses Proverbs 17:17 in NIV words.
- Ask the Man. The opening moved from "the lot" to the door of Harlan's truck, because parking lot is on the never-import list. Paul's unfinished race now points to Philippians 3:12-14, because 2 Timothy 4:7 says he had finished it. The eight seconds now name "Go First." The four marks are named without restating the sentence (flag 2).
- The Problem Inside. Adam stood by, then ate, as Wave A fixed in "The Four Marks." Jeremiah 17:9 and Romans 7:25 are in NIV words. Guilt and shame read "I did a wrong thing" and "I am a wrong thing," which "You Were Made for a Work" echoes. Wade's confession now sits inside his twenty minutes.
- How That Looks on a Man. "I have sinned against the LORD" is six words, not five, and every count says six. David was on the roof one evening (2 Samuel 11:2), not in the afternoon. Bathsheba is named once, where David doesn't blame her. The source's contempt line restated a C. S. Lewis image from Mere Christianity, so it now rests on James 3:9-10. The church lot became the foyer and the truck.
