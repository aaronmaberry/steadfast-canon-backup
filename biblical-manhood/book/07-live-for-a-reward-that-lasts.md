# 7. Live for a Reward That Lasts

I know the drive home with the radio up and a trophy riding shotgun. That night feels like it'll last forever. Then one Saturday, years later, you're walking past a yard sale and there's a cardboard box on a folding table with FREE written down the side in marker. Inside is a gold batter missing an arm, a bowling pin mounted on marble, and a sales plaque from a company that went under a long time ago. Every one of them was somebody's big night. Somebody gave up Saturdays for them. By noon nobody has taken a single one. The box goes back into the garage, and next spring it goes out to the curb.

Rewards you can hold in your hand work like that. I've organized whole months around them. They're wonderful for a night. Then your hand is empty again and the hunt starts over.

The fourth mark says a real man expects the greater reward, God's reward. The other three marks lean on this one. Rejecting passivity, accepting responsibility, and going first all cost you something today. A man only keeps paying if he trusts that the return is real. Take away the fourth mark and the other three are courage spent on a losing trade. Why fix the stair if nobody's keeping the books? Why say "That's mine" if blame never costs anything later? Why open the first door if the street is all there is? Those questions don't have good answers until this mark answers them.

## What you're already living for

You're already living for a reward. Every man is, from the minute he wakes up. The only open question is which one.

I've worked my way through this list myself. You can shuffle the order, but it's always the same list.

A father's approval, whether he's living or dead. More men are working for that than will ever admit it. Comfort, which is the prize a new father is chasing by week six and a retired man is chasing on too many afternoons. Being right, which a young husband will trade a whole evening to win. The number on a screen, checked before he's even out of the building. Applause, including the small nod from across a room. Being wanted, the quiet prize single men chase and married men forget they're chasing. And being remembered, which is the trophy in the box, a name on a plaque, and the hope that your work will outlast you by a little.

Some of those are good in their place. Every one of them runs out, and God is a different kind of reward from all of them. The nod from across the room is gone by the time you reach your truck. A bigger number shows up without the feeling that was supposed to come with it. A father's approval, when it finally comes, is smaller than the version you rehearsed. Comfort never tells you no, and it never fills you up either. Collect prizes that last one night, and a lifetime of them ends up on that folding table.

## Treasure that keeps

Jesus never told us to stop wanting a reward. He pointed the wanting somewhere else, and He named the place to store it.

:::margin
Matthew 6:19-21 (NIV)
"Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moths and vermin destroy, and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where moths and vermin do not destroy, and where thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."
:::

Moths. Vermin. Thieves. He described the yard sale ahead of time. Earlier in that same sermon, He explains how the lasting kind gets stored.

:::margin
Matthew 6:3-4 (NIV)
"But when you give to the needy, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, so that your giving may be in secret. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you."
:::

Every prize on the old list needs an audience. This one needs you to send the audience home. A widow's yard raked before her kitchen light comes on. A check with no story attached to it. Praise for a man who'll never find out you said it. Hidden work still has a witness. He keeps the books.

And it reaches far past Sunday morning.

:::margin
Colossians 3:23-24 (NIV)
"Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving."
:::

Whatever you do covers the invoice, the diaper, the spreadsheet, and the graveyard shift. This reward gets handed out on ladders and at sinks where nobody's watching, when the work is done for the Lord, and almost never on a stage. The size of the job doesn't matter. Who you're working for is what turns the work into treasure.

## The reward is Him

The word reward can sound like a bonus plan. Read the offer before you decide.

:::margin
Matthew 25:21 (NIV)
"His master replied, 'Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you in charge of many things. Come and share your master's happiness!'"
:::

Well done. More to take care of. An open door into His joy. The reward is a welcome. You get closer to Him, you're trusted with more, and you share in His happiness. A son wants a Father who's glad to see him come home.

Moses priced the trade correctly. He grew up in a palace, with the treasure rooms of Egypt down the hall. He walked away from all of it and took his place among slaves.

:::margin
Hebrews 11:26 (NIV)
"He regarded disgrace for the sake of Christ as of greater value than the treasures of Egypt, because he was looking ahead to his reward."
:::

Looking ahead. He looked past the treasure rooms at something worth more, and that look is what let him choose. A man can walk away from almost anything while he has a better thing in view. When all he can see is what he'd be giving up, he can hardly walk away from anything at all.

## What it does to a man

You don't have to interview a man to know whether he's aimed at God's reward. It shows. He does good nobody sees and never mentions it afterward. He lets go of arguments he could win, once being right isn't worth the damage. He stays in his marriage, his church, and his town on the day a justified exit opens up. He takes the low seat and the low job without scanning the room for credit. He forgives a man who hasn't earned it, because the only ledger that matters is the Father's, and he can quit keeping his own. He plants crops he won't eat. He prays for grandchildren who haven't been born yet. He teaches a boy to work, and that boy will teach another boy after he's gone.

A widower I know, Mr. Pruitt, put forty pecan trees in the ground the spring after he buried his wife. He was past seventy. He knows he won't live to see a real harvest. The nuts will be for other people. The watering is his.

Inside this life alone, that way of living makes no sense. Set against eternity, it's the only math that works.

## What it doesn't promise

There's a loud counterfeit of this teaching, so let me name the limits. God's reward doesn't guarantee your health, a bigger house, an easier year, or children who turn out the way you hoped. Staying in a hard marriage doesn't promise it'll be warm by spring. Forgiving a man doesn't promise he'll ever apologize. Men who preached those deals left a lot of damage behind them. Paul wrote the true scale after he'd been beaten and shipwrecked.

:::margin
2 Corinthians 4:17-18 (NIV)
"For our light and momentary troubles are achieving for us an eternal glory that far outweighs them all. So we fix our eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen, since what is seen is temporary, but what is unseen is eternal."
:::

Light and momentary, from a man covered in scars. He was doing the math with eternity's numbers. He never claimed the trouble would disappear. He said it was producing something. The hard years stay on the record. They're the material the reward is made from.

## How to live for it

Every day, fix your eyes. Fix is Paul's word, and a glance won't do it. Say one line before your feet hit the floor. Today is seen. Today counts.

Every week, do one kindness with no witnesses at all. That means your wife doesn't know, your brother doesn't know, and neither do the men you meet with. If the story gets out, you picked the wrong kindness.

On the drive home from a day nobody thanked you for, practice hearing "well done" in His voice. Point your memory at the One you actually work for.

Catch yourself performing while it's starting. Jesus was plain about this. Good done for a crowd has already been paid in full. The moment you look around to see who noticed, you've been paid, and you've been paid small.

Pray past Thursday. Thursday can stay in the prayer, but look further out than that. An apartment is room enough for a single man to live this way, and a porch is room enough for a grandfather. The audience you're giving up is the same for both of them, and so is the Father who sees.

## One man

Stand all four marks up in one life and look at him. He doesn't walk around the job that needs doing. He says "That's mine," and he stops at the period. He opens the first door while the whole street watches. The reward he's aiming at is one no yard sale can touch. There's an old word for a man like that. Steadfast.

**A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.**

Live for a reward that lasts. Then go back to work.

## Before you turn the page

The nearest prizes pay out the same night, which is why men settle for small ones. Aim higher. The Father who sees in secret remembers every entry in the account. May your eyes stay fixed where moths can't get to the treasure, and may the words you most want to hear be kept for you until the day He says them.
