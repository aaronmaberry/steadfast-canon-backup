## What the ones with staying power actually look like

**Wild at Heart.** About 230 pages, 12 chapters, three acts (what a heart is for, what wounded it, how it gets free). One memorable frame. Men finish it. Reviews still say it moves men. Critiques: the frame gets treated as more universal than it is. We do not import battle / adventure / beauty.

**Disciplines of a Godly Man.** About 230 pages, 17 chapters, 4 to 6 pages each. Three clusters (relationships, mind and ministry, character). Every chapter: short teaching, illustration, questions, a verse to keep. Built for a table of men, not a weekend binge of slogans. 450k+ sold. Reviews praise the shortness and the questions.

**The Titus Ten.** Intro + 10 named noun chapters + a last word. One Bible letter as the spine. Each chapter can stand alone. Reflection questions. Group study version exists. Reviews: packed, practical, not a commentary.

**Authentic Masculinity (Troutt, 2026).** About 240 pages. Two parts: virtues, then roles. TGC review: clear categories, comprehensive without being exhaustive, gospel in the same paragraph as the call, give-away-able. Roles named so a single man is not a guest.

**Atomic Habits (secular, structure only).** Short chapters, about 2,500 to 3,000 words. One idea per chapter. You finish one, you start the next. Finished books get recommended. First chapters a bit longer to light the engine. Do not copy his four laws.

**Can't Hurt Me (secular, structure only).** Story, then a Challenge at the end of the chapter. Men like a move, not a lecture fade-out.

## What reviewers keep praising

- A frame they can say out loud
- Short chapters a busy man can finish
- A next move or questions, so a group can use it
- Specific life, not slogans
- Room for more than one season
- Gospel in the call, not gym-talk with a verse taped on
- Not exhaustive. One job per chapter.

## What reviewers punish

- Padding
- Every chapter the same pep talk
- Only married-dad doorway
- A brand of toughness with no Christ
- Long chapters that do not turn the page

## What this means for Steadfast Men

We already have the frame (four marks) and a 35-chapter map. 35 only works if chapters stay Hughes-short, not textbook-long.

Target: 1,200 to 2,000 words a chapter. Intro can sit near 800 to 1,200. Whole book about 50 to 70k words. A man can live in it for a season. A group can do a chapter a week without dying.

Do not steal their lists: priest/protector/provider, battle/adventure/beauty, Hughes's 17 disciplines, Titus Ten nouns, Troutt's virtue/role labels.

Steal this shape:

1. Named parts (we have five).
2. One job per chapter.
3. Original picture, then a clean hit, then a move.
4. Optional two or three table questions at the end, later, if Aaron wants groups. Not required in chapter 1.
5. Keep chapter length even. Do not let 01 balloon because we researched.
