# 34. Write the Next Move

Since he was nineteen, Hollis has kept a notebook in his shirt pocket, about the size of a deck of cards. Every night, before sleep, tomorrow's first three jobs go on the page. Three. In order.

At seventy-four I asked why the habit had survived.

"Because if it isn't written down, it isn't going to happen," he said. "A man who knows what he's going to do tomorrow gets up and does it. A man who's going to figure it out in the morning figures out that he'd rather have another cup of coffee."

The page he opened said this. Fix gate latch. Call Cole. Read Proverbs 14. Fifty-five years of writing the next thing the night before. Hollis is Hollis, in large part, because of that book.

Shut this cover on a feeling alone and the marker will have no Tuesday behind it. Christmas will come and the feeling will be gone from memory. Write the next move before you close it. One. The one that comes next.

## Make it plain

God has told men to put things on a page. Habakkuk was one of them.

:::margin
Habakkuk 2:2 (NIV)
"Then the LORD replied: 'Write down the revelation and make it plain on tablets so that a herald may run with it.'"
:::

Down on the page. Plain. Another man has to be able to run with it. A herald's plan fits in a sentence. Failure comes from two sides. Nothing gets written. A man leaves a retreat wet-eyed, resolve in his gut, and the resolve dies at the first hard week, because unwritten things were never real. Or forty pages get written, vision talk and mission talk, and the stack dies in a drawer from the weight. Nobody runs with forty pages.

A tablet is the Bible's shape. One surface. Ordinary words. Nehemiah had a wall up in fifty-two days. Months of prayer came first. He walked the ruin at night until the breaks were clear to him. He asked the king for the exact help required. Each family received its own section of wall. Men tried to pull him off the job, and a sentence was waiting.

:::margin
Nehemiah 6:3 (NIV)
"I am carrying on a great project and cannot go down. Why should the work stop while I leave it and go down to you?"
:::

Once the plan is written, the project has a name, the distractions have a name, and you can say both out loud.

## One page

One page will hold it. An index card, if you write small. The ends of the chapters have already asked you to name every line. Thinking is finished. Make the thinking plain.

Top line, the mark you are faintest on. "Of the four, the one I'm missing most is ___." A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Choose the faint one.

Next, the exit, a single word. Anger. The itch. Or the drift.

Next, season and assignment in one sentence that contains a verb. A retired man might write that he will assist two younger men and a grandson. A man single again in the first year might write that he will drive straight, drive slow, and call his kids every week.

Next, your brother, with a name, a day, and a time. "Dale, Thursdays at noon, by phone." No name yet means the line is "Ask ___ by Friday." That line is already the move.

Next, the man ahead. His name, and the question you will ask him first. Older than him, flip it. His name, and what you are going to offer.

Next, the rhythm. Five unwitnessed things that do not move, or ten, scaled to the season.

Next, the house. One move toward her, or toward the wife who is not in the house yet. One move toward each child, or toward a fatherless kid if you have no child of your own. A date on the calendar if a son or a daughter is nearing the crossing.

Next, the work, one line. A man might write that the plant gets his whole heart for the Lord. Another might write that his wife will hear the truth about his hours.

Next, the drawer. One line, and a man's name on it. The hidden thing, and which Nathan will hear it. This is the line men skip. Leave it on the page.

Last, the move itself. One action this week, the first action the lines above produce. It may be the small one. "Ask Dale." "Put the phone in the kitchen tonight."

Sign underneath. Put the date. Men need markers, and the page is one of them.

## What a move is

Move is doing work as a word. A goal is a place you hope to arrive. Better husband. Nearer to God. Things fixed with Dad. Thursday cannot execute a goal. Thursday can execute a move.

Three pieces. A verb. A person. A day. After the kids are down on Thursday, ask your wife what she most needs from you. On Sunday, call your father and name one gratitude. Done, or not done. Those are the only two scores.

"Be a better man" has never appeared in Hollis's book across fifty-five years. Something near twenty thousand small moves have. Add them and you get the better man. Solomon had the difference in view.

:::margin
Proverbs 21:5 (NIV)
"The plans of the diligent lead to profit as surely as haste leads to poverty."
:::

Diligent plans. Diligence belongs to weekdays.

## Why plans die

I have watched the deaths.

Bulk kills them. Five lines, or ten, readable from a truck seat in ninety seconds.

Hiding kills them. A drawer holds a wish. Put the page in your brother's hands and give him permission to ask.

No date kills them. Soon is a fog. Thursday is a day on a calendar.

A feeling kills them when the feeling was the only room the plan was born in. Retreat heat. The final page of a book. Fine, if a dull weekday gets a second look once the heat is gone. Keep what still stands. Cross through the rest.

Neglect kills them. Every ninety days, you and the brother and the page. What got finished. What shifted. What the move is now. Then a clean page.

Leaving God off the page kills them, and men miss this death.

:::margin
James 4:15 (NIV)
"Instead, you ought to say, 'If it is the Lord's will, we will live and do this or that.'"
:::

If it is the Lord's will. The whole page stands in that posture. God receives it, and God may revise it. An open hand can hold a plan He will use. Close the hand and the plan is an idol wearing bullet points.

:::margin
Proverbs 16:9 (NIV)
"In their hearts humans plan their course, but the LORD establishes their steps."
:::

Paper. Plain words. You plan. He sets the steps, which moves land, and the hour they land. Write it. Pray it. Let Him keep it.

## The page scales

A baby in the house means three lines, and three is wisdom. Time at the counter with her. The shift you take. Nine o'clock, and your brother on the phone.

Single again, and the first year's page opens with a rule. No turns. Under it, the floor. A weekly call to the kids. A weekend that stays untraded. The brother. Church, awkward room included. Year two can carry the rebuilding.

Three lines belong on an older man's page that a younger page cannot hold. The hill you are asking God to give. The younger man you are walking toward. The talk with a grown child you have delayed, dated. Two months Roy's page carried "Say it to my son on the porch." He said it, because it was written there, and because Hollis asked every Thursday.

Widows' husbands may have the shortest page of all. After she died, Mr. Pruitt's first page said, in its entirety, "Coffee with Ed Wednesdays. Trees."

## As for me and my household

Joshua, near his own end, gathered the nation and forced the choice. Then he named his. Men have set that sentence over doorways for three thousand years.

:::margin
Joshua 24:15 (NIV)
"But as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD."
:::

This day, he told them. The season could wait. Feeling ready could wait. A next move, said where witnesses stand. A direction for the whole roof, spoken where other men can hold him to the words.

Your page is that sentence in your handwriting. Plain. Dated. Signed. Read to a brother. Loose in the hand before God. Then you run.

## Tomorrow's three

On the porch I asked which written move had cost him the most.

"Probably 'Tell Cole he's a good man.' That was on the page about three weeks. Finally I thought, if it isn't written down it isn't going to happen, and it was written down, so I'd better." He shrugged. "Took thirty seconds. He sat in his truck and cried for twenty minutes. I'd have put it off another year if I hadn't had to look at it every night."

The method is that small. Write it. See it each night. Do it because the page says it. Let God set the step. One page. One move. This week. Make it plain.

## Before you turn the page

A move on paper can be checked, so the writing will feel exposed. Mercy lives in that exposure. Give the move to a brother and the intentions stop being a private load. May the Father who sets a man's steps set yours before the week is out. Do whatever sits at the bottom. Hollis has learned across fifty-five years that the next thing was always enough. You can learn it too.
