# 16. Destiny Is Not a Vibe

:::epigraph
Now when David had served God's purpose in his own generation, he fell asleep; he was buried with his ancestors and his body decayed.
Acts 13:36
:::

Warehouse 2. Dock C. A crate of brass fittings, already tagged for a shop across the river, and a young guy with the slip in his fingers hoping this hour might finally feel like what he was born for. It didn't. He set the paper on the pallet and stared past the truck toward the lot, like the answer might pull in and honk. The crate didn't care. Those fittings were crossing the river whether he felt anything or not. An older hand walked over, lifted the slip off the pallet, and put it back in the kid's hand. "Here's your job," he said. "Quit waiting to feel a different one."

I've thought about that dock a lot. God hands a man his destiny. The man does it somewhere specific, with the people of his own time, mostly on days that look like nothing, and then one day it's done. Feeling significant is something else entirely. So is that low hum telling you the big moment meant for you is still out there somewhere. Every man on that dock had an assignment. You've got one too. A lot of us keep waiting to feel chosen with the work sitting right in our hands.

A few stories keep men standing at the edge of the dock, and each one sounds wise. One says you'll know it when you feel it. Out there is a life that will finally fit, and until it does, every restless Sunday night is proof you're on the wrong road. Feelings tell you about the weather, though, not the road. Moses pushed back at the bush. Jeremiah said he was only a boy. Gideon asked God for a sign three separate times. Jonah paid good money to sail away from Nineveh. If feelings got the vote, not one of them leaves the house. Men obey, and the feelings sometimes show up later, and sometimes they don't. God honors the obedience either way.

Another says it has to be big. A stage, a following, something people still talk about after you're gone. So a man frames houses for twenty-five years, coaches his boy's team, is the guy the whole street calls when a pipe bursts, and quietly decides he missed it. Jesus said well done to the servant who'd been trustworthy with a little. The little was the test. When the temple was a half-built foundation, the prophet Zechariah asked who dared look down on the day of small things, because people would be glad to see the plumb line in Zerubbabel's hand. Small days aren't the waiting room for destiny. They're the building.

And one says it's somewhere else. Another job, another state, after the wedding, after the kids are grown, after you retire. The real life is always one move off, and the move keeps sliding down the calendar. If you're single and sure a wedding or a bigger title will finally unlock your work, go back to that dock. Whatever God has put next in front of you is it. God set the first man down on one piece of ground and said work this. Paul stood in Athens and told the city that God decided when and where each nation would live. Your county wasn't an accident. Neither was your decade.

:::

If I could give you one line from the Bible on what a man's life is for, it would be the one this chapter opens with. Paul spoke it in a synagogue in Asia Minor, walking his listeners down through Israel's kings. That's a whole life in about two dozen words. David took God's purpose, not his own, and carried it out with the people God surrounded him with, across the years God allotted. After that he died, and the grave did what graves do. Paul doesn't skip the decay. He wants you to see that the man was never supposed to be permanent. The purpose was. And notice the verb. Served. David had a Master. When David went his own way, with Bathsheba and with the census, he nearly lost everything. Paul leaves all that out of the epitaph and keeps the one thing God gave him to do. That sentence is true about you whether or not you can feel it tonight. And if you're older, in his own generation still means now, not back then. The psalm says the righteous still bear fruit in old age. A Saturday breakfast with a younger man. A Sunday class. A few words over a tailgate. None of that waits on a feeling.

Your destiny was assigned before you got here. You don't go find it like a man looking for a parking spot. Paul says we're God's handiwork, made in Christ Jesus for good works that God prepared ahead of time for us to walk in. Prepared ahead of time. That means you don't have to sniff out one hidden purpose and live terrified you chose wrong. You can trust that the next good work is already set in your path, and go do it. Pick up the slip. It also lives at an address. Your wife's name, if you're married. Your kids' names, if you have them. With a new baby at home, your destiny right now weighs about twelve pounds and has opinions about sleep, and the fact that it doesn't feel grand is part of the lesson. The cul-de-sac, the night shift, the small church where people know how you take your coffee. David's field was Israel. Mordecai's word to Esther still lands on men who'd rather lie low. If you keep silent, help will come some other way, and you'll miss your part in it. Maybe you're right where you are for this exact moment. This desk. This kitchen. This year.

And it gets done by faithfulness. Moods start things. They don't finish them. Jesus told His friends that His food was to do the will of the One who sent Him and to finish His work. Finishing the Father's work fed Him. Paul wrote from a prison he'd never have chosen and said it plainly. He'd fought the fight, run the race, kept the faith. Those are the words of a man who got to the end. The day is coming when the only Judge whose opinion lasts says well done, a Father's voice to a son who was His before the work began. So start with what you're already holding. Adam never had to go looking for his garden. God planted it, walked him into it, and told him to work it. Yours is already planted too. Write down your place, your actual town and street and job and church. If God wants you somewhere else, let Him do the moving. Write down your people, by name. Those names will outweigh anything you could hang on a wall. Use the gift that's really in your hands, and live inside the years you were given.

The young guy stayed. He never did get the feeling. What he got was the older man telling him to give it three years, feel it or not, and see what God would do with somebody who quit walking away. That was a good while back. He leads a crew now. Two men on that crew started going to church because of how his shift treats people. Ask him about his calling and he'll wave you off. He'll talk about purpose, though. He says it was sitting right where he kept putting that slip down. God hadn't hidden a thing from him. It was printed. It just needed picking up.

:::

If your marriage came apart, the story isn't over, and the good chapters aren't either. Joseph's purpose went through a prison. Peter's went straight through the night he swore he never knew Jesus, and Jesus had already prayed about the far side. He told Simon He'd prayed his faith wouldn't fail, and that when he'd turned back, he should strengthen his brothers. Turned back, then strengthening others. His worst night became equipment. There are men who'll never tell their pastor what they'll tell you, because of where you've been. God can serve His purpose in this generation through the exact history you assumed disqualified you.

Every man collects labels along the way. The kid whose dad walked out. The guy whose marriage failed. The one who got passed over. They happened, but they're not your name. Those labels belong to Eden's fig leaves. God's story gives you different ones. Made in His image, placed on purpose, given work, a son with a Father who doesn't leave, a man in Christ, a servant of God's purpose in your day. One day, Jesus says, He'll put a white stone in the hand of the man who overcomes, with a new name on it that only the one who receives it knows. Whatever your history kept calling you won't follow you there.

Most of what God assigns a man gets carried out under his own roof, so from here the book goes home. A wife, if God has given you one. Children. The marriage bed. The supper table. Rooms already full of the people you love, or rooms you're still building toward. Chasing a feeling is like chasing heat lightning across a field. The assignment was never out there. It's been in your town, your people, your hands, and these years. The Father who planned your good works before your first breath will put the next one squarely in your path this week. Do it whether you feel anything or not. It counts. Do what God handed you, where He put you, for the people He gave you, until it's finished. Destiny is not a vibe.
