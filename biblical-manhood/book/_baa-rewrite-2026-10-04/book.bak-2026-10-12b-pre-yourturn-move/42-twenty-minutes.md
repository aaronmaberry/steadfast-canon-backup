# Twenty Minutes a Week

Chapter twelve asks you to find one man and ask him for twenty minutes a week for three months, same hour, no matter what. Most men who make that ask get a yes. Then they sit down across from each other the first week and have no idea what to say. This guide is for those twenty minutes.

It's twelve weeks long. Each week gives you one question to open with, one passage to read, and one question to close with. That's enough to fill twenty minutes and short enough that neither of you can hide behind it. Meet in person if you can, at a diner, in a truck, on a porch, at the edge of a ball field. Meet by phone if you can't. Keep the hour.

A few rules that keep this alive past the first month.

- Same day, same hour, every week. Don't reschedule by text the morning of. If something truly can't be moved, set the makeup before you cancel.
- Both men answer every question. This isn't a mentor and a project. It's two brothers, even if one is thirty years older.
- What's said stays between the two of you, unless one of you is in danger.
- Ask about last week's answer before you start this week's question. That's where the real conversation usually is.
- At twelve weeks, decide together whether to go another round. Most men do.

## Week 1. Where you are

Open. Tell me about last Tuesday, start to finish.

Read. Ecclesiastes 4:9-12.

Close. What made you say yes to this?

## Week 2. Where you came from

Open. What was the table like at your house growing up?

Read. Psalm 68:4-6.

Close. What did you get from your father that you're glad to have? What do you wish you'd gotten?

## Week 3. What you're carrying

Open. What's the heaviest thing on you right now?

Read. Galatians 6:1-5.

Close. Is any of that not yours to carry? Is anything that is yours sitting on the floor?

## Week 4. What you've been putting off

Open. What have you known for a while that you need to do?

Read. James 4:17.

Close. What's the first move, and what day will you make it? I'll ask you next week.

## Week 5. Where you go when it's bad

Open. When the day goes wrong, where do you go? Anger, a screen, a bottle, a long drive, or just away?

Read. Ephesians 4:25-27.

Close. What would it look like to call me instead, one time, before you go there?

## Week 6. What's in you

Open. What's the sin you're most tired of?

Read. 1 John 1:5-9.

Close. Say it to God out loud, right now, short. Then I'll pray for you.

## Week 7. What you steer by

Open. When you want to feel like you're doing okay, what do you compare yourself to?

Read. Psalm 119:105.

Close. What are you reading in the Bible right now? If nothing, what will you start tomorrow?

## Week 8. The people closest to you

Open. Who at home, or closest to you, got the least of you this week?

Read. Ephesians 5:25, or 1 Corinthians 7:35 if you're single.

Close. What's one thing you'll give them before we meet again?

## Week 9. The kids, or the young ones

Open. Who's a young person in your life who's watching how you live?

Read. Deuteronomy 6:6-7.

Close. What's one thing you could say to them this week that they'd remember?

## Week 10. Your work

Open. What's the hardest part of your work right now? What's the best part?

Read. Colossians 3:23-24.

Close. What would it look like to do tomorrow's work for the Lord?

## Week 11. Your feelings

Open. What's the weather inside you this week?

Read. Psalm 42:5.

Close. What will you do this week whether you feel like it or not?

## Week 12. What's next

Open. What's changed in you since the first week? What hasn't?

Read. Proverbs 17:17.

Close. Do we go another round? If yes, set the day and hour now. If no, say thank you, plainly, and pray for each other one more time.

## When it gets awkward

Somewhere around the third or fourth week, one of you will want to quit. The questions get closer to the bone, the novelty wears off, and a busy week makes it easy to text "rain check." Expect that week. Name it out loud when it comes. "This is the week we said one of us would want to bail." Then keep the hour anyway.

Some weeks one of you will have nothing to say. That's fine. Sit with the passage, read it twice, and pray for each other. Twenty quiet minutes with a brother still counts.

Some weeks one of you will say more than the other expected. Don't flinch and don't fix. Say thank you, ask what would help, and pray. Then ask about it next week.

If one of you moves away, keep going by phone. If one of you falls off for a month, the other one calls. Don't wait to be called. That's what you signed up for.

## A second round

If you're going another twelve weeks, the questions go a little further. You know each other now. Use that.

## Week 13. What you hid

Open. What's something you didn't say in the first round that you wish you had?

Read. Psalm 32:1-5.

Close. Is there anything you need to say now? Take your time.

## Week 14. Your marriage, or your singleness

Open. On a scale of one to ten, how are things at home? What would make it one point higher?

Read. 1 Peter 3:7, or 1 Corinthians 7:32-35.

Close. What's the one point higher, and what day will you work on it?

## Week 15. Your money

Open. What does your bank statement say you love?

Read. Matthew 6:19-24.

Close. What's one change you'll make this month?

## Week 16. Your eyes

Open. How are you doing with what you look at? Be straight with me.

Read. Job 31:1 and Matthew 5:27-30.

Close. What guardrail do you need, and who holds the key?

## Week 17. Your temper

Open. When did you last lose it, and who was on the other end?

Read. James 1:19-20.

Close. Do you owe anybody an apology? When will you make it?

## Week 18. Your body

Open. How are you sleeping, eating, and moving? What's your body telling you?

Read. 1 Corinthians 6:19-20.

Close. What's one thing you'll change this week to take care of what God gave you?

## Week 19. Your church

Open. Where do you serve? If nowhere, why not?

Read. 1 Peter 4:10-11.

Close. What's one way you could serve this month, and who do you need to ask?

## Week 20. The man ahead of you

Open. Who's an older man you watch? What do you see in him?

Read. Titus 2:1-8.

Close. What could you ask him the next time you see him?

## Week 21. The man behind you

Open. Who's a younger man God might be putting in your path?

Read. 2 Timothy 2:1-2.

Close. What's one way you could invest in him this month?

## Week 22. Your fears

Open. What are you most afraid of right now?

Read. Isaiah 41:10.

Close. What would you do this week if that fear had no hold on you?

## Week 23. Your hope

Open. What are you looking forward to? What are you hoping God will do?

Read. Romans 15:13.

Close. Pray for each other's hope, out loud, by name.

## Week 24. Your legacy

Open. Twenty years from now, what do you want the people closest to you to say about who you were?

Read. 2 Timothy 4:6-8.

Close. What do you need to start, stop, or keep doing to get there? Then decide whether to keep going, and set the day and hour.


## Praying for each other

Most men have never prayed out loud for another man, and the first time feels clumsy. Do it anyway. End every meeting with prayer, and keep it short. Here's a simple pattern.

Thank God for one thing in your brother you saw this week. Ask God for the one thing he named in the close. Pray for the people closest to him by name. Then say amen and let him pray for you the same way.

Keep a short list in your phone of what your brother asked for, and pray it once during the week, on a drive or before a meal. Then tell him you did. A man who knows somebody prayed for him by name on a Wednesday will show up the next week.

When God answers, say so out loud. Write it next to the request. Over twelve weeks that list turns into a record of what God did between two men who kept an hour.


## If he's older, or younger

Some pairs are two men of the same age. Many aren't, and that's a gift. An older man and a younger man can do these twenty minutes as brothers, but each brings something different.

If you're the older man, resist the urge to teach. Answer the questions first and answer them honestly, including the parts where you got it wrong. A younger man learns more from your misses than your wins. Ask him real questions about his life and remember the answers. Tell him, at least once in the twelve weeks, plainly and in so many words, what you see in him that's good.

If you're the younger man, don't sit back and wait to be told what to do. Bring your own answers. Ask him the questions you've never had anybody to ask, about money, marriage, work, temper, grief, and getting older. Write down what he says. And thank him, out loud, before the last week.

Either way, you both answer every question. That's what keeps it brotherhood and not a program.
