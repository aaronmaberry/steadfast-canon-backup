# 16. How That Looks on a Man

In the church foyer he has one face. He asks you about the sermon. He crosses the room to the man sitting by himself, and when he shakes his hand, he means it. Home is a different climate. Somewhere between the front door and the kitchen, his voice changes. Same shirt. Same ring. Different weather.

That's how sin looks when a man wears it. It doesn't need a wreck. A clean finish will do the job, and so will being good at things. It dresses us differently than it dresses anyone else. Learn the shapes, or you'll look at the man in the mirror every morning and tell him he's fine. I've worn more than one of them.

## The shapes it takes

These are the ones I see most. Yours is on the list.

The locked drawer. The pull is to keep a second set of books. Money she's never seen. A screen. A friendship under the wrong name. Or a fear he has ruled off-limits. A boy's private corner turns into a man's shut room, and the damage finds him in there.

The fortress. Ask how he's doing, and he says fine. Ask what he needs, and he says nothing. Everybody leans on him, and he leans on nobody. This is pride wearing a work shirt and calling itself strength. The fortress passes itself off as the second mark. It looks like a man accepting responsibility, and that's how it hides. A responsible man can still be helped. The fortress was built to refuse help.

The scoreboard. He quietly ranks every room he walks into by truck, job, wife, kids, body, and pay. He can't be glad when another man wins, and his own life never lets him rest. Saul lived by that scoreboard. When the women sang that Saul had slain his thousands and David his tens of thousands, Saul went after the young man who had just won his war for him.

The podium. This is sin dressed up as righteousness, and Jesus saved some of His hardest words for it. He's generous when people are watching and patient in the church hallway, and by the time his truck is out of the church drive he's a storm. Jesus said men like that have already received their reward in full, paid by people who don't really know them. Nothing else is coming.

The throne. Every conversation in the house has to be won. He'll win it by being loud, or by going silent, or with sarcasm that leaves a welt. He calls it leadership. Ask his wife, and the work she'll describe is managing him. His kids can tell what kind of night it'll be from the sound of his truck in the driveway. Paul fenced in that whole shape with one sentence.

:::margin
Colossians 3:19 (NIV)
"Husbands, love your wives and do not be harsh with them."
:::

Harsh is the word. Paul saw that man coming.

Contempt is the throne with the volume turned down. A sneer. Eyes rolled while his wife tells a story. Disdain for a softer man on Sunday, or for a son in tears. James says praise for God and curses for people made in His likeness shouldn't come out of the same mouth.

The recliner. This is sloth dressed up as rest. On most men it doesn't look lazy. It looks like a man cashing in an evening he thinks he earned. The house pays for it in the rafters.

## The man on the roof

One spring, one man in Scripture wore about half of these shapes at once, and nobody in his generation was a better man. If David could fall, so can you. His way in was passivity.

:::margin
2 Samuel 11:1 (NIV)
"In the spring, at the time when kings go off to war, David sent Joab out with the king's men and the whole Israelite army. They destroyed the Ammonites and besieged Rabbah. But David remained in Jerusalem."
:::

Going out to war was the king's work, and it had always been David's. Joab went. David stayed in the city. Nobody at court would have called it sin. It was a rest, and he'd earned it. Empty hands. An evening walk on the roof. You know what an empty evening can do to a man.

He sees her. He asks about her. The answer comes back that she's married. He sends for her anyway. Lay the four marks over that evening and watch every one of them turn inside out. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Passivity had been running David's life since the army marched out. He handed his responsibilities to other people. The war went to Joab, and his conscience went to his appetite. He used power where courage belonged, the way a coward does when his position hands him power. She had no safe way to refuse a king, and he took her. He was living for the hour in front of him.

Then came the drawer, because she was pregnant. David called her husband home from the front and got him drunk, hoping the child would pass as his. Uriah wouldn't go along. He wouldn't sleep in his own bed while his brothers were still in the field. So David sent him back to the army carrying a sealed order. Put Uriah where the fighting is worst, then pull back from him. Uriah was killed, and his widow became David's wife. As far as the court could see, it was covered.

:::margin
2 Samuel 11:27 (NIV)
"After the time of mourning was over, David had her brought to his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing David had done displeased the LORD."
:::

Israel kept going. The wars kept being won. Almost nobody knew. For most of a year, David ran the kingdom with a murdered man shut away and another man's wife in his house, and he ran it well. Running a kingdom well and being a righteous man turned out to be two different things. In a church, a kingdom, or a home, the work can look sound while the man rots.

Psalm 32 is that year, told from inside his body. His bones wasted away, and God's hand was heavy on him day and night. The lock on a drawer can hold, and what's inside it still does its damage.

## What exposed him

David didn't come clean on his own. No morning came when he decided to confess. God sent Nathan.

:::margin
2 Samuel 12:7 (NIV)
"Then Nathan said to David, 'You are the man! This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you from the hand of Saul.'"
:::

You are the man. Four words. Nathan had told David a story about a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb, and David was furious. Then Nathan turned the story around and pointed it at him. A brother walked into the throne room and named the king's sin to his face. Exposure rarely starts with the man himself. A wife comes to the end of her silence. A brother won't take fine for an answer. A statement shows up in the mail. God's hand gets too heavy to stand under. That's the usual way, and it's the way it went for David.

So you need a Nathan, and you need to be willing to be one. That's part of what brothers are for. Someday somebody may have to say four words to you while you sit there like a king.

Here's the sentence to learn.

:::margin
2 Samuel 12:13 (NIV)
"Then David said to Nathan, 'I have sinned against the LORD.' Nathan replied, 'The LORD has taken away your sin. You are not going to die.'"
:::

I have sinned against the LORD. Six words, and the sentence stops. He didn't blame Bathsheba. He didn't blame Uriah for refusing to go home. He didn't bring up how heavy it is to be king. A confession ends at the period. Forgiveness came in the next breath. The consequences came after, and they were terrible, but his standing with God didn't wait for the consequences to run out. The drawer came open, and he was restored.

## Checking the mirror

Sin lies to the man who's wearing it, so you need some tests.

The drawer. What would shame you if a brother saw it? Your phone, your history, your accounts, your calendar, that friendship. Whatever is in it, the drawer itself is already the problem. When nothing is hidden, you have nothing to defend.

No. When was the last time someone told you no and you took it? If you can't remember, you're sitting on a throne.

Your wife. If you're married, pick a quiet hour and ask her where she sees you hiding and where she sees you being harsh. Don't argue. Listen, and thank her. If you're single, ask the brother who knows you best, or ask your mother.

The podium. What do you only do when people are watching? One man in the church foyer, another in the truck on the way home. One man on Sunday morning, another on a night nobody sees. The gap between them is the performance.

The throne. The question here is fear, not respect. Who in your house is afraid of you? If the answer is any name but nobody, get off the seat. Get on your knees, in front of the people who have had to read your weather.

## The shapes in different houses

If you're single, watch the drawer and the podium first. Your unwatched hours, and the reward of looking solid among church people. Let a brother hold your passwords and keep asking. Get rid of the drawer while there's still not much in it.

A baby in the house puts the throne on notice. Worn-out men get harsh, and when sleep is gone the reasons show up fast. She's more tired than you are. Apologize the same night, every time.

Divorce feeds the scoreboard. Her house against yours. The life she's building against the life you're building. Whose holiday table looks better. Comparing steals your peace a little at a time. Drop the scoreboard. The Father sees what's done in secret, and her column isn't the score He keeps.

Older men should take a hard look at the fortress. The longer the road, the better some men get at needing nothing, and it costs them. At the brothers' table, the expensive question is "What do you need from the rest of us?" Plenty of older men have never been asked it. When one of them finally answers, the table changes.

## The Man with no drawer

Jesus had no drawer. Nothing in Him was kept back from the Father. When they arrested Him, He could say He had always spoken openly and said nothing in secret. They had to bring in false witnesses to build a charge against Him. He was known all the way to the bottom, and He had nothing to be ashamed of.

That's the man being formed in you. The lock comes off. The inside and the outside start to match. Your wife, your brothers, and God can look at you without flinching, because the hidden thing has been named and forgiven.

Let a Nathan see it. Say the six words.

## Before you turn the page

You've probably found your shape by now. David needed four words from another man, and six words back restored his standing in a single breath. May the Father put a man beside you who can say those four words, and give you the plainness to answer, I have sinned, and then stop.
