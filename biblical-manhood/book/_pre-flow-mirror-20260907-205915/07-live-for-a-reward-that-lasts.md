# 7. Live for a Reward That Lasts

*Part 1: The man you were built to be*

Mr. Pruitt planted forty pecan trees the spring after he buried his wife. He was seventy-one.

I helped him set a few of them. Bare-root trees, not much thicker than a broom handle, each one in a hole he'd dug himself with a post-hole digger and a lot of stopping to breathe. When I asked him why forty, he said that's how many the orchard company had on the truck. When I asked him why at all, he stood up straight and looked down the row for a while.

"I won't eat one of these," he said. "You know that. My grandson might. Somebody's grandson will." Then he went back to tamping.

I've thought about that afternoon more than almost any sermon I've heard. Because Mr. Pruitt was doing something that made no sense on any scoreboard he'd live to see. He was spending his strength, which was running out, on a harvest he'd never taste. And he wasn't sad about it. He was, as far as I could tell, the most settled man in the county.

That's the fourth mark. A real man expects the greater reward, God's reward. And I've saved it for last in this first part of the book because it's the one that makes the other three hold. You can reject passivity for a while on willpower. You can carry weight for a while out of pride. You can go first for a while because you like being seen going first. But eventually every one of those runs out of fuel, and the only thing that keeps a man doing the right thing for forty years when nobody's clapping is that he's working for Somebody who is.

## Every man lives for a reward

First, an excuse off the table.

Some men hear "live for a reward" and feel uneasy, like it's beneath them. Shouldn't a good man do good for its own sake? Isn't wanting a reward a little mercenary?

You already live for a reward. Every man does. Nobody gets up at 5 a.m. for nothing. The only question is which one.

Some men live for applause. The crowd, the promotion, the compliment from the boss, the pastor noticing, the text from a buddy that says "you crushed it." It pays out fast and it feels wonderful and it runs out by Wednesday.

Some men live for money. Not because they're greedy, most of them, but because money is the one scoreboard that never lies to you about whether you won. It pays out on a schedule. It also never says "well done." It just says "more."

Some men live for being wanted. By women, mostly. A single man can shape his whole life around the next woman noticing him, and a married man can shape his around the next woman noticing him too, and both of them will tell you it's not about sex, and both of them will be about half right.

Some men live for being right. The argument won. The point proved. The person who doubted them shown up.

Some men live for their father's approval, and they'll keep living for it after the man's been in the ground twenty years. I've watched sixty-year-old men still trying to earn a nod from a grave.

Some men live for comfort. Not a big reward. A small one, paid out daily. The recliner, the routine, the not being bothered.

And some men live for being remembered. The building with their name on it. The town that'll talk about them. The kids who'll tell stories.

Every one of those rewards is real. Every one of them pays out. And every one of them has two problems. It runs out, and it's controlled by someone other than God. The crowd can turn. The money can go. The woman can leave. The father can die without saying it. The town forgets faster than you'd think. A man who lives for any of those has handed the steering wheel of his life to something that doesn't love him.

Jesus didn't tell men to stop living for a reward. He told them to pick a better one.

:::margin
Matthew 6:19-21 (NIV)
"Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moths and vermin destroy, and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where moths and vermin do not destroy, and where thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."
:::

Store up. That's an accumulating word. He's telling you to build a fortune. He's just telling you to build it somewhere the weather can't get to it.

## The Father who sees in secret

The heart of this mark is in one sentence Jesus said three times in a row.

:::margin
Matthew 6:3-4 (NIV)
"But when you give to the needy, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, so that your giving may be in secret. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you."
:::

He says the same thing about prayer two verses later and about fasting a few after that. Do it in secret. Your Father sees. He'll reward you.

It's stranger than it sounds. The most important audience in your life is invisible. He's watching the things nobody else sees. The tithe check nobody knows about. The 2 a.m. feeding. The lust you turned away from in a hotel room in Odessa with nobody there. The kindness to your ex-wife at the exchange that your buddies would've called weakness. The fifteen-year-old you keep showing up for who hasn't said thank you once. Nobody saw. He saw.

And He's not indifferent. He rewards. Most Christian men, if you asked them, would say God forgives, God loves, God is patient. Fewer would say God rewards, and fewer still would say it like they meant it. But the Bible says it flatly, over and over, and the man who doesn't believe it is going to go looking for his reward from an audience that can see him.

:::margin
Colossians 3:23-24 (NIV)
"Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving."
:::

Whatever you do. Not church work. Whatever. The pipeline, the classroom, the truck, the spreadsheet, the diaper. Work at it with all your heart because you're working for Him and He pays.

## Is that mercenary?

Here's why wanting God's reward isn't the kind of wanting you should be ashamed of.

There's a kind of man who marries a woman for her money. We call that mercenary and we're right to. There's another kind of man who marries a woman because he wants her, wants the life with her, wants to wake up next to her for fifty years. Nobody calls him mercenary for wanting his reward. The reward is the thing itself. Wanting it is what love looks like.

The reward God offers isn't a payment separate from Him. It's Him. His "well done." His pleasure. A place at His table. More of Him to enjoy forever. When Jesus tells the story of the servants and their master, the reward for faithfulness isn't a bonus check.

:::margin
Matthew 25:21 (NIV)
"His master replied, 'Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you in charge of many things. Come and share your master's happiness!'"
:::

Come and share your master's happiness. That's the reward. To hear Him say it and then to be let into His joy. Wanting that isn't mercenary. Not wanting it would be strange. A son who does his father's work all day and doesn't want to hear "well done" at the end of it isn't humble. He's either lying or he's given up on the father.

Moses understood this. He had Egypt. He had the palace and the title and everything a man could live for on this side. And Hebrews says he did the math.

:::margin
Hebrews 11:24-26 (NIV)
"By faith Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be known as the son of Pharaoh's daughter. He chose to be mistreated along with the people of God rather than to enjoy the fleeting pleasures of sin. He regarded disgrace for the sake of Christ as of greater value than the treasures of Egypt, because he was looking ahead to his reward."
:::

Looking ahead to his reward. That's not a man who didn't care about rewards. That's a man who cared about them so much he refused to settle for a small one. He traded Egypt for a bigger inheritance, and the Bible calls it faith.

## What the reward does to Tuesday

This mark can sound like theology. It's the most practical thing in the book. Here's what happens to a man who really expects the greater reward.

He can do good nobody sees, and not need to mention it. Which means he can be generous with his money without anyone knowing, patient with his kids when no one's watching, and faithful in a hotel room a thousand miles from anyone who'd find out.

He can lose an argument. If your reward is being right, every argument is a matter of life and death. If your reward is the Father's "well done," you can let your wife have the last word and go to bed at peace, and He counts it.

He can stay. In a hard marriage, in a hard job, in a hard church, in a hard season with a hard father. Men leave when the local scoreboard says they're losing. A man with his eyes on a different scoreboard can stay through a losing decade because he knows the game's not over.

He can take the low place. He can hold the baby while other men watch the game. He can sit with the old man nobody visits. He can coach the team that's going to go 2 and 9. Jesus said the man who takes the low place will be moved up by the host, and He wasn't talking about a dinner party.

He can forgive. Forgiveness is expensive. It means giving up the reward of seeing someone pay. A man who's expecting a greater reward can afford to give that one up, because he's not counting on it for his happiness.

He can plant pecans at seventy-one.

## What it isn't

Some men have been sold a counterfeit.

The greater reward is not a guarantee that this life goes well. It's not health, and it's not wealth, and it's not a bigger truck. Moses, after everything, died on a mountain looking at a land he never entered. Paul, who wrote more about the reward than anybody, wrote a lot of it from a jail cell. Jesus, who lived every second for the joy set before Him, got a cross.

So if you're expecting God's reward to show up as things working out on your schedule, you'll be disappointed, and you'll be tempted to quit. The reward is real and it's coming and some of it you'll taste here. Peace you can't explain. A marriage that heals. A son who comes back. But the bulk of it is stored where the weather can't get to it, and you're going to have to trust Him with the timing.

:::margin
2 Corinthians 4:17-18 (NIV)
"For our light and momentary troubles are achieving for us an eternal glory that far outweighs them all. So we fix our eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen, since what is seen is temporary, but what is unseen is eternal."
:::

Achieving. Your troubles are producing something. They're not wasted. Fix your eyes there.

## In your season

If you're young and single, the reward you're most tempted by is being wanted, and the second is being impressive. Both are loud. Neither lasts. Don't stop wanting a wife or a good career. Check, on a Tuesday, who you're performing for. If you'd stop doing the right thing tomorrow if no woman ever noticed, you're not living for the greater reward yet.

If you've got a baby in the house, nobody's clapping. Nobody's going to clap for a while. Your wife's too tired, your friends don't see it, your own parents think they did it harder. This is the season where the fourth mark stops being an idea. He sees the 3 a.m. He counts it.

If you're divorced, you may be waiting for vindication. For people to finally understand what happened, for her to admit her part, for your kids to see it clearly. That may never come, this side. Let it go as your reward. It's too small for you anyway. The Father who sees in secret knows exactly what happened in that house, and He's the only judge whose verdict will last.

If you're older, watch out for the reward of being remembered. It's a subtle one and it comes for good men late. You'll want your kids to be grateful, the town to know what you built, the church to name a room after you. None of that is wrong to want. All of it is too small to live for. Mr. Pruitt didn't put a sign on the orchard. He didn't need to. He knew who was watching him dig.

## How to expect it

**Think about it on purpose.** Paul said fix your eyes. That takes deciding. Once a day, in the truck or on the porch, tell yourself the truth: what I did today that nobody saw, He saw. Say it until you believe it.

**Do one secret thing a week.** Give something. Fix something. Pray for someone. Serve someone. And don't tell anybody, not even your wife, not even your brother. Let it be between you and the Father. You'll be amazed how it recalibrates you.

**Rehearse the sentence.** Well done, good and faithful servant. Imagine hearing it. Not in a proud way. In the way a boy imagines his dad watching from the stands. Let it pull you.

**Notice when you're performing.** Every time you catch yourself telling a story so someone will think well of you, that's a small reward you just cashed early. Jesus said the men who do that have received their reward in full. Meaning: that's all they get. Don't spend it here.

**Pray with heaven in view.** When you pray, don't only ask for the week to go well. Ask for grace to be faithful whether it does or not. Ask for eyes that see what's unseen. Ask to want the right reward.

## The whole picture

So here's a man with all four marks. He's off the tailgate. He's at the corner post. He's the first door open. And he's doing all of it for a Father who sees in secret and rewards in the open, on a day that hasn't come yet.

That's a steadfast man. Not a man who never fails. A man who knows what he's working for, and who he's working for, and doesn't need the local scoreboard to tell him how he's doing.

Mr. Pruitt's trees are about knee-high on me now. They'll bear in another eight or ten years. He won't be here. Somebody will be, standing under a tree they didn't plant, eating a harvest they didn't earn, and if they think about it at all they'll think about an old man who spent his last strength on people he'd never meet.

He wasn't spending it on them. He was spending it on the One who was watching him dig.

Live for a reward that lasts. Then get back to the work.

---

**Name it.** Which small reward have you been living for most this year: applause, money, being wanted, being right, your father's approval, comfort, or being remembered? Say it plainly.

**Do this week.** Do one good thing in secret. Give, serve, fix, forgive, or pray for somebody, and tell no one. Not your wife. Not your brother. Let it stay between you and the Father.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him which small reward you named, and how it's been steering you. Don't tell him the secret thing. Tell him you did one.

**Carry this.** Every man lives for a reward. The only question is which one.
