# 24. What She Actually Needs

*Part 4: The house*

I've asked a lot of wives the same question over the years, usually on an index card so nobody had to say it out loud. *What do you most need from your husband that you're not getting?*

I have never once gotten a card that said money.

Not one. Not from the wife of a man who'd been laid off, not from the wife of a man who worked two jobs, not from the wife of a man who made more than anybody else in the room. Here's what the cards say instead.

*To know he's not going anywhere.*
*For him to be here when he's here.*
*To not be afraid when he's angry.*
*For him to pray with me. Just once a week. Out loud.*
*For him to want to know me.*
*For him to lead so I don't have to carry all of it.*
*For him to ask me out again.*
*To hear him say it.*

I've shown some of those cards to husbands, without names, and watched their faces. Most of them are working themselves to the bone to provide something their wives never asked for, and starving them of the thing they did.

## More is not the thing

Walt's wife said it in a kitchen: "I don't need more. I need you."

Here's why men get this wrong, and it's not that they don't love their wives. Provision is measurable and presence isn't. A man can see the paycheck. He can see the house, the truck, the college fund, the fixed faucet. Those are things he did, and they count, and Scripture says they count; a man who won't provide has denied the faith. But they're not what she actually needs most, and if he pours himself into them because they're the part he can see, he'll build her a beautiful house and leave her alone in it.

Here's what she actually needs. Not a seminar list. The cards, sorted, with Scripture under them.

## Steadiness

Before anything else, she needs to know you're not going anywhere. Not physically, though that too. She needs to know that the covenant is a wall she can lean her whole weight on and it won't move.

:::margin
Malachi 2:15 (NIV)
"Has not the one God made you? You belong to him in body and spirit. And what does the one God seek? Godly offspring. So be on your guard, and do not be unfaithful to the wife of your youth."
:::

Do not be unfaithful to the wife of your youth. That's not only about adultery, though it includes it. It's about a man whose commitment has no exit clause and who lives like it. A wife can tell when her husband keeps a door cracked, when "if this doesn't work out" is somewhere in the back of his mind, when he'd leave if it got bad enough. She'll never fully rest in a house with a cracked door. Close it. Say it out loud, more than once: "I'm not going anywhere. Ever. Whatever this is, we're doing it together." Then live like a man who meant it.

:::margin
Psalm 15:4 (NIV)
"who despises a vile person but honors those who fear the LORD; who keeps an oath even when it hurts, and does not change their mind;"
:::

Keeps an oath even when it hurts. That's a husband. The oath is going to hurt sometimes. Keep it anyway, and let her watch you keep it.

## Presence

She needs you to be here when you're here. This is the card I get most, and it's the one that's grown fastest in the years I've been asking, because the screens got better.

A man can be in the room and gone. Chapter 9's drift. Chapter 5's Walt. And a wife whose husband is home every night and present none of them is lonelier than a wife whose husband travels, because at least the traveling wife knows why she's alone.

Presence means your attention is in the room. The phone's down. The game's off, or it's on and you're watching it with her, not instead of her. You know what she said five minutes ago. You ask a question and wait for the answer. You're a person she's with, not a body she's near.

## Providing without disappearing

Chapter 5 promised we'd come back to this. This is the place, because it's the trap that catches the best men.

A good man provides. He works hard, he carries the weight, he makes sure the house has what it needs. And somewhere along the way, the work starts asking for more, and it always has a good reason, and the man gives it, because giving to the work feels like giving to the family. Then one day he's Walt. Eleven trucks and a wife who's made a whole life in the spaces he isn't in.

Here's how you know you've started to disappear. She stopped asking when you'll be home, because the answer stopped being reliable. The kids tell her things and she tells you. You find out about your own house secondhand. You've missed the last three things and had a good reason for each one. You're too tired when you get home to be anybody. You've told yourself, more than once, that she wants the lifestyle, and you've never actually asked her.

Ask her. That's the first move. "If you could have less of what I provide and more of me, would you take that trade?" Walt asked. His wife said yes before he finished the sentence. She said she'd rather have had him and a smaller house, and the smaller house would've been fine, and she'd been trying to tell him for twenty years. He sold off part of the business the next year. Not all of it. Enough that he was home for dinner four nights out of five. He told me the money was less and the marriage was more and he'd never once regretted the math.

Most men can't sell off a business. Here's what most men can do.

**Keep the floor.** Chapter 5. A floor is the minimum that doesn't move no matter what the job does. Dinner at the table four nights. Sunday morning. The bedtime prayer with the kids, in person or on the phone from the hotel. The floor tells her the family's the corner post and the work is the wire.

**Tell the truth about the season.** Busy seasons happen. Tax season, harvest, the big project, the deployment. A man who says "the next six weeks are going to be brutal, here's the end date, here's what I'm going to protect, here's what I need from you" has given her something she can hold. A man who just vanishes into the season and expects credit when he comes out has made her carry the not knowing.

**Say when you'll be home and be home then.** Not "I'll try to be home by six." "I'll be home at 6:40." Then be home at 6:40. If it changes, call before it changes, not after. This is a small thing and wives will tell you it's the whole thing. Reliability is presence at a distance.

**Bring your best hours, not your leftovers.** If the job gets the first fourteen hours of you and she gets what's left, she's getting a man who's already spent. Find a way to give her some of your good. A lunch. A morning. The first fifteen minutes when you're still a person.

**If you travel, build a road practice.** The bedtime call, every night, no matter the time zone. Knowing what's on the calendar at home on Tuesday so you can ask about it Tuesday night. A note in her drawer before you leave. Men who travel for work can be more present than men who don't, if they decide to.

**Watch the scoreboard.** Work claps. Home mostly doesn't. If you notice you're drifting toward the place that claps, you've noticed the whole problem. Chapter 7. You're not working for the clapping. You're working for a Father who sees the dinner table.

## Safety

She needs to not be afraid of you.

Not fists. Most of you would never. I mean the voice that changes pitch. The silence that lasts three days. The sarcasm that draws blood. The door that gets slammed, the truck that leaves fast, the thing you said that you didn't mean and she's still carrying. A wife who's learned to read her husband's weather before she speaks isn't safe in her own house, and she can't be fully herself with a man she has to manage.

:::margin
Proverbs 15:1 (NIV)
"A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger."
:::

A gentle answer. That's the instrument. Not a weak one; a gentle one, from a man strong enough not to need volume. Chapter 9 gave you the ninety seconds and the truck and the true thing under the anger. Use them. A wife who knows her husband's anger will never land on her has a kind of freedom that most wives never get.

## To be pursued, still

The man who asked her out should still be asking her out.

Something happens to men after the wedding. The pursuit that got her stops, because the pursuit was for getting her, and now he's got her, and he turns his attention to the next thing to get. She feels it as being a thing acquired and shelved. And she'll rarely say it, because it sounds needy, and she doesn't want to be needy.

:::margin
Proverbs 5:18 (NIV)
"May your fountain be blessed, and may you rejoice in the wife of your youth."
:::

Rejoice in the wife of your youth. Not tolerate. Not manage. Rejoice. The blueprint had a man moved to poetry by his bride, and I don't care if you can't write poetry. You can plan a Thursday. You can ask her out with a specific evening and a plan and a shirt you ironed. You can look at her across a room the way you did the first year and let her catch you doing it. Pursuit isn't a stage you pass through. It's how a husband tells his wife, every year, that he'd choose her again.

## Leadership she can rest in

This card comes from tired women. *For him to lead so I don't have to carry all of it.*

She's not asking to be ruled. She's asking to not be the only adult in the house. She's asking for a man who'll say "here's where we're going, here's how we'll get there," so that she can stop holding the whole map. A woman married to a man who won't lead ends up leading by default, and she's exhausted, and she's resentful, and she's not sure she's allowed to say so, because he's a nice guy who lets her have her way.

Leading her means direction. It means going first into the hard decision and owning the outcome. It means she can bring you a problem and it becomes your problem too, not a thing you'll get to. And it means spiritual leadership, which most Christian husbands treat like a nice idea and most Christian wives would trade almost anything for. The card said "pray with me, just once a week, out loud." Once a week. Out loud. That's the ask. Chapter 6 gave you the eleven-second prayer. Say it with her, Sunday night, holding her hand. You will be amazed at what it does to a woman to hear her husband talk to God about her.

## To be heard and known

Chapter 22 owns the study. She needs a man who studies her and lets her know she's known. The card says "for him to want to know me." Not know me. *Want* to.

## Words

She needs to hear it. Men think it's understood. It's not understood. It's needed, out loud, in specific words, regularly.

:::margin
Proverbs 31:28-29 (NIV)
"Her children arise and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praises her: 'Many women do noble things, but you surpass them all.'"
:::

Her husband also, and he praises her. In front of the kids. With specifics. "You surpass them all" is not "you're a good wife." It's a man telling his wife she's the best one there is, and meaning it, and saying it where people can hear. What you call her, she'll become, we said in chapter 18. Call her the best thing that ever happened to you. Out loud. This week.

## Help that isn't "helping"

She needs the load shared, and she needs you to stop calling it helping. It's not her house that you help with. It's your house. The dishes are your dishes. The kids' schedules are your schedules. A husband who "helps out" has told his wife that the whole thing is hers and he's a volunteer. A husband who owns the house with her has told her she's not alone in it. Eve was called a helper, and it's a title of strength. Christ came to serve. Both are true, and neither one lets a man sit while his wife runs the house around him.

## Tenderness

She needs to be touched without it being about the bedroom. A hand on her back in the kitchen. Her feet, after a long day. Holding her when she cries without trying to stop the crying. Most men touch their wives for exactly two reasons: to get past them in a hallway and to initiate. She needs a third kind, and she needs it more than the second, and if she gets the third, the second will go better than it ever has.

## A man who's growing

The last card, the one men don't expect: she needs to see you moving. Toward God, toward being a better man, toward the four marks. A husband who's stuck, who's the same man at forty-five he was at thirty with more weight and less patience, is a weight she carries. A husband who's growing, even slowly, even with failures, is a man she can follow, because she can see where he's headed. Your reading Mark in the truck is for her too. Your twenty minutes with a brother is for her too. A wife married to a man who's becoming something has hope in the house.

## In her seasons

**Under a newborn**, she needs sleep more than anything you can buy, and you can give her that. She needs food she didn't make. She needs one adult conversation a day with someone who's not asking her for milk. She needs to be told she's doing well, specifically, because she's sure she isn't. She needs to not be alone with it: you in the room at 3 a.m. even when you can't feed him. And she needs to be touched without agenda by a man who's not keeping score of how long it's been.

**After forty years**, she needs to be seen as a woman and not as a fixture or a caretaker. She needs plans for the years that are left, made together. She needs you to handle your health, because her greatest fear may be finding you on the floor, and every appointment you skip is a weight on her. She needs gentleness as her body changes, and a husband whose eyes haven't wandered to younger women on a screen while hers aged. She needs your presence now more than ever, because the kids are gone and the work is gone and it's just you, and if you're in the recliner, she's alone in the house you built. Roy said his wife told him, after the coffee and the porch and the ocean trip, "This is what I wanted the whole time. I just wanted you to want to be here."

**If you're single again**, take an honest look at the cards and at what she needed and didn't get. Not to bury yourself. To learn, so that whatever comes next, you're not the same man. And know that your kids' mother needs some of this from you still: reliability, civility, and your support of her authority with the children. A father who undermines the mother across two houses is hurting his kids to win a point. Back her up in front of them, even when you'd have done it differently.

**If you're single**, this whole chapter is a blueprint of the man you're building. Every card is something she'll need on year one, and the man who's practiced steadiness, presence, gentleness, pursuit, leadership, words, shared work, tenderness, and growth before he meets her will hand her a husband instead of a project. And your mother needs a few of these from you now, as a grown son. Presence. Words. A call that's about her.

## What He gives His bride

Everything on the cards, Christ gives the church.

Steadiness: never will I leave you. Presence: surely I am with you always. Safety: gentle and humble in heart. Pursuit: He came looking for us. Leadership: follow me. Known: I know my sheep. Words: this is my body, given for you. Help: He washed their feet. Tenderness: He wept with the grieving. A man who's growing: He grew in wisdom and stature and favor, so that even the Son of God showed His people what it looks like to move.

She's not asking for anything He doesn't give. She's asking you to give her, in a house, on a Tuesday, a small picture of what He gives you. That's the job. It doesn't cost money. It costs you, which is the only thing she ever wanted.

---

**Name it.** Which card would your wife write? Don't guess. Say the one you're most afraid it would be. If you're single or single again, say which one you'd have been weakest on.

**Do this week.** Ask her, plainly: "What do you most need from me that you're not getting?" Then listen to the whole answer without defending yourself, and do the first thing she says before Tuesday. If you're single, pick one card and practice it on the people in your life this week.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him what she said. Ask him whether he's ever asked his wife. Then ask each other the "providing without disappearing" question: where have you drifted toward the place that claps?

**Carry this.** She doesn't need more. She needs you. Provision is measurable and presence isn't, and that's exactly why men get it backward.
