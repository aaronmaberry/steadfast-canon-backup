# 6. Go First

*Part 1: The man you were built to be*

A pickup rolled on Highway 287 north of Wichita Falls a few years back, in the kind of rain that comes sideways. I was maybe six cars behind. And what I remember is the slowing. Every car slowed down. Every driver looked. Brake lights all the way back. You could feel forty people thinking the same thought at the same time: somebody ought to stop.

One man did. He was already out of his truck and running before I'd finished having the thought, a big guy in a feed store cap, and the second his door opened, three more doors opened. Then six. Inside of a minute there were a dozen men in the ditch, and the one in the cap wasn't telling anybody what to do. He didn't have to. He'd gone first, and going first was the whole job.

That's what leadership is. I've read the books with the pyramids and the principles. For a man on a Tuesday it comes down to this: somebody has to go first. Into the ditch, into the apology, into the prayer, into the truth. A real man leads courageously, and leading courageously is mostly the willingness to be the first door open.

## Not in charge. First.

A lot of men hear "lead" and picture a man at the head of a table telling other people what to do. Some men want that picture and some are repelled by it, and both of them have missed the point.

Leadership in the Bible is almost never about the head of the table. When Jesus wanted to teach His men what leading looked like, He got up from the head of the table, took off His outer clothes, and washed their feet. Then He told them plainly what He'd just done.

:::margin
John 13:14-15 (NIV)
"Now that I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also should wash one another's feet. I have set you an example that you should do as I have done for you."
:::

I have set you an example. That's the leadership sentence. Not "I have given you orders." He went first into the lowest job in the room, and the going first was the leading.

Gideon, before his three hundred men went down against an army, didn't give a speech. He said something a lot shorter.

:::margin
Judges 7:17 (NIV)
"'Watch me,' he told them. 'Follow my lead. When I get to the edge of the camp, do exactly as I do.'"
:::

Watch me. Follow my lead. Do exactly as I do. If you can't say that to your sons about how you treat their mother, or to the younger men at church about how you handle your money, or to your wife about how you handle your temper, you're not leading them, no matter what your title is. You might be managing them. That's different.

So when I say a man leads, I don't mean he's in charge of everybody. I mean that in every room he's in, he's willing to be the first one to do the right thing. And the reason that takes courage is that first is exposed. The first man in the ditch might be wrong about what he finds there. The first man to apologize might not get one back. The first man to pray out loud at a table full of relatives who don't will feel every eye. First is where a man can look foolish, and looking foolish is what most of us fear more than we fear almost anything.

## The places a man has to go first

**The apology.** Every marriage has a standoff in it somewhere. Three days of politeness. Everybody knows what happened; nobody's going to say it. Somebody has to go first, and it has to be you. Not because it was all your fault. Because you're the man, and going first is what a man is for. "I was wrong about how I spoke to you Tuesday. I'm sorry." Then stop. You'll want to add "and I know you also," and you must not. A man who apologizes first and cleanly changes the weather in a house.

**The prayer.** I've known men who've been Christians for thirty years and have never once prayed out loud in front of their own wife. They'll pray at church if called on. They'll pray with the men's group. But at their own table, in their own bedroom, with the person who knows them best, they go quiet, and their reason is always the same: it feels awkward. Of course it does. It's supposed to feel awkward the first time. Go first anyway. "Lord, thank you for this food and for this woman. Help us tonight. Amen." That's a whole prayer. It'll take you eleven seconds, and it will do more for your leadership at home than a year of good intentions.

**The truth.** Every family, every crew, every church has a thing nobody's saying. The money's not working. Grandpa's drinking again. The youth pastor hasn't shown up in three weeks. Somebody has to say it first, in the room, kindly and clearly. Nehemiah came to a city that had been sitting in rubble for decades, everybody stepping around the broken walls, and the first thing he did was name it out loud.

:::margin
Nehemiah 2:17 (NIV)
"Then I said to them, 'You see the trouble we are in: Jerusalem lies in ruins, and its gates have been burned with fire. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, and we will no longer be in disgrace.'"
:::

You see the trouble we are in. They did see it. They'd been seeing it for years. What they needed was one man to say it and then say "come." That's going first.

**The turn.** In a marriage, somebody has to turn first. Toward her, when you've both been facing your phones. Toward the hard conversation about the kids. Toward the bedroom, when it's been a long time and neither of you knows how to bring it up. Wives should not have to ask for their husband's attention. If she's asking, you've already made her go first, and that's a weight she shouldn't carry.

**The direction.** A house needs to know where it's going. Not a five-year plan with a logo. A man who can say, "This year we're going to be a family that eats together four nights a week, and here's how," or "We're going to get out of debt, and here's the first step," or "We're going to start going to church again, and I'm driving." Direction is a man saying "come" and then walking. If you're single, you still set direction, for your own life and for the people who orbit it. Your roommates, your brothers, the younger guys at work who watch how you live. A single man who knows where he's going and says so is a leader whether or not anybody's ever handed him a title.

## What we're actually afraid of

First is exposed. Name what the exposure feels like. A fear you can name is a fear you can walk through.

We're afraid of looking foolish. The prayer will sound clumsy. The apology will come out wrong. The truth will land badly and everybody will look at us.

We're afraid of rejection. She won't apologize back. The younger man won't want a mentor. The family will go quiet and change the subject.

We're afraid of being wrong. What if the thing nobody's saying is actually fine and I'm the one who's off?

We're afraid of what it will cost. If I go first on the truth about the money, I'm the one who has to do something about it.

Every one of those fears is reasonable. Every one of them has come true for me at some point. And none of them is an excuse. Paul told a young man who was scared of exactly these things what the Spirit inside him was actually like.

:::margin
2 Timothy 1:7 (NIV)
"For the Spirit God gave us does not make us timid, but gives us power, love and self-discipline."
:::

Not timid. Not "not afraid." God doesn't promise the fear goes away. He says the Spirit He gave you isn't the timid kind. The fear is in you; so is something stronger. Courage is what happens when you let the stronger thing move your feet.

Here's the part that makes this possible at all. You are never actually first.

:::margin
Deuteronomy 31:8 (NIV)
"The LORD himself goes before you and will be with you; he will never leave you nor forsake you. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged."
:::

The LORD himself goes before you. Every time you're the first door open, you're the second, because He's already in the ditch. A man who goes first isn't being brave on his own account. He's following Somebody who went first into everything, including death, and came back out. Which means the worst thing that can happen to you for going first has already happened to Him, and He's fine.

## What this looks like in your season

If you're single and there's a woman you're interested in, go first. Ask her plainly. Not a hangout. Not a "we should get coffee sometime." A specific day, a specific place, a clear intention. And if it's been three months and you know what this is, be the first to say so. Men who make women guess aren't being careful. They're being cowards with good manners. She deserves a man who goes first into the awkward conversation about what you are.

If you're a new father, go first at 2 a.m. Don't wait to be asked. Don't lie there hoping she'll get up because she's the one who can feed him. Get up. Change him. Bring him to her. Take him back. Your wife is learning in these months whether she has a partner or a second child, and she's learning it in the dark, and nobody's watching but God.

If you're divorced, going first is going to cost you more than it costs most men, because there's history in every direction. Go first anyway. First to keep the tone civil at the exchange. First to tell your kids the truth in a way that doesn't make them carry your anger. First to show up to the school thing even though she'll be there. Kenny told me the hardest sentence he ever said was to his ex-wife at a soccer field: "You've done a good job with them." It wasn't strategy. It was going first. He said it changed the next two years.

If you're an older man, going first means you initiate with younger men, not the other way around. They're not going to ask. They think you're busy, or they think you'd say no, or they've never seen it done. Roy started sitting at a different table at the Thursday breakfast, one with two men in their thirties who looked like they'd wandered in by accident. He asked them about their work. Then he asked them about their wives. Then he asked if they'd like to keep doing this. That's the whole method. Watch me. Follow my lead.

## How to go first

**Prepare the first sentence.** Not the whole conversation. Just the sentence that opens the door. "I need to tell you something and I'm nervous about it." "I was wrong about Tuesday." "Can I pray for us before we eat?" A man who has the first sentence ready will say it. A man waiting for the whole speech to form never will.

**Go first in small rooms before big ones.** Pray at your own table before you pray at the church picnic. Tell the truth to your wife before you tell it to the elders. Apologize to your kid before you apologize to your boss. Courage grows in small rooms.

**Lead with a question when you can.** "What do you think we should do about this?" is often stronger than an answer, because it goes first into the conversation without pretending you have it all figured out. Men think leadership means having the answer. Mostly it means being willing to start.

**Expect nobody to follow right away.** The man in the feed store cap was alone in the rain for about eight seconds. That's a long time when you're the only one out of your truck. Go anyway. The doors open after the first one, not before.

**Do it on Tuesday.** Not at the retreat. Not at the New Year. Going first is a Tuesday act, done at a kitchen table, in a truck cab, at a soccer field, with no music playing. The rooms where it feels least dramatic are the rooms where it counts most.

## The Man who went first

Hebrews gives Jesus a title I didn't notice for years.

:::margin
Hebrews 12:2 (NIV)
"fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith. For the joy set before him he endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God."
:::

Pioneer. The one who goes first into the country nobody's been to and makes a way for the rest. He went first into the garden of Gethsemane and the ones He brought fell asleep. He went first into the trial and the ones who'd promised to die with Him ran. He went first into death, alone, and that was the point.

And look at what carried Him. For the joy set before Him. The fourth mark, again. He could go first into the worst thing because He was living for a reward on the other side of it. So can you. The awkward prayer, the clean apology, the truth said out loud in the room, the 2 a.m. feeding, the sentence at the soccer field: none of it is wasted on the Father who watched His Son go first and rewarded Him with a throne.

Somebody has to be the first door open.

---

**Name it.** Where in your life is everybody slowing down and looking, and nobody's stopping? Say the room: "The place I need to go first is ___."

**Do this week.** Pray out loud with your wife, your kids, or your roommate one time before Tuesday. If you already do that, make the apology or say the true thing you've been waiting on someone else to say.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him the first sentence you're planning to say, and to whom. Then tell him afterward how it went, even if it went badly.

**Carry this.** The LORD himself goes before you. You're never actually first. You're the second door open.
