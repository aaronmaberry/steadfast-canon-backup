## 3. Bounce digs, mapped to changes

**Soften and signpost season tours; stop the every-chapter age stamp.**
Chapter 3 carries one signpost paragraph ("A word about seasons") that tells the reader seasons will be handled inside the prose where the counsel changes and gathered in chapter 32. No chapter from 4 through 31 runs a full age tour. Where a season changes the counsel, the chapter turns to that man inside a paragraph or a short "In your season" section that addresses only the seasons where the counsel actually differs. Chapter 32 is the one deliberate tour. The introduction proposal (`proposals/00-introduction.md`, insertion A) offers a parallel signpost; if Aaron accepts it, trim the chapter 3 paragraph to one sentence to avoid a double.

**Thicken the single lane, especially after chapter 21.**
Chapter 21 is rebuilt around three single men (never married, single again by divorce, single again by death), a six-item list of what a single man builds, dating counsel, a single-again section, a widower section, and an explicit case for why a single man should read chapters 22 through 29. Every chapter from 22 through 29 then carries a single-man paragraph or section with a concrete practice (know women as sisters, practice laying down preferences, the covenant before the bed, the single man as safe father-figure in the light, naming and hilling fatherless boys, standing at another father's ceremony). Chapters 30, 32, 33, 34, and 35 keep the single man in the room.

**Infant and first-year marriage under baby load.**
Tomás (pipeline crew, newborn daughter) is a recurring composite. Chapter 5 (the twenty minutes after boots off), chapter 6 (2 a.m. as going first), chapter 9 (anger under exhaustion, the "I'm so tired I'm scared of myself" sentence), chapter 22 (knowing her postpartum, including the depression watch), chapter 23 (the newborn laying-down list), chapter 24 (what she needs under a newborn), chapter 25 (the bed after a baby), chapter 31 (no marriage decisions at 3 a.m.), chapter 32 (a full section on the first year of marriage and the first year with a baby as a season inside a season), chapter 33 (Tomás's scaled Tuesday rhythm), chapter 34 (a three-line plan for the new father).

Chapter 3 also states honestly that the locked sentence was said by men before Wes; no claim of originality is made for the sentence itself. Chapter 19 gives the marks their theological ground as the portrait of Christ in Gethsemane, which no other shelf book does. Original pictures throughout: tailgate, corner post, gun safe in a hallway, stock tank in March, pecan trees, plumb bob, smoke detector, foundation slab at 4 a.m., caprock and calf, wastewater plant. No Wild at Heart phrasing, no "battle/adventure/beauty" line, no "do I have what it takes," no knight ceremony, no "will/work/woman" alliteration. Verified by search.

**Kill review-draft stamp language.**

## 4. Extras (judged improvements, documented)

- **Plumb line as the book's controlling image.** Introduced in chapter 17 (Amos 7:7-8), given as the ceremony token in chapter 29 with an original gift speech, returned in chapters 33 and 35. Ties to the navy/gold/plumb cover direction without any art in the repo.
- **Recurring composites across the whole book** so the reader meets the same men in different chapters and seasons: Roy, Tomás, Kenny, Micah, Walt, Anthony, Cole, Hollis, Mr. Pruitt, Mr. Alvarez, Dale, Buck, Ed, the wastewater operator, the young man with four jobs. Chapter 3 states plainly that they are composites.
- **Every chapter ends at Jesus.** Each chapter's last movement lands on Christ as the pattern (pioneer, the Man with no drawer, the last Adam, the worker, the one who read every gauge), so the four marks never float free of the gospel.
- **The gospel stated plainly** in chapter 15 (Romans 5:8) and chapter 19 (in Adam / in Christ, with a direct invitation), so a non-believing reader is not left with a self-improvement list.
- **Crisis handling.** Chapter 31 names suicidal thinking plainly, models Elijah's rescue (food, sleep, presence, a word, work, a friend), tells the reader to see a doctor and take medication if prescribed, and gives the 988 line. Production must verify the number for the publishing region.
- **Postpartum depression watch** in chapter 22, written so a young husband knows the signs and acts.
- **Daughters not forgotten** in the son chapters: chapter 28 gives daughters hills; chapter 29 gives daughters a day.
- **The one-page plan in chapter 34** pulls every earlier closer into a single surface, so the closers accumulate into something rather than evaporating.
- **Honest attribution.** Chapter 3 says of the locked sentence, "I didn't invent it. Men said it before me." This protects the book without naming or reprinting any other author.
