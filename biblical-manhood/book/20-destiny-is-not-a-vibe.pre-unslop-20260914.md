# Destiny Is Not a Vibe

The slip was already filled in. Warehouse 2. Dock C. A crate of fittings tagged for a shop across the river. The young man holding it wanted a life that felt like a calling, so he set the paper on the pallet and looked past the truck for a sign. The fittings did not care. They were going to Dock C whether he felt like a man of destiny or not.

An older hand put the paper back in his fingers.

"That is the job," he said. "Not the one you're waiting to feel."

Destiny is not a vibe. It isn't a feeling of significance. It isn't a sense that you're meant for something large that hasn't shown up yet. It's an assignment, given by God, carried out in a place, in a generation, on ordinary days, and finished. The man on the dock has one. You have one too. Most of us have been walking past it, waiting to feel chosen.

## The verse that ends the argument

There's a sentence in Acts that I think is the best one-line definition of a man's destiny. Paul is walking through Israel's history, and when he gets to David he says this.

:::margin
Acts 13:36 (NIV)
"Now when David had served God's purpose in his own generation, he fell asleep; he was buried with his ancestors and his body decayed."
:::

He served God's purpose in his own generation. That's the whole thing. Not discovered his passion. Not found what he was born to do. He served God's purpose among the people and in the place and time God had put him. Then he died and his body decayed, which Paul includes so you'll understand that even David's destiny wasn't about David lasting. It was about the purpose lasting.

Notice the word served. Destiny is a service word. A man handed a job by Someone above him, and doing it. And notice it's God's purpose, not David's. David had plenty of purposes of his own, and the ones that came from him nearly wrecked everything. The one that came from God is the one Paul is still talking about.

That is your destiny too. Whether it feels like anything is beside the point.

## The man who picked the slip back up

The young man on the dock stayed. Not because the warehouse felt like a calling. It didn't. He stayed because that older hand told him to stay three years no matter what and see what God did with a man who stopped leaving. He's been there longer than that now. He runs a crew. Two of those men have started coming to church because of how he treats them. He still says he doesn't have a calling. He has a purpose. It turned out to be wherever he stopped setting the paper down.

The assignment was never hiding. It was printed. He had to pick it up.

## Three lies, then three truths

You can't put down a lie you haven't looked at.

The first lie is that you'll know it when you feel it. Somewhere out there is a life that will finally feel right, and the not-feeling-right is proof you haven't found it yet. A feeling is a weather report, not a commission. Moses argued with God. Jeremiah said he was too young. Gideon asked for signs three times. Jonah paid for a boat going the other way. If feelings were the test, none of them would have gone. The feeling, when it comes, comes after the going, and sometimes it never comes at all, and the work counts anyway.

The second lie is that it's big. A real destiny, you're told, has a platform and a story people tell. So a man who has spent years welding, raising children, and showing up for his neighbors concludes he missed it. Jesus praised a servant for being faithful with a few things, only a few. Who dares despise the day of small things. God's eyes range over the whole earth, and they rejoice at a man laying a stone. The small day isn't the waiting room for destiny. It's where destiny happens.

The third lie is that it's somewhere else: the next job, the next town, after a wedding, after the kids are grown, after you retire. Destiny stays one move away, and the move keeps moving. God placed the man. He put him in a specific place and told him to work that one. Paul told the Athenians that God marked out appointed times and the boundaries of their lands. You aren't in your town and your century by accident. The purpose is here, and it won't be more here somewhere else.

Now the other side. Destiny is assigned, not discovered. You don't find it the way you'd find a good fishing hole. It was prepared before you arrived.

:::margin
Ephesians 2:10 (NIV)
"For we are God's handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do."
:::

The works are already laid out. Your job isn't to invent them. It's to do the next one. That frees you from the terror of missing it. A man hunting his one true purpose lives in fear he'll choose wrong. A man who knows the works were prepared in advance gets up and does what's in front of him. He isn't hunting a vibe. He's taking the slip.

Second, it's local. Your purpose has an address: a wife's name, children's names, a street, a crew, a church, the men who already know your face. God's purpose for David was Israel, not the whole world. Esther's cousin told her the warning that still holds: if you stay silent, relief will come from another place, and you will have missed your part. For such a time as this. Not some other time. Not a bigger seat. This one.

Third, destiny is finished by faithfulness, not by feeling. It's a work you complete, not a state you achieve.

:::margin
John 4:34 (NIV)
"'My food,' said Jesus, 'is to do the will of him who sent me and to finish his work.'"
:::

To finish his work. Not to feel fulfilled by it. Paul didn't say he found his passion. He said he'd fought the fight, finished the race, kept the faith. Completion words, said from a cell, in places he didn't choose.

Assigned. Local. Finished by showing up. Every day of it looks ordinary. At the end there's a "well done" from the only One whose opinion of your life will last.

You take it by looking at what God has already put in your hands. Look at the first shape: work a place and keep it, carry a word, speak, don't be alone, love a bride if you have one, rest. Look at your place. Town, job, street, church. If God moves you, He'll move you. Look at your people. Those names are your destiny more than any title. Look at your hands. Use the gift you've got, not the one you wish you had. And look at this stretch, not the one you've left and not the one you're waiting for.

Do those things and one day, probably not a dramatic one, you'll look up and realize you've been serving God's purpose in your generation for years, with nothing but your hands.

## This stretch

If you're single and waiting for a wife or a better title to deliver the assignment, hear the man with the slip. Your destiny isn't on the far side of a wedding or a promotion. It's the next assigned thing. Stay and work it.

If there's a baby in your house, your destiny currently weighs about twelve pounds and doesn't sleep. There's no larger assignment God will ever give you than the one crying in the next room, and the fact that it doesn't feel like a calling is the point.

If you are in the divorced seat, you may think the story is over, or at least the good part of it, and it isn't. Joseph's destiny ran straight through a prison. Peter's ran straight through the worst failure of his life, and Jesus told him what would come after.

:::margin
Luke 22:32 (NIV)
"But I have prayed for you, Simon, that your faith may not fail. And when you have turned back, strengthen your brothers."
:::

When you have turned back, strengthen your brothers. Peter's failure didn't end his purpose. It became part of the equipment for it. The men who will listen now, some of them, are the ones who won't talk to anybody else. That's a man serving God's purpose in his generation with the very thing he thought had disqualified him.

And if you're older, "his own generation" is still yours. This one. The purpose isn't behind you. They will still bear fruit in old age. A breakfast table. A class. A word by a truck. None of that is a vibe.

Everybody has a name his story gave him. The guy whose dad left. The guy who blew up his marriage. The guy who never got the promotion. Those names are real. They aren't your name. They're the first man's names: naked, afraid, hiding, blaming. The master story gives you another: made in the image, placed, given a work, son of the Father who does not leave, in Christ, servant of God's purpose in your generation.

:::margin
Revelation 2:17 (NIV)
"Whoever has ears, let them hear what the Spirit says to the churches. To the one who is victorious, I will give some of the hidden manna. I will also give that person a white stone with a new name written on it, known only to the one who receives it."
:::

God has a name for you that nobody else knows yet. One day He'll hand it to you on a stone, and everything your story called you will be gone.

Most of your destiny gets carried out inside a house. That's where we go soon: the wife, the kids, the bed, the table. Rooms with your family's names on them.

Destiny is not a vibe. It's a man doing the next thing God put in his hands, in the place God put him, for the people God gave him, until it's finished. Go do the next thing.

## Before you turn

If you've been waiting for a feeling, stop hunting the lightning. Look at your place, your people, your hands, and this stretch. That's where the assignment has been. May the Father who prepared your good works in advance show you the next one this week, and may you do it without a vibe, and find that it counted.
