# 12. Leave without slamming the door

A man can refuse to go. A man can also go so hard the glass breaks.

Both look like a life. One stays in a house that is no longer his job to live in. The other leaves a crater and calls the crater proof. Neither is the leaving the Book is after. Leave. Honor them. Do not slam the door to prove you are a man.

I have done both. I have stayed because leaving felt like betrayal. I have also left with a speech in my mouth that was really a score. The speech felt like a spine. It was a boy with a louder door. A slammed door still has you in it. You can hear it from the street.

This page is the leaving. Not the tie. Not a full marriage chapter. A wife can be in the picture. She is not the whole of it. Single men leave too. You do not wait on a wedding to belong to your own life.

That is why a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh. That's Genesis 2:24.

:::margin
**Genesis 2:24 NIV**
That is why a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh.
:::

Leave is the first verb. United is a real second. If you skip leave, the union will have extra people in the union who were never invited. If you slam the door on your way to the union, you will carry broken glass into it. The verse is not a permission slip to be cruel. It is a man going, with honor, into a life that is his.

Leaving is not a city. You can move two states over and still be a son who needs a yes from the old house. You can live three miles away and have actually left. Miles are not the test. Who runs your life is the test. If their weather still sets your week, you have not left. If you cannot say a kind sentence about them without a hook, you have not left either. You have only changed the volume.

Honor is not asking permission for a life God already called you into. Honor is telling the truth without a trial. Honor is a call that is not a performance. Honor is money you do not use as a leash, and money you do not withhold as a punishment. Honor is showing up when it costs, and not showing up when showing up would put you back in a role that is not yours.

I have confused honor with access. I thought if they could still reach me at every hour, I was a good son. Access is not honor. I have also confused leaving with a coldness I could brag about. Coldness is not leaving. Coldness is a slammed door with better manners.

The man who will not go keeps explaining. He has a reason every month. Next year. After this season. After they are less fragile. The calendar does not hand you off. You go, or you do not.

The man who breaks the glass wants a scene that matches his pain. He drafts the message. He tells three friends first. He wants them to feel the years. They might. They will also remember the shatter longer than the years. You do not owe them a fake peace. You also do not owe the room a demolition.

Some of you will not go because you are afraid they will not survive it. Hear me. You are not their husband. You are not their pastor. You are not the extra adult they never got. You can love them and still go. Staying as the extra adult in a house that already had its years is not kindness. It is a refusal to become a man, and it will not save them.

Some of you will go like a storm because you are angry, and the anger is not nothing. You were held too long, or used, or compared, or never blessed. Leaving as revenge will not heal that. It will give you a story you can tell, and a relationship you cannot return to when you are ready to be a son who is also a man. Kind is not the same as staying. Kind is also not a wrecking ball.

If you're young, leaving might be the apartment, the church, the job you already know is yours. It might be one conversation where you do not ask them to vote. You can do that at 22 without a speech about how they failed you. Go. Call on Sunday. Do not make the call a report card they have to grade.

If you're single, this verse still belongs to you. Leave is not a married man's verb. United to a wife is a gift, later, if God gives it. Leave is now. A future marriage will not extract you. If you cannot leave with honor as a single man, you will not suddenly know how when someone says yes.

If you're married, you leave toward her. That does not make her the enemy of your parents, and it does not make them her project. You are the man in the middle who has to actually leave. If you have not, she is living with extra people. If you slammed the door, she is living with the glass. Honor them. Belong to her. Those can sit in the same week.

If you got hurt on the way here, I will not tell you to pretend the house was gentle. Some doors should not be stood in front of every holiday like a test of your manhood. Distance can be wisdom. Distance with a curse in your mouth is still a slam. You can put miles between you and still send a true, short, clean sentence. You can also refuse a room that would undo you, without a manifesto. Leaving is not revenge. Revenge is how the old house keeps you.

If you're older, you can still leave a pattern. A 62-year-old can still be waiting for a blessing that is not coming, or still punishing a slight from 1988. You can still make the call. You can still stop making the call that is really a leash. You do not age out of this. The younger men need to see a man go without making a scene, and stay gone without making a religion of it.

If the job is going well, moving for work is not the same as leaving. The company will relocate you. It will not cut the cord. You can be on a stage in another city and still need the old house to tell you you did well. A title is not a departure. Name how you leave, or how you refuse to. Then do one leave-with-honor move this week that has nothing to do with a boarding pass.

Name how you leave. Or how you will not. Then take one move this week that is actually leaving, with honor. A conversation without a speech. A no that is calm. A yes to your own house that does not require their applause. A 22-year-old can do that. A 62-year-old can do that.

Tell one man which way you lean: stuck, or slamming. Tell him the move. If he only hears a rant about your parents, you have not left. If he hears a man going, and honoring, he can ask you whether you went back through the glass. Let him ask.

Leave. Honor them. Do not slam the door to prove you are a man. The glass is loud. It is not a spine.

Name it. Do you refuse to go, or do you go so hard the glass breaks?

Do this week. One leave-with-honor move. No speech. No crater.
