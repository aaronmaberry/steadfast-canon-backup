Divorced. Still in church. Buyer read of FLOW rebind PDF (`steadfast-men-ebook.pdf`, ~350 pages). Wound chapters were my test again. Bounce only. No rewrite. No Aaron.

Focus: flow vs jabby · my season · closers do+why · gift-readable · buy/pass.

---

## Flow vs jabby

It flows.

The old book jabbed. Short punches. Drill energy. I could respect the points and still feel talked at. This one sits across a table. Paragraphs breathe. Stories land, then the truth, then a next move. Anthony in the doorway with Christmas lights on a chair in the garage — that is a real chapter, not a manifesto.

I still hear a firm line when it needs to land ("The chair explains you. It doesn't excuse you."). Firm is not jabby. Fatherly holds. I stayed with Part 2 without that old flinch of being scolded into manhood.

Part 3 has more bone than last time. Part 5 still earns the close. Length jumped hard (~189 to ~350). For me that is fullness, not padding — as long as a tired man can still do one closer a week. The intro already tells him how to miss a week and come back. Good.

## My season (52, divorced, still in church)

This pass put me in the room on purpose.

Intro names the man on every other weekend in an apartment he never expected. Kenny walks the book — Tuesday calls, mad lonely ghost, older man who would not hang up, daughter on the sideline, single-again seat in chapter 21, divorced season in chapter 32, still a brother in chapter 35. That is what my first bounce asked for: post-divorce as a season, not an aside.

Wound test: empty chair, exits, Father who stays, leave without slamming, problem inside — all still true, and now they talk to the man who may *be* the empty chair without using her exit as cover or handing me a strategy to get her back. Chapter 21's "single again is single" and the two-year counsel for dating land hard and fair. Chapter 26 on speaking well of her mother in the truck — I felt that one.

I am not a guest. I am not the cautionary tale. I am a man still in church with a Tuesday.

Age lanes are less of a stamped drum. Intro promised it would not stop every chapter to run the list. Mostly kept. When seasons show up, they feel like turns in a conversation, not six checkboxes.

## Closers: do this + why

Clearer.

Name it / Do this week / Tell a brother / Carry this still there, and each line now carries a plain "Because…" that echoes the chapter. That was the bolted-on problem before. Now I know why the letter I will not send belongs to the empty-chair week, and why the Tuesday call is not homework for its own sake.

Usable for a man who will miss a week. The intro's grace for that keeps me from quitting in shame. Closers feel like the chapter's last honest sentence, not a youth binder stamped on the end.

## Gift-readable / layout / cover

Body pages: cream, dark type, scripture with a dark margin bar. I can read this in a chair at night. Contrast is no longer the gold-on-bone problem Aaron flagged. Gift bar: yes, I would hand this to a brother without apologizing for how it looks inside.

Cover: still navy and gold, serious, clean. Plumb / line mark works. I would pick it up. It still sits near other men's Christian living books — fine, if the inside keeps this voice.

Still says **Review draft. Not for distribution** up front. Fine for panel. I would not pay, and I would not gift, a file that announces itself unfinished on page one. Kill that stamp before money or a Drive handoff that is meant as finished.

Dedication voice changed (tables, wife, children watching). Honest. Softer than the old Evan line. Not my call — just noting it as a buyer who felt the first one in the chest.
