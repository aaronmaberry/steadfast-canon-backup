# Bounce — Marcus (packets)

- Reader: Marcus, 34
- Date: 2026-09-05
- Source: weeks 1–24 refreshed signed packets (participant primary; leaders skimmed; print sampled week 1, 5, 19, 22)
- Verdict: maybe

I already bounced the book. This is the weekly worksheets — the thing I said I would actually use. I read participant pages for all twenty-four. Skimmed leaders for load. Looked at a few print PDFs. Baby still wakes. Money still tight. Same guy.

## 1. What delivered

The participant sheet is the right size. One letter page. Four marks. One job. Name it / Do this week / Tell a brother / Carry this. Write-in lines. Alone note so I do not hide behind “no group.” I can finish a sheet after the baby is down if I protect thirty minutes. That matters more than pretty copy.

**Week 1 (A quiet Tuesday)** still catches me. Meal without the phone. One small yes. Text a brother about Tuesday. That is my kitchen, not a retreat.

**Week 3–7** (four marks through reward) are truck-usable. **Week 5 (Pick up the weight)** is the one I would pay for by itself: coffee, bedtime prayer, twenty minutes after boots — not money as the only provision. Carry this (“provision that costs your presence isn’t provision”) is my season in one line.

**Week 8–9** (empty chair; anger / itch / drift) are honest without asking me to heal everything on one page. Circle one exit. Do one refusal. Good for a tired man who will not write a journal essay.

**Week 12 (A man with no brothers + Ask the man)** tells the truth: wife is not a brother, and a hint is not an ask. Sample ask is one sentence. Tell a brother across the run is mostly text-sized — that matches what I asked for on the book bounce.

House weeks land. **Week 19 (Lay it down + What she actually needs)** — name what I would not lay down, give the actual need once, do not announce it. Gold box on the print. That is young marriage under load. **Week 20 (The bed is a covenant)** is straight without a technique manual. **Week 22 (Give your son a name)** — false name / true name — already how tired dads talk (bud, dude, siren-name). Even with a little one I heard it.

Print feel (sampled): navy header, plumb bob, bone page, gold “Do this week” well. Looks serious, not LifeWay template, not gym-bro. Participant always 1 page; leader 2. I would print these and write on them.

## 2. What did not

Twenty-four weeks is a long stack when sleep is currency. The sheet is light. The **read** is the tax — doubles on **1, 11, 12, 16, 18, 19**. Two chapters plus an hour room plus a move is a heavy Tuesday for a new dad. I would soft-skip or skim in Part 3 (**16 Before the fall**, **17 Destiny**) when I want the house again. Not wrong. Dense.

**Week 18 (If there is no wife yet + Know her)** splits the page. Know-her half is mine. Not-yet / parked-because-no-wife is a skip for me. Mixed room again — I get why; money-tight me resents half a sheet that is not my lane.

**Week 21 (Your daughter is watching)** — “girl in reach” keeps me from walking out, but with a son it is adjacent. **Week 23 (Give your son a hill)** and especially **Week 24 (Give your son a day)** are beautiful and early. Put a date on a page / memorial stones — I am still learning diaper without the phone. I would write a smaller first-year version in my head and not resent the packet, but I would not pretend that week is for month three.

**Week 12**’s ask for twenty minutes a week for three months is right-sized for some guys. Hard to picture in this season. The text closers elsewhere save it.

Leader guides: early weeks (~600–800 words, 2 pages) a tired dad could lead if the group asked — Open, Hit, Table, closer, Solo note. House weeks **18–21** climb toward ~1000–1100 words. I would sit in as participant. Leading those after a night feed is a maybe with prep, not a casual yes. Not a sermon dump. Still more than “skim and go.”

## 3. Where dig deeper

Same ask as the book, sharper on the worksheets:

- **Infant / first-year fatherhood** on the son stack — not only name / hill / day for later boys. The 3am load. Passivity that sounds like “I worked, you take the night.” Week 5 almost gets there; weeks 23–24 talk past the first year.
- **Marriage under baby fog** — week 19 is strong; one prompt on scorekeeping / who gets the nap would make the sheet hit harder without adding length.
- **Brothers with no spare hour** — keep text-first Tell a brother; soften or alternate the three-month calendar ask on week 12 for men who only have a parking-lot window.
- **Signpost skip / stay** for married-with-newborn so I am not mining every mixed-room line on weeks 18 and 21.

Do not make the sheet longer. Dig with the same plain voice.

## 4. Content / layout / print

Content pattern holds every week: four marks line, NIV margin, one job, closer block. Closers are usable — I would fill the gold box and the name/day lines. Carry-this sentences are sticky (Tuesday; presence; knowing her is a job; she does not need the help that would have worked on you; if you do not give him a name someone else will).

Layout: clean letter, write-in space real, not decorative. Gray scripture well. Small art square — I barely need it. Words carry. Footer “Week N of 24” helps me see the runway.

Print sampled week 1 participant, week 5 leader, week 19 participant, week 22 participant — all same Cloth Store feel. No layout bugs I saw. Leader page one is prep + open + hit; page two (not all opened) carries table/closer — fine for a binder.

Homework load as participant: one written move + one text. That is doable. Homework as “read two chapters and lead an hour” is where I flake.

## 5. Would you buy / stick with this program — why

**Maybe.**

Closer than the book alone. The book was a mixed-room tour I had to mine. These packets are the weekly move I said was the value. I would buy a participant set if the price respected a tight season — not a premium binder tax. I would stick if a brother or a small group held the text ask with me. Solo for twenty-four weeks with a newborn: I would finish the sheet on the weeks that hit (1–7, 8–9, 12, 14, 19–20, 22) and soft-skip or skim 16–18’s single half, 21, 23–24’s “day” framing.

I would not pay extra to lead. Sit in, write the page, text the man. If they handed me the leader for week 19 cold after a bad night, I would pass or ask for a co-lead.

I did not buy anything. Real money: hesitate on 24-week sticker shock, sample weeks 1, 5, and 19, then decide.

## 6. What would make it worth paying for

1. Keep the one-page participant and the closer block. That is the product.
2. Price the participant track for a tight house — or a clear “start here” path (Tuesday → marks → weight → house → son name) without pretending I owe every mixed-room week equally.
3. One first-year fathering beat on the son weeks (same voice, no soft focus) so week 24 is not only a future memorial.
4. Keep Tell a brother text-sized; give week 12 an alternate ask that fits a parking lot, not only a three-month calendar.
5. Leader stays 2 pages — do not grow the house guides; a tired dad leading needs less Hit, not more.

If those land, I move from maybe to yes on the packets. Right now: strong maybe from a guy who will use the gold box this week, and who will not pretend twenty-four weeks is easy with a baby and a thin wallet.
