## Max byline / about the author

Lands.

Title page says **Max**, not a stage name and not a résumé. About the Author reads like a man I would sit across from: husband, dad of two, Texas, tech day job, layman not pastor, started because older men kept a chair and he started keeping chairs. Kitchen table → living rooms → class. That is gift-readable. Not celebrity. Not an Aaron dump.

Dig: first name only is clean; on a bookstore shelf it may feel thin next to full-name spines. If that is intentional for the brand, keep it. If gift buyers freeze on “who is Max?”, a last initial or quiet surname on the title page would help without turning him into a personality. About page already does the human work — do not fatten it.

Dedication (wife, children watching, tables that kept a chair) matches the Max voice. Fine.

## Intro: seasons / no Parts / training pointer

**Seasons, not ages.** Yes. Single, newly married / little ones, providing everything except yourself, **divorced, still a father**, quiet house, badge turned in, buried wife. My seat is named without a birthday. “Find your seat” and “I will not stop every chapter to run the whole list” — that is what I asked for in earlier bounces.

**No Part scaffolding.** Intro walks the spine in plain English (Tuesday → inherited → story → house → long road) without Part 1 / Part 2 labels. Good. I do not need a syllabus before coffee.

**Training pointer.** Honest. Book stands alone. Condensed map in the back. Full 24-week class at walksteadfast.com. “Buy either. Buy both. Just do not binge.” Not a brochure. Not a funnel speech. I would trust that paragraph.

Kenny still named in the men-you’ll-meet list. That matters from my seat.

## Condensed training (back)

Useful map — with one catch.

It is a clean week 1–24 spine for the class, same titles as the early book, closer reminder, do-not-binge line. For a group already running, that is what you want on paper.

Dig: the book’s back half (son trilogy, long road, brothers) is waved off to the site in one italic line. A man who just finished *These Men Are My Brothers* and flips to Condensed Training finds the map stop at *What She Actually Needs*. Either map every chapter that is in the book, or say up top, plain: “This is the first twenty-four weeks of the class; the rest of the book’s chapters are in the full guide.” Right now it feels slightly like the back forgot the last third.
