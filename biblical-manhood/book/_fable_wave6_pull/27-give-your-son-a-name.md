# 27. Give Your Son a Name

The first thing a nurse hands a new father is a pen. There's a box on a form, and he prints a name in it in block letters, and he thinks that's the naming done. It's the least of it. He's going to name that boy ten thousand more times before the boy leaves his house, and most of those times he won't know he's doing it.

Here's the principle. A boy answers to whatever he's called. The only question is who's calling. God gave men the power to name, and on a son it's enormous: what you call him, he becomes, and what you don't call him, he'll go looking for somebody else to call him. The world is full of names for boys, and almost none of them are good.

## The names he's getting anyway

He's being called things at school, and some of them stick for life: lazy, weird, too small, too much. He's being called things by a culture that has decided boys are a problem to be managed and names them out of fear. He's being called things by his own failures, because every boy fails at everything, and a boy with no one to tell him otherwise will decide the failures are his name: I can't, I'm bad at this, I always mess it up.

And he may be getting called things at home, by you, that you don't hear yourself saying. Ray heard himself. His wife was playing back a video from their son's birthday, and off camera, under the singing, there was Ray's voice: "What is wrong with you, bud?" He didn't remember saying it, which is how he knew he'd said it a hundred times. On the screen his son's face did a small thing and then recovered, the way a boy's face does when he's been handed a name he's going to carry. Ray told me he watched those four seconds more times than he watched the rest of the party.

I've sat with grown men who could still tell me the exact word their father used for them when they were small, and their voices changed when they said it. The sigh is a name. The eye roll is a name. "Why can't you be more like your sister" is a name.

## The three names

A boy needs three names from his father, given over and over, from the first week of his life to the last week of yours.

The first is mine. It's the name of belonging, and it says: you're my son, and I'm not going anywhere. Before he's earned anything, he's yours, and nothing he does can unsettle it. Jesus received this name first, at the Jordan: you are my Son, whom I love, with you I am well pleased. Possession comes first in that sentence. A boy who knows he belongs can fail at anything and come home. A boy who isn't sure will spend his life auditioning.

:::margin
Mark 1:11 (NIV)
"And a voice came from heaven: 'You are my Son, whom I love; with you I am well pleased.'"
:::

The second is what I see in you. It's the name of character, and it's about who he is rather than what he did. When he tells the truth about the broken window: "That's honesty. You're an honest man." You're catching him being what God made him and telling him what it's called, and you're speaking the name a little ahead of him, so he has something to grow into. A boy his father has called honest a hundred times will feel a lie sit wrong in his mouth in a way a boy who's only been called a liar never will. Paul fathered in three verbs: encouraging, comforting, urging. Encouraging says, I see it in you. Comforting says, when you fail, you're still mine. Urging says, now live up to it. A father who only urges raises a boy who can't rest. A father who only comforts raises a boy who won't grow.

The third is man. It's the name of what he's for, and it's the one most men never got. At some point, in a way he'll remember, your son needs to hear his father say: you're becoming a man, and here is what that means. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Every boy is asking whether he's a man yet, and he'll ask a girl, a fight, a paycheck, or a truck if his father doesn't answer first. Answer first, on purpose and out loud.

## How to give a name

Naming isn't a speech. It's a habit.

Every day, catch him and call it. Find one thing your son did that a man does and name it: "That's what a man does." Five seconds, and the highest-return five seconds in fathering.

Every week, give the blessing. Pick a night. Put your hand on his head and say three sentences. Who he is: "You're my son, I love you, and I'm not going anywhere." What you see: "This week I watched you tell the truth when it cost you. That's courage." What God says: "God made you on purpose, He has work for you, and I'll help you find it." Moses told fathers to impress God's words on their children when they lie down and when they get up, and impress is a word about pressure over time, like a mark worked into leather.

:::margin
Deuteronomy 6:6-7 (NIV)
"These commandments that I give you today are to be on your hearts. Impress them on your children. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up."
:::

Every year, write the letter. On his birthday, one page: who he is this year, what you've watched him become, what you see coming. Put it in a box for the day you tell him he's a man. A man holding a stack of letters in his father's handwriting, one for every year of his life, can walk into any room on earth.

In public, praise him; in private, correct him. "This is my son. He finished the whole fence with me Saturday and didn't quit." When he's done wrong, take him aside. Shame in public is a name that sticks. Praise in public is a name that lifts.

When he fails, separate the act from the name. He told a lie. You say: "That was a lie. You're not a liar. You're my son, who told a lie, and here's what my son does next: he goes and tells the truth to the person he lied to." "You're a liar" hands a boy a name he'll fight or fulfill. "You're my honest son who did a dishonest thing" hands him a way back.

And always, tell him whose he is. Under your name is a bigger one. Tell him God made him on purpose and that the Father who doesn't leave is his Father too. Your name over him is good, and it isn't enough; you'll fail him, and you'll die. God's name over him holds, and the best thing your name can do is point at it.

Ray did all of it, badly at first. Once he was looking for things a man does, his son turned out to be doing them all day. Then his son's little sister broke something and was crying, and Ray heard the boy tell her, "You're not bad. You did a bad thing. Go tell Mom." That's what a boy does with a name. He passes it on.

## Naming in every season

If you have a baby boy, start now. He doesn't understand the words, but he understands the voice. Hold him, phone in another room, and say "you're my son" over him every day. New fathers feel useless with an infant; naming him is the job. If you're in the divorced seat, your son may be getting other names at another house, from a stepfather or from the silence about you. You can't control that; you can control the names he gets from you. The weeknight call is where you name him. Never make him a messenger or a spy between houses; that's a name nobody wants. Tell him he's not in the middle. Tell him he's yours and that isn't up for negotiation no matter where he sleeps. End every call with the same sentence: "You're my son. I love you. I'll call you next week." If you're an older man, you have grandsons and grown sons, and both need naming. A grandfather's "that's what a man does" carries weight a father's sometimes can't. And if your grown son never got the third name from you, it isn't too late; tell him you're proud of the man he's become. If you have no sons, there are boys on your street with no man to name them. When you see one of them do what a man does, say so, out loud, in front of the others.

## The Name that holds

Every name you give your son is a small copy of the Name God gives His sons: mine, what I see in you, man. He says all three over every man who's in Christ, and He said them over you, whether or not your father ever did.

:::margin
Psalm 127:3 (NIV)
"Children are a heritage from the LORD, offspring a reward from him."
:::

The psalm calls him a reward. He's God's reward to you, and the gift you give back is his name. There's a boy in your house asking who he is. He asks every day, and you get to answer. Give him the first weight worth carrying: a name, from his father, that he can stand on for the rest of his life.

## Before you turn the page

If you're the son who never got a name, you can give what you didn't get. Ray didn't wait to become a better man before he put his hand on his son's head; the hand is how he became one. If you've been giving him the sigh and the eye roll, the fix starts tonight with three sentences. May the Father who called His Son beloved before He'd done a thing put the same words in your mouth, and may your son grow up with a floor under him that nothing can take out.
