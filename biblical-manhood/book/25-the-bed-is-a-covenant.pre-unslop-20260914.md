# 25. The Bed Is a Covenant

Mitch sat in my office and said the thing husbands say when the bed has gone quiet. "I'm holding up my end. She's not holding up hers."

I asked him what he'd promised her. He had it by heart: for better, for worse, in sickness and in health, as long as we both shall live. I asked him to find the ends in it. He couldn't. There's only one, and a man is standing at a graveside when he reaches it.

"End" is a contract word. Contracts have two ends and a term, and each party holds up his and watches the other. That's how most of us learned to think about the bed without anyone saying so.

## A sign, not a term sheet

A contract protects you if the other party fails; it's built for the exit. A covenant binds you regardless; it's built for the staying. In a contract you exchange things. In a covenant you exchange yourselves, which is why a covenant always comes with a sign, a visible thing standing for the invisible one: a ring, a cup, a body.

The old English wedding rite said so. As the groom put the ring on, he said, "With this ring I thee wed, with my body I thee worship." Worship there meant honor, worth-ship, and the sentence meant what it said: his body would spend the rest of his life telling his wife what she's worth. The bed is where that vow gets kept or broken. It's the marriage's sign, renewed as often as two people are able.

## Recreation, dirt, or covenant

The culture calls sex recreation, where the only rules are consent and enthusiasm, and the other person is the equipment. The church, for a long stretch, called it dirt, the thing good husbands did quickly and with a little shame. God says neither. Before the fall, Scripture says a husband and wife become one flesh, naked and unashamed. One thing, nothing hidden, and it's called good.

:::margin
Genesis 2:24-25 (NIV)
"That is why a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh. Adam and his wife were both naked, and they felt no shame."
:::

And the marriage bed is to be kept pure: not quiet, not minimal, but kept for what it's for, between the two it belongs to, and honored. God isn't embarrassed by the bed. He built it, and He guards it.

:::margin
Hebrews 13:4 (NIV)
"Marriage should be honored by all, and the marriage bed kept pure, for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral."
:::

## What the covenant means in the bed

The covenant is exclusive. Drink water from your own cistern, Proverbs says, running water from your own well. Jesus took it inside: the man who looks at a woman lustfully has already committed adultery in his heart. A husband faithful with his body and unfaithful with his eyes has a leak in the cistern, and his wife can tell the water's low even when she can't find the crack.

The covenant is generous. Paul wrote that the wife's body belongs to her husband, then wrote the sentence that turned the ancient world over: in the same way, the husband's body belongs to his wife. Your body is for her, which means the bed isn't about your release; it's about her delight. The husband who goes in asking how she's made and what would bless her gets more out of the bed than the man who takes.

:::margin
1 Corinthians 7:4 (NIV)
"The wife does not have authority over her own body but yields it to her husband. In the same way, the husband does not have authority over his own body but yields it to his wife."
:::

The covenant is present and patient. A man can be absent in bed the way he's absent in the recliner, and she knows the difference between being with you and being used to get somewhere else. Her body runs on a different clock; honor it. Hurry is a kind of theft. Tenderness is the old vow kept.

The covenant is never a weapon. Not withheld as punishment, not demanded as payment, not kept on a scoreboard. The moment the bed becomes a way to get your way, it stops being a sign and becomes a negotiation, and Eden is gone from it. If the bed has gone quiet, the answer is never to make her pay. It's to find out what's wrong, which is almost never about the bed.

The covenant is honest. Most couples never say a sentence about it. Talk to your wife about the bed, gently, not in the bed and not after a disappointment, and ask what she'd want more of and less of.

And the covenant is protected. Beds go quiet from neglect long before they go quiet from age. A man who only touches his wife to initiate will find one day that she flinches at his hand, because he taught her his touch always wanted something.

That last one was Mitch. He'd been counting, and Dana, his wife, could feel the count in every touch. I asked him to put the ledger down for a season: no counting, no initiating, touch that wanted nothing, and one gentle conversation, held in the truck on the way home from her mother's. What she said was, "I want to stop feeling like a bill that's overdue." He told me months later that she comes to find him now. He'd thought the bed was owed to him. It turned out to be the place he owed her the truth about what she's worth.

## The screen, in the covenant frame

You've been given tools for the screen already: one brother with the whole truth, a filter he holds the password to, the phone charging in the kitchen. But the frame changes the fight. You aren't fighting pornography because it's against a rule. You're fighting it because you made a covenant with a real woman, and every image is a small breaking of it. The victim sleeps next to you. It trains an appetite for novelty, it rewires what your body answers to, and it lies to you that the problem is her not being enough when the problem is you not being present.

The four marks show up here, in the dark. A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. He owns what his eyes do, and he trusts the Father who sees what's done in secret and keeps every faithful night on the books.

## In her seasons, and in yours

The seasons change what keeping the covenant looks like. After a baby, her body has been through a war, and your claims are laid down. She's exhausted and touched out, and you aren't counting. You touch her in the way that wants nothing, and when she's ready you go slow and ask and stop if she needs you to. A wife who can trust her husband with her body when it's weakest will trust him with it when it's strong. In illness the covenant doesn't pause; it changes shape into tenderness that has nothing to do with the bed. In the middle years her body will do something new, and a husband who doesn't take it as rejection, who asks and goes to the appointment with her, makes the bed a safe place. Men who keep the well get to drink from it in old age, slower and sweeter. And when it's no longer possible, the covenant holds. A man bathing a wife who no longer knows his name is keeping the marriage bed pure. Nothing is happening in it. He's still one flesh with her.

## If there's no wife yet, and if there was

If you're single and intend to marry, every choice about your body right now is a choice about a covenant you haven't made yet. You're building it or spending it. Paul told the Thessalonians to learn to control their own bodies, and the word is learn. Don't date for years without moving toward marriage; long courtships are where good men fall. Don't be alone with her in a bedroom at midnight and call yourselves strong; nobody is. And if you can carry the weight and you've found the woman, marry her.

If you're in the divorced seat, single again is single. That's harder to hear when you know what you're missing. The culture will tell you a grown man who's been married has earned the right to do as he pleases. He hasn't. The covenant standard has no clause for men who used to have a bed: no sex outside a new covenant, and no moving in because you're basically married, since basically married is not married. The same brother, the same filter, the same phone in the kitchen, with more at stake, because your children are watching how a man handles a body when it's hard.

If you're a widower, the same is true, and I'm sorry, because you kept the covenant for a lifetime and nobody warned you about the loneliness of the empty bed. God doesn't ask you to pretend it isn't there. He asks you to keep the bed pure in the empty years, and to trust that the Father who saw every faithful night sees these too.

## What the bed preaches

Paul quotes the one-flesh verse to husbands and then says the strangest thing about it: this is a profound mystery, and he's talking about Christ and the church.

:::margin
Ephesians 5:31 (NIV)
"'For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and the two will become one flesh.'"
:::

The bed preaches. When a husband is exclusive and generous and present and patient in it, when he never turns it into a weapon or lets it go silent, he's showing his wife a small, real picture of how Christ loves the church: faithfully, self-givingly, with delight, forever. When a man breaks the covenant, with his body or his eyes, he's preaching a lie about God.

Not recreation. Not dirt. A vow kept with a body, for as long as they both shall live. Mitch was wrong about the ends. There aren't any. There's only the whole of you, promised, and a bed where you say so.

## Before you turn the page

The bed is where a great many good men have quietly broken faith and told no one. If that's you, bring it into the light; David did, and his standing was restored in a breath. The covenant isn't a rule you've failed. It's a vow you can start keeping tomorrow. May the Father who honors the marriage bed give you a well of your own and the faithfulness to drink from no other, and may your bed preach the truth about Him.
