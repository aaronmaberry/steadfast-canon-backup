# Two Men, Two Ways

A man stands in the strip of dirt along the garage after the kids are in. Hose in his hand. A tomato cage. Dirt on his boots. It does not look like a garden that could decide a life. Eden didn't look like much from the road either. Neither did the place He prayed with His face in the dirt.

There were two gardens, and two men, and two nights when everything was on the line.

In the first garden the man had everything and lost it in an afternoon without saying a word. In the second garden the Man had nothing left but a choice, and He made it with His face in the dirt, and it saved the world.

Silence, blame, hiding, grabbing: the first garden. The opposite, on an ordinary morning, when it cost you something: the second Man. The Bible puts the whole human story on two men.

:::margin
Romans 5:19 (NIV)
"For just as through the disobedience of the one man the many were made sinners, so also through the obedience of the one man the many will be made righteous."
:::

This is about which one you're in, and which one's way you're going to walk this week.

## The first garden

The serpent comes into the garden. He doesn't go to the man. He goes to the woman and starts asking questions about the word God gave. He twists it. He suggests God is holding out on them. Now, where is the man?

:::margin
Genesis 3:6 (NIV)
"When the woman saw that the fruit of the tree was good for food and pleasing to the eye, and also desirable for gaining wisdom, she took some and ate it. She also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it."
:::

He was with her. Those three words have been indicting men ever since. Adam received the command alone, from God's own mouth, before the woman existed. He was told to work the garden and keep it. When the thing he was supposed to guard against came through the fence, he stood there. He watched, and then he ate. The first sin wasn't grabbing the fruit. It was silence while the word was twisted: passivity, the first mark inverted.

Then God comes walking, and they hide.

:::margin
Genesis 3:10 (NIV)
"He answered, 'I heard you in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked; so I hid.'"
:::

I was afraid, so I hid. That's the third mark inverted: cowardice instead of courage. He heard God's voice and went into the trees. Then God asks what he did, and he says the woman you put here, she gave it to me. He blames his wife and he blames God and he leaves himself out. That's the second mark inverted: responsibility refused.

And under all of it is the fourth. The serpent said you will be like God, a reward he could have right then. He traded the greater reward for the nearer one. He chose the fruit over the Father.

Four marks, four inversions, in one afternoon. Passive, evasive, hiding, and grabbing. That's the first man's way, and the default of every man since.

Then the curse: thorns, death, exile. But even there, two things happen. God makes them clothes with His own hands, from an animal that had to die. The first covering came at the cost of a life; hold onto that. And God says a word to the serpent that turns out to be a promise.

:::margin
Genesis 3:15 (NIV)
"And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel."
:::

Somebody is coming: a son of the woman who will stand in a garden when the enemy comes and will not be silent. It will cost him. But he'll crush the thing that got in through the fence. The second Man was promised in the first garden.

## The second garden

It's night, on a hillside outside the city, a place He had gone many times. He's brought His men, told three of them to watch, and then He goes on alone and falls on His face.

:::margin
Matthew 26:39 (NIV)
"Going a little farther, he fell with his face to the ground and prayed, 'My Father, if it is possible, may this cup be taken from me. Yet not as I will, but as you will.'"
:::

The first man was offered fruit that would make him like God. The second Man is offered a cup that will make Him like a criminal, and He doesn't want it. This is a Man sweating blood. And then: not as I will, but as You will. That's the second mark. He picks up a cup He didn't fill, for a sin He didn't commit, and says it's His.

He prays three times. The men He asked to watch fall asleep. He is completely alone, and He does not go into the trees.

:::margin
Matthew 26:46 (NIV)
"Rise! Let us go! Here comes my betrayer!"
:::

He hears the mob coming and walks toward it.

:::margin
John 18:4 (NIV)
"Jesus, knowing all that was going to happen to him, went out and asked them, 'Who is it you want?'"
:::

He went out. He didn't wait to be found among the trees. That's the third mark. Going first, into the worst thing. He was afraid enough to sweat blood, and He went out anyway. And while they're arresting Him, He covers His men.

:::margin
John 18:8 (NIV)
"'I told you that I am he,' Jesus answered. 'If you are looking for me, then let these men go.'"
:::

Let these men go. Caught, He points at Himself. That's responsibility finished: a man standing between the danger and the people he's been given, saying me, not them.

Hebrews says He endured it for the joy set before Him. And when it was done, He said one last thing about the work.

:::margin
John 19:30 (NIV)
"When he had received the drink, Jesus said, 'It is finished.' With that, he bowed his head and gave up his spirit."
:::

It is finished. His last words before death were a completion report. That's the fourth mark. He was living for the Father's well done, and He got it three days later.

:::margin
Philippians 2:9-11 (NIV)
"Therefore God exalted him to the highest place and gave him the name that is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue acknowledge that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father."
:::

Therefore, Paul says, because He picked up the cup and went out and covered His men and finished. The reward came, given to a Man who looked like a loser to everyone who watched Him die.

## Two ways, laid side by side

The first man was silent when the word was twisted. The second Man spoke: who is it you want?

The first man said she did it. The second Man said take me, let them go.

The first man hid from God's voice in the trees. The second Man sought the Father's face in the dirt, then got up.

The first man grabbed the reward in his hand. The second Man trusted the reward on the far side of the cross.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Those marks might have sounded like a list of qualities. They aren't. They are the shape of the second Man in the second garden. Rejecting passivity is Rise, let us go. Accepting responsibility is not as I will, but as you will. Leading courageously is went out and asked them. Expecting the greater reward is for the joy set before him. The marks aren't a personality profile. They're a portrait of Jesus in the worst hour of His life, and every time you live one of them, you're wearing His face.

## Which man you're in

The Bible doesn't only say be like the second man and not the first. It says you're in one of them.

:::margin
1 Corinthians 15:22 (NIV)
"For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive."
:::

You were born in the first man. His way is your default. That's why passivity comes so easily, why blame is the first thing out of your mouth, why hiding feels natural and grabbing feels right. You were in him when he did it, the way a family is in a father.

But God isn't asking you to try your way out. He's offering to move you into the second Man, so that what He did in His garden counts as yours. His obedience becomes your record. His it is finished becomes the last word over your life instead of the excuse. That's the gospel.

:::margin
Galatians 2:20 (NIV)
"I have been crucified with Christ and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the body, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me."
:::

Christ lives in me. That isn't a metaphor for trying hard. It's a change of location. A man in Christ has the second Man's Spirit in him, the same Spirit that got up off the ground that night, and that's why he can get up off his own.

If you've never done it, do it now. Tell God you've been in the first man's garden your whole life, and that you're done. Tell Him you want to be in the second Man, trusting what He did in His garden to cover what you did in yours. That's the way in, and it's open.

And if you've already done it, hear the rest. The four marks aren't a standard you're trying to reach so that He'll accept you. They're the family resemblance showing up, because you're His.

## This week's garden

It'll be a kitchen. Your wife says something that isn't quite fair, and every fiber in you wants to go quiet, or to fire back. That kitchen is the garden. The first man's way is silence or blame. The second Man's way is a soft, clear word: that landed hard. Can we talk after the kids are down. Rise, let us go.

It'll be a phone late at night with nobody watching. Put it on the charger in the kitchen and go to bed, for the joy set before you.

It'll be two in the morning with the baby crying, and you can hear your wife starting to move. That dark bedroom is the garden, and the sentence for it is not as I will. A mess at work that's partly your fault is the same garden. Own your piece first, out loud. Let these men go.

The gardens are small, and that's the point. Adam's afternoon was one piece of fruit. The size of the garden has nothing to do with the size of what's decided in it.

## The two men in your house

If you're single, notice that the second Man was single. He stood in His garden without a wife, and He was the fullest man who ever lived. Your manhood is not on hold. The garden is wherever you're standing, so stand in it.

If there's a baby in your house, the garden is a two-in-the-morning story. Pray may this cup be taken. He did. Then say the second sentence and get up.

If you sit the divorced seat, look hard at let these men go. The first man's way after a marriage breaks is to point: at her, at the lawyers, at the God who put her here. The second Man's way is to stand between the wreckage and the people you've been given and say me, not them. Your children should never have to carry your war. Cover them, own your part out loud, and let God sort hers.

If you're older, think about last words. The first man's were an excuse. The second Man's were it is finished. Cal started praying, let me finish. Whatever's left, let me finish it. That is a garden prayer from a man farther on.

## The last Adam

:::margin
1 Corinthians 15:45 (NIV)
"So it is written: 'The first man Adam became a living being'; the last Adam, a life-giving spirit."
:::

The last Adam, Paul calls Him, not the second in a series but the last, the one who finally did what the first was supposed to do. There will never be another one, because He finished it. He isn't asking you to be Him. He's asking you to be in Him, and then to walk His way through your small gardens with His Spirit in you and His record over you. Two men, two ways. You were born in one. You've been moved into the other. Now go stand in your garden like the Man you're in. If you've never asked Him to move you, ask Him tonight. May the Father who raised the last Adam raise you up off the ground this week.
