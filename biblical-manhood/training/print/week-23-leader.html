Say it. Do not unpack it. Tonight is ground.

1 Thessalonians 2:11-12 NIV

For you know that we dealt with each of you as a father deals with his own children, encouraging, comforting and urging you to live lives worthy of God, who calls you into his kingdom and glory.

Open (2-3 min)

Friday. New street. Last lot. Caliche mound. Permit box on a stick. Boys charging it. Sister on a plastic trike. Sticks. A broken cone. Badge still on. He thinks: good, he's outside. That is not a hill. That is dirt.

Say the four. Sit down.

Hit

Encourage. Comfort. Urge. A father does all three toward a life worthy of God. A name without a hill is a compliment. A boy becomes what you called him by climbing something that proves it.

Boys do not lack fight. They lack ground. Protect him from every difficulty and he starves. Hand him difficulty slightly beyond him, with a man watching who does not rescue too soon, and he learns he can. Without a hill he will find one: a fight, a dare, a screen with ten thousand hills that cost nothing, or a basement he never leaves because nobody taught him he could climb.

A hill is real. It matters to somebody besides him. Boys smell a made-up job. A hill is slightly beyond him: not what he already can do, not what a man can do. The next thing. A hill has real risk, bounded. He can fail. You chose it with care and you stay in the truck. A hill has an outcome he owns. Not "I tried." Done, or not done yet and here is what I am doing. A hill is followed by a name when he comes down. Light callback to last week. Do not reprint it.

What you have been handing him may be dirt: sport as the whole life, a fence of rules with no cause, an unfinished thing you never finish, himself as the project, or nothing. Name that. Then name the real one: what he is becoming under God, how this house lives, a cause bigger than his comfort. Stand on it where he can see you. Calendar is not a hill. Mission on the glass is not a hill if the boy only gets how-was-school in a tone that wants fine.

If you have no son in the house, stay. Nephew. The boy you coach. The kid who stays after. Daughters-only still has boys in reach. Marriage is good. It is not the start of ground. You already stand for something or you do not. Say the real hill once this week to the younger man who can hear you, and stand on it where he can see.

Do not preview the day. Tonight is the hill.

First-year dad: the 3am feed is not dirt and it is not the whole hill. Show up tired without making the boy pay for your sleep. Stand where he can see. Do not lengthen this into a second sermon.

Point at Chapter 28. Then shut up.

Table

Three. Wait.

  1. 1.What hill have you been handing him, or the younger man in reach?
  2. 2.Was it dirt, a fence, yourself, or nothing?
  3. 3.What is the real hill, and where will you stand on it where he can see you?

Young: delayed manhood wants a later son to be the first person you hand a cause; you already stand for something or you do not; little brother. Single: you are not a guest; nephew, the boy you coach, the kid who stays after. Married: this is your page if you have a son; calendar versus hill; do not punch mom if she already handed a way to live; daughters-only still has boys in reach. Hurt: go slow; no hill, a fence, or dad's pride as a hill; not a tyrant; not a speech to win him by Friday. Older: a grown son still needs a place to stand; voicemail; grandson; empty nest is not a refund; never-a-son is not a mascot. The job going well: mission on the glass; boy gets fine; a paycheck cannot give him a hill.
