Ephesians 4:26-27 NIV

"In your anger do not sin": Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry, and do not give the devil a foothold.

Open (2-3 min)

Three men. Same table. Same eggs. One threw a tool across a shop. One was still on a phone at two. One felt almost nothing when his wife said she was living with a roommate. They would swear they have nothing in common. They took three exits from the same pain.

Say the four. Sit down.

Hit

Pain does not sit still in a man. It goes out as anger, down as appetite, or away as absence. Most men call their home exit personality. It is not. It is how they learned not to feel something a long time ago.

A lot of male anger is grief with its fists up. Under every exit is a boy who is trying not to feel the chair. That explains the exit. It does not excuse the foothold.

Paul did not ban anger. He banned the overnight. A cracked day is a cracked door. The devil does not need a scene. He needs a place to stand.

The itch is comfort now without cost and without being known. Screen, bottle, feed, cart, sports book. Same itch. Flee, not manage.

The drift is leaving without moving. Body in the recliner. Attention somewhere else. The house leaks one evening at a time.

Do not heal him with next week's Father tonight. Name the fruit. Get one refusal on the page. Point at Chapter 9. Then shut up.

Table

Three. One exit. Not a tour of all three.

  1. 1.Which is your home exit: anger, the itch, or the drift?
  2. 2.Finish this: "When things get heavy, I ___."
  3. 3.What is one move this week that is not more of that fruit?

Young: watch the itch and the early drift. Single: a wife will not starve it. Married: she already knows; refuse it, skip the childhood speech. Hurt: the fruit is still his to stop feeding. Older: watch the drift and leftover heat; mellowed is not refused. The job going well: ambition and ease can both be footholds. Competence is not repentance.

Run the closer

One exit. One refusal.

Name it
Which is your home exit: anger, the itch, or the drift? Say it in a full sentence: "When things get heavy, I ___."
Do this week
Anger: the next time it rises, take ninety seconds, then say what is under it. The itch: tell one man the whole truth and set a filter with him holding the password. The drift: one evening this week, screens off, attention in the room, one real question asked and waited on.
Tell a brother
Tell him your exit and the last time you took it. Ask him his. Neither of you gets to say "I'm fine."
Carry this
A lot of male anger is grief with its fists up. Under every exit is a boy who's trying not to feel the chair.

If he only tells the wound, he gets sympathy and keeps the foothold. Get the fruit and the refusal on the page.

Solo note

Read Chapter 9. Name one. Do the refusal the same week. Tell one man both. If it is anger, close the day. If it is the itch, tell the truth and cut the supply. If it is the drift, take one evening back.

Do not open week 10.
