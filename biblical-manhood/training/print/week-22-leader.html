Open (2-3 min)

Saturday. Rec complex. League jersey still in the bag. Last name heat-pressed a little high. Number 14. Folding chair. Coffee gone cold. He knows the useful yells. The boy at the chain-link twists the collar to read his own name. The jersey has a name. The father has not given one.

Say the four. Sit down.

Hit

Summoned you by name. You are mine. God does not shrug a boy into the world. He claims him. A father who will not open his mouth leaves a hole, and the world is full of names ready to fill it.

He is already being named. At school. By the culture that treats boys like a problem to manage. By his own failures. By the sigh and the eye roll at home that you do not hear yourself using. Nicknames that started as jokes and landed as verdicts. What you call him, he becomes. What you will not call him, he will go looking for, and the volunteers are not kind.

Three names, over and over.

Mine. You are my son. I am not going anywhere. Before he earns anything. Belonging first. A boy who knows he belongs can fail and come home. A boy who is not sure will spend his life auditioning.

What I see in you. Character, not a scoreboard. Catch him telling the truth, standing up, helping without being asked, not quitting. Name it. Speak a little ahead of him so he has something to grow into. Encourage. Comfort. Urge. All three. Only urge and he cannot rest. Only comfort and he never grows.

Man. The handoff word, on purpose, out loud. Not "you're the man of the house" over a shoulder on the way out. Every boy is asking whether he is a man yet. He will ask a girl, a fight, a paycheck, a truck, if his father does not answer first. Answer it. Do not preview the hill or the day. Tonight is the name.

How: daily catch and call it. Weekly blessing, three sentences and a short prayer, even when it feels strange. Yearly letter on his birthday. Praise in public. Correct in private. When he fails, separate the act from the name: that was a lie; you are not a liar; you are my son who told a lie, and here is what my son does next. Always tell him whose he is under your name: God's.

If you have no son in the house, stay. Nephew. The boy you coach. The kid who stays after. Daughters-only still has boys in reach. A future son is not the start of a mouth. You already name people. Say the true name once this week to the younger man who can hear you. Point at Chapter 27. Then shut up.
