Time

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Say it. Do not unpack it. Tonight is the marker.

Joshua 4:6-7 NIV

to serve as a sign among you. In the future, when your children ask you, "What do these stones mean?" tell them that the flow of the Jordan was cut off before the ark of the covenant of the LORD. Open (2-3 min)

Rented pavilion. Cake. Phones up. Leftover sheet cake in a box. He thinks he marked something. A party is not a crossing. No stone. No sentence. No before and after.

Say the four. Sit down.

Hit

What do these stones mean. God wanted a pile on a riverbank so a boy fifty years later would ask and get a story. Markers. Days. Stones a man can point to. Birthdays mark time. Graduations mark school. Most houses have no day that marks the thing that matters: the day his father and the men around him say, on purpose, out loud, you are becoming a man, here is what that means, and you are one of us.

Without that day he guesses. First with a girl. First drunk. First fight. License. Truck. Counterfeits. Give him the real one.

Not a party. A marker: men, a word, a weight, a welcome. Father plus two to four men who will still be around in ten years. Outside. Early. Somewhere with weight. He knows something is coming, not what. Each man brings one true thing: a story, a failure and what he did after, a charge. Father brings the names and the fourth word. The four marks, in his hand. A token he can hold in a parking lot when he has forgotten the rest. Prayer. Welcome. Then breakfast where the men talk to him like a man.

The day changes nothing by itself. Tuesday makes it real. More hill. Opinion weighed. Man's jobs. Corrected privately. Trusted, and when he fails held to a man's standard and given a man's grace. Marker without Tuesday is a stone with no story.

If you never had a day, you can still give one. Saying the sentence you never heard will do something in you too. If you have no son in the house, stay. Be one of the men. Or ask a mother of a fatherless boy, in the light, with her blessing. Grandfathers: offer. You have the standing and the token in a drawer. Light callbacks only to name and hill. Do not reprint them as the whole page.

First-year dad: you are not throwing a pavilion at 3am. Put a date and a sentence on a page. A seed is still a stone. Do not lengthen this into a second sermon.

Point at Chapter 29. Then shut up.
