Before the room

Read this week
Chapter 3, The four marks.
Aim
The four as one sentence in his mouth. Not four sermons. Unpacking is weeks 4 through 7.
One job
He can say the four without looking down.
Pitfall
Do not lecture the book back. Do not unpack passivity, responsibility, courage, or reward tonight. Do not improve the line. Get it in his mouth. A mark is what he does over and over, not what he feels.
Time
about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God’s reward.

Say it with them. Do not swap a word.

Genesis 3:6 NIV

When the woman saw that the fruit of the tree was good for food and pleasing to the eye, and also desirable for gaining wisdom, she took some and ate it. She also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it.

Open (2-3 min)

Glad they came. Mixed room on purpose. Nobody is a guest.

Last week he named the sentence his week has been preaching. Some of those sentences pay. They will not hold a life. Tonight is the line that can hold. One sentence. Four marks. Not a stack of talks.

Say the four. Then stop talking.

Hit

The book already said it. You name the job in one breath.

A mark is not a feeling or a title. It is what is left on a man by what he has done over and over. Nobody gets a callus from thinking about a shovel. The four are things you can see on a man the way you could see a fence stretcher on a grandfather’s palm.

Who was with her. Three words. Passivity almost never looks like laziness. It looks like patience. It looks like keeping the peace. The first man stood there, and that was enough to break everything. Tonight is not a garden lecture. It is the sentence.

A man will live some sentence. The only question is which. Personality is not a loophole. A job title will not do this work for him. Hearing the line is not having it. The old sentence is already practiced. This one is not.

Point them at Chapter 3. Do not perform it. Do not start week 4.

Table

Three. Wait.

  1. 1.What line has been paying you that will not hold a life?
  2. 2.Which of the four marks is faintest on you right now?
  3. 3.Where would this sentence have to work this week, on an actual person, or it is only a hobby?

Young: a sentence at last; write it until you can say it. Single: this is already his life. Married: she already knows which mark is faint. Hurt: go slow; the marks are not a scolding. Older: he can still give the line. The job going well: four marks are not a title.

Run the closer

Paper out. Do not skip a beat.

Name it
Which of the four marks is faintest on you right now? Say it in a sentence: “The one I’m missing most is ___.”
Do this week
Write the four marks on an index card in your own handwriting and put it where you will see it on Tuesday morning.
Tell a brother
Read him the sentence. Ask him which mark he would say is faintest on you. Let him answer before you defend yourself.
Carry this
A mark is what’s left on you by what you’ve done over and over. Nobody gets a callus from thinking about a shovel.

If he agrees and leaves unchanged, you missed it. Get the four in his mouth before he walks.

Solo note

If he is alone, he still reads Chapter 3, writes the four until he can say them, and tells one man. Do not explain first.
