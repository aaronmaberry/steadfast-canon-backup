# Packet bounce — Earl, 62

Refresh pass weeks 1–24 (`training/weeks/01`–`24`). Leader + participant. Buyer seat for a man who might pay for the program. Bounce only. No rewrite. No publish.

Date: 2026-09-05  
To: Pace

---

## What delivered

These are not LifeWay sheets with a nicer font. One job. Four marks unchanged. Closer every week: Name it / Do this week / Tell a brother / Carry this. A man can print one week and run it alone or in a room. That is a real product.

Week 1 sits me down without making me a guest. Leader guide names the man who thought he aged out and the 62-year-old who can still put childish ways behind him. Good open.

Part 2 holds. Empty chair (8) is kind and hard. Letter you will not send is the right move. Brothers week (12) makes you ask a man, not wait to be discovered. Leader tells older men to be askable and walk across the lot. That is my seat used right.

Daughter and son weeks (21–24) do not lock me out. Grown daughter still watching. Grandson. Empty nest is not a refund. Never-had-a-son is not a mascot. Week 7 pecan trees at seventy-one — that line alone is worth the sheet.

Scripture is NIV, one primary, cited. No em dash mess. No course names. No fake church binder smell in the copy.

---

## What did not

**This bounce ends on week 24.** Son day. The long road — work, feelings, season, unseen build, next move, brothers who stay — sits in weeks 25–27 and is **not** in this refresh pass. For a 62-year-old, that is the wrong place to stop selling the program. You built me a staircase and cut the last flight. Dig that before anyone pays for a "complete" 24.

The leader table paragraph is a factory. Young / single / married / hurt / older / job going well. Every week. Same hallway as the book. I am glad I am named. By week 14 I can hear the stamp before I read it. Respect is not a checklist.

Participant sheets mostly talk to a general "you." The older seat lives in the leader aside. If I am alone with the worksheet, I get less of myself than the leader gets. Flip that some weeks: put the empty-nest / grandfather / still-a-next-move line on the face he writes on.

Week 12's ask sample pushes him toward a man twenty years ahead. Fine for Caleb. For me, I *am* the man twenty years ahead. The leader says be askable. The worksheet still sounds like I am always the junior. Name both doors on the participant page.

Doubles (11, 12, 16, 18, 19) try to do two book chapters in one hour. Possible. Tight. An older man with a full week will skim. Watch whether the "one job" actually covers both titles or quietly drops one.

## Where they need to dig in more

1. **Refresh 25–27 before you call the program finished.** Next move and brothers who stay are why I would keep paying after the son trilogy.
2. **Break the stamped age paragraph** on a few weeks so the older seat owns the page, not a visit.
3. **Put empty-nest / grandfather language on participant sheets**, not only in leader notes.
4. **Week 12:** ask up and be askable — both on the worksheet.
5. **Doubles:** confirm one hour still lands both jobs, or split where the second chapter is getting robbed.
6. **Creed uniqueness** same as the book bounce: four marks hold; own or credit the shelf memory so it does not smell borrowed under original pictures.

## Content, layout, cover / print feel

**Content.** Sharp. Actionable. Small true moves with a date and a name. Not a vision board. Not a sermon reprint. Fortune 500 bar is visible in the restraint.

**Layout (from md).** Clean hierarchy. Write-in lines. One face for participant when it fits. Leader is short enough to run. I have not scored Binder's printed PDF face in this pass — if the print still reuses the same mast/well every week, watch template fatigue in the hand, same as the age stamp in the copy.

**Program shape.** MAP says 24-week paid program, then also weeks 25–27 on disk. Buyer confusion waiting to happen. Either the paid arc includes the long road, or you say plainly that 24 ends the house and the road is a later pass. Do not let me pay for a finish that leaves off my chapters.

---

## Would I buy / pay

**Buy the weekly packet habit?** Yes — if weeks 25–27 get the same refresh and I am not treated like a mascot.

**Pay for a program that stops at week 24 (son day)?** Hesitant. My kids are grown. Name, hill, and day still matter for a grandson or a younger man in reach. They are not the finish of my road. Without season / next move / stay-with-brothers, I am buying a fathering course with a manhood intro, not the full Steadfast walk.

**Worth paying for:** even closer, ink on a date, a brother who asks, Cloth Store print that does not look downloaded, and the long-road weeks signed like 1–24. Kill any review-draft feel. No fake blurbs on a packet.
