# Week 1. Introduction + A quiet Tuesday
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Introduction and Chapter 1, A quiet Tuesday.

**Aim:** He sits down. He is on time. He looks at last Tuesday, not a future season.

**One job:** Sit down. You are on time. Last Tuesday is the day you answer for.

**Pitfall:** Do not lecture the book back. Do not rewrite the locked pages. Point at the Introduction and Chapter 1. Ask. Write the closer. If you talk more than they write, you missed it.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Say it with them. Do not swap a word.

### Open (2-3 min)

Glad they came. Mixed room on purpose. Nobody is a guest.

This week is not a later version of him. It is last Tuesday. The nineteen-year-old is on time. So is the man in the middle of a career. So is the man who thought he aged out. So is the single man, the husband, the dad, the man whose house broke. If he is holding the page, he sits.

Say the four. Then stop talking.

---

### Hit

The book already said it. You name the job in one breath.

A man can look busy and still not have sat down. He skims for the parts that apply to somebody else. He waits on a better week, a wife, a quieter season, a title that will finally make him serious. None of that is this page. He is not late to this. God is not checking IDs for age, a ring, a clean record, or a title.

Childhood is not only toys. It is a life that will not take a yes unless the mood is already there. Paul said a man puts those ways behind him. Special days will cover for him. A plain Tuesday will not. The little day is not practice for the real one. It is the real one.

Rest is good. Vanishing and calling it rest is not. Hiding leaves a gap. The cereal bowl will tell on him when the street will not.

Point them at the Introduction and Chapter 1. Do not perform either one. Do not open week 2.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. Where in your week do you keep an aisle instead of a seat?
2. What cover is closest right now: living on options, hiding in a good job, or treating a later season as the real start?
3. Who in reach already knows if you showed up last Tuesday?

Young: this day is the job you already have. Single: Tuesday is not a holding pattern until a wife arrives. Married: she needs you in the room before dinner, not a new version in December. Hurt: go slow; do not start with try harder; he can be wounded and still be in the room for a cereal bowl. Older: a 62-year-old can still put childish ways behind him. The job going well: a longer hour than anyone required is not the same thing as being a man.

---

### Run the closer

Paper out. Phones out for the text. Do not skip a beat.

**Name it.** Where did you disappear last Tuesday without calling it that?

**Do this week.** One meal without the phone. One small yes that will still be there if the feeling leaves. Write the move, not a speech.

My next move this Tuesday is:

**Tell a brother.** Text one man. Ask him what he did with his Tuesday. Let him ask you the same. Send it before they walk if you can.

**Carry this.** Tuesday is where a man is made, or he settles.

If he writes a paragraph, cut it to one line. If he writes nothing, sit until there is one honest sentence.
