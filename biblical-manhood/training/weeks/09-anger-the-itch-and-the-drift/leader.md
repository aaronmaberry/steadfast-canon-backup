# Week 9. Anger, the itch, and the drift
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 9, Anger, the itch, and the drift.

**Aim:** The hole bears fruit. Anger. The itch. The drift. Name which one is yours. Do not excuse present sin.

**One job:** When things get heavy, I blank. Anger, itch, or drift. One full sentence.

**Pitfall:** Do not reprint last week's chair as the whole hour. Do not open the Father who does not leave. Do not let him name all three so he faces none. Kind is not a pass. Present sin still counts.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Say it. Do not unpack it. This week is fruit, not the hole.

### Open (2-3 min)

Three men. Same table. Same eggs. One threw a tool across a shop. One was still on a phone at two. One felt almost nothing when his wife said she was living with a roommate. They would swear they have nothing in common. They took three exits from the same pain.

Say the four. Sit down.

---

### Hit

Pain does not sit still in a man. It goes out as anger, down as appetite, or away as absence. Most men call their home exit personality. It is not. It is how they learned not to feel something a long time ago.

A lot of male anger is grief with its fists up. Under every exit is a boy who is trying not to feel the chair. That explains the exit. It does not excuse the foothold.

Paul did not ban anger. He banned the overnight. A cracked day is a cracked door. The devil does not need a scene. He needs a place to stand.

The itch is comfort now without cost and without being known. Screen, bottle, feed, cart, sports book. Same itch. Flee, not manage.

The drift is leaving without moving. Body in the recliner. Attention somewhere else. The house leaks one evening at a time.

Do not heal him with next week's Father tonight. Name the exit. Get one refusal on the page.

Point at Chapter 9. Then shut up.

---

### Table

Three. One exit. Not a tour of all three.

1. When things get heavy, I blank. Anger, itch, or drift?
2. What is under the last time you took that exit?
3. What is the matched first move this week?

Young: watch the itch and the early drift. Single: a wife will not starve it. Married: she already knows; refuse it, skip the childhood speech. Hurt: the fruit is still his to stop feeding. Older: watch the drift and leftover heat; mellowed is not refused. The job going well: ambition and ease can both be footholds. Competence is not repentance.

---

### Run the closer

One exit. One refusal.

**Name it.** When things get heavy, I blank. Anger, itch, or drift. One full sentence, out loud, tonight.

**Do this week.** If it is anger, take your ninety seconds and then say what is under it. If it is the itch, tell one man the whole truth and let him hold the password. If it is the drift, give one evening to the room with the screens dark and one real question you wait for.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him your exit and the last time you took it, then ask him his, and neither of you gets to say I am fine.

**Carry this.** Out, down, or away, every exit leads out of the house. The pain does not need an exit. It needs a Father.

If he only tells the wound, he gets sympathy and keeps the foothold. Get the exit and the refusal on the page.
