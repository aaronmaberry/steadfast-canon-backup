# Week 11. When home never handed you off + Leave without slamming the door
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 11, When home never handed you off, and Chapter 12, Leave without slamming the door.

**Aim:** Name the handoff that never came. Then leave with honor. Kind. Not a slam. One hit on the double.

**One job:** Name who handed you off and when, or Nobody ever told me I was a man. Then say slam or orbit.

**Pitfall:** Do not bash a mother. Do not bash a father. Do not reprint the chair, the fruit, or last week's Father as the whole hour. Do not dump brothers. Do not let him send a speech. Two chapters. One hour. One hit.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Say it. Do not unpack it. Tonight is the house that would not let go, and how a man actually goes.

### Open (2-3 min)

A grown man can still orbit a house that never said go. Job, truck, a key that is his. Sunday still runs through the old kitchen. Then he either never takes his hand off the door, or he goes so hard the glass cracks.

Say the four. Sit down.

---

### Hit

Two pages. One hit.

Handoff: a man, a word, a weight, and a welcome. Most men got none of them, or got the weight without the word. You cannot hand yourself off. Ask an older man to say it. Mark a date.

Leaving: Genesis puts leave before united. Leave and honor, both. Knob or slam are still in the doorway. The knock is the design. Honor does not mean pretending. Tell the truth about the man. Hand the debt to God. Walk out gently, then knock when you come home.

Point at both chapters. Then shut up.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. Who handed you off and when, or Nobody ever told me I was a man?
2. Slam or orbit? Knob or slam, and what has it cost?
3. Ask one older man, or tell one younger man, and what is the leave move or knock date?

Young: the tell is whether his yes is his. Single: leave is not a married man's verb only. Married: he leaves; he does not send her in. Hurt: some houses held too tight; distance can be wisdom; a curse in the mouth is still a slam. Older: years did not hand him off; a grudge with years on it is not wisdom. The job going well: a title is not a departure.

---

### Run the closer

One handoff. One leave. One knock.

**Name it.** Who handed you off and when, or Nobody ever told me I was a man. Then say whether you slammed or kept orbiting. If leaving is the sharper edge for you this week, name knob or slam and what it has cost.

**Do this week.** If you are the younger man, ask one older man whether he sees a man in you, and take whatever he says. If you are the older man, tell one younger man one thing you have watched him do that a man does. And on the leaving side: one decision without clearing it first, told with love, or pray for your father by name every day, and if he is alive and safe, pick a date to go knock.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him whether you were handed off, the date you are going to write inside your Bible, which side of the door you have been standing on, and if the knock is coming, when.

**Carry this.** A man, a word, a weight, and a welcome. Nobody hands himself off. The Father already handed you off, and He keeps men ready to say it out loud. Honor the office, tell the truth about the man, hand the debt to God, and knock when you come home.

If he only complains about home, he told it wrong. Receive and leave. Both. One week.

### Solo note

Read both chapters in one sitting. Name the handoff. Do the ask or the tell. Do the leave move or set the knock date. Tell one man all of it.

Do not open week 12.
