# Week 4. Stop waiting
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 4, Stop waiting.

**Aim:** Stop waiting. Waiting is not peace. A man answers on his feet.

**One job:** Name the one thing you have been going to get to the longest, and how long.

**Pitfall:** Do not lecture the book back. Do not unpack the weight, going first, or God's pay tonight. Do not scold a wounded man into motion. Name one wait. Get one move on paper with a day and a noon.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Say it with them. Do not swap a word. Tonight is only the first mark.

:::margin
**James 4:17 NIV**
If anyone, then, knows the good they ought to do and doesn't do it, it is sin for them.
:::

---

### Open (2-3 min)

Glad they came. Mixed room on purpose. Nobody is a guest.

Last week he named the faintest mark. This week he lives the first one. He knows exactly what he is stepping over. He is going to get to it. That is the honest answer, and it is also the whole problem.

Say the four. Then stop talking.

---

### Hit

The book already said it. You name the job in one breath.

Passivity almost never shows up in its own clothes. It wears patience. It wears peace. It wears humility. It wears spirituality. It wears research. It wears busyness, which is the cleverest disguise of all. Motion is not the opposite of passivity. Motion is often how a man hides from the one thing standing still in the middle of his life.

God asked Adam where he was. He knew. The question was for Adam. Where are you?

James gave the wait a moral name. Knowing the good and not doing it is not caution. It is sin. Name the thing. Ask what you are really waiting for. Find the smallest real move. Move before noon. Expect it to feel wrong.

Point them at Chapter 4. Do not perform it. Do not start week 5.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. What is the one thing you have been going to get to the longest, and how long?
2. Who near you is guessing because you will not move?
3. What is the smallest real move, not the whole conversation, that you can do before noon?

Young: a wife is not a starting gun. Single: when I get married is the most dangerous sentence. Married: the turn toward her happens inside the chaos. Hurt: go slow; frozen is not the same as healed. Older: stop waiting to be asked; find the work. The job going well: hours are not the mark; ask what he is avoiding.

---

### Run the closer

Paper out. One wait. Not ten.

**Name it.** The one thing you have been going to get to the longest, and how long. Honestly.

**Do this week.** Smallest real move before noon on a working day. Do not wait for it to feel right. Put a day and a noon on the calendar before you leave.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him what you have been stepping over and what you did about it. Give him leave to ask you again next week.

**Carry this.** Where are you? He already knows. Answer Him on your feet.

If he only names the wait, he will get sympathy and stay seated. Get the move and the noon on the page.
