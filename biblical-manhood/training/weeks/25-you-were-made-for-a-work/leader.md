# Week 25. You were made for a work + Feelings are the dash, not the wheel
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 30, You were made for a work, and Chapter 31, Feelings are the dash, not the wheel.

**Aim:** You were made for a work. Feelings are information. They are not the driver.

**One job:** Name the hallway, then one piece of the prepared work this week. Name the feeling that drove this week, then do one true thing anyway.

**Pitfall:** Two chapters, one hour. Do not reprint last week's day as the whole night. Do not dump name or hill. Do not preview seasons or a written plan. Do not dump brothers. Kind. Depression and panic can be medical facts. This page is not a whole treatment.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone. Two chapters. One hit. Not two sermons.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Say it. Do not unpack it. Tonight is the work, then who is steering.

### Open (2-3 min)

The badge still works. The week still fills. He answers with a title. A title is a slot. Then he cancelled the true thing because he did not feel it yet. Key in the ignition. Mood did not arrive. He called it honesty. It was a dash light running the trip.

Say the four. Sit down.

---

### Hit

Handiwork. Made. In Christ Jesus. Toward good works that were already waiting. You do not invent a purpose in a journal so you can feel deep. You receive a life that already has work in it, and you enter it.

Passivity loves a hallway. A hallway does not ask you to be a man. It asks you to keep moving. A slot can pay you and still leave you rented. Prepared in advance does not mean the path will feel obvious. It means you are not a guest in your own week. Most of the good work does not arrive with a letterhead. It looks like an ordinary hour that needed a man.

The tell is not whether you like Mondays. The tell is whether the week is aimed at the good God prepared, or only at staying employed and unbothered. "I don't know what I'm called to" can be honest. It can also be a costume. You know the next true work in reach.

Then the dash. Heat is real. A warning is real. A man who ignores every light is a fool. A man who lets the needle choose the destination is a different fool. David talks to the feeling. He does not pretend it is not there. He does not hand it the keys. Downcast is named. Then a command: put your hope in God.

Passivity loves a later mood. Naming the feeling is not the same as obeying God. If you only name, you are still parked. Do both. Then go. Kind: a downcast soul is in the psalm. Get help if the lights will not quit. Then still do not hand the trip to the needle.

Point at both chapters. Then shut up.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. What have you been treating as a hallway? The slot. The hunt. The nothing.
2. What feeling drove you this week? The veto. The rush. The park.
3. What is one piece of the prepared work, and one true thing you will do anyway?

Young: the have-to job still counts; the crush and the slight will feel like the whole map; steer anyway. Single: the work is already the life; a future wife is not coming to regulate your weather. Married: she is not the project that replaces your work; she is not required to manage your mood; you already go to work on a day you did not feel like it. Hurt: go slow; a lost title is not a lost making; feelings were either everything or nothing in some houses; depression can be a medical fact. Older: late is not never; a polished reason for parking can still be a mood with better vocabulary. The job going well: you already finish; the danger is finishing the wrong pile; you already override feelings to close, then hand the house to the needle.

---

### Run the closer

One hit. One blank. Do not make a tired man finish four jobs.

**Name it.** What have you been treating as a hallway? What feeling drove you this week?

**Do this week.** Pick ONE: hallway/slot (one piece of the prepared work) OR dash/true-thing (name the feeling, do one true thing anyway). Do not abandon both.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him which blank you picked and what you did. Have him ask if you did it, or if the needle got the trip again.

**Carry this.** You were made for a work. Feelings are the dash, not the wheel.

If he only hears that you want purpose, or that you have been stressed, he nods and parks again. Get one blank done.
