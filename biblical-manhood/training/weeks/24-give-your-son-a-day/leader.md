# Week 24. Give your son a day
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 29, Give your son a day he will not forget.

**Aim:** The day is the marker. The Tuesdays are the handoff. A stone with no story is only a rock. Series close. No Part 5 on this mic.

**One job:** Name whether you got a day, what you used instead, and whether your son has one coming. Then put date, men, place, token, and the four names on the calendar.

**Pitfall:** Do not reprint the name as the whole hour. Do not reprint the hill as the whole night. Do not dump Part 5. Kind. Men with no son stay. Keep single-ownership / no-son seat + first-year beat on the page. Son trilogy closes tonight.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Say it. Do not unpack it. Tonight is the marker.

### Open (2-3 min)

Rented pavilion. Cake. Phones up. Leftover sheet cake in a box. He thinks he marked something. A party is not a crossing. No stone. No sentence. No before and after.

Say the four. Sit down.

---

### Hit

What do these stones mean. Markers. Days. Stones a man can point to. Without that day he guesses. Counterfeits. Give him the real one.

Not a party. A marker: men, a word, a weight, a welcome. Father plus two to four men who will still be around in ten years. Outside. Early. Somewhere with weight. Each man brings one true thing: a story, a failure and what he did after, a charge. Father brings the three names and the fourth. A token he can hold. Prayer. Welcome. Then breakfast where the men talk to him like a man.

The day changes nothing by itself. Tuesday makes it real. Marker without Tuesday is a stone with no story.

If you never had a day, you can still give one. If you have no son in the house, stay. Ask to stand at a day, or ask a mother about a fatherless boy, in the light.

First-year dad: you are not throwing a pavilion at 3am. Put a date and a sentence on a page. A seed is still a stone. Do not lengthen this into a second sermon.

Point at Chapter 29. Then shut up. No Part 5 tonight.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. Did you get a day, and what did you use instead if not?
2. Does your son have one coming, or has he been guessing?
3. Date, men, place, token: what goes on the calendar this week?

Young: receive one if you can; mark a younger man if you cannot set a son's day yet. Single: you are not a guest; be ready with one true thing. Married: you set the date; not a pavilion party. Hurt: go slow; no day is a wound. Older: grandson; grown son still needs a stone; never-a-son is not a mascot. The job going well: you already mark a close at work; put a date on the page for the boy.

---

### Run the closer

One date. One stone. One handoff.

**Name it.** Whether you got a day, what you used instead, and whether your son has one coming. Out loud tonight. (Son in the house, or younger man in reach? Both own the page.)

**Do this week.** Date. Men. Place. Token. Three names and the fourth written. On the calendar. If no son, ask to stand at a day, or ask a mother about a fatherless boy.

**Tell a brother.** Ask him to be one of the men. Tell him what to prepare: one story, one failure, one charge. If he has a son, ask when his day is and offer to stand.

**Carry this.** The day is the marker. The Tuesdays are the handoff. A stone with no story is only a rock.

If he only hears that he should throw a better party, he nods and goes back to the pavilion. Get the date and the men on the page.
