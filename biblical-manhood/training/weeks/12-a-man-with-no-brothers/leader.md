# Week 12. A man with no brothers + Ask the man
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 13, A man with no brothers, and Chapter 14, Ask the man.

**Aim:** Isolation is not strength. Name the aloneness. Then ask a man. Do not wait to be chosen. One hit on the double.

**One job:** If I fell tonight, the man I could call is blank. Then name the man twenty years ahead you have never asked.

**Pitfall:** Two chapters, one hit. Do not preach both as two sermons. Do not reprint last week's door. Do not dump the next inner-problem page. Do not let a wife or a work team pass as brothers. Do not let him build a twelve-week binder. One hour.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Say it. Do not unpack it. Tonight is who has him, then the ask.

### Open (2-3 min)

The truck is in the driveway. The week is closed. His phone has more names than he will ever use. He can be the one they call. Then the house goes quiet, and there is nobody he would call if he went down. He calls that independence.

Say the four. Sit down.

---

### Hit

One hit. Name the aloneness, then the ask. Do not preach a second sermon.

A brother is a man who can tell you the truth and still be there after it. Twenty minutes. One man. Once a week. Same time. What happened. What is heavy. What is next. A prayer.

Then, the ask. Nobody picks you off a wall. There is a man about twenty years ahead of you waiting to be asked. One real question. When he says ride with me, go. Older men: be askable. Give one young man something to say yes to.

Point at both chapters. Then shut up.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. If I fell tonight, the man I could call is blank. Say the blank, or the name.
2. Who is the man twenty years ahead you have never asked, or the young man you have watched and never spoken to?
3. What is the brother sentence, and what is the one real question?

Young: awkward is allowed. Single: do not park this in a future marriage. Married: tell her you asked; do not make her the wise man. Hurt: asking is not groveling. Older: be askable; walk across the lot and give one young man something to say yes to. Mid-arc: the brother check starts now. The job going well: ask a man who does not report to you. Tight season: parking-lot or text-sized ask still counts.

---

### Run the closer

One blank. One ask. One question.

**Name it.** If I fell tonight, the man I could call is blank. Say the blank out loud, or the name. Then name the man twenty years ahead you have never asked, or the young man you have watched and never spoken to.

**Do this week.** Pick the brother and say the sentence before the week is out: I am trying to be a better man and I do not have a brother; would you talk with me twenty minutes a week for three months. Set the day and time before you hang up. Tight season (parking lot / text): Got twenty minutes next week? Trying to walk with a wiser man. Day and time? And if you are the younger man toward the man ahead, ask him one real question this week and go do whatever he tells you. If you are the older man, give one young man something to say yes to: a breakfast, a job, a call. Be askable if a younger man walks up.

**Tell a brother.** If you have one, tell him about the twenty minutes and the three questions and start. If you do not, the asking is the whole job this week, and it is supposed to feel awkward. Tell him also the question you asked the man ahead and what he said, and let him hold you to doing it and going back to report.

**Carry this.** Twenty minutes with one man, once a week, at the same time: what happened, what's heavy, what's next, and a prayer. There is a man twenty years ahead of you waiting to be asked. Ask him one real question, and when he says ride with me, go.

If he only confesses the wait, he gets a nod and stays undrafted. Get the blank and the ask on the page.
