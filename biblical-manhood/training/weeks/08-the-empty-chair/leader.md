# Week 8. The empty chair
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 8, The empty chair.

**Aim:** The father hole is real. Name the empty chair. Do not heal it all on this page.

**One job:** Finish this sentence: The chair at the end of my table was blank. Then say one thing it taught you.

**Pitfall:** Do not recap Part 1. Do not open next week's fruit. Do not open the Father who does not leave. Do not rush him to a cure. Do not make his dad a devil. Do not let the chair excuse this week's sin. Kind. Slow. Naming is the hour.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Say it. Do not unpack it. This week is the chair. Part 2 starts here.

:::margin
**Ephesians 6:4 NIV**
Fathers, do not exasperate your children; instead, bring them up in the training and instruction of the Lord.
:::

---

### Open (2-3 min)

A kitchen table still has to eat. Three plates go down. One chair sits with nothing in front of it, or a man sits in it and the boy still cannot get to him. Years later a grown man stands in a doorway before he walks into his own full table. He knows why. He has not said it yet.

Say the four. Then stop. We tell the truth about what he inherited.

---

### Hit

You are not healing him tonight. You are putting a name on a hole.

Paul named what belonged in that chair. Not a perfect man. A father. A voice a boy could get to. Training. Instruction. The Lord in the room, not only a paycheck. If that was not his house, he is not inventing a wound.

Some chairs never arrived. Some left. Some died and the plate still gets set. Some came home already gone. Some were a storm. Some were good and still left a gap shaped like the places that man could not reach. Every man in the room inherited something. Do not sand his to match yours.

The chair explains him. It does not excuse him. What sat in the chair was handed to him. What he does with the chair is his. Understanding that forever without changing anything is not progress. Naming is.

Do not preach next week's Father as tonight's cure. Do not open the fruit. Point at Chapter 8. Then shut up. Sit in the silence if you have to.

---

### Table

Three. Wait longer than you want to.

1. Finish this out loud: The chair at the end of my table was blank.
2. What one thing did that chair teach you?
3. Can you say one true sentence about your father without a speech?

Young: stop waiting for a ghost to sit down before you become a man. Single: a future wife cannot sit in that chair. Married: he can father with a hole; he must not dump the chair into her lap. Hurt: cruel is not the same as empty, and both are real. Older: a door can close before the conversation; he can still say what needed saying. The job going well: hours will not father him, and they will not father his kids if all they get is the last ten minutes.

---

### Run the closer

One chair. One sentence. One letter he keeps.

**Name it.** Finish this sentence out loud tonight: The chair at the end of my table was blank. Then say one thing it taught you.

**Do this week.** Write your father a letter you will not send. Tell him what you needed, what you did not get, and what you are grateful for, if anything. Keep it where you can find it. You will want it later in this course.

**Tell a brother.** Read him the sentence you finished, not the letter unless you want to, and let him answer with me too or I am sorry without either of you hurrying past it.

**Carry this.** What sat in the chair was handed to you. What you do with the chair is yours.

If a brother starts a cure, stop him. This chapter is the naming. He cannot sit in that chair for him. He can hear him.
