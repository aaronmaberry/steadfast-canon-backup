# Week 5. Pick up the weight
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 5, Pick up the weight.

**Aim:** Pick up the weight. The weight that is yours does not move until you put a hand on it.

**One job:** Write one line that starts with That is mine and ends at the period. No but.

**Pitfall:** Do not preach last week's wait. Do not preach going first or God's pay. Do not dump a second verse as a second sermon. Do not let him hide in other men's crises. One sentence. One period. One hand.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Say it. Do not unpack it. Tonight is the second mark.

:::margin
**Galatians 6:5 NIV**
for each one should carry their own load.
:::

---

### Open (2-3 min)

He stood up last week. Standing is not carrying. A corner post takes the strain of the whole run. Nobody drives past a fence and admires the corners. They only notice when one leans.

Say the four. Sit down.

---

### Hit

You are putting a name on a load.

Blame is a man setting down weight that is his and pointing at whoever is standing closest. Fairness is how a boy thinks. A man takes responsibility for what is in front of him, not only for what he caused.

Paul says each one should carry their own load. A load is a man's own pack. Carry it without complaint. Presence is part of provision, not a luxury he earns after.

Say That is mine. Stop at the period. No silent but. Own the outcome, not just the effort. Carry your load before you reach for a crushing burden. When it is too heavy, ask for help. That is not setting the weight down.

Point at Chapter 5. Then shut up. Do not start week 6.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. Can you say That is mine about one weight without a but after it?
2. Where have you been providing everything for the house except yourself?
3. What is one non-money provision you will do every day this week?

Young: before you carry for anybody else, carry yourself. Single: the life is already yours. Married: the state of the marriage is yours; she needs you in the room. Hurt: go slow; some loads were never his; this page is the one that still is. Older: ask to be let back in; start small. The job going well: a scoreboard at work is not the table at 6:30.

---

### Run the closer

One weight. Not ten.

**Name it.** One line: That is mine. Period. Say it out loud tonight. If it still has a silent but, cross the but out before he sleeps.

**Do this week.** One non-money provision a day. If single, one thing you have been letting someone else carry. Say the five out loud once: my life, my house, my work, my people, what I did wrong.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him your That is mine sentence and what you did about it. Ask him to check on it next week.

**Carry this.** Provision that costs your presence is not provision. It is a very expensive way to disappear.

If he only confesses the pile, he will get a nod and keep walking around it. Get the period and the hand on the page.
