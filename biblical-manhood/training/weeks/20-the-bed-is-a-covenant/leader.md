# Week 20. The bed is a covenant
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 25, The bed is a covenant.

**Aim:** The bed is a vow kept with a body. A contract has two ends. A covenant has all of you.

**One job:** Name where the crack is, if there is one. Or whether you have been building or spending. Then keep the week as covenant.

**Pitfall:** Do not reprint last week's dying or last week's gift as the whole hour. Do not dump a sex manual. Do not preview daughters. Kind: empty side is not a punchline. Singles stay. Honored by all.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Say it. Do not unpack it. Tonight is the handshake God put in the house.

:::margin
**Hebrews 13:4 NIV**
Marriage should be honored by all, and the marriage bed kept pure, for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral.
:::

---

### Open (2-3 min)

Wednesday. Towels from the dryer down the middle of the bed. He sits on the edge, looks at the stack, and sleeps on his half like a man checking into a room he already paid for. Handshake without a handshake.

Say the four. Sit down.

---

### Hit

Honored by all. Kept pure. A contract protects you if the other party fails. A covenant binds you regardless. The bed is that handshake.

Exclusive, all the way down. Not the neighbor's. Not the screen's. Not the memory's. Generous. Present. That is the keep. A screen is a cheaper bed. It asks nothing. It keeps nothing. Stop managing it. Tell a man who will not flinch.

Seasons change what keeping looks like. Some of you are not in the bed at all. Some of you are in the bed and still not in it.

If you are single, stay. Honored by all is your sentence. You are building it or spending it. If you are single again, single again is single. Widower: keep it pure in the empty years the way you kept it in the full ones.

If the bed is injured, you do not white-knuckle that with a worksheet. A doctor. A pastor. A man who can stand it.

Point at Chapter 25. Then shut up.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. Where is the crack, if there is one?
2. Have you been building or spending?
3. What keep this week: conversation, filter, kitchen charger?

Young: you already have a bed; a girlfriend is not a trial membership. Single: you are not a guest; honored by all is your sentence. Married: she already knows if the bed is a covenant or a storage unit. Hurt: go slow; empty side is not a punchline. Older: two polite cliffs is not a finished vow. The job going well: a hotel can get more presence than the vow.

---

### Run the closer

One crack. One keep.

**Name it.** Where the crack is, if there is one. Or whether you have been building or spending. Out loud to God tonight.

**Do this week.** If married, one gentle honest conversation about the bed, not in the bed, asking more of and less of. If single or divorced, filter with a brother's password and phone charging in the kitchen tonight.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him the crack you named. If it is the screen, tell him the whole truth: what, how often, since when. Let him hold the password.

**Carry this.** The bed is a vow kept with a body. A contract has two ends. A covenant has all of you.

If he only hears that he should try harder in bed, he nods and goes back to the spare room. Get the crack and the keep on the page.
