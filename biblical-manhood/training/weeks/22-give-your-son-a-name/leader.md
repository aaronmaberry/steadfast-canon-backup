# Week 22. Give your son a name
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 27, Give your son a name.

**Aim:** A boy answers to whatever he is called. If you do not name your son, the world will.

**One job:** Name what your son has been hearing from you, even if the honest answer is sigh and eye roll. Then catch one man act daily, and bless once with a hand on his head.

**Pitfall:** Do not reprint last week's daughter page as the whole hour. Do not dump the bed. Do not preview a hill. Kind. Men with no son stay. Keep single-ownership / no-son seat on the page. The younger man in reach still has ears.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Say it. Do not unpack it. Tonight is the mouth.

### Open (2-3 min)

Saturday. Rec complex. League jersey still in the bag. Last name heat-pressed a little high. Folding chair. Coffee gone cold. The boy at the chain-link twists the collar to read his own name. The jersey has a name. The father has not given one.

Say the four. Sit down.

---

### Hit

Summoned you by name. You are mine. A father who will not open his mouth leaves a hole, and the world is full of names ready to fill it.

He is already being named. At school. By the culture. By the sigh and the eye roll at home. What you call him, he becomes.

Three names, over and over. Mine. What I see in you. Man. Belonging first. Character, not a scoreboard. The handoff word, on purpose, out loud.

How: daily catch and call it. One night, hand on his head, three sentences. When he fails, separate the act from the name.

If you have no son in the house, stay. Nephew. The boy you coach. The kid who stays after. Say the true name once this week to the younger man who can hear you. Name one fatherless boy's act out loud, in the light.

Point at Chapter 27. Then shut up.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. What has your son been hearing from you, even if sigh and eye roll?
2. What names have you been giving without meaning to?
3. Where will you catch and bless this week?

Young: you are still waiting for a name. Single: you are not a guest; nephew, the boy you coach. Married: this is your page if you have a son; daughters-only still has boys in reach. Hurt: go slow; named by a wound is not a joke. Older: a grown son still has ears; never-a-son is not a mascot. The job going well: name on the door; boy gets hey from the hallway.

---

### Run the closer

One false name. One true keep.

**Name it.** What your son has been hearing from you, even if the honest answer is sigh and eye roll. Out loud tonight. (Son in the house, or younger man in reach? Both own the page.)

**Do this week.** Every day, catch one thing a man does and name it. One night, hand on his head, three sentences. If no son, name one fatherless boy's act out loud.

**Tell a brother.** Tell him the names you have been giving without meaning to. Ask what his father called him. Ask what he is calling his own son.

**Carry this.** A boy answers to whatever he is called. If you don't name your son, the world will, and the world has no good names for boys.

If he only hears that he should yell less, he nods and goes back to the shrug. Get the names on the page.
