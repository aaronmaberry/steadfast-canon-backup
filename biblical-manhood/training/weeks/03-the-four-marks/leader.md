# Week 3. The four marks
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 3, The four marks.

**Aim:** The four as one sentence in his mouth. Not four sermons. Unpacking is weeks 4 through 7.

**One job:** Name which of the four is faintest on you right now, in a full sentence, and why.

**Pitfall:** Do not lecture the book back. Do not unpack the wait, the weight, going first, or God's pay tonight. Do not improve the line. Get the faint mark named and the four on a card. A mark is what he does over and over, not what he feels.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Say it with them. Do not swap a word.

### Open (2-3 min)

Glad they came. Mixed room on purpose. Nobody is a guest.

Last week he named the crossed maps. Tonight he gets the sentence that can hold. One sentence. Four marks. Not a stack of talks. Do not try to fix all four this week.

Say the four. Then stop talking.

---

### Hit

The book already said it. You name the job in one breath.

A mark is not a feeling or a title. It is what is left on a man by what he has done over and over. Nobody gets a callus from thinking about a shovel. The four are things you can see on a man the way you could see a fence stretcher on a grandfather's palm.

Who was with her. Three words. Passivity almost never looks like laziness. It looks like patience. It looks like keeping the peace. The first man stood there, and that was enough to break everything. Tonight is not a garden lecture. It is the sentence.

A man will live some sentence. The only question is which. Personality is not a loophole. A job title will not do this work for him. Hearing the line is not having it. The old maps are already practiced. This one is not.

Point them at Chapter 3. Do not perform it. Do not start week 4.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. Which of the four marks is faintest on you right now, and why?
2. Where would this sentence have to work this week, on an actual person, or it is only a hobby?
3. If a brother named a different faint mark than you, would you look again?

Young: a sentence at last; write it until you can say it. Single: this is already his life. Married: she already knows which mark is faint. Hurt: go slow; the marks are not a scolding. Older: he can still give the line. The job going well: four marks are not a title.

---

### Run the closer

Paper out. Do not skip a beat.

**Name it.** Which of the four is faintest on you right now? Full sentence, and why.

**Do this week.** Write the four marks on an index card in your own handwriting. Visor or mirror. Ordinary morning.

**Tell a brother.** Read him the sentence. Ask which mark he would say is faintest on you. Let him finish before you answer.

**Carry this.** Find the mark that is faintest on you and hold that one for a while.

If he agrees and leaves unchanged, you missed it. Get the faint mark named and the card where his eyes go before he walks.

---

### Solo note

If he is alone, he still reads Chapter 3, names the faint mark with why, writes the card the same day, and tells one man. Ask which mark he would say is faintest on you. Do not explain first.
