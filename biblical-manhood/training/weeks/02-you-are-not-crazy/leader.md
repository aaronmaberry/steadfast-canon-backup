# Week 2. You are not crazy
## Leader guide

**Steadfast Men**
*The way of Godly Men*
Wes Hart

### Before the room

**Read this week:** Chapter 2, You are not crazy.

**Aim:** The confusion is in the world. He was handed crossed maps. He is not crazy for wanting a north.

**One job:** Name two contradictory instructions you were handed about being a man, and where each came from.

**Pitfall:** Do not lecture the book back. Do not make this the father chapter, the Tuesday chapter, or a four-marks sermon. Point. Ask. Get two manuals and one sentence on paper.

**Time:** about an hour. Church, small group, or a man alone.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Say it with them. Do not swap a word. Do not unpack the marks tonight.

:::margin
**Genesis 1:27 NIV**
So God created mankind in his own image,
in the image of God he created them;
male and female he created them.
:::

---

### Open (2-3 min)

Glad they came. Mixed room on purpose. Nobody is a guest.

Last week he sat down. This week he names the crossed maps. He can run a shop and still go quiet when the question is what a man is. One crowd treats manhood like a punchline. The other treats it like a weapon. He is not crazy for wanting a third thing: a sentence he could actually walk by.

Say the four. Then stop talking.

---

### Hit

The book already said it. You name the job in one breath.

The empty feeling is not proof he is broken. He was handed pictures that do not share a north. Work left the land and left the house. Rooms got shy about saying God made men on purpose. The old words got treated like a problem. Boys still grew. Men came out waiting, performing, or quietly ashamed. The mess and the shrink are cousins.

A tired shrug can pass for wisdom. A younger man will copy it.

God was not guessing. Genesis 1:27 is a making, not a costume and not a mood. The Book is not silent. We got embarrassed, or busy, or clever, and we stopped handing a sentence over. Part of the gap is him. Part of it was the maps. Own both. Do not use one as cover for the other.

Point them at Chapter 2. Do not perform it. Do not open week 3.

---

### Table

Three. Wait.

1. Name two contradictory instructions you were handed about being a man. Where did each one come from?
2. Which is closer right now: you wait, you perform for the room, or you carry a quiet hum of shame?
3. In one sentence, what do you believe a man is supposed to do?

Young: the guessing is not proof you are behind. Single: a wife is not the start of manhood. Married: provider is a real weight and it is not the whole of you. Hurt: go slow; he can be wounded and still not crazy. Older: the years do not retire you from this. The job going well: hitting every room's target is not the same as having a north.

---

### Run the closer

Paper out. Do not skip a beat.

**Name it.** Two contradictory instructions you were handed about being a man, and where each came from. One sentence each.

**Do this week.** Write one sentence what you believe a man is supposed to do, before next week's marks unpack. Keep the paper.

What I believe a man is supposed to do:

**Tell a brother.** Read him your sentence. Ask for his. Do not explain first.

**Carry this.** The confusion is in the world. You were handed crossed maps. The answer is simpler and harder than anything on the shelf.

If he writes the sentence he wishes, have him write the one he would say if nobody was grading him. Ugly on paper beats maps that never shared a north.

---

### Solo note

If he is alone, he still reads Chapter 2, names the two instructions and writes his sentence the same day, and reads that sentence to one man. Ask for his. Do not explain first. A private project he means to finish later is how this week dies.

Do not open week 3 until Dean says.
