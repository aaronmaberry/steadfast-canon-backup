## Script

## 00:00 Hook

[B: early light on worn stone steps beside still water, a rolled mat at the edge]
There was a man who lay beside the same pool for thirty-eight years.
[beat]
Jesus walked up to him and asked him one question.
[A: WIDE]
"Do you want to get well?"
[beat]
That sounds almost rude. Of course he wants to get well.
But the man didn't say yes.
What he said instead is what I say, and I'd bet it's what you say too.

## 00:27 Setup

[A: MED]
I'm Wes Hart. This is Steadfast Men.
Today is the first mark of a steadfast man. Stop Waiting.
[TEXT: Stop Waiting]
By the end you'll have one thing you're done waiting on, and the first ten minutes of it on your calendar before Friday.
And I'll tell you about a bungee cord on my back gate.
It's not a proud story.

## 00:51 The man by the pool

[GFX: The man by the pool]
[B: a covered stone colonnade, shadows across the floor]
This is in John, chapter five.
There's a pool in Jerusalem called Bethesda, with covered porches around it.
Under those porches lay a crowd of people who couldn't get around on their own.
[CARD: John 5:5-6 (NIV) "One who was there had been an invalid for thirty-eight years. When Jesus saw him lying there and learned that he had been in this condition for a long time, he asked him, 'Do you want to get well?'"]
"One who was there had been an invalid for thirty-eight years.
When Jesus saw him lying there and learned that he had been in this condition for a long time, he asked him, 'Do you want to get well?'"
[pause 1s]
Thirty-eight years.
[beat]
If you were born the year that man lay down, you'd be pushing forty with a mortgage and a bad knee by the time Jesus walked up.
Here's what he says back.
[CARD: John 5:7 (NIV) "'Sir,' the invalid replied, 'I have no one to help me into the pool when the water is stirred. While I am trying to get in, someone else goes down ahead of me.'"]
"Sir, I have no one to help me into the pool when the water is stirred.
While I am trying to get in, someone else goes down ahead of me."
[A: MED]
Listen to that.
Jesus asked him a yes or no question.
He answered with a reason.
[TEXT: He answered with a reason.]
And it was a good reason. Nobody would help him. Somebody always beat him to the water.
All true.
[beat]
I have a whole drawer full of good reasons.
So do you.

## 02:11 The gate

[GFX: The gate]
[A: WIDE]
So. My back gate.
The latch broke two Thanksgivings ago.
The post leaned, the latch quit catching, and the gate would swing open in any decent wind.
So I tied it shut with a bungee cord.
Temporary.
[B: a frayed bungee cord hooked around a weathered wood gate post]
I needed a new latch. Probably a new post. That meant digging out the old concrete, and the post-hole digger was at my brother-in-law's, and it was supposed to rain.
Every one of those reasons was true.
That bungee cord made it through two Christmases.
My wife asked about it twice. Then she quit asking, which is worse.
[beat]
Every time I went through that gate I'd unhook the cord and think, I need to fix that.
I bet I thought it a hundred and fifty times.
I wasn't lazy about it. I was waiting.
Waiting on a free Saturday. Waiting till I felt like it.
[pause 1s]
That's the man at the pool.
Not the thirty-eight years. The reason.

## 03:18 What Stop Waiting means

[GFX: Stop Waiting]
[A: MED]
James says it about as plain as anybody ever has.
[CARD: James 4:17 (NIV) "If anyone, then, knows the good they ought to do and doesn't do it, it is sin for them."]
"If anyone, then, knows the good they ought to do and doesn't do it, it is sin for them."
[A: CU]
Most of us think of sin as the bad thing we did.
James says it's also the good thing we knew to do and kept putting off.
[beat]
That's the first mark. Stop Waiting.
[TEXT: When it's yours to do, start now.]
When something is yours to do, you start now, before you feel like it.
Here's what makes waiting so slippery.
It doesn't feel like a decision. It feels like you haven't decided yet.
But every day you wait, you decide again.
[B: a sticky note on a truck dash, sun-faded]
Think about the thing you've meant to do for a year.
The doctor appointment. The apology. Getting a will done. Reading the Bible with your kids.
Every morning you didn't, you weren't standing still.
You picked waiting again.
[pause 1s]
[SUB-MID]
If you're tired of waiting on yourself, subscribe. Two of these a week, Tuesday and Friday.

## 04:27 But he couldn't walk

[GFX: But he couldn't walk]
[SHORT 1 IN]
[A: MED]
Now you might be thinking, Wes, come on. That man couldn't walk.
It wasn't a gate. He was paralyzed. You can't tell a man like that to quit waiting.
[beat]
Fair. Look at what Jesus says to him.
[CARD: John 5:8 (NIV) "Then Jesus said to him, 'Get up! Pick up your mat and walk.'"]
"Get up! Pick up your mat and walk."
[A: CU]
Jesus didn't tell him to try harder.
He didn't hand him a plan to get to the water faster.
He gave him a command the man couldn't possibly obey on his own.
And the man obeyed it.
[beat]
Jesus brought the power. The man brought the yes.
[TEXT: He brings the power. You bring the yes.]
That's how grace works all the way down.
You don't earn being made new. He does that.
But when he tells you to get up, you get up.
Stop Waiting isn't a pep talk. It's answering him.
[SHORT 1 OUT]
[pause 1s]
[A: MED]
Some of what you're waiting on, you really can't fix by yourself. A marriage. An addiction. A grief that won't lift.
You still have a first move. Make the call. Tell a man. Get the help.
The getting up is yours. The healing is his.

## 05:42 Six years

[GFX: Six years]
[SHORT 2 IN]
[B: a two-lane county road between fence lines, round bales in a field]
Let me tell you about a man. I'll call him Dale.
Dale and his younger brother had a falling-out at their father's funeral.
It was about land. Forty acres their dad left to the both of them, and they couldn't agree on whether to sell.
Words got said in the funeral home parking lot. The kind you can't unsay.
They didn't talk for six years.
[A: MED]
Dale told me he thought about calling every one of those years. Christmas especially.
And he had a reason every time.
His brother said worse. His brother should call first. It had been too long. It would be awkward.
All true.
[beat]
One spring Dale heard his brother had hurt his back and was behind on cutting hay.
Dale didn't call. He figured if he called, they'd just fight on the phone.
He drove over on a Saturday morning with his work gloves and a cooler.
[B: hands pulling on worn leather work gloves beside a pickup on a gravel drive]
His brother walked out of the barn, saw him, and stood there a long time.
Then he pointed at the trailer.
They loaded hay till dark.
Dale said they barely talked. Nobody made a speech.
At the end of it his brother handed him a cold drink out of Dale's own cooler and said, "Took you long enough."
[pause 2s]
[BREATHER 4s: truck taillights pulling off a gravel drive at dusk, room tone only]
Six years of waiting. One Saturday of getting up.
They're not best friends. But they eat Thanksgiving together now.
[SHORT 2 OUT]

## 07:25 Pick up your mat

[GFX: Pick up your mat]
[A: MED]
Go back to the pool for a second.
Jesus says, pick up your mat.
For thirty-eight years that mat carried him.
Now he carries it.
[beat]
I think about that a lot.
The thing you've been lying on, the excuse, the waiting, you don't leave it there for somebody else to trip over.
You roll it up and carry it home. So you remember where you were, and who got you up.

## 07:57 Your move this week

[GFX: This week]
[A: MED]
So here's your move.
[TEXT: Name one thing. Ten minutes before Friday.]
Name one thing you know is yours to do and you've been waiting on.
Not five things. One.
Then do the first ten minutes of it before Friday. Put it in your phone with a time on it.
[B: a phone calendar on a kitchen table, a thumb setting a reminder]
If you're married, it might be the conversation you keep putting off with your wife, or the gate, or the budget. Pick the one she'd pick.
If you're single, it might be the call to your dad, the debt, or the church you keep meaning to visit. Pick the one you'd be most relieved to have done.
Then tell one man what it is. Text him tonight.
"I'm starting this before Friday."
That's it.

## 08:47 Recap

[A: CU]
Stop Waiting. When it's yours to do, start now, before you feel like it.
He brings the power. You bring the yes.
[beat]
And the gate.
I fixed it this past spring. New post, new latch.
It took me forty minutes.
[beat]
Forty minutes. After two years.
[pause 1s]

## 09:08 Next

[END SCREEN 20s]
[A: WIDE]
Friday's episode is a short one. It's about one word I say way too much, and a verse in Proverbs that catches me saying it.
It's called "Stop Saying Tomorrow."
Subscribe, and it'll be there Friday.
[beat]
Go get up.
