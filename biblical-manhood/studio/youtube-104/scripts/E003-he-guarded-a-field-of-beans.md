## Script

## 00:00 Hook

[B: a wide field of low green plants under a gray sky, wind moving through it]
There's a list in the Bible of David's mightiest soldiers.
Most of them are famous for things like killing hundreds of men, or a giant, or a lion in a pit on a snowy day.
And one of them is famous for standing in a field of beans.
[beat]
Lentils, if you want to be exact about it.
[A: WIDE]
The whole army ran. He didn't. And God thought that was worth writing down.

## 00:30 Setup

[A: MED]
I'm Wes Hart. This is Steadfast Men.
This is the second mark of a steadfast man. Pick Up the Weight.
[TEXT: Pick Up the Weight]
By the end you'll know which field is yours, and one spot where you've been waiting for somebody else to stand.
And I'll tell you about a cedar tree, a fence, and my dog. That's how I learned this one.

## 00:55 Shammah's field

[GFX: The field]
This is Second Samuel, chapter twenty-three.
[CARD: 2 Samuel 23:11-12 (NIV) "Next to him was Shammah son of Agee the Hararite. When the Philistines banded together at a place where there was a field full of lentils, Israel's troops fled from them. But Shammah took his stand in the middle of the field. He defended it and struck the Philistines down, and the LORD brought about a great victory."]
"Next to him was Shammah son of Agee the Hararite.
When the Philistines banded together at a place where there was a field full of lentils, Israel's troops fled from them.
But Shammah took his stand in the middle of the field.
He defended it and struck the Philistines down, and the LORD brought about a great victory."
[pause 1s]
[A: MED]
Don't skip past the details in there.
Start with the field. It was lentils.
It wasn't a city or a palace. It was a field of lentils.
[B: a hand brushing dirt off small plants in a dry row]
Somebody planted those. Somebody's family was going to eat them that winter.
It was ordinary ground. But that day it was his ground to hold.
Most of us want our stand to be somewhere that matters. A big moment. People watching.
Shammah got a bean field and no crowd. That's most of life.
Your field might be a kitchen table at six-thirty in the morning. A job site. A hospital waiting room. Your kid's bedroom door at night.
Nobody writes a song about it. God still keeps the list.
[beat]
Then look at everybody else. They ran.
The text doesn't call the army cowards. It just says they fled.
Shammah could've run with them, and nobody would've blamed him. He'd have been one more man in the crowd.
Their running didn't change what was his.
[TEXT: Their running didn't change what was his.]
And then the last line. Shammah took his stand. And the LORD brought about a great victory.
Shammah stood. God won.
Hang on to that one. We'll need it in a few minutes.

## 02:45 Half the tree

[GFX: Half the tree]
[SHORT 2 IN]
[A: WIDE]
So, the cedar.
A couple of springs back, a storm took down a big cedar right on the line between my yard and my neighbor's.
It landed across the fence and flattened about twelve feet of it.
[B: a downed cedar lying across a post-and-wire fence after rain]
Whose tree was it? Good question.
The trunk was about a foot over on his side.
So I figured he'd deal with it.
And he figured, I found out later, that I'd deal with it, because most of it fell into my yard.
So we both waited.
Three weeks.
We'd wave at each other over that tree. Nice as you please.
[beat]
Then one morning my dog walked right over the flat part of the fence and was gone.
Found her two streets over, very pleased with herself, in a stranger's trash.
[A: MED]
That evening I went out with a chainsaw. About ten minutes later my neighbor came out with his.
We had it cut and stacked and the fence back up by dark.
[pause 1s]
Here's what I had to admit.
Half that tree was mine.
I'd stood in my own yard for three weeks waiting on him to pick up his half, like that let me off for mine.
[SHORT 2 OUT]

## 04:09 What Pick Up the Weight means

[GFX: Pick Up the Weight]
[A: MED]
Paul says it in Galatians, chapter six.
[CARD: Galatians 6:5 (NIV) "for each one should carry their own load."]
"For each one should carry their own load."
[A: CU]
That's the second mark. Pick Up the Weight.
[TEXT: Carry what's yours, even when somebody else dropped theirs.]
You carry what's yours, even when somebody else dropped theirs.
Your marriage. Your kids. Your word. Your work. Your walk with God. The part of the fence on your side.
Nobody else can carry those for you.
And another man dropping his doesn't make yours any lighter.
[beat]
A few verses earlier, Paul tells the same people to carry each other's burdens.
He means both. Help the man who's buried. And carry your own pack while you do it.
[pause 1s]
[SUB-MID]
This channel is for men who want to get it right at home. If that's you, subscribe. Two of these a week.

## 05:04 I'm already carrying too much

[GFX: Too much already]
[SHORT 1 IN]
[A: MED]
Some of you are thinking, Wes, I don't need more weight. I'm already carrying too much.
Job. Kids. A parent who's sick. A marriage that's tired.
You're not dropping anything. You're about to drop everything.
[beat]
I hear that. Go back to Shammah.
He took his stand. The LORD brought the victory.
[A: CU]
Shammah's job was to stand in the field.
It wasn't his job to win the war.
[TEXT: You carry the load. God carries the outcome.]
A lot of us are worn out because we're carrying our load and God's part too.
How it all turns out. Whether the kid turns out. Whether the business makes it. Whether she changes.
That's not your weight. That's his.
I know men who carry their grown kid's choices like a truck engine up a staircase. Every night. Lying awake running it.
You can carry your part of that. Pray. Show up. Say the hard thing. Keep the door open.
You can't carry his choices. Those were never yours to hold.
Pick up what's yours. Put down what's his.
You'll find you've got more strength for your own field than you thought.
[SHORT 1 OUT]

## 06:20 Sunday afternoons

[GFX: Sunday afternoons]
[B: a quiet care home hallway in afternoon light, nobody in frame]
Let me tell you about a man. I'll call him Curtis.
Curtis's mom started forgetting things about five years ago. Then she started forgetting people.
Curtis has an older brother who lives about an hour closer to her than he does.
When the diagnosis came, the brother showed up to one family meeting, said he couldn't handle it, and quit returning calls.
[A: MED]
Curtis was mad. He had every right to be.
He told me he spent a few months rehearsing what he'd say to his brother.
Meanwhile his mom's bills were piling up on her kitchen table, and nobody was visiting.
[beat]
One day Curtis just stopped rehearsing.
He took over her bills. Set up autopay on everything he could.
And he started driving out every Sunday after church. Every single one.
[B: a man's hand setting a coffee cup on a small bedside table]
Some Sundays she knows him.
Some Sundays she thinks he's the man who comes to fix the TV.
So he fixes the TV.
[pause 2s]
I asked him once if he was still angry at his brother.
He said, "Some. But I quit waiting on him to be a son so I could be one."
[pause 1s]
His brother never picked up his half.
Curtis picked up his anyway.
His wife told me those Sundays changed him. Quieter. Less of a hurry about everything.

## 07:53 Your move this week

[GFX: Your field]
[A: MED]
So here's your move.
Tonight, write down your field. The ground that's actually yours.
[TEXT: Write down your field.]
Your house. The people under your roof or in your care. Your work. Your word. Your body. Your soul.
[B: a pencil on a yellow legal pad, a short list in block letters]
Then circle one spot where you've been waiting for somebody else to stand first.
If you're married, it might be the bills she's been carrying alone, or bedtime, or the hard talk with your teenager you keep leaving to her.
If you're single, it might be your parents, your money, or the church you keep visiting and never joining.
Pick one. Stand in it this week. Before anybody else does.
