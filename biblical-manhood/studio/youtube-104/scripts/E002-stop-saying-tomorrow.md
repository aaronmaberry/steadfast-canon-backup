## Script

## 00:00 Hook

[B: a porch light with a dead bulb at dusk]
There's a verse in Proverbs that quotes something I say about four times a week.
Pretty much word for word.
And it isn't a compliment.
[A: MED]
I'm Wes Hart. This is Steadfast Men.
Tuesday we started on the first mark of a steadfast man, Stop Waiting.
Today is the short, practical half. One word to watch, and a list to make tonight.

## 00:25 The verse

[GFX: Proverbs 3]
[SHORT 1 IN]
[CARD: Proverbs 3:27 (NIV) "Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in your power to act."]
"Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in your power to act."
And then the very next verse says don't tell your neighbor to go away and come back tomorrow, when you've already got it with you.
[TEXT: Tomorrow.]
[A: CU]
Tomorrow.
[beat]
That's the word.
I'll get to it tomorrow. Let's do that tomorrow. Tomorrow, buddy, I promise.
Solomon put that word in the mouth of a man who already had the thing in his hand.

## 01:01 The porch light

[GFX: The porch light]
[A: WIDE]
There's an older man on my street. Widower. Keeps his yard better than I keep mine.
Last fall he stopped me at the mailbox and asked if I'd look at his porch light.
It had quit, and he didn't trust himself on a ladder anymore.
I said, "Sure. Tomorrow."
[beat]
I had a ladder. I had bulbs. I had a free half hour that same evening.
I watched a ballgame in it.
[B: an aluminum ladder leaning against a garage wall, a box of bulbs on a shelf]
Tomorrow turned into the weekend.
The weekend turned into, he probably got somebody.
About two weeks later I drove past after dark, and his porch was black.
He'd been coming home to a dark porch for two weeks, because a younger man with a ladder said tomorrow.
[pause 2s]
I went over that night.
It took six minutes. Four of them were him telling me about his wife.
[SHORT 1 OUT]

## 02:02 Why tomorrow is so easy

[GFX: A polite no]
[SHORT 2 IN]
[A: MED]
Here's why that word is so easy to say.
Tomorrow sounds like yes. It feels kind. Nobody gets mad at you for tomorrow.
But the verse draws a line. When it is in your power to act.
If it's yours, and you can do it, and they need it, tomorrow is just a polite way to say no.
[TEXT: Tomorrow is a polite no.]
Here's what tomorrow buys you.
You get the good feeling of saying yes tonight, without paying for the yes tonight.
You get credit for something you haven't done.
And somebody else carries the cost. They wait.
[beat]
Now, some things really are tomorrow things. You can't rebuild a deck tonight.
So ask two questions. Is it mine? Can I do it now?
If it's yes and yes, it's today.
[SHORT 2 OUT]

## 02:57 The tomorrow list

[GFX: The tomorrow list]
[A: MED]
So here's the move.
Tonight, before bed, make a tomorrow list.
Write down every time in the last forty-eight hours you said tomorrow, or later, or I'll get to it, or let me look at it.
To your wife. Your kids. Your boss. Your buddy. Your mom. The guy at church.
[B: a pen writing a short list on a notepad at a kitchen table at night]
Then go down the list with two questions.
Is it mine? Does it take under ten minutes?
Every one that's yes and yes, do it before lunch tomorrow.
[TEXT: Mine + under 10 minutes = today]
And yes, I hear it. That one tomorrow is allowed.
If you're married, show her the list. That alone might be the best ten minutes of her week.
If you're single, text the list to a buddy and tell him to check on you Saturday.
The bigger ones, put a day and a time next to them. A real day.

## 03:56 Recap

[A: CU]
Stop Waiting. When it's yours and it's in your power, the answer is today.
[beat]
I changed that man's bulb in six minutes.
I'd left him two weeks in the dark.
Don't do that to anybody this week.
[pause 1s]
