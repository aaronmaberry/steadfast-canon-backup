# Steadfast Men YouTube script guide

Internal. Draft for Aaron's sign-off, 2026-10-02. Nothing here is published, uploaded, or paid for.

This is the house format for the 104 public YouTube episodes (two a week for a year). It is separate from the 24 training sessions. Those are live-room lectures in the Option D voice. These are studio episodes in the Wes Hart Podcast voice, built to be found, watched to the end, and followed by the next one.

Read with `CANON.md`, `style/STEADFAST-AUTHOR-PERSONA.md`, and the global unslop rules. If this guide and the canon disagree, the canon wins.

---

## 1. What a Steadfast YouTube episode is

- One man (Wes Hart) at a table, talking to one man watching. Not a stage, not a sermon, not a podcast hang.
- One idea per episode. One scripture leads it. One thing to do this week.
- Two slots a week. Tuesday is long-form (9 to 13 minutes). Friday is shorter (4 to 8 minutes). Friday often puts Tuesday's idea to work.
- Every episode ships with three title options, one thumbnail idea, a full description with chapters, a pinned comment, and two marked Shorts.

## 2. Voice

**Voice ID.** Wes Hart - Podcast (`LxMQekBDeYENHe7wI9hA`), per `locks/WES-VOICE-LOCKED.txt`. Never Option D (that is the lecture hall), never Kepler, never stock, never a clone.

**Sound.** Dry Texas Hill Country. Close mic. Relaxed. A man across a kitchen table who has thought about this longer than you have and is not impressed with himself.

**He does**
- Talk to one man. "You," not "you guys" and not "some of you out there."
- Tell on himself before he tells on anybody else.
- Use contractions, short lines, plain nouns. Trucks, gates, kitchens, phones, ball fields, porches.
- Get one laugh in a long episode, maybe two. Dry and domestic. Then land the point and stop.
- Name the focus mark by its title, plainly, near the top and again in the recap (canon rule).
- Put grace before effort. Effort answers grace. It never buys it.

**He does not**
- Preach. No pulpit build, no "can I get an amen," no altar call, no shouting.
- Use filler. No "uh," no "um," no restarts. The lecture sessions use those to sound live. The podcast voice sounds like a careful man in a quiet room, so the page stays clean.
- Use tone tags that change his volume. Never `[quietly]`, `[whispers]`, `[laughs]`. At most two `[dry]` or `[warmly]` tags per episode, at the start of a line, and only if a take comes out flat.
- Recite all four marks as a stock line. When all four come up, use fresh words each time (canon rule).
- Use the retired Lewis wording, name Robert Lewis, his book, Men's Fraternity, Authentic Manhood, Fellowship Bible Church, Tim Hawks, One Mission, or any course title. Ever.
- Have a job. Wes is a husband and a dad who works for a living and has sat in men's groups a long time (lecture lock, kept here so the two voices match).
- Reuse book or training-session stories, names, or images (Anthony, the front step, the leather notebook, the deleted text, Eli, Tomás, Walt, Roy). Every story is new.

**Stories.** Each long episode carries one short Wes story (a self-own) and one story about another man, told long enough to have a setup, a detail, a turn, and a landing. Other men get first names only and the words "I'll call him." No real people's private lives.

**Theology guardrails.**
- "God's pay" is the inheritance the Lord gives his children (Colossians 3:24), never wages for salvation (Ephesians 2:8-9). Say it plainly whenever that mark is the focus.
- Willpower is not the gospel. Born again, then trained.
- Hard episodes (depression, abuse, porn, divorce, grief) are dead serious, no jokes at the bottom. Any episode that touches suicide or self-harm says on mic and in the description: if you are in danger, call or text 988 in the US, or your local emergency number.
- First Adam and last Adam (1 Corinthians 15:45) in our own words only.

## 3. Scripture

- NIV, exact wording, checked against a published NIV before the script is signed.
- One lead verse per episode, plus the focus-mark verse when it fits. A long episode can carry three verses at most.
- Spoken the way people say it. "John five, verse six." On screen as a verse card with the reference and "(NIV)."
- Do not quote NIV verses that contain an em dash. Quote the clean part and say the rest in your own words, not as a quote.
- Every description carries the Biblica notice (section 9). Canon requires it wherever NIV is quoted.

## 4. Episode shape

### Long-form, Tuesday (9 to 13 minutes, about 1,250 to 1,800 spoken words)

| Clock | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:20 | Hook | Pay off the title and thumbnail inside 20 seconds. No hello, no logo, no music sting, no subscribe. |
| 0:20 to 1:00 | Setup | "I'm Wes Hart. This is Steadfast Men." Once, fast. Name the focus mark. Say what he'll leave with. Plant one open loop ("I'll tell you what my back gate taught me"). |
| 1:00 to 3:00 | The text | Read the lead verse. Two or three plain observations. |
| about 3:00 | Re-engage | A turn the viewer did not expect. Usually Wes's self-own story. |
| 3:00 to 6:00 | The point | The focus mark, defined in one sentence, shown on one ordinary day. |
| after the first payoff | Mid subscribe line | One sentence. See section 7. |
| 6:00 to 8:00 | The pushback | Wes says the objection the viewer is already thinking, then answers it. This is the best Short in most episodes. |
| 8:00 to 11:00 | The other man | One story told long. |
| last 2 minutes | The move and recap | One thing to do this week, with a single and a married version when it's a household move. Name the focus mark again. One line to carry. |
| last 15 to 20 seconds | End screen | Name the next episode by title. Subscribe line lands while the end screen is up. Then stop. |

### Shorter, Friday (4 to 8 minutes, about 600 to 1,100 spoken words)

**Estimating runtime.** Spoken words divided by 145, plus every marked pause, plus 2 seconds per chapter card. Write chapter times from that estimate and correct them after the edit. Cut to fit. Never pad to reach a length; a shorter video that holds beats a longer one that sags (Colin and Samir, "respect their time").

Hook (15 seconds), one verse, one story, one move, end screen. No mid subscribe line. Most Fridays answer "OK, but what do I do Monday?" from Tuesday.

### Why this shape

- The first minute loses the most viewers on every video, so it gets the most work (MrBeast production memo).
- The open has to confirm the click in the first 5 to 10 seconds and give a reason to stay (Paddy Galloway). Grok's YouTube packaging note asked for a payoff inside 20 seconds. We hold to 20.
- A re-engagement near minute 3 keeps the middle from sagging (MrBeast memo).
- Saying the viewer's question out loud gives him permission to stay a beginner (BibleProject). Assume he's smart. Give him the context.
- Allen Parr's rule for faith teaching is the same. Give a concrete reason to stay in the first 15 to 20 seconds, then hold watch time.

## 5. Pauses and breaks

Pause marks are edit marks. They are never spoken. Anything in square brackets is stripped before audio generation, and the edit inserts the silence.

| Mark | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| (none) | normal | Line breaks already get a sentence gap in the edit (about 0.5 to 0.8 s). Most lines need nothing. |
| `[beat]` | 0.6 s | Before the word that matters, or after a dry line so it can land. |
| `[pause 1s]` | 1.0 s | Topic turn inside a chapter. |
| `[pause 2s]` | 2.0 s | After the heaviest line in the episode. Two or three per long episode, at most. Cover it with B-roll so the screen isn't a frozen face. |

Rules
- One spoken thought per line. Most lines run 4 to 14 words.
- No `uh`, `um`, stammers, or restarts on the page.
- No pause longer than 2 seconds of picture without something moving on screen.
- The funny word goes last in its line. No long freeze before a punch line. After a laugh line, a `[beat]` is enough.
- **Chapter breaks.** Each chapter opens with a 2-second title card over B-roll (`[GFX: chapter title]`). That's the viewer's breath.
- **Breather.** One long episode in four can carry a 3 to 5 second B-roll breather with room sound only, after the heaviest line. Mark it `[BREATHER 4s: shot]`.

Production conversion. The audio pipeline wants `spoken line|seconds`. A script line followed by `[beat]` becomes `line|0.6`. A plain line gets the sentence default. Chapters become separate takes under 2,000 characters each, split only at a chapter or a `[pause]` mark.

## 6. Visual cues

Wes's face on screen is the weakest link until a realism test passes (Grok's cut-list note). Plan every long episode so his mouth is on camera for about a third of the runtime and B-roll carries the rest.

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[A: WIDE]` `[A: MED]` `[A: CU]` | Wes at the table. Wide to open a chapter, medium to teach, close-up only for the line that matters. |
| `[B: description]` | Cutaway. Real places, hands, objects, weather, roads. No faces of other men in motion. No crowds. |
| `[CARD: reference]` | Verse card. Exact NIV text, reference, "(NIV)." Navy ground, bone text, Playfair. Stays up long enough to read twice. |
| `[TEXT: words]` | On-screen words, three to seven, lower third or center. |
| `[GFX: chapter title]` | Chapter card, 2 seconds. |
| `[SHORT 1 IN]` `[SHORT 1 OUT]` | Shorts cut points (section 11). |
| `[SUB-MID]` | Mid subscribe line plus the bone/navy Subscribe lower-third, 4 to 6 seconds. |
| `[END SCREEN 20s]` | Last 20 seconds. Next episode tile plus Subscribe. |

Rules
- Change the picture every 20 to 40 seconds in long-form. A new angle, a cutaway, a verse card, or on-screen text all count.
- Open on a wide or a B-roll shot, not on a tight face (Grok cut-list).
- Laugh lines play over B-roll or a wide, never a grinning close-up.
- No music under scripture. A low bed under the hook and the end screen is fine.
- Grade warm, slight grain, no HDR shine. Cloth Store palette (navy `#0B1F38`, gold `#E3C572`, bone `#E6DDCC`).
- Where B-roll comes from is a production decision. No new tool or stock spend without Aaron.

## 7. Subscribe plug rules

The lock on disk stands. Subscribe (and the bell, at most once) for the mission. No website, socials, apps, or store on mic or in lower-thirds. Those live in the description and the pinned comment.

- Never in the hook. Never before the first real payoff.
- Long-form gets two lines total. One mid line after the first payoff (usually around the 40 percent mark), one at the end screen.
- Friday episodes get the end-screen line only.
- One sentence. Say what he gets and how often. Then go straight back to the point.
- Vary the wording every episode. Never "smash," never "like and subscribe," never "it really helps the channel," never begging.
- Ask for a comment only with a real question, and only in the description and pinned comment.

Bank of plug lines (rotate, rewrite, never reuse in back-to-back episodes)
1. "If this is the kind of thing you need on a Tuesday, subscribe. Two of these a week."
2. "Subscribe if you want the next one. Friday's short, and it's the practical half of this."
3. "If you're tired of waiting on yourself, subscribe. We'll keep at it every week."
4. "This channel is for men who want to get it right at home. If that's you, subscribe."
5. "Subscribe. Tuesday and Friday, every week, one thing at a time."
6. "If a buddy sent you this, subscribe so you don't need him to send the next one."
7. "Subscribe if you want to keep going. Next one's about [topic]."
8. "If you're going to do the move this week, subscribe and check back Friday."
9. "No hype here. Two episodes a week. Subscribe if that helps."
10. "Hit subscribe, and the bell if you want to know when Friday's is up."

## 8. Titles and thumbnails

**Titles (three options per episode)**
- Under 55 characters so phones show the whole thing (Ali Abdaal).
- Most important words first. No episode numbers, no "Steadfast Men |" up front (YouTube Help).
- True to the video. The first 20 seconds must pay it off.
- One option searchable ("How to Stop Waiting on Yourself"), one curious ("He Waited 38 Years. Jesus Asked One Question."), one plain ("The Man You Keep Saying You'll Be").
- No ALL CAPS beyond one word. No emoji. No "Go First" as a title (canon lock). No "Four Marks" as a standalone product name.
- Upload with all three in YouTube's title and thumbnail A/B test (up to three options). It picks by watch-time share, not clicks.

**Thumbnails**
- One subject. Wes from the locked look, or one object that tells the story (a bungee-tied gate, a mat on stone steps).
- Three words of text, max. Bone or gold on navy. Readable on a phone.
- A real expression, not a fake shocked mouth. No red arrows, no circles (show bible lock).
- Same series template every week, so a returning viewer knows it's us.
- Make three, test them with the titles.

## 10. End screens

- The last 20 seconds of every episode are built for the end screen. YouTube allows 5 to 20 seconds and up to four elements (YouTube Help).
- Use two elements. The next episode (by name) and Subscribe. Season playlist in place of the next episode on season finales.
- Wes names the next episode out loud while the tile is up. Then the video ends. No blooper, no outro music tail.

## 11. Shorts

Every episode marks two Shorts in the script with `[SHORT 1 IN]` / `[SHORT 1 OUT]`.

- Each Short stands alone. A stranger with no context should get a hook, a point, and an ending (Galloway calls a Short "an intro with a conclusion").
- First choice is the pushback beat (Grok's note was "Shorts from the pushback only"). Second choice is the hook plus its answer.
- 30 to 60 seconds is the target. Up to 3 minutes is allowed for vertical video.
- Reframe to 9:16. Burned-in captions, large, centered low. First line on screen in the first second.
- In the last 5 seconds, Wes or the caption points to the full episode. Attach the long video with the Related Video link so the click goes straight there (YouTube Blog, July 2026).
- The full episode must pay off what the Short promised in its first 10 seconds.
- No subscribe ask inside a Short.

## 12. File format

One file per episode, `scripts/E###-slug.md`, in this order.

1. Header table. Slot, proposed date, season, target length, word budget, focus mark, lead scripture.
2. Packaging. Three titles, thumbnail idea.
3. Script. Chapters as `## mm:ss Chapter name` with estimated times, spoken lines one per line, bracketed cues.
4. Shorts. Two cut points with in and out lines, on-screen hook text, length estimate.
5. Description. Ready to paste.
6. Pinned comment.
7. Production notes. B-roll list, face-time target, anything the editor needs.
