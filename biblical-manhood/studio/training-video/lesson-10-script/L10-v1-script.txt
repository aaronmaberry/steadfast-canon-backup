I'm Wes.

The word father does something. Maybe it sits easy in you. Maybe it catches. I know that catch. I will not make a joke of it. This lesson will not build another list of what was missing. Maybe you have already said the true thing about that man. Maybe you are tired of the list. That tiredness makes sense.

You can be the strong one for years. Run the day. Pay what is due. Keep the people near you steady. And still be listening for a man to finish one sentence. I know that listen. It dresses up as faith. You call it patience. Under the nicer word, there is a boy at the window. That boy has been there a long time.

This lesson walks that boy to a Father who is already here. If the word scrapes on the way out, stay. Receiving is the door. You don't have to feel ready first. I'll stay while you learn to say it. This lesson is who is here. A Father who does not leave.

One sentence holds the four marks. I will not pull them apart today. A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, with being seen kept out of the wage. That last part only holds if the God you picture is the real one. A man will not rest his life on a father he thinks might walk off. So the marks can rest in your mouth. The question under them is simpler. Who have you been living them in front of?

You have been reading God through your dad. Every man does it. Your dad was the first picture of a father you had, so you held that picture up and figured heaven matched. I have done that for years. Scripture turns the picture around. God is the Father. The man you learned the word from was never the definition. You learn that man by looking at Jesus. You don't learn Jesus by staring at the man until the man turns into God. The man will not. Give your dad more years. Your dad is still a man. If you can still call your dad, call him. Honor him. That call is love. That call is a different page than this one.

A lot of men will give God the work and the worry and keep one room inside for a human who might still come through. That room stays hungry. I have fed that room by being the capable one. I have fed that room by being needed. Neither one ever said the sentence. A paycheck can clap you on the back. A full house can need you. The sentence you are hungry for does not live in either place. This page is the Father who stays.

Maybe the father you grew up with was a rumor. You could talk about him. You could not reach him. So God feels like a number you were given and never told how to dial. You pray, and you don't expect a ring.

Or maybe a man was in the house, two feet away, and the only words were about the work. Hand me that. Move that. We're done. You learned that near can still be mute. Your prayers go out like work orders and come back with no voice on them.

Or maybe you learned the house by the way the front door shut. You knew the night before anybody spoke. You still pray like a man listening for which God walked in. You're fairly sure the mad one came for you.

Or maybe your dad was good. Mine was. My dad showed up. He paid what needed paying, and a sentence still never quite got said. So you thank God easily, and you leave the need out, because needing sounds ungrateful.

You held one of those pictures up to heaven. Maybe more than one. The picture has been doing your praying for you. You didn't frame it on purpose. You grew up inside it and carried it into every prayer. A rumor teaches you not to expect much. A mute house teaches you to keep the prayer short. A hard door teaches you to come in small. A good man with a missing sentence teaches you to be grateful and still alone. Name yours. Hold the name loose.

My boy asked what kind of dad I had. I hurried. I called my dad a good man, and I closed it with, we're good. Here is the part under that hurry. I got very good at thanks. I am slow at need. A decent dad can teach you that without ever meaning to. You don't want to sound like a complaint against a man who tried. So you pray the highlights. The health. The work. The day going fine. You carry the heavy part yourself and call it respect. It isn't respect. It is a son who never practiced being carried.

I can say Lord all afternoon. I can say God. Father is the word that sticks. Father means somebody close enough to hand the weight to. I wasn't sure I was allowed. You can believe every true sentence about God and still pray like a man who plans to handle it. That was me. A lot of mornings, it still is. So the move in this lesson is small and hard. Set the picture down.

Maybe you are still hoping the next visit is the one. Your dad will be softer. He will finally see you. I hope you get a kind hour with him. Take the hour. Love him in it. Don't make him God while you wait on the hour. The hope is tender. I have lived in that hope. Say that in your chest if you have to. The hope gets heavy when a man has to be God too. I have been asking a man to do God's job. Stop running the test. Your dad does not become God if you wait him out, or impress him, or need less. Your dad will stay the size he is. The place you have been asking him to fill was made for your Father.

John fourteen, verse nine. "Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father. How can you say, Show us the Father?" Whatever Jesus is like, the Father is like. Not whatever your dad was like. That is the correction. Stay with one thing Jesus shows. The Father comes toward you.

A friend texts that he is in the driveway while you are still editing the message that asks him to come. You had not decided you were allowed to need that friend. The friend was already there. I have rewritten prayers in the truck until I sounded like a man who had recovered. Then I called the wait respect. The Father wants the man you actually are, on the day you actually have.

I was in a hardware aisle with a bolt I could not name. The man in the vest was right there. I nodded like I knew the size. I did not know. I paid. I went home. The bolt fit nothing I own. I still did not go back. Asking felt louder than being wrong. That is a silly story until you hear it in your prayers. You tidy the sentence. You wait until you sound like a man who has it mostly together. You call the tidying reverence. Often the tidying is fear. Bring the Father the unfinished man. The bolt was a small confession. The prayers were the bigger one. A son gets to come in unfinished. The Father comes toward a son who is still a mess in the mouth.

Matthew three, verse seventeen. "And a voice from heaven said, This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased." Before the sermons. Before a healing. Before anybody in the towns had a reason to clap. The Father said the sentence first. My Son. That is belonging. Whom I love. That is the Father's heart toward the Son. With him I am well pleased. That is delight, and it landed before the resume. Maybe you got the order backwards, if you got the words at all. Do enough. Then maybe you will hear it. The Father reversed that order on the day the Son stepped into the water. Blessing first. The work after. If you have been performing for a voice you only hear after a win, you can stop trying to earn your own name. Your own father may have made you earn words that were supposed to be a gift. This Father gives them. If needing that voice feels like weakness, stay with the Son. The beloved Son heard it anyway. You are not more mature than the Son for going without.

I have tried to earn breakfast. List first. Food if the list behaved. Nobody was grading the eggs. I was, in my head, every morning. A son eats because he is a son. The shift comes after the name. If you are in Christ, that sentence is already over you. You don't assemble it by a better month. If you are not sure you are in Christ, don't dress the doubt up. Tell the Father you are not sure. The Father is not hiding from a man who will say the plain thing. Mine. Loved. Well pleased. Before the proof.

Your Father carries you. Your Father knows the need before you get a word on it. Your needing does not make your Father lean away. I watched a brother once get an arm under a sick friend outside a clinic and walk that friend to the door. No speech. No tally at the end. The sick friend kept apologizing. The brother kept walking. That picture is nearer your Father than the scorekeeping you may pray with. Stay with that picture if you grew up apologizing for taking up space. Let the apologizing rest. Your Father is not keeping score on the walk. If needing was dangerous in your house, don't rush past that. Sit in it a minute. The Father you have is not tired of the weight. The Father stays.

John fourteen, verse eighteen. "I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you." The word orphans is in that line because those men were about to feel it. Maybe you have felt it since you were a boy. Hear the other half. I will come to you.

Hebrews thirteen, verse five. "Never will I leave you; never will I forsake you." If the season is hard, hear this before you decide the Father left. Hebrews twelve, verse seven. "Endure hardship as discipline; God is treating you as his children." Discipline and rage were never the same thing. A hard stretch can mean the Father stayed. The man who learned to flinch needs that said slow. Rage throws you out. A Father's correction keeps you in the house. Your Father is not a trick for feeling better before supper. Your Father stays in the hurt with you. The hurt gets a hearing. The hurt does not get the throne. You can bring your Father what your dad did, and what you did. You still belong to your Father.

A man I sat with works on diesel trucks. I'll call him Calvin. Calvin was the man to call. He had a rule he never wrote down. Don't be the one who needs. His old man was in the house and hard to bother. Calvin learned to solve a thing before anybody had to wake up to it. It looked like strength. It was a boy keeping the peace by needing nothing.

One afternoon a starter was half out of a truck. Calvin's back locked. His legs quit the argument. A younger man on the crew did not make a speech. That younger man got an arm under Calvin's chest and took the weight. Calvin told me later he came an inch from shoving him off. The pain was not the reason. Being the one held felt like losing in front of his own men.

Calvin did not go in the house right away. He stayed in the driveway and left the engine off. He tried to pray the usual list. Jobs. Money. His boy's grades. The list ran out, and the back was still wrong. He said the word he had been avoiding. Father. Then one sentence. I can't get myself to the door. He told me it felt foolish, like he had picked up a word from a house he did not live in.

Calvin kept at the word on the drive to work. Father. One plain sentence about the day. After a string of those mornings, his boy had a truck that would not turn over. Calvin's whole body went for the wrench. He stopped his hand. He asked the boy what he heard. He held the light. He let the boy find it. Later Calvin said, I have been the arms my whole life. I didn't know a son was allowed to be carried. And my boy needed to see me allow it more than he needed a running truck.

Maybe you have fathered yourself since you were young. You can nod at every verse you just heard and still lock up when it is time to be a son. Help feels like stepping off a ladder you have been standing on for years. The ground has been there. Your feet don't believe the ground yet.

So practice the receiving. Say Father where your own ears take it in. Keep God and Lord for your other prayers if you want. Give this prayer the name.

Romans eight, verse fifteen. "The Spirit you received brought about your adoption to sonship. And by him we cry, Abba, Father." Abba is a little kid's word. You are allowed to sound like that. It will feel clumsy. Say it while the word still does. The first time I said it in the truck, I checked both mirrors, like somebody might report me. I said it again the next morning. Awkward counted. If you have been the capable one, the word can feel late. A son can start today.

Bring the Father the thing that is still messy, before you polish it for a brother. Father, I am about to go cold on the people in my house. Father, I am headed back toward the thing I already told a man I quit. The Father can hear it uncleaned. The Father can hear the way you leave and still tell you to put it down. Then let the Father tell you who you are. Read a sentence the Father has said about his sons. Answer that sentence as true of you, because if you are in Christ, it is. A man who never heard his name needs it more than once. The Father has the patience for that.

Let the Father carry you in a way you can feel. Sometime soon, something will be too heavy, and your reflex will be to grind it alone. Say the other sentence. This is past me. Father, hold it. Then let a brother in on it. Ask that brother to pray for you out loud, right there, and let him finish. Being prayed over is practice at being carried. Your pride will call the prayer small. The prayer is the ladder step. Stay where the Father's voice actually is. The Father's Word, on a plain day. The Father's people, when you would rather slip out. Prayer, when you have nothing impressive to report. A man who only shows up when something is on fire never learns the voice on an ordinary Tuesday.

Maybe you are young. Learn this while you still think you don't need another father. You will save yourself years of looking for a man who can finish the sentence.

If you are single, don't put the Father on layaway. I watched a younger man try to wait the Father out. I'll call him Ben. Ben was single, early in his working life. Ben told me he would get serious about God as Father once he had a wife and a couple of kids, because that is when a man needs the pattern. I told Ben the order runs the other way. You are a son before you are anybody's husband. A wife will not be the voice from heaven. If you hand her that job, you will wear her out and stay a boy with a plan. Ben did not need more life first. Ben needed the word while the apartment was still quiet.

If you are married, your wife can be a gift and still not be your Father. I have handed my wife a job that was never hers. I still catch myself handing her that job. Take the job back. Let your wife be a wife.

If a man used the word father to harm you, hear this kindly. God is not that man. You can say Father without first making the harm smaller.

If you are older, you are not late to be a son. The word may feel foolish the first mornings. Say it anyway. Foolish and true can share a mouth.

If the job is going well, don't bring a good report to a hunger the report cannot feed. I had a stretch like that. Somebody told me the work was strong. I said thanks. I sat in the truck afterward, and I was still hungry. A nod at work is not, with him I am well pleased.

Once this Father is fathering you, your own dad gets smaller in the right way. You can honor what was good in your dad. You can name what harmed you and put the rage down. Forgiveness gets room when you quit waiting on your dad to pay a debt only God can pay. Maybe you can still move toward your dad. Love can make that call. The call still cannot make your dad into God.

Here is the whole thing. You learned the word father from a man. Jesus shows you the Father that word was always about. That Father comes toward you. He names you before you perform. He stays. He does not leave. What I hope you walk out with is not a fixed childhood before supper. I hope you walk out knowing which picture you have been holding up to heaven, and one true thing Jesus shows about the Father who is actually there. Then I hope you say the word before you feel ready.

Here those marks sit in front of a Father who does not leave. That is the difference.
