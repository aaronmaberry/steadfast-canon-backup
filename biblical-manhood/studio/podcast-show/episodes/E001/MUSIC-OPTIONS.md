# Walk Steadfast music options

Written 2026-10-03, 4:45 PM CT. Nothing has been generated or bought for this. The only ElevenLabs activity was price checks with estimate-only calls, which are free.

## What every direction delivers

Each direction is one original theme. All the pieces get cut from the same take, so the melody matches everywhere.

| Piece | Length | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Title sting | 3-5 s | After the cold open. Per show bible section 5, it's a two-note figure with no voice. |
| Outro bed | 20-30 s | Under the end of the outro. It ends on its own last chord right before the credits line. |
| Quiet intro bed (optional) | 25-30 s | Very low under the open, ending before the slate |
| Full theme | 60-90 s | The source of the three pieces above, and usable for trailers and YouTube end screens |

Ruled out on purpose are ukulele, hand claps or foot-stomp loops, whistling, bright strummed "corporate acoustic," lo-fi hip-hop beats, felt-piano sadness, worship pads and big swells, gospel organ, and anything named after an existing song or artist.

## The five directions

### A. Two chairs

- **Feel.** Two low voices trading a melody, like two men swapping stories at a table after supper.
- **Instruments.** Cello and bowed upright bass take turns on the tune. A fingerpicked baritone acoustic guitar plays under them. No drums.
- **Tempo and key.** 66 BPM, D major with a Dorian lean (a flat seventh).
- **Two-note figure.** A low D rising to A, plucked on the bass and answered by the cello.
- **Reference vibe in words.** A chamber-folk session recorded in a limestone ranch house. Wood floor, close mics, nobody in a hurry. It sounds more like a small film score than a podcast cue.
- **Why it's ownable.** Almost no podcast uses a low string duet. The two instruments are the two hosts.

```

It opens with a two-note figure, a low D rising to A, plucked on upright bass and answered by a bowed cello, left to ring for three seconds. Then a fingerpicked baritone acoustic guitar sets a slow, warm pattern while cello and bowed upright bass trade a simple, reverent melody, like two men in conversation. No drums, no strumming, no claps, no synth pads, no vocals. Intimate, close-miked, wooden-room sound, unhurried and grounded. End on a clean, held D major chord.
```

### B. Limestone

- **Feel.** One man and one guitar at first light. The plainest of the five and the easiest to sit under voices.
- **Instruments.** Solo baritone acoustic guitar in open D tuning, with natural harmonics and a short slide on the low string. A soft muted thump on the bass string keeps time. Upright bass joins late.
- **Tempo and key.** 76 BPM, open D, D Mixolydian.
- **Two-note figure.** A slid low A rising to a D harmonic.
- **Reference vibe in words.** A one-take fingerstyle recording made on a porch at dawn. Open strings ring into each other, and you hear fingers on wound strings.
- **Why it's ownable.** The baritone guitar, open tuning and harmonics keep it away from bright strummed stock acoustic. The risk is that of the five, it's the closest to generic acoustic if the take comes out too pretty.

30 s preview prompt:

```
Instrumental solo fingerstyle baritone acoustic guitar in open D tuning, 76 BPM, D Mixolydian. It opens with a two-note figure, a low A slid up into a ringing D harmonic, held for three seconds. Then a slow fingerpicked pattern with open strings ringing into each other, a soft muted thump on the low string keeping time, and an occasional short slide. An upright bass enters quietly near the end. Warm, dry, close-miked, the sound of fingers on wound strings, grounded and unhurried. No strumming, no claps, no stomps, no pads, no vocals. End on a ringing open D chord.
```

Full theme prompt (90 s):

### C. Dancehall waltz

- **Feel.** An old Hill Country dancehall after the band has packed up. Tender, a little nostalgic and very local.
- **Instruments.** Button accordion played soft and slow, bajo sexto or nylon-string guitar on the chords, upright bass on beat one, and light brushes on beats two and three.
- **Tempo and key.** A slow waltz in 3/4 at 88 BPM, G major.
- **Two-note figure.** The accordion breathes a D, then settles on G.
- **Reference vibe in words.** The last waltz in a wooden dancehall with the screen doors open. It draws on Texas German and Czech dancehall roots, played gently with no polka bounce and nothing comic.
- **Why it's ownable.** No faith or men's podcast sounds like this, and it says Texas Hill Country within two seconds. The risk is that accordion can tip toward playful if the take is too bouncy.

30 s preview prompt:

```
Instrumental slow Texas dancehall waltz in 3/4 time at 88 BPM, G major. It opens with a two-note figure, a soft button accordion swell on D that settles on G, held for three seconds. Then a tender, unhurried waltz with button accordion on the melody, a bajo sexto strumming gently on beats two and three, upright bass on beat one and light brushes. Warm, nostalgic and reverent, like the last dance in an old wooden dancehall. Not a polka, not bouncy, not comic. No claps, no pads, no vocals. End on a soft, held G major chord.
```

### D. Pedal steel evening

- **Feel.** Sunset over the Llano River. Wide open and reverent without stained glass.
- **Instruments.** Pedal steel guitar plays slow swells and a plain melody. Nylon-string guitar plays arpeggios. Upright bass, and a brushed snare enters in the middle.
- **Tempo and key.** 70 BPM, E major.
- **Two-note figure.** Harmonics on the nylon guitar, B rising to E, with a pedal steel swell under the second note. That keeps the sting acoustic-led, as the bible asks.
- **Reference vibe in words.** An ambient Americana instrumental at the end of a long Texas day. Lots of air, no honky-tonk licks.
- **Why it's ownable.** Pedal steel used as atmosphere instead of country radio. The risk is that if the take adds crying-steel licks, it turns into a beer commercial.

- **Tempo and key.** 84 BPM, E minor, resolving to G major in the last bars.
- **Two-note figure.** A low E, then B, on the baritone guitar with a spring-reverb splash.

## My read

- **A, Two chairs,** fits the show best. It's reverent, it's two voices, and it's the least like anything else in the category.
- **C, Dancehall waltz,** is the boldest and the most Hill Country. Hear it before ruling it out.
- **B, Limestone,** is the safe pick and sits under voices most easily.
- **D and E** carry more mood. E needs the bible's "acoustic" line changed.

### Commercial rights

Sources:

- [Eleven Music model-specific terms](https://elevenlabs.io/eleven-music-model-specific-terms) (updated 26 May 2026, covers Music v1 and v2 and their sub-versions such as v2.5)
- [Music service terms](https://elevenlabs.io/music-terms)
- [Terms of service](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use) (updated 31 March 2026)
- [Help article on publishing](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/13313564601361-Can-I-publish-the-content-I-generate-on-the-platform)

What the terms say:

- **Podcast and YouTube use is allowed on Pro.** The Pro plan's media rights are "All online and offline commercial use permitted, except film, TV, radio, & Studio Games." That covers the podcast feed, monetized YouTube, the website and social clips. No attribution is required on paid plans.
- **The right lasts.** Music made while on a paid plan can be used commercially indefinitely, even if the plan later lapses.
- **Not allowed.** Reselling the music, building a music library to license to others, and broadcast film, TV or radio use. Publishing the theme to Spotify or Apple Music as a music release is allowed on Creator and above, but the podcast doesn't need that.
- **Eligibility catch.** Music on the self-serve Starter, Creator and Pro plans is marked "For Individual Use Only." Scale covers "entities or organizations with fewer than 10 employees." If Walk Steadfast is published by a company or ministry rather than by Aaron personally, the matching music tier is Scale. Confirm who the publisher is before generating the final theme.
- **We don't get exclusive ownership.** The terms of service say that, as between us and ElevenLabs, we keep our rights in the output. But:
  - The Music terms say output "may not be unique and may be similar or identical to Output returned to other users," and "ElevenLabs does not guarantee the exclusivity of Output."
  - The terms of service give ElevenLabs a perpetual, nonexclusive license to use our content to run and improve its services.
- **Copyright may not protect it.** The US Copyright Office's position is that material generated by AI from prompts alone isn't protected by copyright. We could use the theme freely, but probably couldn't register it or stop someone who copies it.
- **No Content ID.** Don't enroll the theme in YouTube Content ID or a music distributor's claiming system. AI-generated, non-exclusive music in those systems causes false claims and breaks their terms.

**Bottom line.** ElevenLabs gives a solid, permanent commercial license that is plenty for the podcast and YouTube, but not outright exclusive ownership. If outright ownership matters, the path is a human-made version. A musician records the chosen two-note figure and theme on a work-for-hire buyout, using the AI demo as the brief. That's ownable and registrable, and a local Hill Country player fits the brand. Get a quote first, since I haven't priced it.
