# Field Notes: Which Shape Are You
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 18 · March 5, 2027

The gutter went up on Saturday. Wes's son-in-law worked the ladder, Wes stood on three rungs, and Wes's wife watched from a lawn chair. They hung it perfectly level. When it rained on Monday, the water ran out the wrong end onto the back step, because a gutter needs a slope. A second appointment is set. The rep from Jonathon's 2019 deal finally replied: "Just don't do it again. And the next big one's mine."

Jonathon's shape was skip the house. He managed his first twenty minutes home three nights out of four. On the fourth night, he took an eleven-minute work call in the driveway until Ruby knocked on the truck window holding the phone-jail basket. Wes asked his wife which shape she sees, the way the show suggested: a calm night, his own answer first. She said, "Run the room. With your mother." He didn't argue, and he sat with it for a day. She was half right. The cleaning help had been his mother's idea, but the grab bar was his, ordered without asking her. He called and asked. She wanted it, in a different color. Jonathon's nephew learned from his roommates that he's "only fun when other people are around," so he watched their show with them, phone in another room. "The show is bad. It was fun though."

## From the table: my wife says I run the room, and I think she's wrong

> "The way of fools seems right to them, but the wise listen to advice."
> Proverbs 12:15 (NIV)

Everybody's way seems right to him, so that can't be the test. Wes's order: don't argue for a day; ask for one real example ("When's the last time you saw me do that?"); and if part of it still seems wrong, say so kindly, later. If two people who love you see the same thing, it's probably there.

## Tool on the table: ask one person

Pick someone who sees you up close and isn't scared of you. Ask one specific question. Write down exactly what they said. Say "thank you." Jonathon asked his sister. "Skip the house. After Dad left, you were at everybody else's house. I was home with Mom." "You were nine," she told him later. "But you're not nine now."
