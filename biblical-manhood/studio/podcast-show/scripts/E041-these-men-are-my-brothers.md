WES: Well, first, everybody apologized at once, which was its own kind of awful. And he waved it off. He's a band director. He's used to people being off the beat. And then I asked him, and I'm proud of this one thing, I asked him what he wanted. Not what we could do. What he wanted.

JONATHON: Is she okay?

That's what a brother does on an ordinary Thursday. He notices the chair.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: Can I push on that? Because you make noticing sound easy. I don't notice when Theo's shoes are on the wrong feet. He'll walk around half a day like a duck before anybody says anything.
WES: It's not easy. I didn't notice for three weeks, and I'd have told you I was the noticing kind. That's the whole problem. Nobody thinks he's the one who'd walk past. Everybody at that table would've sworn he'd have called.
JONATHON: So what do you do about it, if you can't trust yourself to notice?
WES: You make it somebody's job. Not a committee. Just, if a chair's empty, whoever sees it first is the one who calls. And you stop waiting for the feeling that something's wrong, because the feeling shows up about three weeks late.

JONATHON: "Daily."

## Reading narrative

# These Men Are My Brothers
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 41 · May 25, 2027

A retired band director at Wes's Thursday table never missed. Then he missed three weeks running. The first week someone guessed grandkids. The second week someone asked if he was still out. By the third week nobody said anything; the chair was just empty. On the fourth Thursday he came in with the good coffee and told them his wife was on her third round of chemo. "I kept waiting for one of you to call."

## Notice the chair

Six men had his number. Wes asked him what he wanted, and the answer was small: "Could y'all come to my kitchen?" For six weeks the table met at his house. His wife came down in a headscarf to tell them they were too loud. She's two years clear now. Since then, if a man misses, whoever notices first calls him by Friday noon.

> "But encourage one another daily, as long as it is called 'Today,' so that none of you may be hardened by sin's deceitfulness."
> Hebrews 3:13 (NIV)

Daily, not at the annual retreat. Today is an ordinary word. Today is a Thursday.

## Two plates

At a work meeting in Austin, Jonathon walked past a rival sitting alone in his running truck at lunch. Then he went back out with two plates of fajitas. After ten quiet minutes the man said his wife had moved out in March, and that he makes his kids pancakes on Saturday mornings. "That's the whole thing I'm holding onto."

Jonathon also asked his father-in-law, married fifty-one years, how he stayed. "I don't know, son. We just didn't leave." Later, carrying a patio umbrella base, the older man mentioned that his wife's brother called him every Thursday night during his worst years to talk about the Rangers. He never asked why. Jonathon has decided to be that uncle.
