**Arc.** *Hook:* Wes texted his oldest "How's your Tuesday?" and got back "Are you dying?" *Tension:* the honest report. Wes's question at supper turns up a Tuesday his wife has carried alone since August, and he has to decide whether to fix it or hear it. Jonathon misses the first night ("Daddy, you said") and makes it on the second try. *Turn:* the question men ask most when they start, and a father in Mark 9 who prayed the most honest prayer in the Gospels. *Resolution:* the phone basket, a weekend version of the challenge, and the close.

---

## OPEN (word for word)

WES: Tuesday I texted my oldest son four words. "How's your Tuesday?" About ten minutes later he wrote back, "Are you dying?"

JONATHON: That's not a text, Wes. That's a report card.

WES: It is. Twenty-one years, and the only reason he could think of for his dad asking was a diagnosis.

WES: Then a question men ask me all the time: where do you start if you've never been the spiritual one? And stay with us, because I'll tell you what I almost said at my own supper table, and what it would've cost.

## SEGMENT 1 · Wes checks the box

JONATHON: You go first, Wes, because you already told the ending, and I want the whole thing.

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the meal. Tuesday night I put the phone in the truck, walked in, sat down, and asked my wife the question. "What's Tuesday like for you?" She put her fork down. She said, "Why?" I said, "I just want to know." And she said, "Well, Tuesdays I take your mother to physical therapy."

JONATHON: Every Tuesday? Because I've heard you talk about your mom's hip, and I don't think you've ever mentioned that.

WES: Every Tuesday, since August. Forty minutes each way. I knew she took her sometimes. I didn't know it was every week. And I didn't know my mother cries in the parking lot afterward, because the hip hurts and she doesn't want anybody to see. My wife's been carrying that every Tuesday for five months, and I've been asking how her day was and hearing "fine."

JONATHON: Okay, so the rule was don't fix anything. And I know you, so I have to ask. How long did you last?

WES: About four minutes. I had it in my mouth. "I'll take her next week." And I stopped, because that's what I always do. She tells me something hard, and I grab the first job I can find so I don't have to sit in it. So I asked her what else. And she talked for twenty minutes. About Mom. About the drive. About how she likes the drive, actually, and hates the parking lot.

JONATHON: That's the part I wouldn't have guessed. She likes the drive. If you'd jumped in with "I'll take her," you'd have taken the part she likes.

WES: That's exactly it. At the end she said, "I don't need you to fix it tonight. I needed you to know it." I've been married since 2000, and I'm still learning that sentence. I don't know yet what I'm supposed to do about Tuesdays. I know I didn't know, and now I do.

[BOARD: Hearing it first is part of carrying it.]

JONATHON: And the text. Give me the rest of the text.

WES: So, "Are you dying?" I wrote back, "Not dying. Just asking." And he called me. Twenty minutes. He's deciding whether to stay with his company once he tops out of the apprenticeship, and he hadn't told a soul. I've texted that boy a hundred times. "Did you change the oil." "Call your grandmother." Never once how he was. Then I asked him to do the single version, since he's the only single man I can still make do things.

JONATHON: Did he actually do it, or did he just say he would? Because I know a twenty-one-year-old's "yeah, sure."

WES: Thursday he texted me. I'll read it. "Phones in the trucks at lunch. The quiet guy, the one who never talks, has a baby coming in March. Nobody knew." That's the most words he's sent me in one text in his life. And afterward I sat out in the truck a while, and caught myself whistling the song my dad used to whistle feeding cattle every morning. Same song, every day. I never knew its name. I don't know what that was about. I'm just telling you.

## SEGMENT 2 · Jonathon checks the box

WES: Now you. All of it.

JONATHON: So I did not check the box the first time. Tuesday night, Dani's on shift, it's me and the kids. I put my phone in the kitchen junk drawer. Felt great. Very spiritual. We sit down, and the watch beeps. Seven o'clock. And right then I hear the phone buzz in the drawer, and I'm a sales manager, so to me a buzz is a customer. So I get up.

WES: You got up from the table you were supposed to be sitting at. Did anybody notice, or did you get away with it?

JONATHON: I opened the drawer, and Ruby says from the table, "Daddy, you said." Three words. Seven years old. That was worse than any performance review I've ever sat through. And it wasn't a customer. It was a reminder about a dentist appointment. So, box not checked Tuesday.

WES: Then what? Because the challenge was this week, not one night.

JONATHON: Thursday, Dani was off. Phone in the truck this time, like you. And I asked her, "What's Tuesday like for you?" She said, "Tuesday I get home at seven-thirty in the morning, I sleep from nine to two, and then I pretend I'm not tired until you get home." Ten years married, and I'd never once thought about the pretending part. And then I offered to buy blackout curtains.

WES: Jonathon. You went straight to a fix, and a shopping fix at that. How did she take it?

JONATHON: She said, "We have blackout curtains. I bought them. You hung them." So that was fixing, and bad fixing, fixing a thing that's already fixed. But then I shut up, and she kept going, and she told me she feels like she misses half the kids' week. She hears about school from me, secondhand. And I just sat there, and it was the best twenty minutes we've had in a while.

WES: That's the whole challenge right there. And the text?

JONATHON: I sent it to the contractor who invited me to that breakfast seven years ago. We still do business. "How's your Tuesday?" He called me. His wife had surgery last week, and he runs a crew of twenty guys, and not one of them knew she was in the hospital. Twenty years he's run that crew. So, box checked on the second try.

WES: The second try counts. The challenge was never do it perfectly the first night. It was do it this week. And if you missed Tuesday, you've still got a weekend.

## SEGMENT 3 · From the Table

WES: We're about halfway. Here's one I get a lot, especially from a man who's just starting. "How do I start if I've never been the spiritual one? My wife's the one who prays. She knows the Bible. I'm the guy who drives everybody to church. Where do I start without looking stupid?"

JONATHON: That was me, word for word. Dani's had faith since she was a kid. She sings alto. She can find things in the Bible without the little tabs. I still use the tabs. And my first move was to buy a whiteboard that said "Family Devotions" across the top. It belongs to Theo now. It's mostly dinosaurs.

WES: That's the first thing I'd tell that man. Don't announce a program. Don't come home with a binder. Start small and honest. Pray one sentence out loud at one meal. "God, thank you for this food and these people. Amen." You don't need beautiful words.

JONATHON: The first time I prayed out loud at supper, I counted it later. Eleven words. And Ruby said, "That was short." Tough room. But Dani squeezed my hand under the table and didn't say anything. And I prayed the next night. Before that I prayed out loud in my truck for about a year, where nobody could hear me mess it up. A couple of times in the Taco Cabana drive-through, and the lady thought I was talking to her.

[BOARD: Ecclesiastes 5:2 (NIV) · "God is in heaven and you are on earth, so let your words be few."]

WES: There's a father in Mark chapter nine who's my favorite picture of this. Jonathon, set it up and read it.

JONATHON: So his son's been sick for years, really sick, and the disciples couldn't help. He brings the boy to Jesus and says, if you can do anything, help us. Jesus pushes on the "if," and the father says this. "I do believe; help me overcome my unbelief!" That's the most honest prayer in the whole book, honestly. Half yes, half "I don't know," and Jesus heals the boy anyway.

WES: And that's where most men really are. Half in. So the second piece is, tell your wife. "I want to grow in this. I don't know how. Will you help me?"

JONATHON: Okay, here's my pushback. That one's hard, because it sounds like I'm admitting she's ahead of me. Some guys would rather read the whole Old Testament than say that one sentence out loud.

WES: She is ahead of you, and she already knows it. You're not admitting anything she doesn't know. You're joining her. Most wives I've sat with aren't waiting for a sermon. They're waiting to not be the only one. Third piece, pick one book and read it on your own. Mark's short and it moves. One chapter at lunch, then tell her one thing you read.

JONATHON: And two other guys are listening. The man whose wife doesn't share his faith, and the man with no wife at all. What changes for them?

WES: If she doesn't share your faith, don't make her your project. Pray for her, not at her. Keep your sentence at supper short, if she's okay with it, and let her see a difference on a Tuesday before she hears about it on a Sunday. The single man has it easier in one way. Nobody's watching, so there's nothing to perform. But somebody has to know you're trying. His version of "tell your wife" is "tell a brother."

[BOARD: Matthew 6:6 (NIV) · "But when you pray, go into your room, close the door and pray to your Father, who is unseen."]

## SEGMENT 4 · Tool on the Table

WES: We've got about four minutes. The tool this week is a phone basket. The details matter. Put it where everybody walks in, by the door or on the counter on the way to the table. Not on the table. At supper every phone goes in it, and yours goes in first. If you're the one who made the rule, you're the first one who obeys it.

JONATHON: Here's the problem at my house. You take a kid's tablet away and suddenly it desperately needs to charge. Like, emergency charge. Life or death. And Dani's a nurse, so if the hospital calls, that one matters for real.

WES: Put a charging cord in the basket, and nobody needs to charge anywhere else. My daughter came up with that for her second graders. And set the one number that matters to ring loud, and let everything else wait. A customer at six-thirty can wait until seven-fifteen. A hospital can't. You know the difference. Your phone just pretends not to.

JONATHON: My phone thinks a coupon for oil changes is a hospital. So, single guy version. Same basket, his roommate's phone, his phone, whoever comes over to eat. And if he eats alone most nights, the phone still goes in, and he eats like a man who's actually in the room. Look out a window. Pray. Call somebody after.

WES: So here's your weekend. If you missed Tuesday, do it Saturday or Sunday. One meal, phones in a basket, and one real question to whoever's at the table. And if you checked the box, ask a second person.

## CLOSE (word for word)

WES: Here's what I want you to carry out of here. Somebody in your house is carrying a Tuesday you don't know about. Ask, and then don't fix it.

JONATHON: And the second try counts. Don't let the first night be the only one.

WES: Tuesday, why nobody ever handed you a sentence for what a man is, and what Jonathon told his four-year-old in the bathtub. Until then: one basket, one question.

JONATHON: Then tell us what you heard at walksteadfast.com, and find us everywhere at @steadfastwalk.
