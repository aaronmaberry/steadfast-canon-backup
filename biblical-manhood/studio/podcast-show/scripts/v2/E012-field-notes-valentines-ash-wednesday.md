**Arc.** *Hook:* Wes took his wife to Mr. Abernathy's diner for the first time in twenty years. "The coffee's terrible." *Tension:* the report. At the doctor's office Wes goes first with "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me." His brother feels ambushed, then admits in the parking lot, "I never asked her either," and Mom announces a monthly pot roast through her car window. Jonathon's mom answers October's dread with "I'll bring the ham." A morning note comes back with "Decaf next time." Jonathon takes the kids to Ash Wednesday alone for the first time. The nephew's chili feeds seven out of three bowls. *Turn:* do you lead when your wife is further along in faith? Yes, because going first isn't being the most mature; it's moving first (Proverbs 27:17). Jonathon asks Wes a hard one, and Wes doesn't make up an answer. *Resolution:* the first apology, with three rules, and a tease of the fourth mark.

## SEGMENT 1 · Wes checks the box

JONATHON: Start with the doctor. I've been thinking about your mom all week.

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on it. Tuesday, two o'clock. My mom, my brother, me, and a doctor who's maybe thirty-five and very patient. We sit down, and my brother takes a breath to start, because my brother always starts. And I went first. I said, "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me." He looked at me like I'd stolen his truck. And my mom sat up straight in that chair and told the doctor her whole plan. The house. Cleaning help every two weeks. The walker in the bathroom and nowhere else.

JONATHON: And what did the doctor say? Because I was half expecting the doctor to side with your brother.

WES: She said, "That's a very good plan." Then she added a grab bar, daytime driving only, short trips, church included, and keep up the therapy. My mother agreed to all of it, because it came from the doctor and not from her sons. And my brother, right there in the room, said, "We talked about this." Kind of short. Meaning, you and I were supposed to decide. I didn't say anything back. I let it sit.

JONATHON: Okay, I'm nervous for you just hearing it, and I wasn't even there. What happened after?

WES: Parking lot. Mom's getting in her car. She drove herself, of course. My brother and I are standing by his truck, and he says, "Well." Then a long time. Then, "I never asked her either." Two grown sons, a hundred and ten years between us, arguing about her life, and neither one of us asked her. Then she rolled her window down and said, "I want to cook Sunday lunch once a month. ## SEGMENT 3 · From the Table

WES: We're about halfway. Here's the question, and I hear it from a lot of men. "Do I lead even if my wife is more mature in faith than me?"

JONATHON: And for the record, we found Habakkuk. Dani found it, in about four seconds. She didn't even look at the contents page. She opened right to it like a magic trick. I asked how, and she said, "Sunday school, 1997." So that's where we're starting from.

WES: That's where a lot of men start. So, plainly. Yes, you still go first. But going first doesn't mean you're the most mature one in the room. It means you're willing to move first. Those are different things.

JONATHON: But how do I go first in something she's better at? I say "let's pray," and then I pray this short, clumsy prayer, and she's been praying since she was six.

WES: Then you pray the short, clumsy prayer. She's not grading it. And I'd bet what she's been waiting for isn't a better prayer. It's for somebody else to start one once in a while, so it's not always her. Here's a short verse, from Proverbs, a book of short sayings about everyday wisdom. Let me get the glasses. "As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another." It doesn't say the duller one has to wait until he's as sharp as the other one before they start. They sharpen each other by rubbing up against each other.

JONATHON: So I don't have to catch up first. That's the part I've been stuck on for seven years.

WES: You'll never catch up first. She's got a twenty-five-year head start. So learn from her out loud. Ask her to teach you. "How'd you find Habakkuk?" is going first. Asking her what she prays about is going first. And then you start the prayer at supper, badly, on purpose, so she's not carrying the asking.

JONATHON: Can I ask you a hard one? You and your wife don't pray together. Is it because she was further along, and you never caught up?

WES: Maybe. I've wondered that. She was the one praying for twelve years while I was in the recliner. Maybe I still feel like the guy who showed up late. I don't know. I'm not going to make up an answer.

JONATHON: Okay. That's an honest okay. And single guys, this one's yours too. If you're dating somebody who's been in church her whole life and you're new, don't fake it, and don't hide behind her. Ask her to teach you something. That's not weakness. That's going first.

[BOARD: 1 Peter 3:7 (NIV) · "heirs with you of the gracious gift of life, so that nothing will hinder your prayers."]

## SEGMENT 4 · Tool on the Table

WES: We've got about four minutes. The tool is the first apology. If you went first this week, there's a good chance it went sideways somewhere, and the next first thing is usually an apology. Mine was my brother. I went first in that doctor's office, but I didn't warn him, and he felt ambushed in front of the doctor. So Wednesday I called him. Here are the rules. One, go first even if you think you're less wrong. If it's seventy-thirty and you're the thirty, you apologize first for your thirty. You don't wait for the seventy.

JONATHON: That's brutal. Every part of me wants to wait for the seventy.

WES: It's brutal, and it works. Two, name it in their words, not yours. My brother would say "you ambushed me." I'd say "I took the lead." So I said, "I'm sorry I ambushed you in front of the doctor." Not "I'm sorry you felt ambushed." That's not an apology. That's a weather report. Three, after you say it, stop. Let the silence belong to them. Count to ten in your head if you have to. The silence is where they decide whether to believe you.

JONATHON: So what did your brother do with his silence? Because I know how he answers the phone.

WES: He said, "Well." Then the silence. Then, "It was the right call. I'd have liked to know." Which is fair. Next time, I tell him first. And this works anywhere. The buddy you stopped texting back. The roommate you've been cold to for a week. The kid you yelled at in the truck. Seventy-thirty, their words, then quiet. If you're single and you live alone, your first apology might be to a man you haven't talked to in two years. Make the call this weekend.

JONATHON: I did mine about the coffee, by text, because she was asleep. "I'm sorry I made you rocket fuel at bedtime." She sent back a coffee cup and a sleeping face. In emoji, that's full forgiveness. And Tuesday the coffee was decaf.
