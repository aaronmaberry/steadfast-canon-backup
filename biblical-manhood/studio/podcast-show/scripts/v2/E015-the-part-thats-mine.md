**Arc.** *Hook:* "I took credit for a kid's deal once." "When?" "Eight years ago. He works for me now." *Tension:* six weeks of what was handed to us, and now the part that's ours. Jonathon put his name on a rookie's forty-one-school deal and went to Cancun on it. His dad explains why winning matters, but his sister, same dad and same house, would drive back to return a nickel. Wes faked a headache in 2010 to skip his five-year-old's Christmas program and watch a game. His dad never came to anything, but his dad never lied about it. *Turn:* whoever's in the last sentence of your story is who you think is responsible. A man who has dealt with his part tells his story short. Romans 7:18: the problem is inside, and so is the wanting. Paul's next breath is rescue. Jonathon pushes back: I just learned my dad did damage, and now it's my fault too? Both are true. If someone hurt you, that's theirs, all of it. *Resolution:* tacos and one sentence ("I know... You owe me Cancun"), a Cancun picture Dani defends in her socks, and a son who asks, "Was I a good shepherd?" The challenge: tell one thing you usually explain, with no "because."

## SEGMENT 1 · Forty-one schools

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on that deal.

JONATHON: 2019. I'm a senior rep. Commercial HVAC, so big systems, schools, hospitals, warehouses. We've got a rookie, twenty-three, first real job, works like a dog. He finds a school district that's about to replace the rooftop units on forty-one schools.

WES: Forty-one schools. That's a career deal for a twenty-three-year-old.

JONATHON: He works it six months. Knows every facilities guy by name. Knows which roofs leak. He's at board meetings at seven at night in a tie that doesn't fit. Then it gets close, and my manager says, "Jonathon, go to the last meeting with him, make sure it closes." Which is fair. That's my job. It closes. And on the way back to the office I'm entering it in the system, and there's a field for lead rep. And I put my name.

WES: Just like that? Did you think about it at all?

JONATHON: I told myself it was because I closed it. I told myself he'd get his share of the split, which he did, a little. And that year I made President's Club. Top ten in the company. They send you and your wife to Cancun. Dani and I went to Cancun on that kid's deal. Five days. There's a picture of us on a boat on our fridge right now.

WES: Did he ever say anything?

JONATHON: Never. He shook my hand at the awards dinner. Two years later I got promoted, and now he works for me. He's thirty-one. One of my best reps. And every time he's in my office, I know. For years, when I thought about it, I had an explanation. Dad left when I was nine. A kid whose dad leaves grows up needing to win. My sentence was "win the room." And that's real.

WES: It is real. I'm not going to argue with that part.

JONATHON: But here's what wrecked my explanation. My sister. She's forty-one. Same dad, same house, same day he left. She was fourteen, and it hit her harder than me. And my sister would return a nickel. If a cashier gave her a nickel too much, she'd drive back. I'm serious. She has driven back. So if it's all the dad, how come she'd never have touched that field, and I did it without slowing down?

WES: So what's your answer? You've had a week with it.

JONATHON: I wanted it. That's my answer. The dad thing explains why winning matters so much to me. It doesn't explain why I was willing to take it from a twenty-three-year-old. That part's just me. And every year when the President's Club list comes out, I think about telling him, for about a day. Then I think, it's been too long, it'd be weird, he's probably over it, and what's he going to do with it now anyway?

WES: That last one's interesting. "What's he going to do with it now." You were deciding for him whether he needed to hear it.

JONATHON: I was protecting him from the truth. Real generous of me. Eight years of generosity, and he never asked for any of it.

WES: That's usually how it goes. The longer you wait, the more you can tell yourself it's for him.

## SEGMENT 2 · The shepherd

WES: Mine's smaller than yours, and it bothers me more than it should. December 2010, the fog years. My oldest son is five, in the kindergarten Christmas program. He's a shepherd. Bathrobe, dish towel on his head, and he's been practicing his line at the supper table for two weeks.

JONATHON: What was the line? Do you remember it? Because I'd remember Ruby's line forever.

WES: I don't remember. That's part of it. About an hour before, I told my wife I had a headache. Bad one. I'd stay home. She took the other two and went. And I didn't have a headache. There was a Monday night game on, and I wanted to watch it in the quiet. For years, when I thought about the fog, I put it on my dad. My dad never came to anything. He worked, came home, sat in his chair. I learned the chair from him. That's true too, and I'm not taking it back.

JONATHON: But you're about to take something back. I can hear it coming.

WES: My dad never told anybody he had a headache. He just didn't go. He didn't lie about it. He didn't teach me to fake a headache so I could have the game and look like the victim at the same time. I came up with that one on my own.

JONATHON: Can I push on that? Because I hear "my dad did it too" and it sounds like an excuse, and then I hear "but I did it," and it sounds like you're beating yourself up. Where's the line?

WES: I think the line is whether the story ends with you or with him. An excuse ends with him. "And that's why I am the way I am." Period. The other way, you tell the same true stuff about him, and you keep going, and the last sentence is about you. Whoever's in the last sentence is who you think is responsible. Listen to how you tell your own story sometime, and see who's in the last sentence.

JONATHON: Did your wife know? About the headache? Because Dani always knows when I'm faking something.

WES: She told me last week, when I finally said it. "I knew. I could hear the game from the driveway." Sixteen years, and she never brought it up once. I asked her why not. She said, "Because you came back. I wasn't going to make you pay for the ones from before." Then, "Also, I was really mad for about a month." Then, "Did they win?" I said, "They lost." She said, "Good."

## SEGMENT 3 · The short story

WES: Can I say one thing from twenty years at men's tables? Every man, when he tells you his story, has a "because" in it. "I drink too much because my dad drank." "I'm hard on my kids because nobody was ever soft on me." And most of those becauses are true. But a man who hasn't dealt with his part tells his story long. Twenty minutes, almost all about other people. His dad, his ex, his boss, the church that hurt him. He's the only one in his own story who never did anything.

JONATHON: And the man who's dealt with it? What does his version sound like?

WES: Short. Two or three sentences. "My dad drank. I drank too, for a long time. I quit in 2009." Then he asks about you. He's not hiding the dad. He's just not living in that part of the story anymore. I used to tell the fog years in about forty-five minutes. My dad's chair, my dad's silence, all of it. Mr. Abernathy let me tell it once, start to finish. Then he said, "Now tell me the part you did, in one sentence."

JONATHON: So what was the one sentence? Because that's a mean question from a man that nice.

WES: I sat in a recliner for twelve years while my wife raised three kids. That's a short story. It's the true one.

JONATHON: My version of my story is pretty long. I've told the dad-left story at three men's retreats, and I'm good at it. People cry. It's got a tailgate and a dent in it. I don't think I've ever told the short one.

## SEGMENT 4 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway. Let me get the glasses. This is Paul, a man who did everything right on paper. Raised right, best schools, kept every rule. He's writing to the church in Rome, describing a man who wants to do right and keeps not doing it. "For I know that good itself does not dwell in me, that is, in my sinful nature. For I have the desire to do what is good, but I cannot carry it out." Notice where he puts the problem. Not in how he was raised. Not in Rome. In me.

JONATHON: The desire part is what gets me. Because I wanted to be a good teammate to that kid. I really did. I liked him.

WES: And part of me wanted to be at that program. Wanting it isn't the problem. Nobody's short on wanting.

JONATHON: The year I came to faith, I read Dani's old Bible from high school, because I didn't have one yet. Romans 7 is underlined. In pink. And in the margin, next to that verse, in her handwriting, it says, "me." And she was sixteen. I remember thinking, what could a sixteen-year-old in youth group have possibly done? She sang alto. She was in bed by ten. Now I think she just noticed it earlier than I did. I needed a lot more years and a forty-one-school deal.

WES: That's a good margin note. And this is where it turns. If the problem's in you, and you can't carry it out, you might think you're just stuck. Paul asks that a few verses later. He says, I'm a wreck, who's going to get me out of this? And his very next breath is, thank God, Jesus is. So it's not "you're the problem, try harder." It's "you're the problem, you can't fix it, and somebody already came to." If it's all your dad, there's nothing to bring to God but a complaint. If part of it's yours, you've got something to hand him.

JONATHON: So naming it is how you get it off you. Not how you get buried under it. That's the part I had backwards for years.

WES: Naming it is how you get it to him. Same thing, I think.

[BOARD: Romans 7:25 (NIV) · "Thanks be to God, who delivers me through Jesus Christ our Lord!"]

## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

JONATHON: Okay, real pushback, because I've sat with this all week. For six weeks I finally started understanding that my dad leaving did something to me. That took me most of my life. And now, the very next week, it's "yeah, but it's your fault." That feels like whiplash.

WES: That's fair, and I want to be careful. Your dad leaving did something to you. That doesn't go away because of today. Both things are true at once. He left. You took the deal. You don't have to pick one. Most of us have only ever heard one of them. Either nobody told you your dad did damage, or nobody told you that you did damage too. You need both. The first without the second is an excuse. The second without the first is just shame.

JONATHON: And the man who got really hurt? Not "dad worked too much." I mean somebody did something to him as a kid.

WES: Then that's on them. All of it. Hear me say that clearly. Nothing we're saying today moves one ounce of that onto you. If somebody hurt you, that's their weight, and they'll answer for it. What we're talking about is the stuff you did later. With your own hands, as a grown man. That's the only part that's yours.

JONATHON: And the man who hears "Lent" and thinks, great, forty days of feeling bad about myself.

WES: I grew up around some of that. I don't think that's it. You don't name the thing so you can sit in it for forty days. You name it so you can hand it over and walk out lighter.

JONATHON: So it's more like cleaning out the truck than wearing a hair shirt. And I have a very dirty truck.

WES: That's better than anything I was about to say. And I've seen your truck. One more. The man with a clean record. Awards on the wall. Nothing you could put in a story like this. I'd ask him to look at the awards. Somebody else probably did some of the work on a few of them.

JONATHON: That's a low blow, Wes. That's my story you're pointing at.

WES: I'm just pointing. A clean record isn't the same as a clean heart. Some of the cleanest records I know belong to men who've never been caught wanting something bad enough. And single guys, if you think getting married will fix the stuff that's yours, it won't.

JONATHON: It just moves it into a smaller house. Dani's met every part of me I thought I'd outgrown by twenty-five, and a couple I didn't know about.

[BOARD: 1 John 1:9 (NIV) · "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness."]

## SEGMENT 6 · What we actually did

WES: So what did you do?

JONATHON: Monday I took him to lunch. The rep. Tacos, because that's apparently my thing now. I didn't plan a speech. I had one sentence, and I said it before the chips came, because I knew if I waited I'd talk myself out of it. "The district deal in 2019 was yours. I took it. I'm sorry."

WES: And what did he do? That's a lot to drop before the chips.

JONATHON: He said, "I know." Just "I know." Then he ate a chip. Then he said, "I always figured you knew." Which was worse. Eight years he's been sitting in my office figuring I knew. Then he said, "You owe me Cancun." And he's completely right, and I don't know what to do with that one yet. Thursday I'm telling my regional VP, because it's in the system with my name, and the system's still wrong. One sentence, then stop. "The 2019 district deal should have been his, and I put my name on it." I'm not asking for anything. I'm scared of that one. The lunch was a guy I'd wronged. The VP is the room, and I've spent my whole career winning that room.

WES: And Dani? Because she's in that picture.

JONATHON: Monday night I told her about the lunch. Then I said, "Do you want me to take the Cancun picture off the fridge?" She'd been asleep. She came out in her socks, looked at me like I'd lost my mind, and said, "No. We went to Cancun. I was on that boat. I got sunburned on that boat. That's my picture too." Then she went back to bed. She was mostly mad I'd woken her up.

WES: I told my wife first, which you heard. Sunday afternoon I called my oldest. He picked up and said, "What's wrong?" Because I don't call him on Sundays. I said, "Nothing's wrong. I want to tell you something I did." The program, the shepherd, the headache, the game. "There wasn't a headache. I wanted to watch a game. I'm sorry." He was quiet. Then, "Dad, I don't remember that at all." Then he said, "Was I a good shepherd?" And I said, "I don't know, son. I wasn't there."

JONATHON: Oh, Wes. That one's going to stay with me. What did he say to that?

WES: He said, "Okay." Then he told me about a transformer at work for about ten minutes, and I listened to every bit of it.

## SEGMENT 7 · This week's challenge

WES: Before we're done, the challenge. Pick one thing you did that you usually explain. The story where there's always a "because." Tell it to one person this week with no "because." Just what you did.

JONATHON: Married guys, that might be your wife. One sentence, no explanation after it, then stop talking. That's the hard part for me. Single guys, it's a friend, a brother, or it's out loud to God in your truck. Out loud. Not in your head. Your head's where the "because" lives.

WES: Divorced men, carefully. Don't use this to reopen the divorce. Say it to God first, and to one man. If something's owed to her, a man you trust can help you see what and when. And for everybody, if the thing you'd say is the kind that changes a marriage, an affair, money she doesn't know about, don't drop it on her on a Tuesday night because two guys on a show told you to. Talk to a pastor or a counselor first, and get help with the big ones.
