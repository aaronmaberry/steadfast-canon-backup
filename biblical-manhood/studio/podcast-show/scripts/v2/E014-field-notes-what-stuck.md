**Arc.** *Hook:* "Did you do it?" "Yes." "What was it?" "No." *Tension:* the box that's checked and empty. Wes's wife asks if he's buying her a boat. Dani says "Don't. You'll ruin it." The nephew texts "did it. not telling." The wills get signed, followed by tacos in the truck. Then six weeks, honestly: phone jail stuck, decaf stuck, nine mornings out of twenty-one didn't, asking before deciding is spreading, and a ceiling fan that has clicked through every episode. *Turn:* Paul's review in Philippians 3: not there yet, one thing, forget what's behind in both directions, press on. *Resolution:* keep what stuck, date one thing that didn't, and the Thursday text that has run since January 14.

## SEGMENT 1 · The empty box

JONATHON: You start, since you already failed this one once on Tuesday by telling everybody about the coffee.

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on it. I did it. Wednesday afternoon. That's all you get. But here's the part that isn't the thing. I got home, and my wife said, "Where were you this afternoon?" I said, "Out." She looked at me a good while. Then she said, "Are you buying me a boat?" I said, "No." She said, "Then I don't care," and went back to her crossword.

JONATHON: She's not even curious? Dani would've had it out of me in four minutes.

WES: She's very curious. She's also been married to me twenty-seven years. She knows if she waits, I'll tell her. So now it's me against her patience, and I've never won that. Not this week. Probably by Easter. I'm weak.

JONATHON: Mine was Thursday. And it was the hardest one so far, and it's not close. I'm a salesman. If I do something good and nobody hears about it, it's like it didn't happen. That's how I'm wired. I got home, Dani was off, I sat down on the couch next to her and said, "So, today." And she put her hand up and said, "Don't. You'll ruin it."

WES: She's been listening to this. She knew exactly what the challenge was.

JONATHON: She's my accountability now, which I did not sign up for. And it was weird, sitting there not saying it. I wanted to say it so bad. Then about ten minutes later, I didn't. It went quiet in me. Like it got put somewhere.

WES: That's where it goes. I couldn't tell you exactly where that is. But it's safer there than on a leaderboard.

JONATHON: And the thing I can tell you, because it happened at a lawyer's office. Yesterday morning, ten o'clock, Dani and I signed our wills. A month ago I didn't know our mortgage number. Yesterday I initialed fourteen pages. There was a notary who stamped everything really hard, like she was mad at it. And Dani signed faster than me. I'm reading every line, because I read contracts for a living, and she's just sign, flip, sign, flip. I said, "Are you reading these?" She said, "We read them two weeks ago."

WES: And after? Because that's a big morning. Did either of you say anything?

JONATHON: We walk out to the truck, and I'm thinking, this is a moment, I should say something. And before I can, she says, "I want tacos." Eleven in the morning. So we ate tacos in the truck in the church parking lot. It was the most adult thing I've ever done, followed immediately by eating a taco over a steering wheel.

WES: That's the whole thing, though. Your kids are taken care of, and you got tacos. Neither one cancels the other out.

JONATHON: And the single check. His version was keep one thing off the board on purpose. My nephew texted Wednesday, all lowercase: "did it. not telling." Then a minute later: "is that right." No question mark. I wrote back, "That's right." He sent a thumbs-up. That's two thumbs-ups in my life this month, and one was from you.

WES: That's a young man who's never done a good thing without somebody seeing it. And mine was better.

## SEGMENT 2 · What stuck

WES: We're about halfway, and here's the review. "We've done a bunch of stuff. What do I keep?" Nobody keeps all of it.

JONATHON: I wrote it down. Every challenge since January. Supper with no phone. Write your sentence. Read it to somebody. The nightly check. The thing you've been waiting on. Ten minutes. Pick up the weight. Go first. One thing only God sees. That's a lot for six weeks, Wes.

WES: It's a lot. So let's be honest. You first. What stuck?

JONATHON: Supper with the phone off the table. Most nights. And it stuck because Ruby took it over. She got a basket from the garage and wrote PHONE JAIL on a piece of tape, all capitals, stuck it on the front. She checks Dani's too. Dani gets home at seven-thirty in the morning, so it's breakfast for her, and Ruby doesn't care. Phone jail. She jailed Dani's phone for twenty minutes Tuesday. And the coffee. I make it before Dani gets home. Decaf now. I learned.

WES: And what didn't stick? Because that list is shorter than I expected.

JONATHON: The ten minutes in the morning. Nine times in three weeks. I keep starting, then the kids are up, and it's gone.

WES: Nine out of twenty-one. That's almost half. Six weeks ago it was zero. And look at why phone jail stuck. It's not because you got more disciplined. It's because a seven-year-old picked it up and made it hers. The things that stick usually stop being yours alone. Somebody else in the house starts carrying the other end. If something on your list keeps dying, ask who could help you hold it. Maybe it's not a willpower problem. Maybe it's a one-man problem.

JONATHON: Don't do math at me. But okay. That's true. Now you, and don't say the coffee, because you were already doing the coffee.

WES: What stuck: asking before I decide. It started with my mom, and it's spreading, which is a little annoying for everybody. Saturday my youngest said, "Do you want me to drive?" and I almost said yes before I asked whether he wanted to. He did. I held the handle. What didn't stick is the ceiling fan in his room. It clicked the week my wife said my sentence was "I'll get to it," and it's clicking right now, as we speak. I did a whole episode on picking up the weight with your name on it, and that fan has clicked through every word.

JONATHON: Why haven't you fixed it? Because I know you know how to fix a ceiling fan.

WES: Honestly? It's ten feet up. I don't want to get on a ladder with these knees, I don't want to say that out loud, and I don't want to pay a man to do something I could've done at forty. So it clicks.

JONATHON: That's not a fan problem, Wes. That's a knees-and-pride problem wearing a fan costume.

WES: That's not a fan problem.

## SEGMENT 3 · From the Table

WES: There's a verse for a review. Paul is about as far along as anybody ever got, and here's how he does his own review. He's writing from prison to a church that loved him. Let me get the glasses. "Brothers and sisters, I do not consider myself yet to have taken hold of it. But one thing I do: Forgetting what is behind and straining toward what is ahead, I press on toward the goal to win the prize for which God has called me heavenward in Christ Jesus." The first thing he says is, I'm not there yet. Paul.

JONATHON: So your fan's in good company. If Paul wasn't there yet, I don't feel so bad about nine out of twenty-one.

WES: Good company. Then he says, one thing I do. Not nine. One. And "forgetting what is behind" cuts both ways. You don't live off the will you signed yesterday, and you don't live under the ten minutes you missed. Both are behind you. You press on.

JONATHON: Okay, but I'll push back. "Forget what's behind" is how a lot of guys skip the apology. "That's in the past, man." My dad would love that verse.

WES: That's fair. Paul's not saying pretend it didn't happen. He's saying don't camp there. The man who skips the apology isn't forgetting what's behind. He's dragging it. You deal with it, then you put it down and walk forward. So if you only kept one sentence from six weeks, it's this, and I'll say it once. A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

JONATHON: That's four things, though. You just finished telling us Paul only did one thing.

WES: It's one man. Four things one man does. But for this week, don't pick four. Look at your own list. Whatever stuck, keep doing it, and don't make a speech about it. Whatever didn't, pick one, just one, and put a date on it.

JONATHON: Mine's supper. I'm keeping supper. Phone jail every night I'm home. And the one that didn't stick, the ten minutes, I'm putting on the kitchen counter next to the coffee maker. No date. Just every morning I make the decaf.

WES: Mine's the fan, by March first. I'm calling my son-in-law, he's holding the ladder, and I'm going up three rungs and no higher. And if that's not enough, I'll pay the man. That's picking up the weight at fifty-two. You still carry it. You just ask for help with the ladder.

[BOARD: Ecclesiastes 4:10 (NIV) · "If either of them falls down, one can help the other up."]

JONATHON: And single guys, I'll say this, because I did six weeks as a married guy and my nephew did it single. Some of these were built for a man with a wife at the table. That's on us. So your review is the same question, just shorter. What stuck? What didn't? One date.

WES: And if you're past sixty and you've been listening to two younger men talk about phone jail, the question's the same for you. What do you want to be doing a year from now that you're not doing today? Pick one, and start it this month. The man who held my ladder starts his car-care Saturdays March sixth. I'm on coffee.

JONATHON: Decaf, probably, since that's what we do now. The single moms are going to love you.

## SEGMENT 4 · Tool on the Table

WES: We've got about four minutes.

JONATHON: And this tool I can show, because we've been doing it the whole time and I never mentioned it. The Thursday text. Every Thursday since January fourteenth, I've texted Wes the same four words. "Did you do it?" And he's answered every time. Twice the answer was "K."

WES: "K" is an answer.

JONATHON: "K" is not an answer to "Did you do it?" "K" is what you text when you've seen a text. Here's the tool. One man. Every Thursday. Same four words. Thursday because it's two days after Tuesday's challenge and a day before you have to admit anything on Friday. It's the nudge in the middle.

WES: And three rules. One, same man, same day. Don't rotate. The point is somebody's expecting it. Two, the answer can be one word. "Yes." "No." "Tonight." You're not writing a report. If he wants to talk, he'll call. Three, if he says no, you don't fix him. You text back two words. "Next week." Not "Why not?" Not a verse.

JONATHON: Why not a verse? I thought you'd want a verse in there.

WES: Because he already feels bad, and a verse on a Thursday afternoon is a lecture in a nice outfit. Single guys, you probably already have a group chat. Pull one man out of it. Same four words. Divorced dads, this is a good one for the week you don't have the kids. That's the quiet week. That's when you need somebody asking.

[BOARD: Hebrews 10:24 (NIV) · "And let us consider how we may spur one another on toward love and good deeds,"]
