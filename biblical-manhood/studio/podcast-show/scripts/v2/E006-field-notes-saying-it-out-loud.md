**Arc.** *Hook:* Dani tells Jonathon the mark she'd love to see more of: "Stop waiting." "On what?" "On your dad." *Tension:* the report. Wes tells his son-in-law his weakest mark and they stand by the trucks for forty-five minutes. His wife names his best mark, then asks for "Go first. With me." Wes admits one thing he hasn't fixed. Jonathon's contractor has a binder and a second career, and a friend request from Lake Charles has sat unanswered since Christmas. *Turn:* do you need all four marks? Your strongest one can become your hiding place, and Philippians 1:6 says you aren't the one who finishes you. *Resolution:* the sixty-second voice memo, recorded live, and Wes's one-letter reply.

JONATHON: No. She'd been holding that one since Christmas.

## SEGMENT 1 · Wes checks the box

JONATHON: You first. Weakest mark, and I think I already know the answer.

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on mine. Stop waiting, which surprises nobody. The gutter, the brothers, all of it. And I told my son-in-law. He's twenty-seven, works for the county, married my daughter two years ago. And I realized this week I've never had a real conversation with that young man. Football, rain, the county budget.

JONATHON: Rain's a big topic out there. I've heard you talk about rain for twenty minutes straight.

WES: Rain's a big deal in the Hill Country. But I'd never told him anything true about me. So Sunday after lunch we're out by the trucks, and I said, "Can I tell you something?" I showed him the card and told him my weakest one. He was quiet a second, and then he said, "Mine's probably the same." And we stood out there forty-five minutes. My daughter came out twice to see if somebody was hurt.

JONATHON: Two men by trucks for forty-five minutes, not talking about rain. She figured somebody died.

WES: Pretty much. Then I asked my wife which mark I do best. She said, "Pick up the weight." Didn't think about it. And she reminded me of when her dad was sick, years ago. I drove him to treatments for most of a year. I'd forgotten that. Honestly forgotten. She hadn't.

JONATHON: That's the one I'd want to hear. And the other question, which one she'd like to see more of?

WES: She said, "Go first. With me." And then, "Like last Friday. More of that." The Christmas money conversation. She noticed the one time, which tells you how rare it is.

JONATHON: Okay, but you're making it sound like everything wrapped up nice this week, and I know you better than that.

WES: It didn't. Here's one I haven't fixed. She and I don't pray together. Out loud, just the two of us. Twenty-seven years. We pray at meals, at church. I pray for her every morning. But the two of us, out loud, at the kitchen table? We don't. And I don't know why. I've thought about it a lot. I don't know. That's where it is.

[BOARD: Some things you only get to name this week. Fixing comes later.]

JONATHON: Okay. I'm not going to push on that. And the nightly check?

WES: Five nights out of five. One, two, zero, one, one. The card's in my Bible, so I see it every morning and remember every night.

## SEGMENT 2 · Jonathon checks the box

WES: Now you.

JONATHON: Nightly check, two out of five. The card's on my truck visor, and I'm not in the truck at bedtime. That's a design flaw. So I took a picture of the card, and it's my lock screen now. I look at my lock screen about ninety times a day. It's the one note I'll actually see.

WES: I'm not a fan of notes on phones. They go there to die. But I'll allow it.

JONATHON: Weakest mark is the pay one. I said that Tuesday. And I told the contractor, the guy from the breakfast. He's kind of my guy now, which I didn't expect. I told him mine, and he told me his. He said, "Go first." His wife was in the hospital and he still hasn't told his crew, because he doesn't want to look weak. He's sixty. Twenty years running that crew.

WES: Did he do anything with it? Or did he just say it to you and go back to work?

JONATHON: He said he'd tell them Monday, and I'm checking on him Monday. Then he told me something else. He wants to sell the company in five years and teach welding at the community college. Never told anybody. And he's got the whole thing worked out. He's got a binder. Tabs. A sixty-year-old man with a binder he's never shown a soul.

WES: Saying a weak mark out loud opened a door to the thing he really wanted. That happens a lot.

JONATHON: And the single check. My nephew texted me his weakest one. "Pay. I post everything." And then a second text: "Like everything." That's twenty-two, honestly, and that's pretty brave for a guy who doesn't want to be cringe.

WES: That's honest. Then Dani. You started the show with half of it.

JONATHON: So I asked her which one I do best. She said, "Go first." And I said, really? Because I'm always the one talking about what I'm bad at. And she reminded me that in year three of our marriage, I was the one who said we should see a counselor. Not her. Me.

WES: I remember. I told you to do it.

JONATHON: You did, but I'm the one who said it to her. Then I asked which one she'd like to see more, and she said, "Stop waiting." On your dad. He sent me a friend request on Christmas Day. From Lake Charles. It's still sitting there. I haven't said yes, haven't said no. Every time I open the app, it's the first thing I see, and I close the app. And she's watched me do that for weeks.

WES: So you're good at going first except where it costs the most.

JONATHON: That's what she was saying. She said it really kindly. But yes.

WES: You don't have to do anything about that this week. I mean it. That's not a Friday challenge. That's a season. You don't solve your dad on a Friday.

JONATHON: I know. I just wanted to say it out loud, because I think saying it out loud is the first step I've taken on it in about twenty-seven years.

## SEGMENT 3 · From the Table

WES: We're about halfway. So, the question from the men's table. "Do I really need all four? Or can I just work on the one I'm weak in?" Work on the one you're weak in. That's where your effort goes. But don't drop the other three while you do it. They come together.

JONATHON: But in sales you coach your weakest skill, and you sell with your strongest one, every day. Isn't that the same thing?

WES: That's close to right. Your strong mark is how you carry your weak one. Mine's pick up the weight. So if I'm going to stop waiting, I do it by picking up something specific. The gutter was a weight. But here's the warning I learned the hard way. Your strongest mark can turn into your hiding place.

JONATHON: Okay, say more about that, because I don't think I've ever heard it put like that.

WES: For years, when things got hard with my wife, I fixed things. The sink. The car. Her dad's rides to treatment. All good things. And all of it was a way to not go first in the conversation we actually needed. I picked up every weight in the house except the one that mattered. My strength was a recliner. A busier recliner, with tools.

JONATHON: Mine's go first. So my version is, I go first on everything that's easy to go first on. The counselor. The scary text to you. And I use those to feel like a guy who goes first, so I don't have to go first on my dad. You didn't say that. I'm saying it.

WES: And every mark has its own hiding place. The man who's great at stop waiting stays so busy starting things he never sits still with anybody. The man who's great at God's pay serves at church four nights a week, and his kids get the leftovers. He's working for God, sure. He's also hiding from his own living room. Nobody ever criticizes that man, which is exactly what makes it a good place to hide. So when you look at your card, don't only ask which mark is weakest. Ask which one you'd be proudest to be caught doing, and whether you're doing it so you don't have to do the hard one.

[BOARD: James 5:16 (NIV) · "Therefore confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed."]

WES: So the answer's yes, you need all four, and no, you don't need all four this week. Work on one. Watch your strong one so it doesn't turn into a place to hide. And don't panic that you're not all four yet. Nobody is. Paul wrote a letter from prison to a church he'd started, and in the first few lines he tells them why he's not worried about them. "Being confident of this, that he who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus."

JONATHON: He who began it. That's not me. That's the part I keep skipping. I read that verse like it says I have to finish it.

WES: It's good news. You're not the one who has to finish you. Your job is to keep showing up while he works.

JONATHON: Here's my pushback, though. "He'll finish it" can turn into "so I'll wait on him." Which is the exact thing Dani just called me on.

WES: That's fair. He finishes it, and he does it through a man who shows up. He's not asking you to finish yourself. He's asking you to open the app someday. Not this week. Someday.

## SEGMENT 4 · Tool on the Table

WES: We've got about four minutes. And this week Jonathon brought the tool.

JONATHON: I finally brought a tool. My tool is my phone, and Wes hates this. The tool is a sixty-second voice memo. Once this week, you open the voice memo app and record sixty seconds. You say your sentence out loud. You say your weakest mark. And you say one specific thing you'll do about it tomorrow. In your head it's mush. Out loud it's a promise.

WES: And then what? Because I've got voice memos on my phone from 2019 I've never listened to.

JONATHON: Two choices. Send it to your one man instead of a text. A text is two seconds. A voice memo is your actual voice, which is harder and better. Or keep it and listen once, the next morning in the truck, and hear yourself make a promise.

WES: You made me write on a receipt. Do it right now.

JONATHON: Okay. Here goes. "A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's. My weakest one is God's pay. Tomorrow I'm going to do one thing at work nobody will see and nobody will thank me for, and I'm not telling anybody I did it. Including Wes." Fifty-one seconds.

WES: Who are you sending it to?

JONATHON: You. And you're not allowed to reply with a thumbs-up. Mostly because you don't know how. He replies to everything with the letter K.

WES: It's efficient. And single guys, this might be the best tool you've got. Nobody's at home to hear you say it, so say it to your phone and send it to a brother.
