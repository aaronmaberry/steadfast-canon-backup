**Arc.** *Hook:* Jonathon opens the October conversation with Dani with one sentence: "I've been making you pay for my mom." *Tension:* what going first costs. For Jonathon, being the good son. For Wes, a yelled-at sixteen-year-old with earbuds in, and an apology that gets "Okay," then twenty minutes of silence, then "Sorry about the stop sign" at the cattle guard. *Turn:* John 10:4. Out front is where the snake is, and the sheep follow a voice they've heard on ordinary days. Jonathon pushes back with a friend's father who moved the family four times and called it headship. The bossy man goes first into the decision, and the shepherd goes first into the cost. *Resolution:* coffee made before anyone else is up, an eighty-one-year-old with a sign-up sheet, the two hosts disagreeing about whether going first counts when your wife asked five times, and Wes's plan for his mother's doctor appointment: "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me."

WES: What did she say?

JONATHON: Two words, and she didn't look away from me. "Keep going."

## SEGMENT 1 · Sunday night

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on Sunday. The October conversation.

JONATHON: After the kids were down. Eight-fifteen. I made two cups of decaf, which in my house means a meeting. And I knew how it would go if I waited. She'd start, because she always starts. She'd say something about Thanksgiving, I'd get defensive, and we'd be right back in October. So I went first. I'd practiced that sentence in the truck ten times, out loud, on 1604. On purpose this time. Great place to practice. Nobody's moving.

WES: That's a big first sentence. And then she said, "Keep going." So what did you say next?

JONATHON: I said, every holiday we build the whole thing around whether my mom's going to be sad. Your family gets whatever's left. And when my mom's sad anyway, I can't be mad at her, because she raised us by herself, so I get short with you instead. She didn't say anything for a while. Then she said, "I thought you were going to make me be the bad guy again."

WES: Again. That's the word that would've gotten me.

JONATHON: Because that's what I'd done every year. I'd let it get to where she was the one who said, "We're not doing three houses on Christmas." Then she was the bad guy to my mom, and I got to be the nice son. I made her go first so I didn't have to. It went about an hour. Not pretty the whole time. She brought up last Easter. I said something about her sister I shouldn't have. But we didn't stop. And at the end we had a plan. Easter lunch at our house, everybody invited, my mom included, and nobody drives to three houses.

WES: So what did it cost you? Going first.

JONATHON: Being the good son. As long as Dani said the hard stuff, I got to be the one my mom liked best. The minute I said it first, I'm not the nice one anymore. I'm the one who changed Easter. And I've been the good son since I was nine. That's a long time to be good at something. And I haven't told my mom yet. That's this week.

WES: I want to stop on "keep going," because that's what going first usually gets you. Not applause. Not "thank you, honey." Just a door open about two inches. A man goes first expecting the whole thing to resolve, and what he gets is "keep going." That's not her being cold. That's her checking whether you'll still be out front on the second sentence.

JONATHON: That's exactly what it felt like. Like the first sentence bought me the second one, and that was all it bought. I had to keep paying.

## SEGMENT 2 · The earbuds

WES: Can I tell a small one? Because I don't want anybody thinking going first only counts when it's a big talk at a kitchen table. You know the rolling stop. "It's just a truck." What I didn't tell you is how I handled it. I yelled. In the truck. Not a little. My voice did the thing my dad's voice used to do, and I heard it come out of me.

JONATHON: I didn't know that part. What did he do when you yelled?

WES: What I used to do. He went quiet and looked out the window. We didn't talk about it, and it just sat there for weeks, even through the body-shop talk. Then Saturday I'm driving him out to my brother's place for his first Saturday working off the dent. Forty minutes, and he's got both earbuds in.

JONATHON: The earbuds are a wall. I've got a seven-year-old, and she's already figured out headphones are a wall.

WES: And I'm thinking, he's not going to bring it up. Sixteen-year-olds don't open with "about the stop sign." If anybody's going first, it's me. So I tapped his arm, and he took one earbud out, which is the most a sixteen-year-old will give you. And I said, "I yelled at you about the stop sign. The yelling was wrong. The stop sign wasn't." And he said, "Okay," and put the earbud back in.

JONATHON: That's it? You go first, you say the hard sentence, and you get "Okay"?

WES: For twenty minutes. Then, right as we came over the cattle guard at my brother's place, he took both earbuds out and said, "Sorry about the stop sign." And got out of the truck.

JONATHON: He waited until he had an exit. That's the most sixteen thing I've ever heard.

WES: He's my son. I don't have a big point on it. I just know if I'd waited for him, we'd still be not talking about it. And he heard me say "wrong" about myself, out loud, in my dad's truck voice. I didn't hear that much growing up.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway. Let me get the glasses. John chapter ten. Jesus is talking to religious leaders who'd been bad shepherds, using the people instead of leading them, and he paints a picture every one of them would know. "When he has brought out all his own, he goes on ahead of them, and his sheep follow him because they know his voice."

JONATHON: Why out front, though, practically? Wouldn't you want to be behind them, where you can see the whole flock?

WES: Because out front is where the trouble is. When my kids were little, and we'd walk out back through the tall grass, I always walked first. Not because I'm brave. Because it's the Hill Country, and something in that grass might bite. Whoever goes first finds the snake. That's the part men skip. We hear "leader" and think it means the good seat. In the picture Jesus paints, out front is the dangerous seat.

JONATHON: And the second half, "they know his voice." That's the part I keep thinking about.

WES: They don't follow him because he announced he's in charge. They follow because they've heard that voice a thousand times on ordinary days. The voice that fed them and found them when they wandered off. So when it says "this way," they come.

JONATHON: So you can't go first on Valentine's Day if nobody's heard your voice since Christmas.

WES: That's about right. Going first on the big night only works if they've heard you on the little ones.

JONATHON: I don't know my dad's voice. I'd know his truck. I don't know what his voice sounds like saying my name. I was nine. So when I read that, I think, Ruby and Theo know mine. From bath, from the car, from me yelling about shoes. I'd like it to be a voice they'd follow.

WES: They already do. You just can't see it from out front.

WES: And look at where this shepherd goes first. A few verses later he says he'll give himself up for them. He doesn't send anybody ahead to check the grass. He walks out front all the way to a cross. Every man who goes first into the cost is following somebody who went first into the worst cost there is.

JONATHON: That actually helps with the fear part. Because I'm not inventing going first. I'm walking behind somebody who already did it, and I'm just trying to stay close enough to hear.

[BOARD: John 10:11 (NIV) · "The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."]

## SEGMENT 4 · Pushback

JONATHON: Okay, here's my problem with go first. I've been sitting on it since the day you gave us the sentence. I grew up around men who used this. A buddy from youth group, his dad moved the family four times in eight years for his job. Never asked. Just came home and said, "We're moving. I'm the head of this house." Like that settled it.

WES: I've met that man. More than once, at more than one table.

JONATHON: So how is "go first" not just a nicer way to say "I decide"?

WES: Because of where you go. That dad went first into the job he wanted and let his family pay the cost. That's not out front. That's walking off and calling it leading. Here's my test. Did he ever go first into anything hard for himself? The apology. The conversation where he might hear he was wrong. Asking his wife what she wanted before he decided. Or did he only go first into decisions?

JONATHON: Only decisions. I never once heard that man apologize. My buddy actually said so in his wedding toast. It was a weird toast.

WES: And the other test is the voice. If a man has to say "I'm the head of this house," I'd bet he isn't out front very often. Nobody has to announce the man who walks first through the grass. You can see him.

JONATHON: So the bossy guy goes first into the decision, and the shepherd goes first into the cost. And Sunday was a cost one. I didn't decide anything for Dani. I said the hard sentence about me first, and then we decided together.

WES: That's it. Going first into the decision is easy. Going first into the cost is the whole thing.

JONATHON: Okay, a Valentine's one. What about the man whose marriage is cold right now? Not a fight. Just cold. Two roommates with a mortgage. A reservation isn't going to fix that.

WES: No. Don't start with the big night. Start with something small enough she can't argue with, and do it every day until she notices. After the fog years, when I finally came back to the table, I didn't have the words for a talk. So I started making the coffee. She'd made it every morning for twelve years. I'd never once gotten up before her. I set an alarm for five-fifteen.

JONATHON: How long until she said something? Because Dani would've asked on day one if I was sick.

WES: Eleven days. On day eleven she said, "Why are you making the coffee?" And I said, "I'd like to be the one who's up first." And she cried, a little, into the coffee. For twelve years she'd been up first, alone, in a quiet kitchen. And now somebody was in there with her.

JONATHON: Dani gets home at seven-thirty on shift mornings, and I'm out the door at seven-fifteen. So I could leave the coffee made, and a note. That's fifteen minutes earlier. Okay. I'm doing that.

[BOARD: Ephesians 5:25 (NIV) · "Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her"]

WES: And for the man who thinks this is a young man's mark, remember the man who held my ladder? Eighty-one. Last month he went to our pastor with an idea. One Saturday morning a month, he and a couple of retired guys do oil changes and brakes for single moms at church. No charge. He's got a sign-up sheet. Nobody asked him to. He saw the snake and walked out front. I'm on the sheet for March. I'm in charge of the coffee.

JONATHON: You're in charge of the coffee. That's perfect. You know your gifts.

JONATHON: Okay, one I'm just going to plant, because it's Friday's question. What if your wife is further along in faith than you? Dani's been a Christian since she was six. I've been one for seven years. She knows where Habakkuk is. How do I go first spiritually with a woman who could teach the class?

WES: Where is Habakkuk?

JONATHON: I genuinely do not know. That's my point. I'm supposed to lead family devotions, and the family devotions whiteboard is covered in dinosaurs.

WES: That's a real question, and it deserves its own time. Friday.

## SEGMENT 5 · Two chairs

JONATHON: Can we do one where I don't agree with you? Because I don't think we'll settle it. Sunday night. Dani brought that conversation up five, six times since October. She asked for it. So when I finally did it, was that going first? Or was it just doing what my wife asked, late?

WES: It was going first. Going first doesn't mean you thought of it. It means you moved first among the people who were stuck. She'd asked, sure. But asking isn't opening it. You said the first hard sentence.

JONATHON: See, I don't think so. She'd already found the snake. She told me where it was, five times. I just walked over and stood on it. I want it to count more when I see it myself, before she has to say it.

WES: And that, right there, is the pay thing. You want credit for being the one who thought of it.

JONATHON: Maybe. Or maybe I want to be the husband who sees it before she has to say it five times. That's not about credit. That's about her not having to carry the asking.

WES: That's fair. I hadn't thought about the asking as a weight. So I still think Sunday counted. You think the real version is seeing it before she asks.

JONATHON: I think Sunday counted, and I'd like to need fewer Sundays. We can leave it there.

## SEGMENT 6 · What we actually did

WES: Today's the doctor. Two o'clock. When my brother asked me to come, my first thought was, I'll listen to the doctor, and then my brother and I will figure out what Mom needs. That's what we did at Thanksgiving. Two grown men arguing across a table about whether a seventy-eight-year-old woman should live alone. And she was sitting right there. Nobody asked her.

JONATHON: So did you ask her? Please tell me you asked her before you walked in there.

WES: Last night I called her. Before I said anything about the appointment, I said, "Mom, before we see the doctor tomorrow, what do you want?" She was quiet. Then she said, "Nobody's asked me that." And she talked for half an hour. She wants to stay in her house. She'll take help cleaning every two weeks. She will not have a walker in the living room. "I'll use it in the bathroom, but not where people can see it." She's had a plan the whole time. We just never asked. So today, the first thing I say in that room won't be my opinion or my brother's. It'll be, "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me."

JONATHON: Does your brother know you're doing that? Because I've heard about your brother.

WES: He does not. Now you.

JONATHON: Monday I texted my mom, because I'm the one telling her about Easter, not Dani. I didn't want to do it by text, so I texted to set up a call. "Can I call you Thursday at seven? Want to talk about Easter." And she wrote back, "Is everything ok??" Two question marks.

WES: That's my son's "Are you dying?" with punctuation.

JONATHON: Because I've never once scheduled a call with my mother. So Thursday at seven, I say it first, and kind, and I don't know how she'll take it. And Saturday, at a place with real tablecloths, because Dani works Sunday night, I'm going to say the first real thing instead of waiting for her to. I've got a sentence picked out.

WES: Are you going to tell us what it is?

JONATHON: No. It's hers. And yesterday morning I left the coffee made, with a sticky note. I'll tell you Friday how that went.

## SEGMENT 7 · This week's challenge

WES: Before we're done, the challenge. Find one place where everybody's waiting for somebody else to move, and go first. One small, true thing before Friday. Name it to your one man first, so you can't back out. Married, it's Valentine's week, so here's the hard one. Think of the conversation you two keep not having. Don't try to have the whole thing. Just say the first sentence, on a calm night, not at the restaurant.

JONATHON: And if she doesn't pick it up, that's okay. You still went first. You don't get to grade her answer. Single guys, Valentine's week is weird to be single. Make the plan. Don't wait for the group chat. Text two guys, "Dinner Sunday, my place, I'm making chili." Or go first at church. Find the man who sits by himself and leaves before the last song, and introduce yourself.

WES: Divorced dads with your kids this week, help them make a valentine for their mom. Buy the paper. Sit with them. Your kids will remember who went first. And for every dad, if you blew it with a kid this week, say "I was wrong" first. Then get out of the truck.
