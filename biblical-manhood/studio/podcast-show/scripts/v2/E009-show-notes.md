**Episode description (for podcast apps and YouTube)**
How does a man tell the weight that's actually his from the weight that isn't? Most of us have the two piles mixed up.

Jonathon confesses that he and Dani have no will after three years of "we really need to do that," because one line on the form means picturing his kids without him. Wes tells how he paid for his sixteen-year-old's dented tailgate, called it grace, and heard "It's just a truck" three weeks later.

They open Galatians 6, where Paul says both "carry each other's burdens" and "each one should carry their own load." Every man carries his own duffel, and nobody carries a couch alone. Jonathon pushes back that this sounds like bootstraps, then names the weight he's carried since he was nine that was never his. Plus a mortgage he doesn't know the number of, a terrible bed, a body-shop bill on the kitchen table, and twelve Saturdays at a brother's place.

This week's challenge: put your hand on one weight that's yours, and name one you're carrying that isn't.

## This week's challenge

1. **Pick up one weight with your name on it** that somebody else has been carrying. One real step before Friday: a call, an appointment, a hard sentence.
2. **Name one thing you're carrying that isn't yours.** Write down whose it actually is. Don't set it down yet.
3. **Married:** on a calm night ask, "What do you carry around here that's really mine?" Write it down. Don't argue.
4. **Single:** your budget, your health, your landlord, your emergency contact.
5. **Divorced dad:** your weekends are yours, and they count.
6. **Parents:** look for the dent. What are you paying for that your kid should carry?

Then tell us what you picked up at walksteadfast.com, or tag @steadfastwalk with #WalkSteadfast.
