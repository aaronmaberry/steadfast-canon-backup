**Arc.** *Hook:* every Monday at eight for eleven years, a ranked email with Jonathon's name on it, opened before anything else. *Tension:* the cheap prize. Jonathon's leaderboard resets by Thursday, and he can remember the fall he was number one in the country but not his kids that fall. Wes's cheap prize has a pulpit: being the man the church calls on to pray, exposed by a moment in a grocery aisle hoping someone would notice the good coffee. *Turn:* the room pays fast, measurably and publicly, so you choose God's pay on purpose. Colossians 3:23-24 (an inheritance, not a paycheck) and Hebrews 12:2 (the joy set before him). Then pushback: isn't heaven a bribe? I need my paycheck. And one Wes doesn't try to fix: "I've never heard 'well done' from a father." *Resolution:* the backs of Mr. Abernathy's receipts, a list of names on Wes's card, a son-in-law at the front of the church, an email moved to ten o'clock, and "Booked two." The challenge: name your cheap prize, and do one good thing nobody sees and tell no one.

## SEGMENT 1 · The email

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on that email.

JONATHON: Every Monday at eight, corporate sends the regional leaderboard. Every sales manager in the country, ranked by the number. Your name, your rank, a little green arrow or a little red one. And I don't look at it. I wait for it. I'm in my truck in the driveway at seven fifty-eight with the email open, refreshing. Eleven years. When I was a rep, it was the rep board. Now it's the manager board.

WES: What's the feeling when it comes in?

JONATHON: If I'm top five, I walk in the house like a king. I'm funny at breakfast. I'm patient with Theo's shoes. If I'm not, I'm short with everybody until about Wednesday, and nobody at my house knows why. I told Dani Saturday night. She said, "I always wondered why Mondays were weird." She just thought I didn't like Mondays.

WES: So she'd noticed for years and never knew the reason.

JONATHON: And here's what got me this week. The board resets every Monday. Say I'm number three on Monday. By Thursday the new week's numbers are rolling in, somebody had a big Tuesday, and I'm number nine. The prize is gone by Thursday. Every week. I did the math in the truck. That's about five hundred and seventy Monday mornings. That's a lot of my kids' breakfasts riding on a spreadsheet in Ohio.

WES: What's the best week you ever had on that board?

JONATHON: Number one in the country. Fall of 2023, three weeks running. I had a plaque made. It's in my office.

WES: What else happened that fall?

JONATHON: I don't know. I genuinely don't know. Theo was one. Ruby was in pre-K. I bought a grill. That's all I've got. I remember the plaque, and I don't remember my kids that fall. And that was the 1604 years. Same fall.

WES: Is the job wrong, though? I want to be fair to the job.

JONATHON: No. I like my job. Selling air conditioning in Texas is honest work. The thing is who I'm doing it for. And on Monday at eight, it's obviously for the email.

WES: And I want to say this for the man who isn't in sales. You might not have an email. You might have a boss who grunts when you do it right, or a father-in-law who's never once said "nice work," or a group chat. Everybody's got a board somewhere. The question isn't whether you have one. It's whether it gets to decide what kind of man walks in your front door on Monday.

## SEGMENT 2 · The coffee told on me

WES: Mine's more embarrassing, because it's at church. When our church needs somebody to pray in front of everybody, at a funeral, a dedication, a big Sunday, they call me. Have for years. And people say nice things after. "Wes is a rock." And I love it. That's the problem. I love the walk up to the microphone and the hand on my shoulder after. I didn't think there was anything wrong with that, because what's wrong with praying at church?

JONATHON: Nothing, on the outside. I'd have said you're a rock too, honestly.

WES: Here's what I caught. You remember the car-care Saturday, the oil changes for single moms? I'm on coffee. Last week I was at the store buying the coffee, the cups, the cream, and I caught myself thinking, I hope somebody notices I got the good coffee. For single moms getting their brakes done in a church parking lot, I'm standing in aisle seven hoping somebody notices the coffee. And then I thought, if I want credit for the coffee, I definitely want credit for the microphone. I've been working for the room at church for twenty years, and from the outside it looks exactly like serving God.

JONATHON: So you're the email, Wes. Yours just has a pulpit and better lighting.

WES: The coffee told on me.

JONATHON: Can I say why I think the cheap one wins? From the sales side. The best incentive plans pay fast. You close Tuesday, you see it Monday. If you made reps wait a year for commission, they'd quit. Fast, measurable, and other people can see it. And the room pays exactly like that. The Monday number. The hand on your shoulder. A like that comes in eleven seconds after you post. I've timed it. I'm in sales. And God's pay is the opposite of a good incentive plan. It's slow, you can't measure it, and mostly nobody sees it. If I designed a comp plan like that, I'd be fired.

WES: Which is why you have to choose on purpose which one you're working for. The cheap prize will always feel better on Monday. It's built to. If you go with your gut, the room wins every time. You don't wait to feel like the inheritance is worth more. You decide it is, live like it, and the feeling catches up later.

JONATHON: How much later, for you? Because you've been at this a lot longer than me.

WES: Some days it hasn't caught up yet. I'm fifty-two, and I still want the hand on the shoulder. I just know which one I'd rather have at the end.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway. You've been reading this one all week.

JONATHON: All week. Paul's writing partly to servants in other people's households. People whose work nobody thanked and nobody ranked. "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving." "Whatever you do" is a big whatever.

WES: It's the email and the coffee. The HVAC quote and the diaper. Every bit of it.

JONATHON: But here's where I get stuck. That part about an inheritance as a reward sounds like pay. Work hard, God pays you. Which sounds like earning your way in, and you've told me a hundred times you can't.

WES: You can't. Paul says it plainly in Ephesians 2. You're saved by grace, through faith. It's a gift, not from works, so nobody gets to brag. So look at the word. An inheritance isn't a paycheck. You don't get it for putting in hours. You get it because of whose family you're in. Ruby didn't earn your last name. She was born into it. The reward isn't God paying you for being good. It's a son working in his father's house with his whole heart, because it's his family's house, and his father sees him.

JONATHON: So the email ranks you. And the Father already named you. That's very different.

WES: That's it. And one more, short. Hebrews 12, about how Jesus did the hardest work anybody's ever done. "Fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith. For the joy set before him he endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God." Nobody at the cross was cheering. The whole room was against him. The room's pay was less than zero. It was shame. And he did it anyway, looking at a joy the room couldn't see.

JONATHON: Can I tell you the one time I think I felt it? The other pay? Last spring, Theo had croup. Two in the morning, Dani on shift, and he's barking like a seal and scared, and I'm sitting in the bathroom with the shower running hot for the steam, holding him. About an hour. Nobody's watching. No board. And at some point he stopped barking and fell asleep on my chest in the steam, and I just sat there, thinking, this is the best thing I've done all year, and there's no board for it. And it didn't feel like nobody saw it. It felt like somebody saw it.

WES: Somebody did. That's the thing I'm talking about.

[BOARD: Matthew 6:4 (NIV) · "Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you."]

## SEGMENT 4 · Pushback

JONATHON: Okay, pushback. The obvious one. "God's reward." Isn't that just a bribe? Be good, get a prize? That's how a lot of guys I know hear it. Heaven's the bonus check.

WES: That's fair, because a lot of preaching makes it sound that way. Here's the difference. A bribe is something you'd take instead of the person. The reward here is the person. It's God himself. Say it with your email. If corporate sent you a bonus check with no name on it, no rank, nobody watching, would you still feel like a king on Monday?

JONATHON: No. I'd cash it. But no. So it was never the money. It was being seen and ranked.

WES: Both prizes are about being seen. The question is who's looking.

JONATHON: Second one, and this is the real one. I need my paycheck. My boss signs it. My kids eat because of that email. The room pays my mortgage.

WES: And Paul isn't saying blow off your boss. If anything, the man working for God works harder for his boss. Here's how you can tell the difference. What do you do at four on a Friday, when nobody's checking?

JONATHON: That's my worst hour. I'm on the phone with my college buddy every Friday at four. He's in commercial real estate. He's not working for anybody at four.

WES: Everybody's worst hour is Friday at four. That's where you'll find out who you work for.

JONATHON: Third one, and I don't want you to fix it. I just want to say it. You keep saying the reward is "well done," from the Father. I've never heard that from a father. Not once. My dad left when I was nine. So when you say "your Father sees you," I don't know what that's supposed to feel like.

WES: I'm not going to fix it. I'll just say that's Easter. We're going to spend real time on fathers, the one you had and the one who doesn't leave. And I don't think you're the only man listening who just felt that.

JONATHON: Okay. Last one. What's God's pay to a sixty-year-old who's already made his money? Like my contractor. He told me, "I've made money for forty years. I'd like to do something for a while that nobody pays me much for." He's going to teach nineteen-year-olds to weld for about a third of what he makes now, and he's more excited than I've ever seen him.

WES: That's a man who's figured out which pay he wants. He's not winding down. He's switching rooms. And the man laid off in January, with no Monday email at all, only applications nobody answers? God's pay is the only pay he's getting right now, and it's still real. The way he talks to his kids when he's scared about money. Some of the best men I know learned this in a year with no paycheck.

JONATHON: And the twenty-four-year-old whose whole life is the room? The gym, the posts, the group chat. He doesn't even have a job with an email, and his whole week's a leaderboard.

WES: That young man has the hardest version of this and the best chance. Hardest, because his room is in his pocket and it never closes. Best, because he can pick which pay he wants before he's spent eleven years on the wrong one. If you're twenty-four and listening, you're not too young for this. You're right on time.

[BOARD: Matthew 25:21 (NIV) · "Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a few things"]

## SEGMENT 5 · The back of the receipts

WES: You know about the receipts. Thirty-some in Mr. Abernathy's Bible, each with a man's bad sentence on the front. Mine was in there. What I've never said is what was on the back. On the back of every one, in his handwriting, was the man's first name and a little column of dates. His daughter asked me if I knew what they were.

JONATHON: What were they? I'm honestly a little scared to hear it.

WES: The days he prayed for that man. Mine went back nine years. Every week or so. Some weeks twice. Nine years of dates on the back of a diner receipt, and he never said a word to me about it. No microphone. No hand on his shoulder. I told my wife at the diner last week. I don't really know what to do with it. I just know that's the kind of work I want to be doing at eighty-eight.

And here's what's strange about it. I spent those six years of Thursdays thinking I was the one showing up for him. Every other Thursday, the eggs, the booth. I thought I was the project and he was the volunteer. Turns out the real work was happening at his kitchen table on mornings I never saw. The breakfast was the part I could see. The receipts were the part that counted.

JONATHON: That's the opposite of the email. Nine years, and he never once checked his rank.

## SEGMENT 6 · What we actually did

WES: So, two things, and you're going to catch me on the first one. I bought the coffee for the car-care Saturday and told the man running it, it was donated. Which it was. By me. He doesn't know that, the moms won't know that, and I'll stand there pouring it like it came from nowhere.

JONATHON: Wes. You just told everybody listening. That's the opposite of nobody knowing.

WES: I know. I thought about that. I'm telling you because I said I'd tell you what I did. Next time, I won't. And two, I started doing what he did. On the back of my index card, the one in my Bible, there's a short list of men's first names now. I pray for them by name most mornings.

JONATHON: Who's on it? Come on. Am I on it? I'm definitely on it.

WES: Not telling. That's the closest I can get to secret on this show. And one more, and it was public anyway. Sunday they asked me to pray at the start of service, and I said, "Ask my son-in-law." Twenty-seven, never prayed in front of the church in his life. I sat in the third row. He was nervous, it was short, and it was good. And afterward a couple of people put a hand on his shoulder, and I watched it from the third row.

JONATHON: Did you want one? A hand on the shoulder? Be honest.

WES: A little. Less than last week. Now you.

JONATHON: The email. I set a rule in my settings, so it doesn't come to my phone until ten. Monday morning the first thing I opened was breakfast. I was weird about it all morning. Checked my phone eleven times to see if it came early. At ten I opened it, I was number six, and I felt bad for about four minutes, and then a customer called and I forgot about it.

WES: Four minutes. That's a big drop from Wednesday.

JONATHON: Down from three days. And the small one. The rep I reviewed, the one who said, "I've been waiting for somebody to say it." Thursday after work I stayed an hour and went through his call list with him, line by line, and didn't tell my boss. It felt like nothing, like doing my job. Then at nine that night he texted me, "Booked two." And that felt like more than number three ever did.

## SEGMENT 7 · This week's challenge

WES: Before we're done, the challenge, in two parts. First, name the cheap prize you've been working for, out loud, to your one man. The email, the hand on the shoulder, the likes, being the nice guy, your dad's approval. You'll know it, because it's the thing that changes your mood when it shows up or doesn't.

JONATHON: Second, do one piece of good work this week that only makes sense if God's reward is real. Nobody sees it. And the hard part, don't tell anybody. Not your one man, and not us on Friday. Which means we can't check that box Friday, and that's the point. Married guys, pick something at home she won't find. Not the coffee. She'll find the coffee.

WES: Single, the next time you're about to post something you did, don't. Let it stay done. Live alone, start a list on the back of something. Three men's first names. Pray for them every day this week, and don't tell them. Divorced dads, stop keeping score with your kids. Who's the fun house, who bought the better present. Do the good thing at your house and let God see it.
