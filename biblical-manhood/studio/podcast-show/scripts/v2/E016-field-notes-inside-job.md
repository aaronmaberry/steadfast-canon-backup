"He said, 'Let's not make this a thing.'" *Tension:* the room Jonathon feared for eight years shrugs, and the rep he wronged is annoyed that he went over his head. Jonathon made it about himself again, a little. Ruby keeps better records than her dad. Wes tells his brother leaving Thanksgiving early was wrong and hears about a pecan pie. The fan has an appointment. Wes's son gets one sentence out before the "because" comes back. *Turn:* "Isn't this just blaming myself for everything?" No. Find your part, not everybody's part. ## SEGMENT 1 · The room shrugged

WES: You go first. Let's take the next few minutes on it, because you've been sitting on this since yesterday.

JONATHON: Thursday, two o'clock, scheduled call, fifteen minutes on his calendar. I wrote my sentence on a sticky note and stuck it to the dash of my truck, because I knew if I didn't have it in front of me I'd start explaining. And I said it. "The 2019 district deal should have been his, and I put my name on it." Then I stopped talking, like I said I would.

WES: How long was the quiet? I'm guessing it felt longer than it was.

JONATHON: Felt like a year. Probably four seconds. Then he said, "Appreciate you telling me." Then he explained that deals older than three years can't be reassigned in the system. Some database thing. Then he said, "Let's not make this a thing." An hour later, an email. Two lines. "Thanks for the call. Let's keep this between us."

WES: Huh. So what did you do with that?

JONATHON: Here's what got me. I'd built that call up as the room. The big scary room I've been winning my whole career. And the room shrugged. It didn't punish me. It didn't care. It wanted the paperwork to stay clean. I spent eight years scared of a room that couldn't be bothered.

WES: That's usually how it goes with the room. It's never as interested in you as you think. How'd it feel?

JONATHON: Relieved for about an hour. Then worse. Thursday night the rep texted me. Somebody in the regional office said something to him, and he wrote, "You didn't have to do that. Now he's going to be weird with me." And he's right. I went to the VP because I needed to say it to the room. I never asked the rep if he wanted his name dragged back into it. I made it about me again. A little.

WES: What'd you text back? Because that's the part that matters now.

JONATHON: "You're right. I should've asked you first. What do you want me to do?" He hasn't answered. So I'm going to let him decide what's next. It's his deal. It was always his deal. The lunch was a movie. This is a Thursday.

[BOARD: Lamentations 3:40 (NIV) · "Let us examine our ways and test them, and let us return to the Lord."]

JONATHON: And one more, because the VP was leftover business and the challenge was its own thing. Tuesday morning I yelled at Ruby about her shoes. We were late, she couldn't find the other one, and I yelled. Normally the next thing out of me is, "Sorry, buddy, Daddy's just tired." That's my because. Tired. It's always available.

WES: That's the best because there is. Every dad in the world has it in his pocket.

JONATHON: So Tuesday night at bedtime I said, "I yelled about your shoes this morning. I'm sorry." And I stopped. No tired. She thought about it, and she said, "You also yelled about the cereal." Which I'd forgotten. So I said, "I also yelled about the cereal. I'm sorry about that too." She said, "Okay," and asked if Jesus has a dog.

WES: That's Theo's question. She's stealing his material now?

JONATHON: She's stealing his material. I said I'd look into it. You.

## SEGMENT 2 · A pecan pie and three rungs

WES: Mine was my older brother. We've been better since the doctor's appointment. But there's a thing I never said. Thanksgiving, at Mom's, I left early. And the story I've told my wife, and told right here, is that I left to beat the traffic, because he was bossing everybody around about Mom.

JONATHON: Which he was. I've heard enough about that Thanksgiving to know he was.

WES: Which he was. But Wednesday I called him, and I said, "I left Thanksgiving early. That was wrong." And I stopped. He said, "Well." Then a long time. Then he said, "Mom made a pecan pie for you. I ate it." That's all he said about it. Then we talked about her grab bar for twenty minutes. I don't know if that's forgiveness. For my brother, I think a confession about a pie might be forgiveness.

JONATHON: And the fan. You promised everybody that fan by March first, so I have to ask.

WES: The fan has an appointment. Tomorrow morning, eight o'clock. My son-in-law's coming. He's holding the ladder, I'm going up three rungs, no higher, and my wife says she's sitting in the doorway with coffee because she wants to see it with her own eyes.

JONATHON: So it's not done. I just want everybody listening to hear that it's not done.

WES: It's not done. It has an appointment. That's further than it's gotten in a long, long time. And the single check is mine this week. My oldest son. Twenty-one, electrical apprentice. He listens, it turns out. He texted me Wednesday, and here's the whole thing. "told my foreman i wired the 200 amp panel wrong. didn't say the drawings were bad." Then a second text, one minute later. "they were bad though."

JONATHON: That's the best text we've ever read on this show. One whole sentence, then the because just walks right back in.

WES: He got one sentence out before it came back. That's better than I did until I was forty. And the drawings were absolutely bad. He still wired it.

## SEGMENT 3 · From the Table

WES: From the Table. After Tuesday this is the one I heard the most, and it's a good one. "Isn't this just blaming myself for everything?"

JONATHON: That was me Thursday night. After the rep's text I sat in the driveway and thought, okay, the deal's my fault, the VP thing's my fault, the shoes, the cereal. I'm the problem. Everything's me.

WES: That's not what Tuesday was. Tuesday was, find your part. Not, take everybody's part. Some men hear "it's not only what was done to you," and they swing all the way to "it's all me, I'm garbage." That's not humility. It's a different way of not looking at it straight. If it's all you, you don't have to sort it. You feel bad about everything in one big pile. Sorting is harder. This part's mine. That part's his. This part's nobody's, it was just a hard year.

JONATHON: So the VP shrugging isn't mine. That's what you're telling me. Because I took that one home too, and I carried it into supper Thursday, and Dani could tell before I sat down.

WES: The VP shrugging is the VP's. Not asking the rep first, that's yours. Two different things. And there's a man this hits hardest. The one who grew up blamed for everything. If you were the kid who got yelled at when your dad had a bad day, Tuesday probably sounded like home. Hear me. That wasn't yours then, and the big pile isn't yours now. And if what you're hearing in your head is "I'm worthless," that isn't conviction. Tell somebody today. A pastor, a counselor, your doctor.

JONATHON: I read something about this at two in the morning, actually. Dani was at work, the kids were asleep, and I was running the whole thing back. The lunch, the call, the email, every version where I did it better. I was the judge and the jury and the defendant, and I was losing. So I opened to Psalm 139, because you'd mentioned it once. "Search me, God, and know my heart; test me and know my anxious thoughts. See if there is any offensive way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting."

WES: David's a king when he writes that. Most powerful man in his world. And he doesn't search himself. He asks God to do it.

JONATHON: That's what I noticed. When I search me, I either find nothing or I find everything. There's no middle. God's the only one who finds what's actually there.

WES: You're a bad judge of you in both directions. He's not. He's thorough, and he's kind. He'll find the one thing that's there, and he won't make up nine more. And look where it ends. It doesn't end in punishment. He finds the thing, and then he leads you out.

[BOARD: Romans 8:1 (NIV) · "Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus,"]

JONATHON: And for the single guys, I think that might be harder, because nobody at the house says, "Hey, that wasn't all your fault." If you live alone, the voice in your head never gets interrupted. So get it interrupted. Tell one man what you think your part is, and let him tell you if you're taking too much.

WES: And divorced men. After a divorce everybody picks a side, and you end up with a story where it was all her or all you. Neither one's ever true. Find your part of the marriage, name it to God, and let the rest be hers. That's not letting her off. That's not carrying her half too.

## SEGMENT 4 · Tool on the Table

WES: The tool is old. It's called the examen, and Christians have been doing some form of it for about five hundred years. Here's the stripped-down version, three questions at the end of the day. First, where was God today? Where'd you see something good? Start there. Especially if you're the man who blames himself for everything, because if you start with the bad, you'll never get to the end.

JONATHON: Mine Tuesday was Ruby and the cereal. That was a good thing. She was honest with me, and she wasn't scared to be.

WES: That counts. Second question, what's mine from today? One thing. Not a list. The thing you'd most want to undo. And you say it to God, short, with no "because."

JONATHON: Only one? Because some days I've got a top ten, and Tuesday was one of them.

WES: Only one. If there are three, pick the biggest. The other two will still be there tomorrow, and you'll do better with them if you're not drowning tonight. Third question, what do I need for tomorrow? Patience with the kids. Courage for a phone call. Sleep. Ask for it. Then you're done. Three minutes. If it's taking fifteen, you're doing the "everything's me" thing again.

JONATHON: I've been doing it on the drive home. Twelve minutes from the office. I turn the radio off at the light by the Whataburger, do the three, and by the time I pull in, I'm done. I walk in the door already having said the thing, so I'm not carrying it into supper.

WES: I do mine with the lights out. My wife falls asleep in about ninety seconds, which is a gift, and I do the three before she starts snoring.

JONATHON: She snores? I'm writing that down for later.

WES: She does not snore. Don't put that in. Here's the weekend challenge, before we're done. Try the three questions tonight, Saturday and Sunday. Three nights. Start with the good, one thing that's yours, one thing you need. If you miss a night, don't make the missing into a fourth question.

[BOARD: Psalm 4:4 (NIV) · "Tremble and do not sin; when you are on your beds, search your hearts and be silent."]
