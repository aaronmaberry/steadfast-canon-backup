## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: Not Your Load
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 10 · February 5, 2027

On Tuesday the challenge had two parts: pick up one weight with your name on it, and name one weight you're carrying that isn't yours. On Friday, Wes Hart and Jonathon report back.

## Check the box

Wes got his knee results. It's arthritis in both knees, worse on the right, with no surgery for now. His father had both knees replaced and was never the same, and Wes had quietly feared the same road. Sitting in his truck afterward, he said out loud to nobody, "It's not Dad." His physical therapy turned out to be at the same clinic as his mother's, on the same day. Now they do their exercises side by side, and she counts his reps.

When he asked his wife what she carries that's really his, she said, "Your family's birthdays." For twenty-seven years she had bought the cards, signed his name and mailed them. "She remembered, and I got the credit." Every birthday is now in his phone. On the other list, he wrote down his oldest son's job decision: "His job. His."

Jonathon and his wife spent two hours on Wednesday night deciding who would raise their children if both of them died. Her parents for now, his sister as the backup, and they'll revisit it in five years. His wife said, "Okay. Good," went to bed, and came back a minute later to say, "Thank you." The lawyer meeting followed on Thursday. Then he asked his wife the same question Wes had asked his, She said "the garage," took it back ("the garage is mine"), thought for a second, and said, "Your mom. I'm the one who calls her back." On Tuesday he'd named his mother's holiday happiness as a weight that wasn't his. Calling her back was. "I carried her feelings and dropped her phone calls."

## From the table: what if I'm carrying stuff that isn't mine?

Wes watches for three signs: quiet, constant resentment; exhaustion with no progress for anyone; and control, carrying something because you're afraid of how someone else would handle it. Setting a weight down isn't throwing it on the floor. Every weight belongs to somebody. You hand it back to the person it belongs to, or you hand it up.

> "Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you."
> 1 Peter 5:7 (NIV)

For his son, Wes is handing it back with a sentence: "That's your call. I trust you. And if you want to talk it through, I'm here." And no more articles. "The articles are me taking it back."

## Tool on the table: the two-column list

Draw a line down a page. Write "Mine" on the left and "Not mine" on the right. Spend ten minutes listing everything you're carrying and sort each item with Tuesday's test: if I carry it, does it get lighter for somebody? Next to every item on the right, write a name, whose it is, and some of those names will be God's. Then say the sentence to that person or pray it by name. Next to every item on the left, write one next step. Read the right column to your one man, because those are the things you'll try to pick back up.

## Carry this

> If it's not yours, it still belongs to somebody. Hand it back, or hand it up.
> Sometimes you're so busy with the part that isn't yours, you drop the part that is.
