## Reading narrative

# The Itch and the Drift
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 21 · March 16, 2027

Last week the subject was anger, the loud way a man handles pain. This week Wes and Jonathon take on the two quieter ways. The itch takes a man down into something to feel better: a screen, a bottle, a betting app, and for many men, porn. The drift takes him away while his body stays in the room. The focus mark is Stop Waiting, because both are things a man plans to deal with "when things calm down."

## One-fourteen

With his nephew's permission, Jonathon tells about the text that came at 1:14 on Thursday morning: "how do you stop." Jonathon called back a minute later. It was porn, since the nephew was thirteen. It got worse when he was lonely and better once the Sunday chili table started, and then came a bad week. Jonathon didn't quote a verse or bring up a filter. He listened for an hour. Then he said, "I've been there. Longer than you'd guess." When the nephew asked how he stopped, Jonathon said, "Not by myself." The next day the nephew told the roommate he trusts the whole truth, and Jonathon now texts him every Thursday. He's on day five. "He'll probably slip. Nobody's pretending day five is the finish line."

## The fog again

Since October, Wes had spent two hours most evenings watching a man in Ohio restore a 1952 tractor and checking a weather radar that showed no weather. Last Wednesday his wife asked him the same question three times. Then she said, quietly, "You're doing the fog again." She hadn't used that word in fifteen years. When he asked himself what the drift was guarding, the answer was his mother, his knees, and his father's knees. "I could watch somebody fix something."

## The Word

> "Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it."
> Proverbs 4:23 (NIV)

A father in Proverbs tells his son what to guard above everything else. Anger is the wrong guard. The itch and the drift leave the gate empty. The good news is that a heart can be guarded, though not alone.

## What they did

Jonathon folded laundry with Dani for forty minutes instead of drifting to the couch. Wes asked his wife to sit on the back porch for twenty minutes with no phones. On the third night she fell asleep in the middle of a sentence, and he let her sleep. "There wasn't any weather anyway."
