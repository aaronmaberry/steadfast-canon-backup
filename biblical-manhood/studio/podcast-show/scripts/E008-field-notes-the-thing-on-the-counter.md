## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: The Thing on the Counter
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 8 · January 29, 2027

On Tuesday, Wes Hart put a phone call on his calendar, Wednesday at 7:00, and told everyone watching. On Friday he reports back.

## Check the box

Wes called his older brother at 6:59 from his truck. His brother picked up with "Well." It runs in the family. When Wes finally said, "What you said at Mom's was mostly right," the line went quiet long enough that he checked for a dropped call. Then his brother said, "I didn't need you to say I was right. I need you at Mom's doctor appointment on the ninth." Wes is taking the day off. "It's not fixed," he says. "It's started."

When Wes asked his wife what she'd been waiting on him for, she said, "Your knees." He'd been limping since October. He made the appointment. "I'm not asking because of your knees," she told him. "I'm asking because I want you around when we're old."

Jonathon finally held the review he'd postponed three times. The rep thanked him. "I knew. I've been waiting for somebody to say it." Jonathon and Dani picked a night to finish an October argument. And on her shift nights, he finally learned where the kids' pajamas are, with zero texts.

## From the table: how do I stop waiting when I'm genuinely tired?

Tired is real, and it isn't a character flaw. But there are two kinds of tired, and they feel the same at seven in the evening. Wes's test: if someone offered free brisket at the end of the street, would you get up? If so, you're avoiding. If not, rest. For the truly worn out:

> "Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest."
> Matthew 11:28 (NIV)

Come to God tired. Then give him ten minutes.

## Tool on the table: the ten-minute start

Set a timer for ten minutes (Wes uses his wife's ceramic chicken). Do the one thing you've been waiting on and nothing else. When it dings, you're allowed to stop without guilt. Most of the time you won't. If it's a phone call, the ten minutes is preparation, and when the timer dings, you dial.

## Carry this

> Come to God tired. Then give him ten minutes.
> You don't have to fix it. You have to start it.
