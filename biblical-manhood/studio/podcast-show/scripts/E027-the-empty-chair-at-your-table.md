## Reading narrative

# The Empty Chair at Your Table
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 27 · April 6, 2027

Jonathon has always told it short: his father left on a Saturday in October when he was nine, and what he remembers is the dent in the tailgate. This week he told the whole Saturday in order. He was dressed for his first start as a pitcher by eight o'clock, cleats on in the house. His father carried things out to the truck: a duffel, a box of cassettes, his fishing rods, and finally the tall lamp his mother read under. "You don't take a lamp to the dump." On the way out, his father touched his head and said, "Be good for your mom." Jonathon watched the dent until the truck turned left at the stop sign. His mother drove him to the game twenty minutes late, wearing sunglasses on a cloudy day. He pitched four innings and doesn't remember a single pitch. She never bought another lamp. She read at the kitchen table for twenty years.

## What belonged in the chair

> "Fathers, do not exasperate your children; instead, bring them up in the training and instruction of the Lord."
> Ephesians 6:4 (NIV)

"Nobody taught me how to lose," Jonathon says. A pitching coach's "Never let them see you hurt" filled the chair because nothing else was in it. "Be good for your mom" is not a sentence you can build a man on, he says. "It's a sentence you can build a kid who's very, very good."

Then the verse turned on Wes. Three Saturdays before his sixteen-year-old finished working off the truck, Wes added the insurance increase to the deal. "Nothing's ever done with you," his son said. "You keep moving it." That night Wes wrote "TRUCK. APRIL 24. PAID IN FULL. NO MORE CONDITIONS.," signed it, and left it on the boy's pillow. On Sunday morning it was on the fridge, with his son's signature under Wes's.

## A story that doesn't end

A divorced man at Wes's Thursday table texts his seventeen-year-old every Sunday night, "Love you. Dad." He hasn't had a reply in eight months. "I don't know if he reads them. I know he's getting them."
