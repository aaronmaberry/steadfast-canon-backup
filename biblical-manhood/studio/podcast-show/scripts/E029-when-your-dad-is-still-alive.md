## Reading narrative

# When Your Dad Is Still Alive
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 29 · April 13, 2027

Jonathon's father calls on his birthday and on Christmas afternoon. "Hey, bud." They talk about the weather in Houston and the weather in Lake Charles. "How's work?" "Good." "How are the little ones?" "Good." Then comes a small silence, and then: "Well. I'll let you go." Fifty-some calls in twenty-eight years, about five minutes each. Jonathon used to time them as evidence. This month he noticed something he couldn't un-notice. He had written his VP four careful emails in two weeks, and he had never once called his father. Every one of those calls, his dad dialed.

## Weight

> "Honor your father and your mother, so that you may live long in the land the LORD your God is giving you."
> Exodus 20:12 (NIV)

The word for honor carries the idea of weight. Wes put it this way: "It doesn't say give him a halo. It says give him weight." Some men let a father weigh everything, so one remark at Thanksgiving still knocks the week over. Others make him weigh nothing: twice a year, the weather, whatever. "A man who weighs nothing can't hurt you." Honor is the right weight, and it doesn't require a single lie.

## Eleven seconds

On Sunday, after the pot roast, Wes and his brother asked their seventy-eight-year-old mother what she wanted, and then they both stopped talking. It took eleven seconds. She wanted neither son's plan. She wanted the senior apartments behind the church, dominoes on Tuesdays, and her own door. She had wanted it for two years and said nothing "because he bought a chair." His brother's answer: "Well. I'm keeping the recliner."

## Going first

Jonathon will call his father from his truck in the driveway on Wednesday night and ask him one question: "What was your dad like?" He won't bring up the lamp or make a speech. When his father says "I'll let you go," Jonathon plans to answer, "Not yet."

## This week

If your father is alive and safe, call him first and ask him one thing about his life before you. If he's dangerous, don't call. Honor isn't access.
