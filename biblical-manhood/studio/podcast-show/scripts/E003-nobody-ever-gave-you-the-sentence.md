## Reading narrative

# Nobody Ever Gave You the Sentence
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 3 · January 12, 2027

Most men can't say what a man is in one sentence. That isn't a reason to be ashamed. It's a reason to look.

Jonathon found out in the bathtub. Hours after he told his scraped-knee four-year-old to "be a man about it," Theo asked, "Daddy, what's a man?" Jonathon said, "A big boy." Theo pointed out that his dad wasn't big. He was medium. Later, sitting on the stairs, Jonathon admitted the best answer he had was "a man works hard and loves his family," which, he notes, also describes a golden retriever.

## The sentence you've been living

Wes tells on himself. About a year into meeting with his mentor, Mr. Abernathy slid a diner receipt across the table and asked him to write what he actually believed a man is. Wes wrote, "Pay the bills and don't make trouble." He'd lived that sentence for fifteen years, mostly picked up from a good, quiet father who never said it out loud. "I paid every bill. I never made any trouble. I also never made anything else." When Mr. Abernathy died, his daughter found thirty-some receipts in his Bible. Every one was a man's bad sentence, and he'd kept them all.

## Loud everywhere but your table

Jonathon pushes back on the claim that the world went quiet about manhood. His feed is screaming with answers. Wes takes the correction and sharpens it. It's loud everywhere you don't live and quiet at your own table. "A sentence you get from a feed is a costume. A sentence you get at a table is a fit."

## Made on purpose

> "So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them."
> Genesis 1:27 (NIV)

Before sin and before any opinions, the first thing Scripture says about people is that they bear God's image, male and female, on purpose. That's why a man's hunger for a sentence isn't insecurity. It's "a design looking for its instructions." And it's no ranking either. Both bear the image in the same breath.

## Everybody has one

Can't a man just be a good person? Wes's answer is that everyone has a sentence, and most men have never read theirs. If you don't choose, the loudest voice in your life chooses for you. Your kids are already writing theirs by watching you. Wes's sixteen-year-old put it plainly when Wes confessed his old sentence: "That's kind of what I thought you thought."

## This week's challenge

Write down the sentence you've actually been living, even a bad one, and read it to one man. Married men can ask their wife what she thinks their sentence is. Single men can ask a longtime friend.

## Carry this

> Everybody's got a sentence. Read yours before your kids finish writing it for you.
> You're not crazy for wanting one. You were made by somebody. It makes sense to ask what for.
