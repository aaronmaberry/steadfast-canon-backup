## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: Valentine's, Ash Wednesday, and Going First
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 12 · February 12, 2027

The challenge was to go first in one small, true thing. On Friday, both men report back, and both find out that the people they'd been carrying already had plans of their own.

## Check the box

In the doctor's office, Wes's brother drew a breath to start, as he always does. Wes went first: "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me." His seventy-eight-year-old mother sat up and laid out her plan. The doctor called it a very good plan and added a grab bar and daytime-only driving. His brother was short in the room ("We talked about this"). In the parking lot he said "Well," and then, after a long pause, "I never asked her either." Then their mother rolled down her window and announced she would cook Sunday lunch for the whole family once a month, starting in March. She recited the menu. "That's not a woman who needs handling," Jonathon says. "That's a woman who needed somebody to ask."

For Valentine's, Wes took his wife to breakfast at the diner where Mr. Abernathy met him every other Thursday for six years. In twenty years she had never been. "The coffee's terrible," she said, and then, "I get it now."

Jonathon called his mother about hosting Easter at his house, braced to hurt her. "Well, I'll bring the ham," she said. His sister had other plans anyway. "Everybody's mom has a plan. We are not that important." He left his night-shift wife coffee and a note, and got the note back with a correction: "Decaf next time. I'm trying to sleep." He also took both kids to Ash Wednesday alone, his first time taking the family to church without her. His four-year-old informed him he had dirt on his face. Jonathon's twenty-two-year-old nephew invited two friends for chili, and seven came.

## From the table: do I lead if my wife is more mature in faith?

Yes, Wes says, but going first isn't the same as being the most mature person in the room. It means being willing to move first.

> "As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another."
> Proverbs 27:17 (NIV)

Two pieces of iron, and both of them move. The less experienced man doesn't wait to catch up before he's allowed to start. He learns out loud ("How'd you find Habakkuk?"), and he starts things: "let's pray" at supper, the kids to church when she's at work. "What she's been waiting for isn't a better prayer. It's for somebody else to start one once in a while." When Jonathon asks whether that's why Wes and his wife still don't pray together, Wes doesn't pretend. "Maybe. I don't know. I'm not going to make up an answer."

## Tool on the table: the first apology

Go first even when you're less wrong; if it's seventy-thirty, apologize first for your thirty. Name it in their words, not yours. ("I'm sorry you felt..." isn't an apology. "That's a weather report.") Then stop, and let the silence be theirs.

## Carry this

> Ask her what she wants before you decide what she needs.
> You don't have to be the most mature man in the room to be the first one to move.
