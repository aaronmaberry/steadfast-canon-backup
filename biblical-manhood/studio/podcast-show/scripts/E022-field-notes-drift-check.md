## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: Drift Check
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 22 · March 19, 2027

This week Jonathon checked Wes first. Wes managed his twenty minutes on the porch four nights out of seven. On Tuesday, "one" tractor video turned out to be three. On Wednesday his wife was at her sister's. On Friday, he said, he forgot. Jonathon leaned in: "Did you forget? Or did you drift?" After a long pause Wes said, "I drifted. I told myself I forgot."

On Sunday Wes's mother held her first monthly pot roast lunch: eleven people at a table for eight, the leaf in for the first time since her husband died. When everyone looked at Wes to pray, he said, "Mom, it's your table." She prayed for all eleven by name, including her grandson's "truck situation," and then said, "Well, eat. It's getting cold." In seventy-eight years he had never heard her pray aloud that long. "I always took it."

Jonathon's twenty minutes turned into bath time, the job he used to pay a sitter to cover. Theo asked, "Why are you at bath?" and then, "Can you be the shark?" Jonathon's nephew slipped on day six and told him forty minutes later. "That was faster than last time." He meant the telling.

## From the table: how do I set up my phone so it doesn't own me?

Both men read their weekly reports on air: 3 hours 41 minutes a day for Wes, and 5 hours 12 for Jonathon (1 hour 50 of it work).

> "Teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom."
> Psalm 90:12 (NIV)

Wes's average comes to about fifty-six days a year. "The phone already numbers them," Jonathon says. "It's just that nobody reads the report."

## Tool on the table: four settings

Turn on grayscale. With it on, Wes's radar became gray blobs on gray, which made it useless. Charge the phone outside the bedroom and buy a cheap alarm clock. Send your weekly number to your Thursday-text man. Move the app that eats you three swipes from the home screen.
