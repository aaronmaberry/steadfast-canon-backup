## 2. What the show is

**Promise.** Two men at a table going deeper into what the Steadfast Men book and the 24 training sessions cover. They tell their own stories, disagree honestly, and say what they actually did that week. Each week there's one challenge, and on Friday they report back honestly, including when they failed.

**Who it's for.** Men in every season: the 22-year-old, the new dad, the man twenty years into his marriage, the single man, the divorced dad, the man over 60. It's especially for the man who has never sat at a men's table and isn't sure he'd fit.

**What it isn't.** It isn't a sermon with two microphones, a study guide read out loud, an "alpha" podcast, a news show, or a sales channel. It also never replays the training sessions. It sends men to them.

**Why it can compete.**
- The two hosts have a real dynamic: an older man and a younger man who started late, and they don't agree on everything.
- There's a weekly challenge with accountability on air, which the top shows don't offer in this lane.
- Every episode is tight, 25-35 minutes, because the edit cuts the dead air.
- The look is TV-grade on a TV screen, which is where YouTube podcasts are now watched (research dossier, section 1).

## 4. Formats

### 4a. Main episode (Tuesday, 25-35 minutes, about 3,600-5,000 spoken words)

| Clock | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:06 | **The slate** | The episode number, release date and title, spoken by both men (section 5). |
| 0:06-0:40 | **Cold open** | The best exchange of the episode, played as a 20-30 second clip, or a fresh one-liner that pays off the title. Then a 3-second title sting (logo, two notes, no voice). |
| 0:40-2:30 | **Pull up a chair** | The welcome line, under 45 seconds of banter, then the question of the day said out loud. The focus mark is named plainly if the episode has one (canon). |
| 2:30-9:00 | **First story** | One host's real story on the topic. The other asks, "Then what?" The verse comes in here as a card. |
| about 9:00-12:00 | **The Word** | The passage, read once and plainly, with the context in two or three sentences. Then what it means at a kitchen table. |
| after the first payoff, about 40% in | **Stop and subscribe** | One line, varied (section 8b). |
| 12:00-20:00 | **Pushback** | Jonathon raises the objection the viewer is thinking ("That works if..."). They work it through. This segment is the main source of Shorts. |
| 20:00-26:00 | **What I actually did** | Each man says something concrete he did, or didn't do. No "you should." Only "I did." |
| 26:00-29:00 | **This week's challenge** | One specific challenge, with a single-man version and a married-man version when it's a household move. It gets checked on Friday. |
| 29:00-30:30 | **Carry This** | One line each, said to the viewer. |
| last 25-35 seconds | **Standing outro** | Section 9. The end screen sits over the last 20 seconds. |

The timings are for planning. The story decides the real clock. Plugs from the rotation (section 8) go in where they fit naturally: never in the first five minutes, and never inside the Word.

## 5. Opening ritual

**The slate** (Aaron's rule: every episode opens with the hosts saying the episode number, date and title). It's modeled on Jocko's spoken slate. It takes six seconds, and nobody says "hello" yet.

Pattern (the lines rotate between the two men):
- WES: "Walk Steadfast, episode seven."
- JONATHON: "Tuesday, January twenty-sixth, twenty twenty-seven."
- WES: "Stop Waiting." (the title)

Variants:
- JONATHON: "Episode twelve. Friday, February twelfth." WES: "Field Notes. 'The Valentine's Report.'"
- WES: "This is episode thirty. It's Tuesday, April thirteenth." JONATHON: "And today's called 'Honor Without Being Run.'"

Dates are spoken in full the first time and naturally after that. The date is the release date, not the recording date.

**Cold open.** This is a clip from later in the episode (the DOAC/BibleProject model) or one fresh line that pays off the title. It's about 20-30 seconds and always the strongest exchange. It's marked in the script as `[COLD OPEN: from 14:10-14:35]` or written fresh.

**Title sting.** Three seconds: the navy card with the gold wordmark and a two-note acoustic figure. No voiceover.

**Welcome line** (varied every episode, never "Welcome back to the show"):
- WES: "Pull up a chair."
- JONATHON: "Coffee's hot. Mine is, anyway."
- WES: "Glad you're here. Wherever here is. Truck, treadmill, kitchen."

## 7. Delivery and script conventions

**Speakers.** Lines are written as `WES:` and `JONATHON:`. Every spoken line belongs to one man.

**Pauses are edit marks and are never spoken.** These are the same conventions as the training scripts and the YouTube guide.
- `[beat]` is about 0.6 s. `[pause 1s]` and `[pause 2s]` are for the heaviest lines only, at most three per episode.
- Normal gaps between lines and speakers come from the edit, like `retime.py` does for the sessions: about 0.15-0.35 s on a quick exchange, 0.5-0.8 s at a sentence end, 1.0-1.3 s at a turn in the topic.
- Mid-line ellipses (`...`) give an in-take hesitation, at most 4 per script. Never in scripture.
- No "uh", "um", "eh" or stammer spellings. A man correcting himself does it in words ("Well, not mad. Embarrassed.").
- No whisper or quiet tags and no `[laughs]` audio tag. Laughter is a picture direction such as `(both laugh)`. Its audio is handled in the edit: a short real-laugh take or room tone under the picture. That gets tested before any episode is locked.

**Balance and audience rules (focus groups, rounds 1-2).**
- Wes carries no more than about 60% of the spoken words, averaged across a season. A single episode can run to about 67% when it's built on Wes's story (E007), and the next one should swing back. Jonathon reads a verse card in about one episode in three.
- Every silent visual beat (writing, a prop reveal, a long look) gets one spoken cue, so audio-only listeners don't hit dead air.
- Single-man and married-man challenge versions are equally concrete. When a challenge depends on a wife or kids, add a line for the man who lives alone and for the divorced dad.
- Jokes framing a wife's honest answer as a threat ("Brace.") appear at most once a season.
- Don't promise "we read every comment" unless someone actually does. Until Aaron assigns someone, no outro promises reading (E001 line removed, round 3).

**Reader panel round 3 rules (2026-10-02, real panel: Drew, Marcus, Caleb, James, Earl).**

- **Not every story lands on a polished line.** At most one coined line per story. At least once a month, Wes tells something with no lesson ("I don't know what that was about. I'm just telling you.").

**Interruptions.** Mark them `[cuts in]` at the start of the interrupting line. The line before it ends on an incomplete thought, with no dash. The editor overlaps the two lines by about 0.2-0.4 s.

**Stage and action directions** are in parentheses and italic in spirit, never spoken:
- `(sips coffee)`, `(leans in)`, `(turns his mug a quarter turn)`, `(hands flat on the table)`, `(points at Wes)`, `(leans back, arms crossed)`.
- Use about one direction every 30-60 seconds of dialogue. Each host drinks two or three times per main episode. Gestures come with the thought, not as decoration.

**Picture and edit cues** go in square brackets on their own line:
- `[CAM: WIDE]`, `[CAM: 2S]` (two-shot), `[CAM: WES MS]`, `[CAM: JON MS]`, `[CAM: WES CU]`, `[CAM: JON CU]`, `[CAM: OTS-WES]` (over Jonathon's shoulder onto Wes), `[CAM: INSERT mug]`, `[PUSH-IN]`.
- `[B: description]` for B-roll inside a story. Never as the first shot of an episode.
- `[CARD: reference]` for a verse card: exact NIV text, reference, "NIV". It's on screen while the verse is read. No music under it.
- `[TEXT: words]` for on-screen text, 3-8 words.
- `[LOWER THIRD: Wes Hart]` the first time each man speaks in each episode.
- `[CHAPTER: title]` marks each chapter boundary. These mirror the description chapters.
- `[SUB-MID]` marks the mid subscribe line, with a lower-third.
- `[PLUG: BOOK]`, `[PLUG: TRAINING]` and `[PLUG: SITE]` mark the plug slots, for the plug-rotation log.
- `[SHORT 3 IN]` / `[SHORT 3 OUT]` mark the Shorts cut points.
- `[BREATHER 3s: shot]` is a held shot with room tone only, after a heavy line.
- `[OUTRO]` and `[END SCREEN 20s]` mark the outro and end screen.

**Pipeline conversion.** For audio, strip every bracket and parenthesis. Each speaker line becomes `SPEAKER|line|pause` (the pause value comes from the marks above or the defaults). Takes split at `[CHAPTER]` lines, each under about 2,000 characters per speaker run. ## 8. Plug rules

### 8a. Mid-episode plugs on a rotation (Aaron, 9:15 PM: every now and then, spaced naturally)

- **Book** (*Steadfast Men: The way of Godly Men*): on mic in about one main episode in three, when the topic matches a chapter. Say what's in the chapter in one or two sentences, under 20 seconds. Never twice in one episode.
- **Training** (the 24 sessions on walksteadfast.com): on mic in about one main episode in four, at a different point from the book plug, when the episode's topic matches a session. Say what it is (24 sessions, video, built for men alone or with a group), under 20 seconds.
- **Site and socials:** carried by the standing outro every episode. Mid-episode only when there's a reason ("the challenge card is on walksteadfast.com").
- **Never** a book plug and a training plug in the same episode. The exceptions are E001 and E103 (year review). A season premiere carries one or the other, alternating. The schedule is fixed in CALENDAR-104.md: 18 book slots and 11 training slots across 52 main episodes.
- **Field Notes** carry no mid plugs, only the outro.
- **Never on mic:** prices, "limited time," "smash that like," "link in bio," "use code," a hashtag wall, or "follow us" stacked three ways.
- **Before the book is out:** the plugs say "coming" and never invent a date. The calendar marks the planned slots `B` and `T`. Aaron sets the launch dates, and the wording updates in one pass.
- **How it sounds.** A plug sounds like a friend mentioning something useful. "Chapter nine of the book is basically this whole conversation. If you want it on paper, it's at walksteadfast.com." It's never a pivot into a pitch.

### 8b. Stop and subscribe (mid-episode, main episodes only)

- Once, after the first real payoff, around 40% of the way through. It's never in the first five minutes.
- One or two lines, said by whichever host just landed the payoff, then straight back in.
- The wording varies every episode. Bank (rotate, rewrite, no repeats within 6 episodes):
  1. JONATHON: "Quick thing. If this is helping, hit subscribe. We do this twice a week." WES: "He means it. He checks."
  2. WES: "If you're new, subscribe. Tuesday's the long one, Friday's the honest one."
  3. JONATHON: "If you just thought 'that's me,' that's what the subscribe button's for."
  4. WES: "Subscribe if you want to keep going. We'll be here Friday."
  5. JONATHON: "If a buddy sent you this, subscribe so he doesn't have to keep sending them."
  6. WES: "We're two guys at a table, twice a week. If you want a seat, subscribe."
  7. JONATHON: "Stop for one second. Subscribe. Okay. Back to Wes's sad truck story."
  8. WES: "If you're watching on a TV, the button's down there somewhere. Subscribe. I can never find it either."
- The bell gets mentioned once a month at most.

## 9. Standing outro (every episode, last 25-35 seconds)

Aaron (9:17 PM): every episode ends with where to find us on the socials, the website, and the links in the description or pinned comment. Research basis: the dossier, section 7. Keep it short, payoff first, and make the spoken ask match the end screen. Ten Minute Bible Talks shows how saying it the same way every time turns it into a sign-off rather than an ask.

**Skeleton (four beats, about 30 seconds):**
1. **Next.** Name the next episode in one line. The end screen shows it.
2. **Subscribe and follow.** "Subscribe here on YouTube, or follow wherever you get podcasts."
3. **Where to find us.** "walksteadfast.com. We're @steadfastwalk on Instagram, X, TikTok and Facebook."
4. **Links.** "Everything we mentioned is in the description, and the pinned comment."

Then the sign-off ritual. WES: "We'll see you at the table." JONATHON: "Go walk it out." (Or swap who says which.)

Audio-only feed version: "description" becomes "show notes," "pinned comment" is dropped, and "subscribe" becomes "follow."
