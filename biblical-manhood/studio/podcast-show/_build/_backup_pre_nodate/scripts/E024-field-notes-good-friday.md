## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: Good Friday
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 24 · March 26, 2027

Every Good Friday, Wes's father turned the radio off at noon and back on at three, in the truck, the shop or the kitchen, without a word. Wes was about twelve when his mother explained: "Noon to three is when the sky went dark. Your daddy keeps it quiet." Wes dropped the practice during his fog years. On Wednesday his oldest son, now twenty-one, called to ask whether he'd remembered it right. He was doing it on his job site this year, and his foreman had told him, "Fine, but you're telling the crew." Wes is doing it too.

## From the table: what do I do with Good Friday as a dad?

> "When he had received the drink, Jesus said, 'It is finished.' With that, he bowed his head and gave up his spirit."
> John 19:30 (NIV)

"Not 'I am finished,'" Jonathon says. "He's saying the job's done." A father doesn't need to make the day big, because the work is already finished. He needs to sit in it with his kids for a while. "Don't rush to the eggs."

## Tool on the table: read it out loud

On Wednesday, Jonathon's family rehearsed John 18-19 at supper. Ruby made sticky tabs (NARATOR, one R). Theo lasted four minutes, then sat under the table holding his father's ankle. Ruby read the crowd's lines, and after one of them she stopped and asked, "Why do I have to be the mean people?" Three weeks earlier, Jonathon had handed Ruby's question about his father to Dani. This time he answered himself: "Because all of us are, a little. That's why he did it. He knew all of it, and he came anyway." "Even Mommy?" Ruby asked. "Especially Mommy," said Dani.

## The check and what stuck

At 4:10 on Wednesday afternoon, Wes's daughter called him during a meeting. He walked out and answered. She wanted Grandma's roll recipe. The father of the girl who struck out wasn't early to practice. He was ten minutes late, and he walked straight to his daughter and played catch with her for an hour. Jonathon is still deciding about his father, by Sunday night.

For the season review, each man named what he'd seen change in the other. Jonathon said Wes asks now, where in January he told. Wes said Jonathon has stopped landing every story with a joke. Each also named one thing that hasn't stuck: Jonathon's coach voice at home, and Wes praying out loud with his wife.

## Production notes

- **Order varied on purpose** (polish pass): From the table, Tool, Check the box, What stuck. Good Friday leads with the question. Chapter labels follow the new order.
- **Jonathon carries the episode.**
  - He reads the question and the only card, and he gives the "It is finished" reading.
  - He tells the rehearsal and teaches the tool.
  - He runs the season review and names Wes's change first.
  - Ruby's question pays off E023's "Ask your mother" without anyone saying so: he answers this time, with Dani right there.
- **Single man goes first.** Wes's oldest son reaches him by phone call, not a lowercase text (polish rule). His foreman's line is the only deadpan-reply payoff in the episode. "Especially Mommy" is Dani's warmth, not a zinger.
- **No resolution.**
  - The friend request: "Don't ask me again till Tuesday."
  - Ruby's "Do you have a dad?" is still owed.
  - Wes hasn't told his daughter about the five hours.
  - The out-loud prayer with his wife: "Still no," held until about E083.
  - The E013 hidden thing: "Off mic." Never reveal it.
- **Theology.** "It is finished" means the work is complete, not that Jesus was defeated. "He bows" follows the text ("bowed his head"). Ruby's "mean people" answer is plain substitution in a seven-year-old's words. Nothing is said about Sunday until the outro, by design.
- **Closing sound.** A real radio click off-camera, then 3 seconds of silence before the cut. No music under the end screen this week, if the platform allows it. Otherwise use the quietest bed.
- **Wes's dad canon.** "I'm proud of you" at 43, from a hospital bed, matches the host brief. Wes's mother's line ("Your daddy keeps it quiet") is new.
