[CHAPTER: Pull up a chair]
[CAM: WIDE]
WES: Pull up a chair. This week is feelings. And I can hear about half of you reaching for the dial. Stay with us, because I don't think this is the episode you think it is.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Here's the whole thing in one picture. You've got a dash in your truck. Speed, fuel, temperature, the lights. That dash tells you the truth about what's going on under the hood. A man who never looks at it is going to cook an engine on the side of the highway someday. But a man who stares at the dash and lets it steer is going in the ditch. You read the dash. You drive with the wheel. Feelings are the dash. They're telling you something true about you. They're not supposed to be driving.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: And men miss it both ways. Some of us put a piece of cardboard over the dash and say, I don't do feelings. And the feelings drive anyway, from the back seat, where you can't see them. And some of us hand the feeling the keys. I felt it, so I did it. I was mad, so I said it. I felt done, so I left.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: And I've been both men. There were years I had the cardboard up so tight my own wife had to tell me what I was feeling. And there was a fall when a feeling had both hands on the wheel and I leaned on the horn with my daughter in the back seat. So I'm not up here as the man who figured this out. I'm the man who's been in both ditches and has the scratches on the truck.
[CAM: 2S]
WES: The mark this week is Stop Waiting. Because a lot of men are sitting in the driveway waiting to feel like it. Waiting to feel like loving her again, or feel like going to church, or feel like making the call. And the feeling's not coming first. It almost never does. You do the true thing, and sometimes, later, the feeling catches up. Sometimes it doesn't, and you still did the true thing.

It took me another ten years and a region of my own to learn that one on purpose.

## Reading narrative

# Feelings Are the Dash, Not the Wheel
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 73 · September 14, 2027

On a Friday night Wes's sixteen-year-old came in the back door with a red face and went for the truck keys. "Just driving," he said. His girlfriend had broken up with him by text. Wes said, "You can be as mad as you want. You can be mad all night. You don't drive mad. I'll drive. You ride." They took the farm road the long way with the windows down. Twenty minutes in, his son said, "I really liked her."

A feeling is a true reading of what's going on inside you, like the lights on a dash. Cover it up and it drives from the back seat. Hand it the keys and it drives you into the ditch. Read it, name it, and keep your hands on the wheel.

> "Why, my soul, are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me? Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise him, my Savior and my God."
> Psalm 42:5 (NIV)

The psalm doesn't pretend. It asks the soul why. Then it tells the soul where to put its hope. "I will yet praise him" doesn't wait for the feeling to arrive first.
