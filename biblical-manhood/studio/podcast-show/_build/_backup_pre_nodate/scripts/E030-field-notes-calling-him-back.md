## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: Calling Him Back
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 30 · April 16, 2027

Jonathon sat in his truck in the driveway with his thumb on "Dad" for nine minutes. Dani had turned the porch light on. When his father picked up, the first thing he said was, "Who died?" After twenty-eight years, it was a fair question.

Jonathon asked him, "What was your dad like?" There was a long silence, and then his father gave him a name. Jonathon wrote it on the back of a receipt and is saving it for his daughter. His grandfather worked offshore, two weeks on and two off. When he was home, he fished. "I don't remember him saying ten words to me that weren't about fish." Jonathon's father took his fishing rods when he left, and he never took his son fishing.

There was no apology. When the familiar "Well. I'll let you go" came, Jonathon said, "Not yet." He told his father that Ruby had asked about him. "She looks like your mama," his father said. At the end: "Call me sometime, bud." "I just did." His father laughed. Jonathon went inside angry. "If he could do twelve minutes, he could've done twenty-eight years."

## From the table: do I have to forgive a dad who isn't sorry?

> "If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone."
> Romans 12:18 (NIV)

Forgiveness depends on you: you release the debt and hand it to God to collect. Peace takes two, and usually it takes the other man saying something true. You can leave the door unlocked. You can't drag him through it. For most men, Wes says, forgiveness starts as a decision and only later becomes a feeling, if it ever does.

## Tool on the table: the two-call rule

Make the first call to God. Name the actual debt and hand it over. Make the second call to your father, only if he's safe, and don't ask it to do the first call's job. If he's dangerous or gone, the first call is enough.
