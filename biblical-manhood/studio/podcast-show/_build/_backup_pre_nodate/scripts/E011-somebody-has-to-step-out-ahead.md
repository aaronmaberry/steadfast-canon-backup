## Reading narrative

# Somebody Has to Step Out Ahead
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 11 · February 9, 2027

In every house there's a conversation everybody's waiting for somebody else to start. This week Wes Hart and Jonathon take up the third mark: go first.

## Sunday night

Since October, Jonathon and his wife had left a fight unfinished, about his mother and the holidays. On Sunday they sat down after the kids were in bed, over two cups of decaf ("decaf means a meeting"). Jonathon knew how it would go if he waited. She would start, he would get defensive, and they'd be back in October. So he went first. "I've been making you pay for my mom."

"Keep going," she said. He did. Every holiday was built around whether his mother would be sad, and when she was sad anyway, his frustration landed on his wife. After a long silence she said, "I thought you were going to make me be the bad guy again." For years he had let her be the one who said no, and then let her take the hit for it. The hour that followed wasn't pretty, but they finished with a plan: Easter lunch at their house, his mother invited, and Jonathon will be the one to tell her.

## The earbuds

Wes yelled at his sixteen-year-old about a rolling stop, in a voice he recognized as his father's. A week later, on the forty-minute drive to the boy's Saturday work at his uncle's place, Wes tapped his son's arm. "I yelled at you about the stop sign. The yelling was wrong. The stop sign wasn't." His son said "Okay" and put the earbud back in. Twenty minutes later, as they crossed the cattle guard, the boy took both earbuds out, said "Sorry about the stop sign," and got out of the truck. Wes doesn't wrap it in a lesson. He only knows that if he'd waited for his son, they'd still be not talking about it.

## Out front

> "When he has brought out all his own, he goes on ahead of them, and his sheep follow him because they know his voice."
> John 10:4 (NIV)

Jesus was talking to leaders who had used people instead of leading them. A shepherd walked in front, and Wes knows why from walking his kids through tall Hill Country grass: "Somebody's going to find the snake, and it ought to be the guy with boots on." Going first isn't the good seat. And the sheep follow because they know the voice from a thousand ordinary days. "Going first on the big night only works if they've heard you on the little ones."

## Isn't this just bossy?

Jonathon grew up around a father who moved his family four times without asking, announcing, "I'm the head of this house." Wes's test: did that man ever go first into anything hard for himself, the apology or the conversation where he might be wrong, or only into decisions? "If your family's the one getting hurt every time you 'go first,' you weren't out front. You were just gone." The bossy man goes first into the decision. The shepherd goes first into the cost.

Is it a young man's mark? Wes answers with a man. The eighty-one-year-old retired Air Force mechanic who once held his ladder has started a monthly Saturday at church where retired men do oil changes and brakes for single mothers. "Nobody was doing it, so I figured somebody ought to start."

Going first in a cold marriage starts small. After his fog years, Wes's first move wasn't a talk. He started getting up at 5:15 to make the coffee his wife had made alone for twelve years. On the eleventh day she asked why. "I'd like to be the one who's up first," he said, and she cried into her cup. Jonathon, who leaves at 7:15 as his wife comes home from night shift, worked out his own version on the spot: leave the coffee made, with a note.

## What they actually did

Wes realized that at Thanksgiving, two grown sons had argued over whether their seventy-eight-year-old mother should live alone, while she sat at the table. Nobody had asked her. So the night before her appointment he did. "Nobody's asked me that," she said, and talked for half an hour. She wants her house, help with cleaning, no walker in the living room, and above all, to keep driving herself to church. In the doctor's office, Wes plans to go first with one line: "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me." His brother doesn't know yet.

Jonathon texted his mother to set up a call on Thursday about Easter. Her reply: "Is everything ok??"
