## Reading narrative

# Skip the House, Run the Room
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 17 · March 2, 2027

If the trouble is partly inside a man, what does it look like on the outside during an ordinary week? Wes and Jonathon name three shapes they see most often. Skip the house: the best of you goes to everyone except the people you live with. Run the room: you win every conversation and call it leading. Wear the job as your name: you answer "who are you" with a title.

## The coach voice

Jonathon coaches his daughter's coach-pitch team, the Bluebonnets (0-6 last fall). He's good with the kids. He knows all twelve names and one fact about each, and he can talk a crying seven-year-old down from a bucket in ninety seconds. By noon he's home on the couch with his phone. When his four-year-old brought his glove over, Jonathon said, "Not now, buddy," without looking up. Theo asked, "Why do you only do the coach voice at practice?" Dani covered for him: Daddy's tired. Jonathon says she was wrong. "I wasn't tired. I was done performing." He had skipped the house from four feet away.

## The title

In 2019, at forty-four, Wes lost the title he'd held for nine years in a reorganization. His pay stayed the same and his desk moved. For three weeks he told no one at home and got short with everyone. Then his seventeen-year-old daughter asked, "Are you mad at us?" "I'd lost my name, and I was taking it out on the people who never called me by it." When he finally told his wife, she said, "Honey, I didn't even know what your title was."

## The crew

Their contractor friend ran his house for thirty years the way he runs a job site, until his wife put down a dish towel and said, "I'm not on your crew." At sixty he wants to teach welding, "with somebody who's allowed to talk back."

## The Word

> "There is a way that appears to be right, but in the end it leads to death."
> Proverbs 14:12 (NIV)

All three shapes look right to the bleachers. What dies is usually slow: a kid who stops bringing his glove, a daughter who asks if you're mad. "Don't ask the bleachers. Ask the house."

## What they did

Jonathon played twenty minutes of catch with Theo in the coach voice. Theo threw the ball over the fence, and on Monday Jonathon was back on the couch. When a new man at church asked Wes what he does, Wes answered first with his kids and his mother, and gave the job second.

## Production notes

- **Round 4 applied.**
  - Jonathon's arena is home and the field, with no sales story.
  - Dani is wrong once ("Daddy's tired"), and the show lets it stand without making her look foolish. She was being kind.
  - There's one deadpan-reply payoff ("I didn't even know what your title was").
  - The Sunday catch ends badly (over the fence) and doesn't stick (Monday, the couch).
  - Single and older beats have substance: the nephew's oranges, and "what do you want to be known for at seventy."
- **Wes's job, still unnamed (for Aaron).** This episode leans on Wes's title without saying what it is, so the unnamed job is more noticeable here. If Aaron keeps it unnamed, there's an in-world reason available that this script does NOT use yet: Wes stopped leading with it after 2019. That could be one line in a later episode ("I quit introducing myself by it after the reorg. Habit now."). The decision stays with Aaron.
- **The whistle.** A real prop, one insert. The closing gag (Wes pockets it) pays off "Please don't."
- **Safety rail.** The "ask your wife" rail sits in Tuesday's challenge (calm night, say yours first, don't argue, sit with it a day), per the round 3 rule.
- **No David.** The book and W16 both use David, Uriah and Nathan. Keep them out of the edit and the Shorts.
- **Theology.** "There's another way, and he called himself that" alludes to John 14:6 and isn't carded, which keeps it to one verse per episode.
- **Setups.**
  - E018: the gutter result. The From the Table question ("My wife says I run the room").
  - The rep's reply (any time).
  - The Bluebonnets' first game (a running thread). Win one by May.
