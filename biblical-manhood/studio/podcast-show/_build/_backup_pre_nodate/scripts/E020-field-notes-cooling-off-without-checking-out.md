# Field Notes: Cooling Off Without Checking Out
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 20 · March 12, 2027

The first car-care Saturday drew eleven cars and nine single moms. The eighty-one-year-old retired Air Force mechanic had his tools laid out on a towel from smallest to largest and did fourteen oil changes. All morning a quiet twelve-year-old handed him the wrenches, and at the end the mechanic gave him a socket set and told him, "Next month. First Saturday." Wes was on coffee, real coffee, and he'll defend that.

Tuesday's challenge was to ask what your anger is guarding. Jonathon kept three lines in his phone's notes app. The third came after Dani mentioned the electric bill: "I feel dumb about money." He told her so. "You're not dumb about money," she said. "You're dumb about the spreadsheet." It has eleven colors, and she is cutting it to four. Wes snapped at his physical therapist. What that was guarding: his father had both knees replaced and was never the same, and Wes is afraid of ending up like him. He told his wife. "Me too, a little," she said, and held his hand. He called his little brother on his birthday for the first time in twenty years. "Is Mom okay?" his brother asked. They talked for forty minutes. Wes's twenty-one-year-old son texted that his foreman yelled at him, and that the yelling was guarding "scared i'm not good at this."

## From the table: is it ever right to be angry?

> "He looked around at them in anger and, deeply distressed at their stubborn hearts, said to the man, 'Stretch out your hand.' He stretched it out, and his hand was completely restored."
> Mark 3:5 (NIV)

Jesus was angry, and his anger carried grief and healed a man's hand. Wes offers three questions: Who got wronged? Is there any grief in it? What does it make you do? When a mom arrived on four bald tires her ex had left her, Wes's anger passed all three. By Wednesday she had four used tires with good tread.

## Tool on the table: the twenty-minute rule

Say when you're coming back ("I'll be back at 8:15"), because leaving without a time is checking out. Move your body instead of picking up your phone. Don't rehearse comebacks; ask what the anger is guarding. Come back on time and start with the line you wrote.
