## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: What Stuck
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 14 · February 19, 2027

Tuesday's challenge was to do one thing that only God sees and then keep quiet about it. Both men did it, and neither will say what it was. When Wes got home, his wife asked where he'd been. "Out," he said. "Are you buying me a boat?" "No." "Then I don't care." Jonathon, a salesman who is used to good work counting only when someone hears about it, got as far as "So, today" before Dani stopped him: "Don't. You'll ruin it." The urge to tell faded about ten minutes later. "It got put somewhere," he says. On Thursday morning he and Dani signed their wills. She signed faster, and afterward said, "I want tacos." Jonathon's twenty-two-year-old nephew texted, "did it. not telling," and then, "is that right." It was.

## From the table: what do we keep from six weeks?

Nobody keeps all of it. Jonathon's list runs to nine challenges. What stuck: supper with the phone off the table, enforced by his seven-year-old with a basket labeled PHONE JAIL. What didn't: ten minutes in the morning, nine times in three weeks. For Wes, asking before deciding stuck, along with Tuesday therapy alongside his mother. The back-porch gutter did not. It has been loose for fifteen months, because he doesn't want to climb a ladder on bad knees and doesn't want to say so.

> "Brothers and sisters, I do not consider myself yet to have taken hold of it. But one thing I do: Forgetting what is behind and straining toward what is ahead, I press on toward the goal to win the prize for which God has called me heavenward in Christ Jesus."
> Philippians 3:13-14 (NIV)

Even Paul says he isn't there yet, and he leaves both his wins and his misses behind him. Wes gives the whole season in one sentence: a steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's. This week the practice is smaller than that. Keep what stuck, pick one thing that didn't, and give it a date.

## Tool on the table: the Thursday text

One man, every Thursday, four words: "Did you do it?" Keep the same man and the same day. A one-word answer is fine. If he says no, text back "Next week," and don't fix him. "A verse on Thursday afternoon is a lecture in a nice outfit."
