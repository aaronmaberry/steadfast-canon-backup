## Reading narrative

# What Your Anger Is Guarding
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 19 · March 9, 2027

Anger almost always stands in front of something, the way a dog stands at a gate. This week Wes and Jonathon each tell the story of a moment of anger and work out what it was guarding. The focus mark is Pick Up the Weight: your anger is yours to carry, even when the other man started it.

## The steering wheel

It was fall 2009, and layoff rumors were going around Wes's office. He wasn't sleeping, and he hadn't told anyone. On a Saturday errand, with his seven-year-old daughter in her booster seat, a driver changed lanes without signaling. Wes hit the steering wheel so hard the horn went off, and he kept holding it down. Afterward he told her, "That man's a bad driver." She didn't say anything to him. At home she told her mother, "Daddy honked for a long time."

## "I've got it"

At Saturday's coach-pitch practice, Jonathon was throwing batting practice on the shoulder he hurt in college. A kind dad offered to take over. "I've got it," Jonathon snapped, in front of everyone, and kept throwing through the pain. Four hours later he went cold on his daughter over a spilled juice box. The anger was guarding his old pitching coach's rule: never let them see you hurt.

## Mr. Abernathy's question

Years after the steering wheel, Wes told his mentor about it. Mr. Abernathy ate his eggs, set down his fork, tapped the table twice, and asked, "What's it guarding?" Behind the anger was Wes's father's sentence, "Pay the bills and don't make trouble," and the fear that he was about to fail at the one thing he was for.

## The Word

> "'In your anger do not sin': Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry, and do not give the devil a foothold."
> Ephesians 4:26-27 (NIV)

The verse assumes a man will get angry. It forbids what anger makes him do. A foothold is a ledge about as big as a thumb, just enough for a climber to reach the next one. Don't go to sleep still holding the horn down.

## The parking lot

A divorced man at Wes's table was angry at every custody exchange for five years. Underneath was his fear that his kids liked the other house better. Then he started getting out of the truck and spending five minutes talking with the stepdad about the Astros. "We're not friends. We're Astros people." His daughter says it's her favorite part of the weekend.

## What they did

Jonathon apologized to the other dad and told him the truth about his shoulder. He also told Dani it hurts, though he hasn't seen a doctor yet. Wes called his daughter, who is now twenty-five. She remembered the steering wheel right away. "I thought it was because I was kicking your seat."

If your anger has ever turned physical, or the people in your home are afraid of you, please get help this week.
