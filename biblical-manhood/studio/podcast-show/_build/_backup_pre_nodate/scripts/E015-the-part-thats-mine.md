## Reading narrative

# The Part That's Mine
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 15 · February 23, 2027

For six weeks the show has talked about what gets handed to a man: his father, the sentence written on him, the men who were there and the ones who weren't. This week, at the start of Lent, Wes and Jonathon add the part that makes the rest honest. Some of the weight with your name on it is weight you picked up yourself.

## The district deal

In 2019 Jonathon was a senior rep in commercial HVAC. A twenty-three-year-old rookie spent six months working a school district that was replacing the rooftop units on forty-one schools. He learned every facilities manager's name and sat through board meetings at night in a tie that didn't fit. Jonathon's manager sent him to the last meeting to make sure the deal closed. On the drive back, Jonathon entered it into the system and typed his own name in the "lead rep" field. That year he made President's Club, and he and Dani went to Cancun. The rookie never said a word. Today he's thirty-one and works for Jonathon.

For years Jonathon had an explanation ready. His father left when he was nine, and a boy whose father leaves grows up needing to win. Then he thought about his sister. Same father, same house, same day he left, and she would drive back to return a nickel. "The dad thing explains why winning matters so much to me. It doesn't explain why I was willing to take it from a twenty-three-year-old. That part's just me."

## The headache

In December 2010, during what Wes calls the fog years, his five-year-old son was a shepherd in the kindergarten Christmas program. Wes told his wife he had a headache and stayed home to watch a Monday night game. For years he blamed the fog on his father, who never came to anything. "But my dad never told anybody he had a headache. I came up with that myself." When he finally told his wife, she said she had known all along. She could hear the game from the driveway.

## The Word

> "For I know that good itself does not dwell in me, that is, in my sinful nature. For I have the desire to do what is good, but I cannot carry it out."
> Romans 7:18 (NIV)

Paul had about the cleanest religious upbringing a man could have, and he still located the problem inside himself. Wanting to do good was never the trouble. A few verses later he asks who will rescue him, and he answers: Jesus Christ our Lord. "You can't be forgiven for something your father did. You can only be forgiven for what you did."

## What they did

Jonathon took the rep to lunch and said it before the chips arrived: "The district deal in 2019 was yours. I took it. I'm sorry." The rep said, "I know," and then, "You owe me Cancun." On Thursday, Jonathon plans to tell his regional VP. Wes called his son, now twenty-one, and told him about the headache. The son didn't remember the program. He asked, "Was I a good shepherd?" Wes said, "I don't know, son. I wasn't there."
