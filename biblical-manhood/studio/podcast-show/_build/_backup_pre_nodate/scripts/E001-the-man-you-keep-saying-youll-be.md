## Reading narrative

# The Man You Keep Saying You'll Be
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 1 · January 5, 2027

Most men are measured by their highlight days. Walk Steadfast starts with the day that actually makes a man. An ordinary Tuesday.

Wes Hart and Jonathon open their new show at a walnut table with two mugs and one question. What kind of man are you on a regular day? Not the day with the pictures. A Tuesday.

## Two men at one table

Wes is 52, lives in the Texas Hill Country, and has been married 27 years. He calls his thirties "the fog years," when he coached, sat in the third row at church, and was gone in his own living room. At 36 an older man at a men's breakfast asked him, "What are you waiting on?" and then met him every other Thursday for six years. Wes still drinks from that man's diner mug.

Jonathon is 36 (37 in February), a sales manager from Pasadena, Texas, married to Dani, a night-shift labor and delivery nurse, with two kids. His dad left when he was nine. He came to faith at 29, after Dani told him, "I'm not raising two kids," and he sent Wes what he calls "the scariest text I've ever sent a man." They've met most Thursdays for seven years.

## The long way home

For two years, Jonathon took the long way home on 1604, fifteen extra minutes, so bath time would be over when he walked in. "I got the highlight," he says. "I skipped the Tuesday." Dani asked one question about the traffic and then said, "Okay." That okay followed him around for a week.

Wes tells on himself too. He did the big things, the deck, the summer trips to the coast, and then ate supper in the recliner while his family sat fifteen feet away. His wife stopped asking him to come to the table. "That's when I should have been scared," he says.

## A man puts something down

> "When I was a child, I talked like a child, I thought like a child, I reasoned like a child. When I became a man, I put the ways of childhood behind me."
> 1 Corinthians 13:11 (NIV)

Paul wrote to a gifted church that chased the impressive and neglected the ordinary. His verb matters. He *put* childhood behind him. Manhood isn't a birthday. It's a decision about the recliner, the long way home, the phone at the table. Jesus put it plainly in Luke 16:10: whoever can be trusted with very little can be trusted with much. Tuesday is the very little.

## Rest or hiding?

Jonathon pushes back. Some days he's spent. Is ten minutes in the car a sin? No, Wes says. Rest isn't hiding. "When you rest, you come back." The two of them don't settle whether Wes undersells the big days, and they say so. They also talk about Tuesday for the single 22-year-old, the 62-year-old in a quiet house, and the man who gives everything at work and brings home what's left. "It's still the recliner," Wes tells him. "It's a nicer recliner."
