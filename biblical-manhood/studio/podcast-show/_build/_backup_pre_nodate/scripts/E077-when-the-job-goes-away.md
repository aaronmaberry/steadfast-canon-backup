[CHAPTER: Pull up a chair]
[CAM: WIDE]
WES: Pull up a chair. This week is the job going away. Laid off, let go, plant closed, title taken, retired before you were ready. If it hasn't happened to you, it's probably happened to the man you sit next to at church, and he hasn't told you.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: And for most men, that's not a money problem first. It's a name problem. Because when you meet a man, what's the second question? What do you do. And for thirty years he's had an answer. And then one Thursday he doesn't, and he feels like he's disappeared. Like he's a man-shaped hole where a job used to be. And the people who love him most usually don't see it that way at all. To them he's exactly the same size he was on Wednesday. He's the only one who thinks he shrank.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: And then he waits. He waits for the next job to feel like a man again. He stops going to church because somebody'll ask. He stops calling his friends. He sits at the kitchen table with the laptop open from seven in the morning to midnight, and his kids learn to walk quiet past him. I've watched it happen to two men I used to work with. Good men. In about three months they went from laid off to gone, and the job had nothing to do with the gone part. It was the waiting.
[CAM: 2S]
WES: The mark this week is Stop Waiting. Because a man between jobs is still a husband this week. Still a father this week. Still a man God made on purpose this week. The job was never your name. You don't get to wait for the next one to start being him.
[TEXT: The job was never your name.]

[CHAPTER: Nine years]
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: So, Thursday. New construction's slowed way down in our area this year, and the company decided to cut two installers out of my region. Not the techs who fix things. The guys who put new systems in houses that aren't getting built. Two names came down. I didn't pick them, but I didn't fight them either, because the numbers were the numbers. And one of them was a man I hired nine years ago, when I was still a sales rep and they let me sit in on interviews.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: HR said they'd do it on a video call. And I said no. I'm not going to let a man find out he's done on a laptop screen with a stranger reading a script. So I drove out to the job he was on. A new house, no drywall yet. And I asked him to walk out to my truck. And he knew. You could see it in his face before I opened my mouth. Men always know when the boss drives out to the job site at ten in the morning.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: I told him straight. No speech. "They're cutting two installers, and you're one of them. It's not about your work. Your work is good. It's the building slowdown." And he stood there with his hands in his back pockets, and the first thing he said was, "Did I do something wrong?" And I said, "No. You didn't." And then the second thing he said, and this is the one I can't shake, was, "Can I finish the job I'm on?"
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: What'd you tell him?
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: I said yes. I called and got him through Friday. And he went back in that house and finished running the line set, perfect, like it was his own house. Because it's who he is. The company didn't make him that man, and the company couldn't take it away from him either.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: And then I sat in my truck in front of that house for about an hour and called every contractor I know. Seven calls. By three o'clock he had two interviews for next week. That's not me being a hero. That's the one thing I could actually do. I couldn't keep his job. I could make sure he wasn't standing in his kitchen Monday with nobody to call.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: How's he doing?
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: I don't know yet. I called Saturday. His wife answered, and she said he was out back with the kids, and she said, "He's okay. He's mad. He'll be okay." I'm calling again this week.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: The other installer was twenty-six. Two years in. And he took it completely different. He shrugged and said, "I'll be fine. My cousin's been trying to get me on his crew." And he probably will be fine. But I noticed something on the drive home. I was relieved with the young guy and sick about the older one. And it wasn't because I liked one more. It's because the young guy's job was still a thing he did. The nine-year man's job had become a thing he was. That's the one that hurts to take away. And that's the one I'm watching for in myself, too.
[TEXT: "Can I finish the job I'm on?"]

[CHAPTER: The work clothes]
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: And I'll tell you why I couldn't let HR do it on a laptop. My mom worked two jobs when I was a kid. After my dad left, it was her and two jobs. And when I was about thirteen, she lost the day job. A front desk at a dental office. They brought in a computer system and didn't need her anymore.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: And I didn't know. For six weeks, she got up every morning, put on her work clothes, made our lunches, and drove off at seven-thirty like always. I found out about two years ago, on the phone, almost by accident. I asked her what she used to do at that dental office, and she said, "Oh, honey, they let me go when you were thirteen." I said, "Mom, you went to work every day." And she said, "I went to the library every day. I filled out applications in my work clothes. I wasn't going to let you kids see me sit home."
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: Now, part of that's hiding, and I wish she'd had somebody to tell. But part of it is exactly what that man on the plant floor needs to hear. She didn't wait for the next job to be who she was. She got up, got dressed, and went to work at finding work. Six weeks later she had a job at a pharmacy, and she kept it eighteen years. I never once saw her sit home.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: She kept a schedule.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: Every single day. Today's trouble, and nothing more. She didn't know the verse, I don't think. She lived it anyway.
[TEXT: "I filled out applications in my work clothes."]

[CHAPTER: The Word]
[CAM: WIDE]
JONATHON: Here's the Word. Wes has it.
[CARD: Matthew 6:34]
WES: "Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own."
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: Now, I want to be careful with this one, because people throw it at men who just lost their job like it's a pat on the head. Don't worry about it, brother, God's got it. And the man wants to throw his coffee at them. That's not what Jesus is doing here.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Look at the "therefore." He just got done talking about birds and flowers, and how the Father feeds them and clothes them, and how much more He knows what you need. And then He says, so don't live in tomorrow. Not, don't plan. Not, don't look for work. Don't live in tomorrow. Because that's where a man without a job lives. Every minute of the day is spent in next month. The mortgage in December. Christmas. What if nobody hires a fifty-five-year-old.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: "Each day has enough trouble of its own." That's not a promise that there won't be trouble. It's permission to only carry today's. Today you send three applications. Today you take your daughter to practice. Today you eat supper at the table with your family instead of in front of the laptop. That's enough. Tomorrow's trouble can wait in the truck. And I'd say that to the man who still has a job, too. Half the men listening aren't laid off, they're just scared they will be. You can spend a whole year of good work living in a layoff that never comes. I'd rather carry today's.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: And that's Stop Waiting, too. If you're living in tomorrow, you're not here. Your kids are getting a dad who's somewhere in December. Jesus says, come back to today. There's enough to do in it.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: I lived in tomorrow for a whole fall once. 2009, layoff rumors at work, every week a new one. And I spent about three months in next year. What if it's me. What about the house. What about the kids' school. And I didn't lose my job. They didn't cut me. But I lost that whole fall anyway. My daughter was seven. I couldn't tell you one thing she did that fall, because I wasn't there. I was in next year, worrying about a layoff that never came. That's what Jesus is protecting you from. Not the trouble. The losing of today to it.
[TEXT: Only carry today's.]

[CHAPTER: The title]
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: 2019. I was forty-four. I'd had the same title at work for nine years. And a reorganization came through, and they took it. Same pay. Different desk. Somebody else had my title, and I had a desk by the copier and a job description that was mostly the same work with a smaller word on it.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: And I didn't tell my wife for three weeks. Think about that. Same pay, nothing changed at home, and I couldn't say it. Because I was ashamed. I went to work every day and came home and sat in the chair, and my daughter, who was seventeen, finally asked me, "Are you mad at us?" Because I'd been so quiet and short that she thought it was them.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: What did your wife say when you told her?
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: She said, "Honey, I didn't even know what your title was." And I laughed, and then I didn't, because it hit me. That title had been my name for nine years, in my own head. And to the people I loved most, it had never been my name at all. They didn't know it. They didn't care. They cared that I came home.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: That's actually where "I work. Same as you." came from. People ask me what I do, and I've said that for years, and some folks think I'm being cute. I'm not. I started saying it in 2019, because I'd just found out how much I'd let the answer to that question carry. So I quit letting it carry anything. I work. It's honest work. It's not my name.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: And I'll tell you the part I got right, eventually. After that, I started asking my kids things. Not because I'd read a book. Because I'd just found out the job wasn't who I was, so I had to go find out who I was, and the only place to look was home. My daughter told me last year, "That was the year you started asking us stuff." So the worst thing that happened to me at work turned out to be one of the best things that happened at my house.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: And you've got a review in October.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: I do. And I'm not going to lie and say it doesn't scare me. But I'm not forty-four. If they take something else, I'll tell her the same day. That's the difference eight years makes. Not that I'm not scared. That I don't hide it.
[TEXT: "Honey, I didn't even know what your title was."]

[CHAPTER: His job. His.]
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: One more, and it's a good one. My oldest is twenty-one, an electrical apprentice, about a year and a half from topping out. And I've had about a hundred opinions on it, and I've kept every one of them in my pocket. I told my wife back in the spring, "His job. His." And I meant it. It's been the hardest thing I've kept my mouth shut about in years.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: Did he decide?
[CAM: WES CU]

That's a good decision, and it's yours." And then I hung up and went out to the porch and sat there for a while.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: Would you have told him to stay?
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: I honestly don't know. Some days yes, some days no. And that's why I'm glad I didn't. If I'd pushed, it'd be my decision he was living in. Now it's his. If it goes well, it's his. If it doesn't, it's still his, and he'll learn from it. A man's work has to be his own, or the day it goes away, he'll blame whoever chose it for him. And I'll tell you what I did with all those opinions. I wrote them on a feed-store notepad back in May, folded it up, and put it in my glove box. I'm going to throw it away this week without reading it again. It did its job. It kept them out of my mouth.
[TEXT: "I wanted you to hear it from me first."]

## Reading narrative

# When the Job Goes Away
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 77 · September 28, 2027

Jonathon drove out to a job site to tell an installer he'd hired nine years earlier that his position had been cut. The man asked two questions. "Did I do something wrong?" No. Then: "Can I finish the job I'm on?" He went back inside and finished the work, perfectly, as if it were his own house. The company hadn't made him that man, and it couldn't take that away.

> "Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own."
> Matthew 6:34 (NIV)

This isn't a pat on the head. It follows Jesus' words about the Father who feeds the birds. A man without a job tends to live in next month. Jesus calls him back to today: three applications, a daughter's practice, supper at the table. Today's trouble is enough to carry.
