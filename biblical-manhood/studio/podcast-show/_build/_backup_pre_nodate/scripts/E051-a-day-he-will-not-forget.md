## Reading narrative

# A Day He Will Not Forget
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 51 · June 29, 2027

At six on a Saturday morning, near the end of feeding, Wes asked his older brother to be one of the men at a day for Wes's sixteen-year-old: a few men saying one true thing to the boy, on purpose. His brother kept throwing hay. "Well." Another bale. "Dad never did one of those for us." Then: "What am I supposed to say?" One true thing. "I've only got about one." Then he said their father's loppers were hanging in the tack room, and he'd bring them.

## Be good for your mom

Jonathon got a day he'll never forget. He was nine, dressed for his first start as a pitcher, when his father walked past him in the hall and said, "Be good for your mom." The day had a man, words, and a before and after. It marked him with the wrong thing. A boy gets a day either way. You only decide what's in it.

> "We will not hide them from their descendants; we will tell the next generation the praiseworthy deeds of the LORD, his power, and the wonders he has done."
> Psalm 78:4 (NIV)

The psalm promises to tell the next generation "the praiseworthy deeds of the LORD," not the deeds of the fathers. When a son asks, the story is about what God did.

## The one who didn't get one

When Wes asked his oldest son to stand at the barn for his brother, the line went quiet. "You didn't do one for me." Wes said he hadn't, and that he was sorry. His son will come, for his brother.
