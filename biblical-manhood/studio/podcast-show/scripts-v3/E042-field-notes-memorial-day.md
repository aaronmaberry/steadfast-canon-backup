## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: Memorial Day
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 42 · May 28, 2027

A coach-pitch dad named Shane has spent nine years of nights building a model of the battleship Texas in his garage. On the bridge there's a brass plate smaller than a stick of gum. It's for Christen Christensen, the helmsman, who was twenty-one when a German shell hit the bridge off Cherbourg in June 1944. He was the only man aboard the Texas killed in action in the whole war. Shane's grandfather was below in a gun mount that day: "Everybody on that ship got to come home but one." Every time Shane sits down to work, he says, "Evening, Chris." Jonathon's daughter says it now too, and she practiced his last name in the car so she'd get it right.

## You can't love a phrase

"The fallen" is a phrase. Every name on a memorial was somebody's boy. Wes has driven past the granite memorial on his courthouse lawn more times than he can count and has never read the names on the back. On Monday he will, out loud. A post with a flag graphic is for the people watching. Reading a name at a stone, where nobody's watching, isn't a costume.

> "Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends."
> John 15:13 (NIV)

Jesus said it the night before He did it. A soldier isn't Jesus, but the love points to Him.

## The harder versions

You don't have to have served to read the names. If your father came home from a war and was hard on your house, you can honor the men on the stone and still grieve what came home. And honoring a twenty-one-year-old at a ship's wheel isn't a vote on the war.

## Read the names

Find your town's memorial and read every name out loud, one to a breath. Pick one man and look him up, then tell your family one true thing about him. Pray for whoever he left behind.

If the day is heavy for you, call someone who knew him. Veterans and their families can dial 988 and press 1.
