## Reading narrative

# Your Daughter Is Watching
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 45 · June 8, 2027

When Wes's daughter was fifteen, he took her to the feed store every Saturday. He asked her the same three questions every week: school, friends, practice. She answered "fine" three times. One Saturday on the way home she asked him, "Daddy, can you ask me something you don't know the answer to?" He drove a mile before he could think of anything. Years later he realized the three questions were his own father's.

## Where the good voice goes

Jonathon has a phone voice. It's warm and slow, and it laughs at things that aren't funny. His seven-year-old learned it from the kitchen doorway, holding a cereal bowl to her ear. Later, in the truck, she asked, "Dad, why do you talk nicer on the phone?" Then, "Can I have it?" Jonathon argues the voice isn't fake. Wes agrees, and says what she wants is the attention under it, starting with the first ten minutes after he walks in.

> "Impress them on your children. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up."
> Deuteronomy 6:7 (NIV)

Sitting at home, the road, bedtime, the morning. None of those is church. Something is getting pressed into those hours either way, so the only choice you get is what. And a man who's new to faith impresses what he has, including "I don't know. Let's find out."

## A phone call and a bedtime prayer

Wes called his grown daughter and asked what she'd learned about men from watching him. "I learned to wait," she said. Then she asked what he'd been thinking about all those quiet Saturdays, and he told her the truth. She'd thought he was mad at her. Jonathon prayed over his daughter by name for the first time and borrowed her sentence to finish. She told him he could keep it.

## The challenge

Pick one ordinary hour with her. Ask her one thing you don't know the answer to, then a second question. Then give her something you usually save for the room.
