## Reading narrative

# A Man With No Brothers
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 37

Wes went out on his mother's porch with a drill to rehang the screen door she'd shut behind him when he left home at eighteen. The door was heavy, and halfway through it got lighter. His older brother had come out and taken the other side. For an hour they talked about the rain and a bull. At the end Wes asked the question he'd carried for three weeks. "Who do you talk to? Out here." His brother said, "Well." Then he said, "Dad. I still talk to Dad. Out at the barn. Mornings." Their father has been gone nine years. "What does he say back?" "Not much. Same as always."

Jonathon thinks Wes should have said something then. Wes thinks one more word would have closed the door for good. They agree the barn needs words in it eventually.

## Known by name

Jonathon scrolled through the men he'd texted that year. Harper's Dad, Twins Dad, Theo Soccer Coach, Plumber Good, Plumber Bad. When he moved to San Antonio, he hired a moving company rather than find out whether his friends would come. Years earlier, a senior salesman covered his floor for three days while his mother was in the hospital and never mentioned it. Jonathon never thanked him and can't remember his last name. "I'm a man with no brothers," he says, "and a phone full of other men's kids."

> "If either of them falls down, one can help the other up. But pity anyone who falls and has no one to help them up."
> Ecclesiastes 4:10 (NIV)

The verse doesn't admire the man who gets himself up. It pities him. Two verses earlier, the writer describes a man with no son and no brother who never stops working and never asks who it's for.

## Bass seeks three

Merle, a seventy-one-year-old widower, put a handwritten card on his church's bulletin board. "Bass seeks three. Gospel, the old way. Must read shape notes or be willing to fake it." Now an eighty-year-old baritone and a thirty-four-year-old youth pastor sing with him on Thursday nights. They need a lead. "If I don't come," Merle says, "the baritone drove forty minutes for nothing."
