[CHAPTER: Intro]
[CAM: 2S]
(Walk Steadfast open written for E077. Same beats each episode, new words. SHOW-BIBLE section 5.)
WES: This is Walk Steadfast. I'm Wes Hart. In 2019 I lost a title and hid it for three weeks.
[LOWER THIRD: Wes Hart]
JONATHON: I'm Jonathon. I manage people for a living.
[LOWER THIRD: Jonathon]
WES: Following Jesus as a man, even when the work goes away. The Steadfast book and the video training, both on walksteadfast.com.
JONATHON: Today's question is who a man is without his job.
WES: Episode seventy-seven. When the Job Goes Away.

[CHAPTER: Pull up a chair]
[CAM: WIDE]
WES: Pull up a chair. Today is the job going away. Laid off, let go, plant closed, title taken, retired before you were ready. If it hasn't happened to you, it's probably happened to the man you sit next to at church, and he hasn't told you.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: Or he's told you it's fine. Which is worse.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: And for most men, that's not a money problem first. It's a name problem. Because when you meet a man, what's the second question? What do you do. And for thirty years he's had an answer. And then one Thursday he doesn't, and he feels like he's disappeared. Like he's a man-shaped hole where a job used to be. And the people who love him most usually don't see it that way at all. To them he's exactly the same size he was on Wednesday. He's the only one who thinks he shrank.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: And then he waits. He waits for the next job to feel like a man again. He stops going to church because somebody'll ask. He stops calling his friends. He sits at the kitchen table with the laptop open from seven in the morning to midnight, and his kids learn to walk quiet past him. I've watched it happen to two men I used to work with. Good men. In about three months they went from laid off to gone, and the job had nothing to do with the gone part. It was the waiting.
[CAM: 2S]
WES: So the mark today is Stop Waiting. Because a man between jobs is still a husband on Tuesday. Still a father on Tuesday. Still a man God made on purpose on Tuesday. The job was never your name. You don't get to wait for the next one to start being him.
[TEXT: The job was never your name.]

[CHAPTER: Nine years]
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: So, Thursday. New construction's slowed way down in our area this year, and the company decided to cut two installers out of my region. Not the techs who fix things. The men who put new systems in houses that aren't getting built. Two names came down. I didn't pick them, but I didn't fight them either, because the numbers were the numbers. And one of them was a man I hired nine years ago, when I was still a sales rep and they let me sit in on interviews.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Do you remember the interview?
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: I remember one sentence. The manager asked him why he wanted the job. And most guys say something about benefits, or growth, the stuff you're supposed to say. And he said, "My dad did this. I'd like to be better at it than him." That's it. And the manager looked at me, and I nodded, and that was the hire. I voted yes on that one sentence. Nine years ago.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: HR said they'd do it on a video call. And I said no. I'm not going to let a man find out he's done on a laptop screen with a stranger reading a script. So I drove out to the job he was on. A new house, no drywall yet. And I asked him to walk out to my truck. And he knew. You could see it in his face before I opened my mouth. Men always know when the boss drives out to the job site at ten in the morning.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: I told him straight. No speech. "They're cutting two installers, and you're one of them. It's not about your work. Your work is good. It's the building slowdown." And he stood there with his hands in his back pockets, and the first thing he said was, "Did I do something wrong?" And I said, "No. You didn't." And then the second thing he said, and this is the one I can't shake, was, "Can I finish the job I'm on?"
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: What'd you tell him?
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: I said yes. I called and got him through Friday. And he went back in that house and finished running the line set, perfect, like it was his own house. Because it's who he is. The company didn't make him that man, and the company couldn't take it away from him either.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: And then I sat in my truck in front of that house for about an hour and called every contractor I know. Seven calls. By three o'clock he had two interviews. That's not me being a hero. That's the one thing I could actually do. I couldn't keep his job. I could make sure he wasn't standing in his kitchen Monday with nobody to call.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: Can I ask you why you drove out? Not what you said. Why you went.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: (beat) The answer I've been giving people is, because he deserved it face to face. And that's true. But I sat with it Thursday night, and it's not the whole answer. Part of it was, I didn't want to be the bad guy in his story. If HR did it on a screen, I'm the manager who let a stranger do it. If I drove out, I'm the manager who cared. I wanted him to like me while I took his job.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Does that make it wrong that you went?
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: No. I'd go again. But I want to know it about myself, because if the only reason I'm decent is so people will like me, then the first time decent costs me something, I'll find a reason not to. And the other thing I noticed was on the drive home. The other installer was twenty-six. Two years in. He shrugged and said, "I'll be fine. My cousin's been trying to get me on his crew." And I was relieved with the young guy and sick about the older one.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: And it wasn't because I liked one more. It's because the young guy's job was still a thing he did. The nine-year man's job had become a thing he was. That's the one that hurts to take away. And that's the one I'm watching for in myself.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: How's he doing?
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: I called Saturday. His wife answered, and she said he was out back with the kids, and she said, "He's okay. He's mad. He'll be okay." And Thursday night, after all of it, I got home and Theo wanted the wagon. And I took him. Left my phone on the counter. I didn't say a word for about three blocks. Theo did all the talking. Every mailbox. I needed somebody to be four at me for a while.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: I'll tell you why I'm glad you called him. In 2009, when the rumors were going around my work, they did cut some men. Not me. A man two desks down from me. We'd eaten lunch together for about four years. And I didn't call him. Not once.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: Why not? You'd eaten lunch with the man for four years and you never called him once?
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: I told myself he'd want to be left alone. That's what I'd have wanted. That was a lie I told so I wouldn't have to stand in his kitchen not knowing what to say. He moved to Amarillo that spring. I never saw him again. Every time I hear "Can I finish the job I'm on?" I think, I didn't even finish the lunch I was on.
[TEXT: "Can I finish the job I'm on?"]

[CHAPTER: Not his name]
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: And I want to push back on your line a little. "The job was never your name." I get what you mean. But I don't want that man to hear it the wrong way.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Which way's the wrong way?
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: That his work didn't matter. That it was just a paycheck and he should've held it loose. Because "Can I finish the job I'm on?" is the best thing anybody's said to me in a year. That man's work was a little bit his name. When he runs a line set straight, that's him. That's his dad, and him wanting to be better than his dad. If you tell him the job was never his name, I think he hears, the thing you were proudest of was nothing.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: (long exhale) That's a real distinction, and I think you're right about him. Let me say it tighter. The work can carry your character. It can't carry your name. The way he finished that line set came with him. It got in his truck and drove home with him. The company can't lay that off. What the company could take was the badge and the truck and the schedule. That's the part that was never his name.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: Okay. That one I'll sign. The work was his. The job was theirs.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: And I'll admit why that one's tender for me. When I was thirty-one, my office got merged, and I had to interview for my own job. Same desk, same reps, and a man from Dallas asking me what I'd bring to the role. I'd been a Christian about two years, and I prayed about it like a man who'd been a Christian about two years. Loud, and with a lot of bargaining.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: What'd you bargain with?
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: Everything. Tithing, small group, I think I offered to quit cussing in traffic. I kept the job. And I'm a little ashamed to say I quit about half of what I promised within a year. But I remember sitting in that interview thinking, if this goes, who am I? And I didn't have an answer. I had a title and a region I wanted. That was the whole man.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: And a man who knows the difference loses a job. A man who doesn't loses himself. I've been both. We'll get to 2019.
[TEXT: The work was his. The job was theirs.]

[CHAPTER: The work clothes]
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: And I'll tell you why I couldn't let HR do it on a laptop. My mom worked two jobs when I was a kid. After my dad left, it was her and two jobs. And when I was about thirteen, she lost the day job. A front desk at a dental office. They brought in a computer system and didn't need her anymore.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: And I didn't know. For six weeks, she got up every morning, put on her work clothes, made our lunches, and drove off at seven-thirty like always. I found out about two years ago, on the phone, almost by accident. I asked her what she used to do at that dental office, and she said, "Oh, honey, they let me go when you were thirteen." I said, "Mom, you went to work every day." And she said, "I went to the library every day. I filled out applications in my work clothes. I wasn't going to let you kids see me sit home."
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Did you notice anything? At thirteen.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: I've gone back over it about a hundred times since that call. Two things. She started asking about my day before I could ask about hers. Every night, right when she walked in. "How was school? What'd you eat?" Like she was getting there first. And on Saturdays she ate lunch out of a brown bag at the kitchen table, like she'd packed it for work. I thought it was weird. I didn't think about it again for twenty-two years.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: She was keeping the shape of the day. So it wouldn't fall apart on her.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: Now, part of that's hiding, and I wish she'd had somebody to tell. But part of it is exactly what a man on a plant floor needs to hear. She didn't wait for the next job to be who she was. She got up, got dressed, and went to work at finding work. Six weeks later she had a job at a pharmacy, and she kept it eighteen years. I never once saw her sit home.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Every single day.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: Every single day. Today's trouble, and nothing more. She didn't know the verse, I don't think. She lived it anyway.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Did you ever ask her why she didn't tell anybody? A sister, the church, a friend from the dental office.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: Same call. I said, "Mom, why didn't you tell somebody?" And she said, "Who? Everybody I knew needed something from me. Nobody needed to hear that." And I didn't have anything to say back. I sat in my car in the garage with the engine off for about twenty minutes after we hung up.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: That's most men I know. Everybody needs something from them, so nobody gets told. The plant supervisor we're going to hear from later, I'd bet he's got a whole list of people who need him to be fine. His wife, his kids, the men who used to work under him. And for thirty years he's been the one they set things down with. Nobody ever set it up to go the other direction.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: So who'd you have told in 2019, if it wasn't going to be your wife? You had the men's breakfast by then. You had a table full of men.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: I did. And I sat at that breakfast three times in those three weeks and talked about everybody's gutters. Men who would've driven to my house at midnight if I'd asked. I didn't ask. A title by the copier felt too small to bring and too big to say. That's the trap. Small enough you think you ought to handle it yourself, big enough that it's eating you anyway.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: Too small to bring and too big to say. That's every rep I've ever had who quit without telling me what was wrong. I'd find out in the exit interview. "I didn't want to bother you." Bother me. That's the whole job. I'd rather be bothered on a Tuesday than read about it on a form.
[TEXT: "I filled out applications in my work clothes."]

[CHAPTER: The Word]
[CAM: WIDE]
JONATHON: Here's the Word. Wes has it.
[CARD: Matthew 6:34]
WES: "Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own."
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: Now, I want to be careful with this one, because people throw it at men who just lost their job like it's a pat on the head. Don't worry about it, brother, God's got it. And the man wants to throw his coffee at them. That's not what Jesus is doing here.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: Then what is He doing?
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Look at the "therefore." He just got done talking about birds and flowers. The birds don't plant or harvest or fill a barn, and the Father feeds them. The flowers in the field don't work or spin, and they're dressed better than Solomon. And He asks, can any of you add one hour to your life by worrying? And then He says, so don't live in tomorrow.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: So don't plan? Because I plan for a living.
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: Not, don't plan. Not, don't look for work. The birds still go get the food. Don't live in tomorrow. Because that's where a man without a job lives. Every minute of the day is spent in next month. The mortgage in December. Christmas. What if nobody hires a fifty-five-year-old.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: Mm. Next month's a loud place.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: And look at the question in the middle of it. Can any of you add a single hour to your life by worrying? I used to lie awake doing that math. Two in the morning, figuring how many months we'd last. And it never once added an hour. It took about six hours of sleep a week off me and added nothing.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: And right before this verse there's the line everybody knows, seek first his kingdom. And I always read that like a to-do list. Seek first, then everything else. But in the middle of a layoff I think it's more like, where's your head living? Seek first means, before you go live in December, live in today with God in it. Then go send the applications.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: "Each day has enough trouble of its own." That's not a promise that there won't be trouble. It's permission to only carry today's. Today you send three applications. Today you take your daughter to practice. Today you eat supper at the table with your family instead of in front of the laptop. That's enough. Tomorrow's trouble can wait in the truck.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: And I'd say that to the man who still has a job, too, because I'm him. I live in tomorrow in quarters. I've been living in Q4 since about March. I'm at Ruby's game, and I'm in December. I'm at supper, and I'm in a meeting that hasn't been scheduled yet. You can spend a whole year of good work living in a layoff that never comes.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: I did exactly that once. 2009, layoff rumors at work, every week a new one. And I spent about three months in next year. What if it's me. What about the house. What about the kids' school. I'd lie awake doing math on the ceiling.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: And did it? The layoff?
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: And I didn't lose my job. They didn't cut me. But I lost that whole fall anyway. My daughter was seven. I couldn't tell you one thing she did that fall, because I wasn't there. I was in next year, worrying about a layoff that never came. That's what Jesus is protecting you from. Not the trouble. The losing of today to it.
[CAM: JON CU]
JONATHON: Wait. Is that the same fall?
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: Same fall. That's the fall I told you about with the horn. The man in the next lane cut me off, and I held the horn about ten seconds with my daughter in the back seat, and I said, "That man's a bad driver," like that explained it. JONATHON: So I'll ask you the same thing he asked. What was the horn guarding?
[CAM: WES CU]
WES: My dad's sentence, the one he said about providing. And the fear of not paying the bills. I was so scared of losing the job that I lost the fall and honked at a stranger with my seven-year-old watching. The job never went anywhere. The fear did all of that by itself.
[CAM: JON MS]
JONATHON: That's the scariest version. You lose the fall and you keep the job.
[CAM: WES MS]
WES: And that's Stop Waiting, too. If you're living in tomorrow, you're not here. Your kids are getting a dad who's somewhere in December. Jesus says, come back to today. There's enough to do in it.
[TEXT: Only carry today's.]

[CHAPTER: The title]
[CAM: WES MS]

JONATHON: By the copier. That's a message. Nobody puts a desk by the copier by accident.

JONATHON: What did you do instead? For three weeks. Because I know what I'd do. I'd get very busy.

[CAM: WES MS]
WES: And I laughed, and then I didn't, because it hit me. That title had been my name for nine years, in my own head. And to the people I loved most, it had never been my name at all. They didn't know it. They didn't care. They cared that I came home.

JONATHON: So, the thing you say. "I work. Same as you."

JONATHON: Did they notice?

# When the Job Goes Away
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 77

Jonathon drove out to a job site to tell an installer he'd hired nine years earlier that his position had been cut. The man asked two questions. "Did I do something wrong?" No. Then: "Can I finish the job I'm on?" He went back inside and finished the work, perfectly, as if it were his own house. The company hadn't made him that man, and it couldn't take that away. The work was his. The job was theirs.

> "Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own."
