Tool on the table. A handwritten note. Not a text, and not a store card. Three or four sentences in your own handwriting. One true, specific thing she did that you see now that you're grown, then thank you, then your name. No "but," no apology she has to answer, no request, and no fixing. If she was hard, write only what's true, even if it's small. Married, write one to your wife about how she mothers your kids. Divorced, hand your kids the pen for their mom. If your mom has died, write it anyway. If she was dangerous, you don't owe her a note. Write it for yourself, and get help with what comes up.

### Walk Steadfast, Episode 36

"This is a real one."

## When it's complicated

After the game, the twin's father asked Jonathon a question in the parking lot. His mother is in memory care. She raised two kids alone on night shifts and was hard, with no games and no "proud." Now she's gentle with everyone. Last month she held his hand and said, "You have kind eyes. My son had kind eyes." He has been waiting forty years for her to say something like that. She said it to a stranger, and the stranger was him.

Jonathon thinks he should visit Saturday and keep Sunday for his wife and girls. Wes thinks he should go Sunday morning, briefly, so he's there on the day. They leave the choice with him.

> "Her children arise and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praises her:"
> Proverbs 31:28 (NIV)

The verse doesn't say her children call her perfect. It says they stand up and say the good thing out loud, even if it's only one thing. His one thing is "We never once went to school hungry."

## The note

Write it by hand, and keep it short. One true thing, thank you, your name. Leave out any apology she has to answer and anything you're asking for. She won't remember Sunday, Jonathon told him, but a card stays on her table, and the nurses read cards aloud. Every morning she may hear it for the first time.
