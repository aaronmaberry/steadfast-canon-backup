Jonathon wrote three anger lines in his notes app. The hardest: "I feel dumb about money." He told Dani. She said, "You're not dumb about money. You're dumb about the spreadsheet," and took it from eleven colors to four. Wes snapped at his physical therapist and found the guard underneath: "I'm scared I'll end up like Dad." His wife said, "Me too, a little." He called his little brother on his birthday for the first time in about twenty years. His brother's first words were "Is Mom okay?" Wes's oldest son texted: "foreman yelled. guarding: scared i'm not good at this."

## A question men ask

> "He looked around at them in anger and, deeply distressed at their stubborn hearts, said to the man, 'Stretch out your hand.' He stretched it out, and his hand was completely restored."
> Mark 3:5 (NIV)

Jesus's anger had grief in it, and it healed a man's hand. Two questions test yours. Who got wronged? What does it make you do? A mom driving her kids on four bald tires made Wes angry in the right way, and by Wednesday she had tires. Jonathon adds the other side. A man who never gets angry at anything may not be calm. He may just be absent.

## The tool

The twenty-minute rule. Say when you're coming back. Move your body. Don't rehearse. Come back on time and start with what it was guarding. Leave the temperature, not the conversation.
