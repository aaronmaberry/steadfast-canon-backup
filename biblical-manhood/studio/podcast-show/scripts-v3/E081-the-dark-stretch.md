## Reading narrative

# The Dark Stretch
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 81 · October 12, 2027

For most of a year in his late thirties, Wes ate supper in the recliner. One evening his nine-year-old daughter carried her plate in and sat on the carpet beside his chair, and he reached over and turned the TV up. He never saw a doctor that year. He thought it was a character problem, and he tried harder, and it didn't work.

> "LORD, you are the God who saves me; day and night I cry out to you. May my prayer come before you; turn your ear to my cry."
> Psalm 88:1-2 (NIV)

Most of the sad psalms turn toward praise before they end. Psalm 88 doesn't. It ends with darkness as the writer's closest friend, and God kept it in the book anyway. The man who wrote it was a temple musician and one of the wisest men in Israel, praying day and night and not yet feeling any better. That still counts as faith.

## The second question

At their Thursday table, if a man misses, whoever notices calls him by Friday noon. Once, when the answer was "I'm good, just slammed," Jonathon asked again: "No. How are you?" The man had been sleeping in his truck in his own driveway for a week. That evening Jonathon sat in the passenger seat for an hour, and then the man went inside.

## The challenge

Call one man who's gone quiet before Friday noon, and ask the second time. If you're the one in the dark, go first: call him, your doctor, or 988.
