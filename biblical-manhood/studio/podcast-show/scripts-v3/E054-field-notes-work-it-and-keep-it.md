# Work It and Keep It
### Walk Steadfast, Field Notes, Episode 54

On Tuesday at noon Wes knocked on a coworker's truck window. The man had been eating lunch alone in his running truck since April. The window came down two inches: "You need something?" Wes asked if he could eat in the air conditioning. "Door's open." They talked about baseball. On Wednesday the cooler had moved to the back seat. On Thursday the man said, "You don't have to do this." Wes said, "I know. It's air-conditioned." He doesn't know what's wrong, and he isn't going to ask. His wife wrote the man's name on the grocery pad on the fridge, so Wes will remember to ask about his kids.

## Is it wrong to like your job?

One of Jonathon's reps likes his work so much he doesn't want his vacation days, and his small group makes it sound like a problem.

> "A person can do nothing better than to eat and drink and find satisfaction in their own toil. This too, I see, is from the hand of God, ..."
> Ecclesiastes 2:24 (NIV)

The man who wrote that had tried everything and called it chasing the wind. What was left was a meal and work he liked. Liking your work is a gift. Needing it is the problem, and from the inside the two feel the same for a long time. You find out which one you have when the work is taken away for a while. Wes told his youngest, out loud, that he likes his job. "Huh. Can I have the rest of the potatoes?"

## The end-of-day shutdown

Write down the first thing for tomorrow. Name what you kept today. Close it, and say out loud, "That's enough for today." Wes found out he's been doing a version of this for twenty years: emptying his pockets on the dresser before he walks into the kitchen.
