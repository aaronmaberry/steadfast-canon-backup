# Field Notes: Saying It Out Loud
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 6

This week's challenge asked men to name their weakest mark, tell one man, and (for married men) ask their wives which mark they do best and which they'd love to see more.

## Check the box

The gutter is a miss. The carpenter's number is still on a sticky note on Wes's fridge, and his wife has written "CALL" across it in red. But Wes named his weakest mark, stop waiting, and told his 27-year-old son-in-law, a man he'd known for two years and had never told anything true about himself. The young man's answer was "Can I tell you mine?" They stood by the trucks for forty-five minutes. His wife said he does pick up the weight best, and reminded him of the year he drove her father to treatments. Then she said she'd love more going first "with me." And Wes named the thing he hasn't fixed: in twenty-seven years, he and his wife have never managed to pray together out loud, just the two of them.

Jonathon's weakest is working for God's pay. He told the contractor who first invited him to a men's breakfast, who admitted he hasn't told his crew about his wife's surgery. Dani said Jonathon goes first best. He was the one who suggested counseling in their third year of marriage. Then she named what she'd like to see more: stop waiting, "on your dad." A friend request his father sent on Christmas Day is still sitting there, neither accepted nor declined. Wes's response: "You don't have to solve it on a Friday."

## From the table: do I need all four?

Work on the weakest one, Wes says, but the four come together, and your strongest usually carries your weakest. One warning. "Your strongest one can turn into your hiding place." For years Wes fixed sinks and cars instead of having the conversation his marriage needed. "My strength was my recliner. A busier recliner. With tools." Jonathon pushes back: sometimes a good thing is just love. Wes agrees, and offers a test. "What am I not doing while I do this?"

Jonathon offers Wes one small thing on the praying. Before Dani's night shifts, they sit in the driveway. She prays a few sentences. He holds her hand and says one.

> "being confident of this, that he who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus."
> Philippians 1:6 (NIV)

"You're not the one who has to finish you."
