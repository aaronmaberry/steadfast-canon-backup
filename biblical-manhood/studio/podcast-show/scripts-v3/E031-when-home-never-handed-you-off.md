# When Home Never Handed You Off
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 31

The morning Wes left for school, his father shook his hand at breakfast, said "Drive careful," and went out to the barn. His mother carried the last load of laundry to the car, warm and folded, and set her hand on the roof. "Your daddy's not going to say this, so I'm going to. You're a man now. You act like one. And don't come home every Friday." Then she went inside. He came home every Friday that semester with his laundry, and she washed it without a word. "She said the true thing once," Wes says, "and then she let me take my time growing into it."

Jonathon's list is shorter. At nine his mother called him the man of the house, which was a job, not a handoff. At fifteen a pitching coach told him, "Never let them see you hurt." At his first sales job, the highest praise he ever earned was "Not bad, kid." Last month he heard himself say "Not bad" to his own rep, looking over his screen the same way.

## He went home

> "'Why were you searching for me?' he asked. 'Didn't you know I had to be in my Father's house?'"

At twelve, Jesus already knew whose house he belonged to. Then he went home to Nazareth and was obedient to his parents, and his mother treasured it all in her heart. "When a man knows whose he is," Wes says, "he can go home and be kind."

## Her guy, or her son

Jonathon still answers when his mother's air conditioner makes a noise, even from a dugout three and a half hours away. He has sat at the dealership desk for every car she's bought since he was sixteen. She leaned on the only man left in the house because she had to, Wes says. That's no sin. But the job had an end date, and nobody ever told either of them. "Her guy is needed. Her son is loved." Jonathon pushed back. His mother lives alone, and to her the capacitor is a hard thing. They agreed on one point: the dugout isn't the answer.

## Can we go fishing?
