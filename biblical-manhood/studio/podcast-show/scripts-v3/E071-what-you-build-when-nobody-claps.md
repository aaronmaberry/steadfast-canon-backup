## Reading narrative

# What You Build When Nobody Claps
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 71

On Labor Day morning Jonathon brought tacos to the shop at six. One of the younger techs started clapping, and the whole room joined in, for the man who'd brought breakfast. Those men had spent the summer in attics. Nobody claps for a capacitor. The senior tech had already left on a call to a dialysis patient's house. He ate his tacos cold on his truck seat at ten.

In his thirties Wes coached Little League for three seasons. He was there an hour early to drag the infield, he bought the ice, and he made the rain calls. At the banquet the coach-of-the-year plaque went to his assistant. At breakfast the next Thursday, Mr. Abernathy tapped the table twice. "Who were you doing it for?" And then, "If it was for the kids, you already got paid. They played ball."

> "Therefore, my dear brothers and sisters, stand firm. Let nothing move you. Always give yourselves fully to the work of the Lord, because you know that your labor in the Lord is not in vain."
> 1 Corinthians 15:58 (NIV)

"Therefore" points back to the resurrection. Because Jesus rose, there is a day when everything gets seen, and nothing given to the Lord is wasted. Wanting to be seen isn't the sin. Jesus sat across from the offering box and watched, and He pointed out the widow nobody else noticed.

## The handshake isn't the pay

For three Augusts Wes drove two neighbor boys to six a.m. football practice, and his wife wrapped their biscuits in foil every morning. This July one of the boys, home from the Army, found Wes at the feed store. "I just wanted you to know I knew." It was a gift. It wasn't the pay.
