# If There Is No Wife Yet
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 57 · July 20, 2027

Three men from a seventy-one-year-old widower's quartet sing hymns at a nursing home once a month. One Thursday, a young man visiting his grandmother sang the melody loud from the back row, next to her wheelchair. The widower stopped the song. "Son, come up here." His grandmother pushed him, and they finished with four.

The young man is twenty-four. He works overnights, takes the bus, and does his grandmother's nails because the lady who did them quit. Afterward he wrote: everybody at church asks him the same two questions, whether he's seeing anybody and when he's getting a car. When the widower called him up, "it was the first time anybody at a church asked me to do something instead of asking me what im waiting on."

## The pregame and the razor

Jonathon married at twenty-seven and spent his single years treating life as the pregame. He visited six churches and scored them like restaurants. The man who walked up to the altar was the same man who'd been drinking on Thursdays. "Whoever she is, if she comes, she's going to marry what you're practicing."

At twenty-two, Wes let his uncles sit with his mother's dying father, because he'd decided that was a job for men with families. He went once. His grandfather couldn't hold a razor, so Wes shaved him. "You did all right, son."

> "Nevertheless, each person should live as a believer in whatever situation the Lord has assigned to them, just as God has called them. This is the rule I lay down in all the churches."
> 1 Corinthians 7:17 (NIV)

The place you're in right now is the assignment. Want a wife, and tell God plainly when the wanting hurts. But wanting a wife and living your life aren't two seasons. They're the same Tuesday.
