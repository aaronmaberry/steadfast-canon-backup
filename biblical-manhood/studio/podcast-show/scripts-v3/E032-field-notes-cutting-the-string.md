# Field Notes: Cutting the String
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 32

Wes called his older brother to tell him about their mother in the driveway: the warm laundry, the hand on the car roof, "You're a man now." He expected a laugh. Instead his brother said, "She never said that to me. I never left. Nobody stands in the driveway for the one who stays." His brother has run their father's place for thirty years. Wes said he was sorry, and his brother said it was just how it went. Then they talked about cattle prices.

Jonathon's twenty-two-year-old nephew did the challenge and texted: "the challenge thing. i said you. dont make it weird." It took Jonathon fifteen minutes to write back "ok."

## From the table: my mom still calls me every day

Jonathon's rep is thirty-one, and his mother calls every morning at 7:15. He tells her everything, every meeting and every bad day. He didn't know her best friend's name.

> "not looking to your own interests but each of you to the interests of the others."
> Philippians 2:4 (NIV)

Daily isn't the problem, Wes says. Plenty of good sons call their mothers every day. The question is which way the call runs. Jonathon pushed back: he would have taken a daily call about nothing from his own father. They settled on both: the call is a gift, and it can still be a string. In his first year of marriage, Wes called his mother from a pay phone at a used-truck lot before he told his wife. "Did you call your mother before you asked me?"
