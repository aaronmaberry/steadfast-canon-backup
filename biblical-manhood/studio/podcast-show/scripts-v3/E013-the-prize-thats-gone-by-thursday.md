## Reading narrative

# The Prize That's Gone by Thursday
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 13 · February 16, 2027

Every man is working for something underneath his paycheck. The last of the four marks asks what. Wes Hart and Jonathon call it working for God's pay, not the room's.

## The Monday email

For eleven years Jonathon has sat in his driveway at 7:58 on Monday mornings, refreshing his email for a ranked list of every sales manager in the country. Top five, and he's funny at breakfast. Lower, and he's short with everyone until Wednesday, and nobody at home knows why. When he told his wife, she said, "I always wondered why Mondays were weird." The board resets every week. Whatever he felt Monday is gone by Thursday. His best run, number one in the country three Mondays straight, came in a fall he can't remember with his kids.

## Aisle seven

Wes is the man his church calls to pray in public. "Wes is a rock." He loves it, and he didn't think anything was wrong with that, until he caught himself in the coffee aisle hoping somebody would notice the good coffee he was buying for a car-care Saturday for single moms. "The coffee told on me."

## Why the cheap prize wins

Jonathon knows incentive plans. The good ones pay fast, are easy to count, and are visible. The room pays exactly that way. God's pay is slow, unmeasurable and mostly unseen. "If I designed a comp plan like that, I'd be fired by lunch." So it's not a feeling. It's a choice you make every Monday, and the feeling catches up later.

## An inheritance, not a paycheck

> "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving."
> Colossians 3:23-24 (NIV)

You can't earn your way in; that's settled by grace. An inheritance comes from whose family you're in, the way a daughter has her father's last name. "The email ranks you. Your Father already named you." And Jesus endured the cross "for the joy set before him," scorning the room's verdict entirely (Hebrews 12:2).

Jonathon remembered one night when he felt the other pay: holding his three-year-old with croup in a steaming bathroom at two in the morning. "This is the best thing I've done all year, and there's no board for it."

## The truck door

Most mornings Wes says one sentence out loud in his truck before he walks into work: "This is for you." Jonathon thinks it sounds like a sales trick, and he knows he'd start counting it. Wes: don't count it. The point is to turn your head toward the one who's watching. You won't stop wanting to be noticed. You put the want in second place.

## Keeping score at home

Early in his marriage Wes left a pan soaking in the sink so there'd be evidence he did the dishes. His wife: "You do the dishes like you're waiting for a parade." Jonathon folds the laundry and stacks it on his wife's side of the bed so she'll see it. "I just wanted you to see it." Thank each other often, Wes says. The trouble starts when the thanks doesn't come and a man starts a list. Wes's freshest one: a text to his brother after putting a grab bar in their mother's bathroom, sent so his brother would know who did it.

## The receipts

After Wes's mentor died, his daughter found thirty-some diner receipts in his Bible. On the back of each was a man's first name and a column of dates. They were the days he had prayed for that man. Wes's went back nine years. He never said a word.

## Two chairs

If you tell your one man about the good thing nobody saw, haven't you built a small room? Wes says there's a difference between telling a man so he'll clap and telling him so he'll check you. Jonathon thinks a man needs a whole drawer of things nobody knows. They leave it unsettled.

## What they actually did

Wes donated the coffee anonymously (and then told everyone, which he admits). He keeps a short list of men's names on the back of an index card and prays for them most mornings. He asked his son-in-law to pray from the front of the church instead of him. Jonathon moved the Monday email to 10 AM. First Monday: number six, four minutes of feeling bad.
