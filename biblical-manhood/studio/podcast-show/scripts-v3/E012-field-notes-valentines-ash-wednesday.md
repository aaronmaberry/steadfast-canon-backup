# Field Notes: Valentine's, Ash Wednesday, and Going First
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 12 · February 12, 2027

Tuesday's challenge was to go first in one small, true thing. Wes and Jonathon report, and then take a question a lot of men carry quietly.

## "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me"

At his mother's appointment, Wes's brother took a breath to start, and Wes went first: "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me." She laid out her plan. The doctor called it "a very good plan" and added a grab bar and daytime-only driving. In the room his brother said, "We talked about this." In the parking lot he said, "I never asked her either." Then their mother rolled down her window and announced a monthly Sunday lunch, starting in March.

For Valentine's, Wes took his wife to the diner where he'd met his mentor every other Thursday for years. She'd never been. "The coffee's terrible," she said, and then, "I get it now."

## Bring the ham

Jonathon called his mother to tell her Easter lunch would be at his house. He'd practiced for her to be hurt. She said, "Well, I'll bring the ham." An hour later she texted his wife to make sure. Dani wrote back: "Yes. Bring the ham."

He left Dani coffee and a note on her shift morning. She wrote back on the same note: "Decaf next time. I'm trying to sleep." And on Ash Wednesday, with Dani at work, he took both kids to church by himself for the first time. Theo told him he had dirt on his face. Ruby held his hand in the parking lot.

## From the table: do I lead if she's further along?

Yes. Going first doesn't mean being the most mature one in the room. It means being willing to move first.

> "As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another."
> Proverbs 27:17 (NIV)

Two pieces of iron. Both of them move. A wife who's been carrying the faith in a house for years would rather have a man who starts badly than one who waits until he's good. When Jonathon asked whether that's why Wes and his wife don't pray together, Wes said he didn't know, and wouldn't make up an answer.

## The tool: the first apology

Go first, even if you're less wrong. Name it in their words, not yours. Then stop, and let the silence belong to them. "I'm sorry you felt" isn't an apology. It's a weather report.

## Carry this

> Ask her what she wants before you decide what she needs.
> You don't have to be the most mature man in the room to be the first one to move.
