## Reading narrative

# What Your Anger Is Guarding
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 19

Anger almost always stands in front of something, like a dog at a gate. Wes and Jonathon spend this episode on one question an old lineman used to ask. What's it guarding?

## The steering wheel

In the fall of 2009, with layoffs rumored at work, Wes hit his steering wheel and held the horn down for ten seconds at a man who changed lanes without a blinker. His seven-year-old daughter was in her booster seat. "That man's a bad driver," he told her. Years later, Mr. Abernathy tapped the table twice and asked, "What's it guarding?" The answer was his father's sentence, "Pay the bills and don't make trouble," and the fear that he couldn't.

## "I've got it"

At Saturday's practice Jonathon's old pitching shoulder was burning, and when another dad offered to throw, he snapped, "I've got it," in front of everyone. Four hours later he went cold on Ruby over a juice box. What it was guarding was his college coach's rule, "Never let them see you hurt."

## Is it just a fancy excuse?

Jonathon pushes back. "What's it guarding" can become one more "because." Wes draws the line. The answer is for you. The apology stays plain. The one piece of the why a child is owed is "It wasn't you."

## The Word

> "'In your anger do not sin': Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry, and do not give the devil a foothold."
> Ephesians 4:26-27 (NIV)

The verse assumes you'll be angry. A foothold is small, like the bump on a climbing wall that Ruby used to reach the next hold. "It doesn't take a ledge. It takes a bump."

## What they did

Jonathon apologized to the dad in the church lobby and told Dani his shoulder hurts. ("Then go to a doctor.") Wes called his daughter, now twenty-five, about the steering wheel. She remembered. "I thought it was because I was kicking your seat."
