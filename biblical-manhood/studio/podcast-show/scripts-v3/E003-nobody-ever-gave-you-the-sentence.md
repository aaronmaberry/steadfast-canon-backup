[CHAPTER: What a sentence does]
[CAM: WES MS]

# Nobody Ever Gave You the Sentence
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 3

Jonathon's four-year-old asked him in the bath, "Daddy, what's a man?" He said, "A big boy." Theo's verdict: "You're not big. You're medium."

## The receipt

About a year into their breakfasts, Mr. Abernathy slid Wes a diner receipt and told him to write what he thought a man was. Not the church answer. What he'd been doing. Wes wrote, "Pay the bills and don't make trouble." It cost him a semester with his fourteen-year-old daughter, when he left every hard talk to her mother. When Mr. Abernathy died, his daughter found thirty-some receipts in his Bible. He had prayed over every one.

On air, Wes hands Jonathon a feed-store receipt. Jonathon writes, "Win the room," and traces it to the year his father left. Then he defends it: reading people is a gift. Wes agrees. "A gift is something you use. A sentence is something that uses you."

## Loud everywhere, quiet at your table

Jonathon argues the world isn't quiet about manhood at all. Wes takes the correction. The noise is everywhere, but nobody in it knows your name. "A sentence you get from a feed is a costume. A sentence you get at a table is a fit."

## Made on purpose

> "So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them."
