## Reading narrative

# Give Your Son a Name
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 47 · June 15, 2027

On the farm road, a funeral procession came the other way. Wes's sixteen-year-old pulled all the way onto the gravel, took his cap off, and held it on his knee until the last car went by. Wes's father used to do exactly that. The boy was six when his grandfather died. He got the knee from Wes. In the truck, Wes said, "Your granddad used to do that," and gave the credit to a dead man instead of to the son sitting next to him.

## Bud

Jonathon counted for one day: "bud," thirty-four times, mostly to his four-year-old. It's his father's word, the one he's heard on twice-a-year phone calls for twenty-eight years. It fits every boy, so it costs nothing. His son still calls him "Daddy," the whole word, every time.

> "Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have summoned you by name; you are mine."
> Isaiah 43:1 (NIV)

God says it to people who had just failed Him. The name comes before the earning.

## Name what you saw

"The man of the house" was a name Jonathon got at nine, and it was a job. Wes once called his oldest "my steady one," and the boy didn't cry at his grandfather's funeral. A name that's really a job describes what you need. A name that's a mirror describes what you saw, and it still holds on the bad day.

## Spend it

Wes sat one step above his son on the porch, said his name, and told him what he'd seen. "I'm proud of the man you're turning into." His son said, "Okay." Ten seconds later he said, "Thanks." When Wes said it to his oldest on the phone, the first thing he heard back was, "Are you sick?"

## The challenge

Listen for one day to what you call him. Then say his name first, and one thing you saw, with a "what" in it.
