## Reading narrative

# Leave Without Slamming the Door
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 33

On the last of the truck Saturdays, Wes bought donuts and planned to say something on the drive. His son ate four of them and said, "These are good." That afternoon the boy took the signed agreement off the fridge, folded it into the visor of a truck that was now entirely his, and said, "I'm going to the lake with the guys." He didn't ask. Wes waved from the driveway until the truck reached the mailbox. Two honks. Then his wife called from the porch, "Wes. Come inside." He finally understood why his own mother had gone inside thirty-four years earlier without waving. "She went inside so I could leave."

## What am I for?

Jonathon drove three and a half hours to his mother's house with nothing to fix. He asked about her life before he said a word about his own. He learned about her Thursday card group, six years running, and a watercolor class she'd been taking since January. Then he asked what was bugging her. "I don't know what you need from me anymore," she said. "I just don't know what I'm for." She had been doing a job since he was nine, and so had he, and the job had ended without either of them noticing.

## For, not from

> "That is why a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh."
> Genesis 2:24 (NIV)

Adam had no parents to leave, so the verse looks forward to every son born after him, and it puts the leaving on the son. The leaving also has a direction. A man who left from his parents still talks about them all the time. A man who left for something talks about what he's building. Jonathon pushed back. Some men had to get out of dangerous houses, and they were right. Wes agreed, and named the second leaving still in front of them, from the fight. Jonathon admitted he left late. He drove home from college three nights a week, bought a house ten minutes from his mother, and once walked out of his own anniversary dinner to jump her car. Dani paid the check and asked one question. "Who's your wife, Jonathon?"

## The other mother

A divorced man at Wes's table had kept taking Sunday calls from his ex-wife's mother, the woman who had called him "son" for twenty years. When his ex asked him to step back, he drove out and told the woman in person. "I know, honey," she said. "I just didn't want to be the one." Nobody slammed anything, and it still cost.

## The challenge

Name how you left home, and make one leave-with-honor move. If you're the parent, go inside first. If you're a divorced dad, stay findable.
