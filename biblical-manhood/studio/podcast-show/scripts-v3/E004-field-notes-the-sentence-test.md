## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: The Sentence Test
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 4

Wes asked his wife what she thinks his sentence is. She didn't pause. "I'll get to it." He thought of the back-porch gutter, loose for fourteen months, the clicking fan in his son's room, and a phone call to his brother he's put off since Thanksgiving. He read the card to an eighty-one-year-old Air Force mechanic who sits behind him at church. The man showed up Saturday at 7:40, and the two of them found the board behind the gutter rotten. "That's a carpentry job, not a gutter job." It's started, not finished.

Jonathon asked Dani. She said, "Make sure everybody's okay," and then, carefully, "I married the one who checks on everybody. I'd like less of the other one." His nephew answered by text: "mine's probably don't be cringe."

## What if your sentence is a bad one?

Everybody's is. Don't hide it, and don't try to erase it. Trade it, once you have a better one. Lamentations 3:22-23 was written by a man sitting in the rubble of his city, and he says God's mercies are new every morning. "Your bad sentence isn't a diagnosis. It's a starting line."

If what you heard was cruel, and it's a pattern, that isn't a sentence test. Talk to a pastor or a counselor, and tell a friend.
