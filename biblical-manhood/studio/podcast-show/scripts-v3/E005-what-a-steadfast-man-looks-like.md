## Reading narrative

# What a Steadfast Man Looks Like
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 5

For two weeks, Wes Hart and Jonathon have asked men to write down the sentence they've actually been living. This week they give the sentence to put on the back of the card.

> A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Jonathon's first reaction is that it's all verbs. There's nothing about being tall, tough or loud. "You can't feel your way into this sentence," Wes says. "You can only do it." The first draft, written on a church bulletin, had nine items. Mr. Abernathy handed it back and said, "Say it to me without looking." Wes got to four. There's no fifth. (Jonathon tried to add "grills.")

## Four pictures

**Stop waiting.** When it's yours to do, start now, before you feel like it. James warns the man who's sure about tomorrow: "You are a mist that appears for a little while and then vanishes" (James 4:14). Wes's gutter is still hanging, and the carpenter's name is on a sticky note on his fridge.

**Pick up the weight.** Carry what's yours, even when somebody else dropped it. "For each one should carry their own load" (Galatians 6:5). Jonathon's father left when he was nine. "I don't carry his weight. I can't. But my kids are mine." As Wes puts it, "You didn't drop it. But it landed in your yard."

**Go first.** Step out ahead of your people, scared or not. The shepherd in John 10:4 goes on ahead of the sheep, and they follow because they know his voice. Jonathon worries that men will hear "boss." Wes answers that going first means going first into the cost. "The shepherd walks in front because the front is where the wolf is."

**Work for God's pay.** Do it for the Lord's reward, not the room's applause (Colossians 3:23-24). Jonathon asks whether "pay" means earning salvation. It doesn't. Salvation is grace, a gift no one can boast about. The pay is an inheritance. "You don't earn an inheritance. You get it because of whose kid you are."

## The fake versions

Every mark has a counterfeit that a good man falls into when he's trying hard. Hurry looks like stopping waiting. Carrying everybody's pack looks like picking up the weight. Bossing looks like going first. And grim, scorekeeping religion looks like working for God's pay. Wes's test for all four: "Is it costing me, and is it free?"

## Where the sentence comes from

> "In your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus: ..."
> Philippians 2:5 (NIV)

Paul goes on to describe Jesus, who made himself nothing, took the nature of a servant, and obeyed all the way to the cross. He didn't wait for us to improve. He carried what wasn't his. He went ahead into the worst of it. And he wasn't working for the crowd that yelled "crucify." Jonathon notices the first words: "with one another." Every mark is done toward somebody.

## Lists don't change people

Jonathon has sat through plenty of sales trainings with acronyms, and none changed him. Wes agrees, and says this isn't a technique. It's a mirror, and a mirror works better with a brother. Most nights a man will score one out of four. Jonathon also notes that his wife does all four better than he does. Wes says that's true, and the mindset is for everyone. But men tend to fail in a particular way: "absent while standing in the room."

## Carry this

> The room's pay is gone by tomorrow. God's pay keeps.
> The marks aren't four rules. They're what Jesus looks like on a man on a Tuesday.
