## Reading narrative

# Give Your Son a Hill
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 49

Every winter Saturday from the time Wes was twelve, his father took him to his granddad's place to cut cedar. His father believed every tree drank thirty gallons of water a day. He never said a word about character. He pointed at the creek that went dry every August and said, "Every one you cut gives some back." The year Wes was fifteen they cleared the whole south slope, and the next summer the creek ran into July. On the Fourth his father walked him down to look at running water and didn't say anything.

His father was on the chainsaw out in front every Saturday. Wes was behind him with the loppers. The hill was his father's before it was his.

## The shoes

Jonathon had always put his four-year-old's shoes on for him: forty seconds, done, in the car. Thursday he left fifteen minutes early and let Theo do it himself. It took nine minutes. Around the fifth minute, Jonathon noticed his keys in his hand. The last time his own father stood in their kitchen, he was holding his keys. Jonathon put his keys in his pocket and sat down on the floor.

## A butter knife

At ten, the "man of the house," Jonathon went after a jammed garbage disposal with a butter knife, the switch still up. Nobody had ever shown him the reset button underneath. A boy handed a hill with nobody on it finds one he can see the top of. For Jonathon that became the scoreboard.

> "For you know that we dealt with each of you as a father deals with his own children, encouraging, comforting and urging you to live lives worthy of God, who calls you into his kingdom and glory."
> 1 Thessalonians 2:11-12 (NIV)

Paul was in Thessalonica only a short while before he was sent away by night. He kept writing, and he sent Timothy back. The father in this verse points each child at the biggest hill there is.

## Not "don't rescue him"

Wes had always said a boy needs a father who won't rescue him. Jonathon pushed back: a boy with no rescuer doesn't learn he's strong; he learns he's alone. Then he described Lorraine, his first sales manager, who rode in his passenger seat for a month and never took over a call. Wes changed his mind. His father was ten feet away every time he hollered. He just didn't come and get him out. Be there, and don't take over.
