## Reading narrative

# These Men Are My Brothers
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 41 · May 25, 2027

A retired band director at Wes's Thursday table never missed. Then he missed three Thursdays running. The first Thursday someone guessed grandkids. The second, someone asked if he was still out. By the third nobody said anything; the chair was just a chair. On the fourth Thursday he came in with the good coffee and told them his wife was on her third round of chemo. "I kept waiting for one of you to call."

## Notice the chair

Six men had his number. Wes asked him what he wanted, which is a different question from what they could do, and the answer was small: "Could y'all come to my kitchen?" For six weeks the table met at his house. His wife came down in a headscarf to tell them they were too loud. She's two years clear now. Since then, if a man misses, whoever notices first calls him by Friday noon.

> "But encourage one another daily, as long as it is called 'Today,' so that none of you may be hardened by sin's deceitfulness."
> Hebrews 3:13 (NIV)

Daily, not at the annual retreat. Today is an ordinary word. Today is a Thursday. And hardening happens alone, slowly, the way caulk goes stiff without anyone noticing.

## Two plates

At a work meeting in Austin, Jonathon walked past a rival sitting alone in his running truck at lunch. Then he went back out with two plates of fajitas. After ten quiet minutes the man said his wife had moved out in March, and that he makes his kids pancakes on Saturday mornings. "That's the whole thing I'm holding onto."

Jonathon also asked his father-in-law, married fifty-one years, how he stayed. "I don't know, son. We just didn't leave." Later, carrying a patio umbrella base, the older man mentioned that his wife's brother called him every Thursday night during his worst years to talk about the Rangers.

## The floor isn't the house

Jonathon's objection: his father left when he was nine, so "don't leave" has been his whole plan, and he's spent ten years of Sunday lunches on the couch, in the house and gone. Staying in the building isn't staying. Wes agreed, and pointed back to the porch. "Don't leave" by itself is the floor. "Don't leave" plus a man who keeps calling on Thursdays is something else.

## The challenge

Write two names. On an ordinary day, call one of them, with no reason. If he says "Fine," ask once more.
