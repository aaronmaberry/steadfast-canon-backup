## Reading narrative

# Somebody Has to Step Out Ahead
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 11 · February 9, 2027

In most stuck places, a marriage, a family, a friendship, everybody is waiting for somebody else to move. The third of the four marks is go first, and in this episode Wes Hart and Jonathon ask what it actually means and what it costs.

## Decaf means a meeting

Jonathon and Dani have been circling the same fight since October: his mother and the holidays. Sunday night, after the kids were down, he made two cups of decaf and opened with a sentence he'd practiced in traffic: "I've been making you pay for my mom." Dani said, "Keep going." Then: "I thought you were going to make me be the bad guy again." Every year she had said the hard thing, and every year he got to stay the good son. That was the cost of going first. They ended with a plan: Easter lunch at their house, his mother invited, and Jonathon is the one who tells her.

Wes named his own version. At Thanksgiving he and his brother argued across the table about their mother, and Wes left early. He called it keeping the peace. "Keeping the peace is when you keep the thing from getting settled so nobody's mad at you."

## The stop sign

Wes yelled at his sixteen-year-old over a rolling stop, in a voice he recognized as his father's. Four days later, on a forty-minute drive, he tapped the boy's arm: "The yelling was wrong. The stop sign wasn't." The boy said "Okay." Twenty minutes later, at the cattle guard, he said, "Sorry about the stop sign," and got out of the truck.

## Who finds the snake

> "When he has brought out all his own, he goes on ahead of them, and his sheep follow him because they know his voice."
> John 10:4 (NIV)

Jesus aimed this picture at leaders who had just thrown a healed man out of the synagogue. A shepherd in that country walked out front. Wes's version: when his kids were little and they walked through tall grass, he went first, because somebody's going to find the snake and it ought to be the man with boots on. A few verses later the good shepherd lays down his life, and the hired hand runs. Jesus didn't just tell us to go first. He went first, into death and out the other side.

## The cap on the chair

Seven years ago a customer told Jonathon, "Men's breakfast Thursday. I'll save you a seat." Jonathon sat in the parking lot for eleven minutes. When he walked in, the man's cap was on the chair next to him. Three months later Jonathon sent Wes "the scariest text I ever sent a man." Wes answered in under a minute. "When somebody goes first toward you, you make it easy to have done it."

## Isn't this just "I decide"?

Jonathon grew up around a father who moved his family four times and called it leading. Wes's test: did he ever go first into anything hard for himself, like the apology, or only into decisions? "The bossy guy goes first into the decision. The shepherd goes first into the cost."

For a cold marriage, Wes doesn't start with a big night. After his fog years, he started making the coffee at 5:15. On the eleventh day his wife asked why, and cried into the cup. And at eighty-one, the man who held Wes's ladder started a car-care Saturday for single moms. "Nobody was doing it, so I figured somebody ought to start."

## Two chairs

Did Sunday count, if Dani had asked five times? Wes says yes. Jonathon wants to be the husband who sees it before she has to ask, because the asking is a weight too. They leave it there.

## What they actually did

Before his mother's appointment, Wes called and asked what she wants. "Nobody's asked me that." She wants to stay in her house and keep driving herself to church. His plan for the doctor's office: "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me." His brother doesn't know. Jonathon scheduled a call with his own mother for Thursday. Her reply: "Is everything ok??"
