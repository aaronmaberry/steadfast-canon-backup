## Reading narrative

# The Part That's Mine
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 15 · February 23, 2027

For six weeks the show has talked about what gets handed to a man: his father, the sentence written on him, the men who were there and the ones who weren't. At the start of Lent, Wes and Jonathon add the part that makes the rest honest. Some of the weight with your name on it is weight you picked up yourself.

## The district deal

In 2019 Jonathon was a senior rep in commercial HVAC. A twenty-three-year-old rookie spent six months working a school district that was replacing the rooftop units on forty-one schools. Jonathon's manager sent him to the last meeting to make sure the deal closed. On the drive back, Jonathon typed his own name in the "lead rep" field. That year he made President's Club, and he and Dani went to Cancun. The rookie never said a word. Today he's thirty-one and works for Jonathon.

For years Jonathon had an explanation ready. His father left when he was nine. Then he thought about his sister: same father, same house, and she would drive back to return a nickel. "The dad thing explains why winning matters so much to me. It doesn't explain why I was willing to take it from a twenty-three-year-old. That part's just me."

## The loss report

When a sales rep loses a deal, he fills out a loss report with a dropdown: price, timing, competitor. Jonathon has read about a thousand of them, and no rep has ever picked himself. Every option is a little bit true, which is what makes it easy. Wes: the part that's yours is the only part you can work on.

## The headache

In December 2010, during what Wes calls the fog years, his five-year-old son was a shepherd in the kindergarten Christmas program. Wes told his wife he had a headache and stayed home to watch a Monday night game. For years he blamed the fog on his father, who hardly came to anything. "But my dad never told anybody he had a headache. I came up with that myself." When he finally told his wife, she said she had known all along. She could hear the game from the driveway. "Did they win?" "They lost." "Good."

## The last sentence

Whoever is in the last sentence of the story you tell about yourself is the one you think is responsible. An excuse ends with someone else. "I'm the worst" is another way off the hook. Owning it is plainer: what you did, that you're sorry, and what you'll do next.

## The Word

> "For I know that good itself does not dwell in me, that is, in my sinful nature. For I have the desire to do what is good, but I cannot carry it out."
> Romans 7:18 (NIV)

Paul had about the cleanest religious upbringing a man could have, and he still located the problem inside himself. A few verses later he asks who will rescue him, and he answers: Jesus Christ our Lord. "You can't be forgiven for something your father did. You can only be forgiven for what you did."

## What they did

Jonathon took the rep to lunch and said it before the chips arrived: "The district deal in 2019 was yours. I took it. I'm sorry." The rep said, "I know," and then, "You owe me Cancun." On Thursday Jonathon plans to tell his regional VP. Wes asked whether the rep should get a say first; Jonathon thinks the record is his to fix. Wes called his son, now twenty-one, and told him about the headache. The son didn't remember. He asked, "Was I a good shepherd?" Wes said, "I don't know, son. I wasn't there."

## The challenge

Pick one thing you did that you usually explain, and tell it to one person with no "because." If it's the kind of thing that could change a marriage, talk to a pastor or counselor first and get help doing it right. If what you carry is what someone did to you, that one is theirs.
