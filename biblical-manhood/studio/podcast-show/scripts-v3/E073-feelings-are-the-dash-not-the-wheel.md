# Feelings Are the Dash, Not the Wheel
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 73

On a Friday night Wes's sixteen-year-old came in the back door with a red face and went for the truck keys. "Just driving," he said. His girlfriend had broken up with him by text. Wes said, "You can be as mad as you want. You can be mad all night. You don't drive mad. I'll drive. You ride." His son set the keys on the table, not in his hand. They took the farm road the long way with the windows down. Twenty minutes in, his son said, "I really liked her."

Jonathon was that boy once, at seventeen, on the highway to Galveston near midnight, and nobody was home to take the keys.

A feeling is a true reading of what's going on inside you, like the lights on a dash. Cover it up and it drives from the back seat. Hand it the keys and it drives you into the ditch. Read it, name it, tell a man, and keep your hands on the wheel.

> "Why, my soul, are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me? Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise him, my Savior and my God."
> Psalm 42:5 (NIV)

The man who wrote it led worship for a living, and he was far from the house of God, crying at supper, with people asking where his God was. He doesn't pretend. He asks his own soul why. Then he tells it where to put its hope. He has to say it three times before the song is over.

## Cardboard with better manners

Jonathon pushed back. "Do it anyway" with no reading is how a man ends up gone one Monday with no warning. Read it, say it to a man, and then do the true thing anyway.

## The challenge

Once a day, name what you feel. Ask if it's driving anything. Do the true thing anyway, and tell one man your word.
