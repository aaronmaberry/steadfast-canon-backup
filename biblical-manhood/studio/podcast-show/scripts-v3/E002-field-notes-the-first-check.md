## Reading narrative

# Field Notes: The First Check
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 2

On Tuesday, Wes and Jonathon gave themselves and their listeners a small challenge. One meal with no phone, a real question at the table, and one text to one man: "How's your Tuesday?" Friday is where they report back.

## Check the box

Wes asked his wife what Tuesday is like for her. "You've never asked me that," she said, and then told him she drives his mother to physical therapy every week, forty minutes each way, and has since August. His text to his oldest son got an answer that was funny and then wasn't: "Are you dying?" Every text Wes had ever sent him had an errand in it. His son called back and talked for twenty minutes about a decision he hadn't told anyone about.

Jonathon didn't check the box the first night. The phone buzzed in the junk drawer, he got up, and his seven-year-old said, "Daddy, you said." He tried again on Thursday and offered his wife blackout curtains they already own. Then he stopped fixing and listened.

The rule: the second try counts. And there's good fixing and bad fixing. Bad fixing ends the listening. Good fixing asks first.

## From the table

How do you start if you've never been the spiritual one at home? Start where nobody's grading, and keep it too small to be bad at. Pray one sentence out loud at one meal. Tell your wife, "I want to grow in this. Will you help me?" Read one book of the Bible on your own (Mark is short) and tell her one thing you read.

## Tool on the table: the phone basket

Put a basket where everyone walks in, not on the table. Every phone goes in at supper, starting with yours. Keep a charging cord inside. Set the one number that truly matters to ring loud, and let the rest wait.

## Carry this

> The second try counts. Don't let the first one be the only one.
> Somebody at your house is carrying a Tuesday you don't know about. Ask.
