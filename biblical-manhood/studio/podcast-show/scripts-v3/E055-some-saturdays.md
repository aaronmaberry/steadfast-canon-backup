## Reading narrative

# Some Saturdays
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 55

At supper during Vacation Bible School, in the middle of the hand motions to a song about fire, Jonathon's daughter asked him if he was on fire for Jesus. "Some days." "Which days?"

His first year of faith was all fire: crying in the church parking lot, Matthew to Revelation in two months, a conference in Dallas. Then his daughter was born and it went flat for a year. He kept going because his wife kept getting up on Sundays and a man at a breakfast kept texting, "You coming Thursday?" One night at bath time he realized he'd been home for bath every night for a month. "The flat year was when it took."

Wes had the opposite: a decade when everybody at church said he was on fire. Three committees, a ball team, a class. Then one night in the truck his wife asked, "Where are we going? You and me." He offered her Saturday. "I'll burn down my own house to feel warm." Jonathon pushed back: he came to faith through the feeling, and he doesn't want his daughter to learn that feeling God is suspicious. Wes agreed. The feeling isn't the problem. Making it the boss is.

> "And he died for all, that those who live should no longer live for themselves but for him who died for them and was raised again."
> 2 Corinthians 5:15 (NIV)

There's no feeling in that verse, only facts: He died, He was raised, and you live for Him. The fire is what He did.

## Some Saturdays

Virgil is seventy-seven. He has fixed widows' porches every Saturday since his wife died in 1998, and he keeps every job in a pencil notebook in his truck. Jonathon asked him if he likes it. "Some Saturdays."

## The challenge

Name one thing God has put in front of you that you've been waiting to feel like doing. Do it anyway.
