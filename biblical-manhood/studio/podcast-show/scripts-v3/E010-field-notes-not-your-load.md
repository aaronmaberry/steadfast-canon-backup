Tuesday's challenge had two halves: pick up one weight with your name on it, and name one thing you're carrying that isn't yours. Wes and Jonathon report.

## The knees and the birthdays

Wes's knees have arthritis, the right one worse. No surgery, physical therapy twice a week, at the same clinic as his mother, on the same Tuesday. She counts his reps out loud. In the truck after the appointment, alone, he said, "It's not Dad."

When he asked his wife what she carries that's really his, she didn't hesitate: "Your family's birthdays." For twenty-seven years she has bought the cards, signed his name and reminded him to call. Wednesday night they put every birthday into his phone. His "not mine" was his oldest son's job decision, and his first step was three words on paper: "His job. His."

## The kids, the spreadsheet, and the phone calls

Jonathon and Dani spent two hours at the kitchen table deciding who would raise their children. They chose her parents for now, his sister as backup, and agreed to revisit it in five years. Dani went to bed, then came back out to say "Thank you." They still disagree about one thing, whether to ask her parents before signing. Jonathon thinks you ask first. Dani knows her mother. They're doing it her way.

When Jonathon asked Dani what she carries that's really his, she said: "Your mom. I'm the one who calls her back." On Tuesday he'd learned that his mother's happiness isn't his. Calling her back is. "You carried her feelings and dropped her phone calls," Wes said. Thursday night Jonathon called his mom himself.

## From the table: what if I'm carrying what isn't mine?

Wes watches for three signs: resentment, exhaustion with no progress, and control. Setting a weight down isn't throwing it. Every weight belongs to somebody, so you hand it back to that person or hand it up to God.

> "Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you."
> 1 Peter 5:7 (NIV)

Jonathon pushed: doesn't "hand it up" sometimes mean "ignore it"? Wes agreed it can. You hand up what you can't carry. You still carry what you can.

## The tool: two columns

Draw a line down a page. "Mine" on the left, "Not mine" on the right. Write down everything you're carrying. Next to each item on the right, write a name, and either say the sentence to that person or pray it by name. Next to each item on the left, write one next step. Then read the right side to one man.

## Carry this

> If it's not yours, it still belongs to somebody. Hand it back, or hand it up.
> Sometimes you're so busy with the part that isn't yours, you drop the part that is.
