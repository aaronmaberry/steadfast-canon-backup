## 2. What the show is

**Promise.** Two men at a table going deeper into what the Steadfast Men book and the 24 training sessions cover. They tell their own stories, disagree honestly, and say what they actually did that week. Each week there's one challenge, and on Friday they report back honestly, including when they failed.

**Who it's for.** Men in every season: the 22-year-old, the new dad, the man twenty years into his marriage, the single man, the divorced dad, the man over 60. It's especially for the man who has never sat at a men's table and isn't sure he'd fit.

**What it isn't.** It isn't a sermon with two microphones, a study guide read out loud, an "alpha" podcast, a news show, or a sales channel. It also never replays the training sessions. It sends men to them.

**Why it can compete.**
- The two hosts have a real dynamic: an older man and a younger man who started late, and they don't agree on everything.
- There's a weekly challenge with accountability on air, which the top shows don't offer in this lane.
- Every episode is tight, 25-35 minutes, because the edit cuts the dead air.
- The look is TV-grade on a TV screen, which is where YouTube podcasts are now watched (research dossier, section 1).

## 4. Formats

### 4a. Main episode (Tuesday, 25-35 minutes, about 3,600-5,000 spoken words)

| Clock | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:15 | **Cold open** (optional) | Very short: 15 seconds at most (about 40 spoken words). The best exchange of the episode as a clip, or a fresh one-liner that pays off the title. Then a 3-second title sting (logo, two notes, no voice). |
| 0:15-0:50 | **Standard intro and slate** | The standard intro (section 5): who Wes and Jonathon are and what the show and Steadfast are, about 25-30 seconds. Then the episode number, release date and title. No banter. |
| 0:50-2:30 | **Pull up a chair** | The welcome line, under 45 seconds of banter, then the question of the day said out loud. The focus mark is named plainly if the episode has one (canon). |
| 2:30-9:00 | **First story** | One host's real story on the topic. The other asks, "Then what?" The verse comes in here as a card. |
| about 9:00-12:00 | **The Word** | The passage, read once and plainly, with the context in two or three sentences. Then what it means at a kitchen table. |
| after the first payoff, about 40% in | **Stop and subscribe** | One line, varied (section 8b). |
| 12:00-20:00 | **Pushback** | Jonathon raises the objection the viewer is thinking ("That works if..."). They work it through. This segment is the main source of Shorts. |
| 20:00-26:00 | **What I actually did** | Each man says something concrete he did, or didn't do. No "you should." Only "I did." |
| 26:00-29:00 | **This week's challenge** | One specific challenge, with a single-man version and a married-man version when it's a household move. It gets checked on Friday. |
| 29:00-30:30 | **Carry This** | One line each, said to the viewer. |
| last 25-35 seconds | **Standing outro** | Section 9. The end screen sits over the last 20 seconds. |

The timings are for planning. The story decides the real clock. Plugs from the rotation (section 8) go in where they fit naturally: never in the first five minutes, and never inside the Word.

### 4b. Field Notes (Friday, 12-16 minutes, about 1,900-2,500 words)

| Clock | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:15 | Cold open (optional) | Very short, 15 seconds at most. A one-liner from the Check the Box segment, usually a confession. Then the sting. |
| 0:15-0:50 | Standard intro and slate | The same standard intro as Tuesdays (section 5), then the number, date, "Field Notes" and the title. |
| 0:30-6:00 | **Check the Box** | Did each man do Tuesday's challenge? Honest report, in detail, including failures. Jonathon checks Wes and Wes checks Jonathon. (The model is Armchair Expert's Fact Check: the accountability between the hosts is the draw.) |
| 6:00-11:00 | **From the Table** | One question from a listener, answered straight. Before real listener mail exists, these are questions men commonly ask at men's tables, and the show labels them honestly ("a question I get a lot"). It never invents a listener's name or city. |
| 11:00-13:00 | **Tool on the Table** | One practical tool: a prayer pattern, a text to send, a calendar trick, a question to ask your wife. |
| 13:00-14:00 | Carry This plus a tease of Tuesday | |
| last 25 seconds | Standing outro (short variant) | |

There's no mid-episode subscribe line on Fridays.

## 5. Opening ritual

**Standard intro** (Aaron, 2026-10-03 10:35 AM CT: every episode opens with the same short intro, close to identical each week). It says who the two men are and what the show and Steadfast are. **Rotation.** By episode number: E001 A (launch wording), E002 B, E003 C, E004 A, and so on (the variant is A, B, C for episode number minus one, mod 3). The words don't change between uses. Only the slate changes.

**The slate** (Aaron's earlier rule, now folded in) follows the intro straight away, with no "Walk Steadfast" (the intro already said it) and no year after E001:

**Script markup.** `[CHAPTER: Intro]`, then `[CAM: 2S]`, then the stage note `(Standard intro, variant X.)`, then the intro lines with `[LOWER THIRD: Wes Hart]` and `[LOWER THIRD: Jonathon]` after each man's first line, then the three slate lines. **Cold open.** This is optional and very short: 15 seconds at most (about 40 spoken words). It's a clip from later in the episode or one fresh line that pays off the title, and always the strongest exchange. It comes before the intro. If it can't be cut to 15 seconds, drop it.

**Title sting.** Three seconds: the navy card with the gold wordmark and a two-note acoustic figure. No voiceover.

**Welcome line** (opens Pull up a chair right after the slate; varied every episode, never "Welcome back to the show"):

- JONATHON: "Coffee's hot. Mine is, anyway."

## 7. Delivery and script conventions

**Speakers.** Lines are written as `WES:` and `JONATHON:`. Every spoken line belongs to one man.

**Pauses are edit marks and are never spoken.** These are the same conventions as the training scripts and the YouTube guide.
- `[beat]` is about 0.6 s. `[pause 1s]` and `[pause 2s]` are for the heaviest lines only, at most three per episode.
- Normal gaps between lines and speakers come from the edit, like `retime.py` does for the sessions: about 0.15-0.35 s on a quick exchange, 0.5-0.8 s at a sentence end, 1.0-1.3 s at a turn in the topic.
- Mid-line ellipses (`...`) give an in-take hesitation, at most 4 per script. Never in scripture.
- No "uh", "um", "eh" or stammer spellings. A man correcting himself does it in words ("Well, not mad. Embarrassed.").
- No whisper or quiet tags and no `[laughs]` audio tag. Laughter is a picture direction such as `(both laugh)`. Its audio is handled in the edit: a short real-laugh take or room tone under the picture. That gets tested before any episode is locked.

**Balance and audience rules (focus groups, rounds 1-2).**
- Wes carries no more than about 60% of the spoken words, averaged across a season. A single episode can run to about 67% when it's built on Wes's story (E007), and the next one should swing back. Jonathon reads a verse card in about one episode in three.
- Every silent visual beat (writing, a prop reveal, a long look) gets one spoken cue, so audio-only listeners don't hit dead air.
- Single-man and married-man challenge versions are equally concrete. When a challenge depends on a wife or kids, add a line for the man who lives alone and for the divorced dad.
- Jokes framing a wife's honest answer as a threat ("Brace.") appear at most once a season.
- Don't promise "we read every comment" unless someone actually does. Until Aaron assigns someone, no outro promises reading (E001 line removed, round 3).
