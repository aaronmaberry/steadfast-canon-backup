## 1. Verdict on the open system

The open system in SHOW-BIBLE section 5 is right in its bones. It has fixed beats and new words each week, and two short name shapes that never move. The hook goes last and points at the question. The research file's own table supports the shape. The shows that feel alive (Armchair Expert, My Brother, My Brother and Me, Ferriss) keep a fixed frame with a slot for change. The shows that stay word for word (Huberman, The Daily) get away with it because they run under 15 seconds. This open runs about 22 seconds on mains, so word for word would wear on a man who hears it twice a week. Aaron's call to retire the fixed intro was correct.

### The anchor sentence

Keep "This is Walk Steadfast." at the top of every open, said by whoever leads.

- It costs four words, about a second and a half.
- On audio there's no logo and no lower third. This sentence is the only thing that tells a man in a truck which show he's hearing. That matters most on the feed, which is where a twice-a-week show gets its habit listeners.
- The alternative, no fixed sentence at all, means 104 different first lines. That is the problem Grok's critique of draft 1 named ("80 intros unanchored"), and I agree with it there.
- The seasonal tag ("This is Walk Steadfast, on Good Friday.") is fine on holidays, premieres and season reviews, as now. Keep it off ordinary weeks so the plain sentence stays plain.

### The lengths

Keep the windows as they are. That's 42-62 words on mains, 32-52 on Field Notes and 60-75 on E001. Don't adopt Grok's 40-55 cap or the lean to a 40-word floor by midyear.

- **The beats cost about 38 words before any fact.** That's the anchor and the two names (9), a mission or report clause (5-8), the offer (10-14) and a hook (9-12). A 40-word floor leaves one or two words for two facts, and the facts are what make each open worth hearing.
- **The set already sits inside the research band.** On the script clock (Wes 145 wpm, Jonathon 170 wpm), mains average 22.0 seconds (range 17.7-24.3) and Field Notes 18.7 seconds. The research file's own target is 18-25 seconds for the sign-on plus the hook.
- **The real time sink is the cold open.** 34 of 80 cold opens run past 15 seconds. Pull up a chair starts at a median of 0:45 on mains and 0:38 on Field Notes, so the hook, which names the topic, is already said inside the 0:50 gate Grok proposed. Cutting cold opens to 40 words saves more time than cutting opens to 40 words, and it costs nothing a man would miss.
- **Do update the bible's stated averages.** After this pass the set averages 56.2 words on mains and 48.9 on Field Notes, not 57 and 48.

### The offer line every episode

Keep it, in one line, in every open. Aaron asked for an open that hits the same points every episode, and the book, the training and the site are three of those points. The open and the mid-episode plug do different jobs.

- **The open names them.** One line, about 5-7 seconds, with no pitch and nothing about what's inside. It's a name check, the way a show credits its sponsor.
- **The mid plug sells them.** It describes the chapter or the session in under 20 seconds, and the calendar's spacing rule governs it (section 8a sets about one main in three for the book and one in four for the training, and never both in one episode except E001 and E103). The open doesn't touch that rule, and it shouldn't carry chapter talk.

The research cuts against this in one place, and it should be said plainly. The Podcast Host found that self-promotion in the intro tends to run long, and that calls to action work best mid or late. The answer is to keep the offer short and never put it last. The hook always comes after it, so the last thing a man hears before the slate is the question, not a URL. The Spotify figure in the research file (a one-minute intro losing about 10%) is one example from a help page, not a study. It supports short intros in general, and it isn't evidence about a six-second offer line.

Three things around the offer are wrong today.

1. **E001 contradicts it on mic.** In the launch walkthrough (E001 script line 398), Wes says, "After today, you'll hear about the book or the training every now and then, when it actually fits what we're talking about. Not every week. That's a promise." Every open after E001 names both. A listener who took him at his word hears the promise broken on Friday. Section 2 gives the replacement line, and it has to go in the same recording session as the new open.
2. **The calendar's plug key still says "Say 'coming' until launch dates are set"** (CALENDAR-104 line 33). That conflicts with the locked tense rule, and it's why "coming" is still in E001 and E079 (section 5 lists the lines). Delete the line from the key.
3. **Four fresh-topic episodes carry B slots** (E081, E093, E095, E099), but the calendar's own note says fresh topics "carry no book plug unless the chapter link is real." Each needs a named chapter or a "-". The real links are chapter 32, Every season still counts, for E081 (or move the slot, since a book plug sits badly on the depression episode with 988 in it), chapter 6, Go first, for E093, chapter 4, Stop waiting, for E095, and chapter 30, You were made for a work, for E099.

### What else I'd change

- **Lint the cold open against the open.** The rule that a fact never retells the cold open is the one most often broken. Sixteen of the 27 Fix rows in section 3 are a fact or hook that retells the cold open or Check the Box's first line. A simple check would catch most of them. Flag any open line that shares three or more content words with the cold open or the first line after the slate.
- **Measure the hook on the whole last line.** `hook()` in `intro_lint.py` takes the last line that contains "today" and keeps only the text from "today" to the next period. A hook like "A man's mom calls daily, and today he asks if that's a problem." is checked as "today he asks if that's a problem." The 14-word cap and the three-word stem rule both see only the tail. Measure the whole line.
- **Fix the lint docstring.** It says Steadfast is required in the offer, and the code doesn't check for it. Nothing needs it, so delete the claim rather than add the check.
- **Shape by phase is right.** Five lines while the men are new, then four from E025 on mains and E010 on Field Notes. Keep it.
