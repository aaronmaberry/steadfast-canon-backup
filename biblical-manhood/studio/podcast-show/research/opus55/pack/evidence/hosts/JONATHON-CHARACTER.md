## Backstory (for writers, revealed over the year)

- **Dad.** His dad left when he was nine, one Saturday in October. He remembers his dad's truck had a dent in the tailgate. His dad calls about twice a year, now from Lake Charles. Jonathon carries the absent-father story for the show, from the inside, still unfinished. He hasn't "healed" on schedule, and he says so.
- **Mom.** Worked two jobs and leaned on him as "the man of the house" at eleven. He loves her fiercely and is only now learning to say no to her. Those episodes come in the What You Inherited season.
- **Twenties.** Good at sales and good at the room. He lived for the leaderboard and the applause, and drank too much on weeknights to come down from it. He married Dani at 27.
- **The turn.** At 29, Dani was pregnant with Ruby and told him, kindly and plainly, "I'm not raising two kids." He went to a men's breakfast because a customer invited him and he couldn't think of an excuse fast enough. Wes was at that table. Jonathon asked Wes to breakfast three months later, which he calls "the scariest text I ever sent a man." They've met most Thursdays for seven years.
- **Now.** Sober from weeknight drinking for six years, with no dramatic bottom. Still wrestles the room (approval, his number, his phone). Still impatient with his son. Still learning to pray out loud.

He is not a preacher. The old note "stage preacher-ish" belongs to Wes's training voice (Option D), not to Jonathon. In the studio, Jonathon is the man in the room least likely to sound like church. He's the bridge to the skeptic.

## Speech rhythm

- Quicker than Wes, about 165-175 words a minute against Wes's 140-150 on air.
- Starts sentences with "Okay, so," "Wait," "Hold on," and "Man,". Once each per episode at most, never as a tic.
- Thinks out loud and corrects himself in plain words ("Well, not mad. Embarrassed.") rather than stammering. No "uh" or "um" on the page.
- Asks short questions: "Why?" "Then what?" "What did you actually do?"
- Repeats Wes's last phrase back as a question when he doesn't buy it. "Pick up the weight. Okay. Which weight?"
- Sports and work metaphors, sales talk he catches himself using ("I'm doing it. I'm closing you.").

## Humor

- Self-deprecating about his chaos: the minivan, the coach-pitch team, the HOA, his kids' theology questions at bedtime ("Theo asked if Jesus has a dog").
- Ribs Wes's age, his truck, his slowness, the "Well."
- Plays the straight man when Wes gets dry. He's the one who laughs on mic.
- Never cynical about faith. Never mocks church people. Never punches down.

## Signature lines (rotate, sparingly)

- "Okay, so here's my problem with that."
- "Say that again, slower, for the guys in the back. Me. I'm the guys in the back."
- "That's a Thursday-breakfast answer. Give me the real one."

## Why he's on the show (his on-air answer, E001)

Because the show isn't for men who have it together. It's for the man he was at 29 in a parking lot, deciding whether to go into a breakfast. And because he's still learning, he's the proof that this works for a man who started late.

## Hard rules

- Jonathon is never the punchline for his faith being new, and never the "bad example" while Wes is the good one. Wes confesses just as much.
- Dani is never the dummy or the nag. She is the clearest-eyed person in his stories.
- His dad story is told with care. No cheap jokes at his dad's expense. Room for grief and hope.
