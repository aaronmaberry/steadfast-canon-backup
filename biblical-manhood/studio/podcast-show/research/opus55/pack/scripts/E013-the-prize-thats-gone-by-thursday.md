## Reading narrative

# The Prize That's Gone by Thursday
### Walk Steadfast, Episode 13 · February 16, 2027

Every man is working for something under the paycheck. This week Wes Hart and Jonathon take the last of the four marks: work for God's pay, not the room's.

## The Monday email

Every Monday at 8:00 a.m., Jonathon's company sends a leaderboard ranking every sales manager in the country. For eleven years he has waited for it in his truck in the driveway, refreshing. In the top five, he walks into the house "like a king." Otherwise he's short with everyone until Wednesday, and no one at home knew why. The board resets every week. By Thursday the top spot is sliding, and by Friday the feeling is gone. "That's a lot of my kids' breakfasts that depended on a spreadsheet in Ohio."

## The coffee told on him

Wes's cheap prize is at church. For years he has been the man they call on to pray in public, and he loves the walk to the microphone and the hand on his shoulder afterward. "Wes is a rock." He didn't see a problem until he was in aisle seven, buying coffee for a church car-care day for single mothers, hoping someone would notice he'd bought the good coffee. "If I want credit for the coffee, I definitely want credit for the microphone."

## Whatever you do

> "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving."
> Colossians 3:23-24 (NIV)

Paul wrote this partly to servants in other people's households, people nobody thanked or ranked. Jonathon gets stuck on the word "reward." Doesn't that sound like earning your way in? Wes is clear. Salvation is a gift of grace through faith, not works (Ephesians 2:8-9). An inheritance isn't a paycheck; it comes because of whose family you're in. "The email ranks you. Your Father already named you."

> "fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith. For the joy set before him he endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God."
> Hebrews 12:2 (NIV)

At the cross, the room's pay was shame. Jesus worked for a joy the room couldn't see.

## Pushback

Isn't God's reward a bribe? A bribe is something you'd take instead of the person. Here the reward is the person, the "well done" from the One who already loves you. What about the boss who signs the check? Work harder for him, Paul says. But watch what you do at four o'clock on a Friday, when nobody's looking. And when Jonathon says he has never heard "well done" from a father and doesn't know what it should feel like, Wes doesn't try to fix it. That conversation belongs to Easter.

For the sixty-year-old who has already made his money, Jonathon's contractor friend has an answer. He's leaving forty years of earning to teach young welders: "I'd like to do something for a while that nobody pays me much for."

## The receipts

After Mr. Abernathy died, his daughter found thirty-some diner receipts in his Bible, each with a man's bad sentence on the front. Wes had never mentioned what was on the back: each man's first name and a column of dates. They were the days Mr. Abernathy had prayed for him. Wes's went back nine years, and the old man never said a word about it. "I still don't know what to do with that. I just know that's the kind of work I want to be doing when I'm eighty-eight. The kind with no microphone."

## What they actually did

When the church asked Wes to pray from the front on Sunday, he asked his twenty-seven-year-old son-in-law instead, and watched from the third row as other people put a hand on the young man's shoulder. Wes donated the coffee anonymously (and then told everyone on the show, which he admits was a failure). He now keeps a short list of men's names on the back of his index card and prays for them by name. He won't say who's on it. Jonathon set his leaderboard email to arrive at 10 a.m., after his kids are at school. This week, coming in at number six cost him four minutes instead of three days. He also spent an hour after work, unrecorded, helping a struggling rep with his call list. At nine that night the rep texted: "Booked two."

## This week's challenge

Name your cheap prize out loud to one man. Then do one piece of good work no one will see, and tell no one. Men who live alone can write three names on a card and pray for them daily. Divorced dads can stop keeping score with the kids. Men who've made their money can ask what they'd do for a while that nobody pays them much for.

## Carry this

> The room pays by the week. His "well done" doesn't reset on Monday.
> Do one thing this week only he'll see. Then keep your mouth shut about it.
