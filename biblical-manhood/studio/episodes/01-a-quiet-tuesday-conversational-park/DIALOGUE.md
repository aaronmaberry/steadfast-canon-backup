Sit down. You are on time.

I am going to say that twice this week, because most of the men who press play on this are already half convinced they should have started years ago. You should have. So should I. That is not the point. The point is the chair you are in right now and the next twenty four weeks.

This is Steadfast Men. The Way of Godly Men. My name is Max. I am going to be the man across the table from you for twenty four weeks, one chapter a week, one job a week. That is the spine. Everything hangs on those four marks. Reject passivity. Accept responsibility. Lead courageously. Expect the greater reward, and get the reward right, because it is God's reward, not the room's.

## SEGMENT 1: WHO IS IN THE ROOM [02:00]

**MAX:**
Before we open the chapter, I want to talk about who is in the room. Because the introduction of this book does something I have not seen a lot of men's material do. It refuses to pick a demographic.

Most of this material is aimed at one guy. The married dad, two kids, mortgage. If that is you, good. But the introduction lays out every season a man can be in and puts a chair in front of each one. So let me do the same, and I want you to find yourself. Not the version of you that you would describe at a dinner. The actual one.

The young man. Nineteen, twenty two, twenty five. Nobody is asking much of you yet, and that is the danger. You have been told you have time. You have less than you think, and what you build in the years when nobody is watching is exactly what you will have when they start.

The single man. You do not have a wife to hide behind or to blame. You have more unstructured hours than anyone else in this room, and no witness to how you spend them. I am going to come back to you specifically, because this chapter is harder on you than it looks.

The married man. You have a witness. She sees the Tuesdays. She has seen every one of them. That is either the best news in this room or the worst, and you already know which.

The wounded man. Something happened. Maybe something was done to you, maybe you did it. And you have been operating with a limp and calling it a personality. I want to say something to you directly, because you are the one most likely to turn this off in the first five minutes. A program about responsibility sounds like a program about blame, and you have had enough of that. So hear me. The four marks are not a verdict on what happened to you. They are a description of what is still available to you. The book does not ask you to be un-wounded. It asks you to stop letting the wound pick your Tuesday.

The older man. You have more Tuesdays behind you than ahead, and there is a temptation to grade yourself on a curve. You may be doing the math right now and thinking the useful Tuesdays are gone. I would push back. You have a thing nobody else in this room has, which is evidence. You know what settling costs because you have watched men settle, some of them at your own table. That is not a reason to sit in the back. That is a reason to sit in the front.

And the high performer. I want to talk to you for a second, because that was me. I ran commercial construction sites for fourteen years. Superintendent. On a big job I had a hundred and sixty people moving under me on any given morning, and I could walk a site at six a.m. and tell you what was going to go wrong by ten. I was very good at being watched. I am going to tell you exactly how bad I was at not being watched before this hour is over.

Let me add one more chair. The man who is doing fine. Not wounded. Not failing. Steady job, decent marriage, kids are okay. Nobody is worried about him. And that man has quietly decided that fine is the finish line. This chapter is going to have a word for that, and the word is settling.

If you found yourself in one of those, good. If you found yourself in three, also good. The chair does not get smaller because you are more than one thing.

Now, the first seat. Some of you bought this book. Some of you had it handed to you. Some of you are watching because a man you respect sent you the link and said, start with week one. Here is what I need you to understand. The man who handed you the book may have done it because he sees something you do not. Fine. But you do not get to sit in his seat. You do not do this for him. You do not do this to get him off your back. Twenty four weeks is too long to do for someone else. You will quit by week six. The first seat is yours, even if someone else pulled it out for you. Decide that now, before I open the chapter. Because chapter one is going to ask you a question you cannot answer for anyone but yourself.

## SEGMENT 2: HIGHLIGHT REELS [07:00]

[Max opens the book. Does not read from it.]

**MAX:**
Chapter one. A quiet Tuesday.

Here is the claim, and I am going to put it as plainly as the chapter does. Manhood is proven on ordinary Tuesdays, not highlight reels.

Let me define highlight reel, because I do not want you to hear this loosely. The highlight reel is every moment where you know you are being graded. The wedding toast. The job interview. The night your kid is sick and the whole house is watching to see what dad does. The game. The crisis at work when the thing breaks and everyone looks at you. Men are good at those. I would go further. Most men are excellent at those, because there is a camera, real or imagined, and a camera produces a version of you that is better than you.

I want to be careful here. The highlight reel is not fake. When you rise in a crisis, that is really you rising. The book is not saying the crisis does not count. It is saying that the crisis is not most of you. Most of you is Tuesday. Most of your life, and I mean the actual arithmetic, is a day nobody will remember and nobody will ask you about. The chapter's argument is that the man is built there, in the days nobody is grading, and the highlight reel just shows what was already there. Or what was not.

There is a line in the chapter I underlined twice. A lot of men can rise for a camera. Fewer rise for a cereal bowl.

I know exactly which one I am. So let me tell on myself.

This is a Tuesday from about four years ago. I get home at six fifteen. I have had a good day. A genuinely good day. We had been fighting a schedule for eight months on a hospital job and that afternoon we had closed the last inspection two weeks early. Nobody does that. I pull into the driveway and I do not get out of the truck. I sit there. I read the texts coming in from the project team. I answer a few. I check the news. I check a score. And my daughter, who was seven at the time, comes out onto the front step and sits down and waits.

Twenty two minutes. I know the number because my wife told me later. She watched from the kitchen window. She did not come out. She wanted to see how long it would take. Twenty two minutes, and my daughter did not knock on the window. Because she had learned not to.

That is the part. She had learned.

Now, a man watching this might say, Max, it was twenty two minutes. You had a big day. Give yourself a break. And if it were one Tuesday, fine. It was not one Tuesday. You do not learn not to knock on the window from one Tuesday. You learn that from a hundred. The twenty two minutes was not the problem. The twenty two minutes was the evidence.

The highlight reel version of that day is a man who closed a hospital two weeks early. The Tuesday version is a man who could not get out of a truck for a seven year old. Both are true. Only one of them is what my daughter knows about me.

Here is the sentence in the chapter I could not get past. One more throw while dad watches the clock.

Because the kid is not asking for the camera moment. He is not asking for the trip, or the speech, or the big Saturday. He is asking for one more throw. It costs nothing. Ninety seconds. And the man cannot give it because he is somewhere else. He is in the truck. He is in the meeting he left two hours ago. He is in tomorrow. His body is in the yard and he is checking the clock like the clock is going to do something.

Let me be precise about what is being said and what is not. It is not that Tuesday matters more than the crisis. It is that the crisis reveals whatever Tuesday built. When the real thing comes, and it will come, you do not become a different man. You become more of the man you were on Tuesday.

## SEGMENT 3: CHILDISH WAYS [13:00]

**MAX:**
Now the phrase in the chapter that I think is the most important one. Childish ways.

The chapter defines childish ways like this. A life that never takes a yes that survives the feeling.

I am going to say that again slowly for the man who is driving. A life that never takes a yes that survives the feeling.

Meaning this. I said yes to something. I meant it when I said it. I felt it. And then the feeling left, and the yes left with it. A child's yes is real. It is just that it lasts exactly as long as the emotion that produced it.

The Apostle Paul says it this way. First Corinthians thirteen, verse eleven, NIV. When I was a child, I talked like a child, I thought like a child, I reasoned like a child. When I became a man, I put the ways of childhood behind me.

Two things I want you to notice. First, Paul does not say the childish ways fell off. He says he put them behind him. There is a verb in there. It is something a man does. Nobody grows out of childish ways by getting older. Plenty of sixty year olds have never taken a yes that survived a feeling.

Second, he does not say he stopped feeling like a child. He says he stopped talking, thinking, and reasoning like one. That is the whole point of a yes that survives the feeling. The feeling may never grow up. The man does.

I will give you a plain one on myself. Not dramatic. That is on purpose.

When my father died, I told my mother I would call her every Sunday. She lives alone, about six hours from us. I meant it. I felt it as strongly as I have felt anything. I called four Sundays in a row. The fifth Sunday I was tired, and I told myself she would understand. And she did. That is the worst part. She understood. I called maybe fifteen Sundays out of the next fifty two. On every one of those thirty seven Sundays I was a man who had said yes and a boy who did not have to mean it.

Here is why this matters for the program and not just for my mother. Everything I am going to ask you to do for twenty four weeks is a yes that has to survive the feeling. You will feel it tonight. You will feel it this week. You will not feel it in week nine. Week nine is a Tuesday. Week nine is the fifth Sunday. And the program only works if your yes is still standing when the feeling has gone home.

So what does a grown yes look like?

It looks boring from the outside. It looks like a man who is not excited about the thing anymore and does it anyway, at the same time, in the same way. My wife's father has carried her mother a cup of coffee in bed every morning for forty one years. I asked him once if he still enjoyed doing it. He looked at me like I had asked a strange question. He said, that is not really what it is for.

That is a yes that survived the feeling. Fourteen thousand cups. Nobody filmed one of them.

## SEGMENT 4: THE DISAPPEARANCE [18:00]

**MAX:**
The chapter uses a word I want to stay on for a few minutes. Disappearance.

A man disappears on Tuesday. Not dramatically. He does not leave. He is right there. His body is on the couch. His name is on the mortgage. His truck is in the driveway. And he is gone.

Let me name the forms, because it is not just the phone. The phone is the obvious one. But I have watched men disappear into a job. Into a hobby that grew legs. Into the garage. Into the news. Into being tired, real or performed. Into being the funny guy so nothing at the table ever has to get serious. Into a screen with a game on it. Into a screen with something worse on it.

And here is one that is sneaky. Into being agreeable. The man who never has an opinion at home because opinions cost something. Sure. Whatever you want. You decide. It looks like humility. It is not. It is a man who has left the building and taped a pleasant note to the door. His wife is not married to a tyrant. She is married to a vacancy.

And for the high performer, here is your exit. You disappear into work you are genuinely good at. Nobody ever confronted me for staying late. My disappearance came with a bonus attached. I got promoted for it. I want you to hear that clearly, because the world will pay you to disappear from your own house, and it will call it commitment.

Now the household adjusts. That is what my daughter did on the step. She adjusted. The tragedy is not that the man is gone. The tragedy is that everyone around him learns to route around the hole. They stop asking. They stop knocking on the window. From the outside it looks like peace. It is not peace. It is a family that has quietly agreed to stop expecting him.

The first mark, again. A real man rejects passivity. I want to correct a definition, because I had it wrong for years. Passivity is not doing nothing. I was doing a great deal. Fourteen hour days. Passivity is being absent from the thing that was actually yours while you were busy with something that was not. The Tuesday question is not what did you do. The Tuesday question is where were you.

And the third mark, lead courageously, lands right here too. We think of courage as the highlight reel. Running toward the thing. On a Tuesday, courage is mostly having an opinion about your own household and staying in the room when it costs you something. Saying no to the second screen. Saying, I was not here last night, and I know it. That is not a small courage. It is just an unphotographed one.

Jesus put it this way. Luke sixteen, verse ten, NIV. Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much, and whoever is dishonest with very little will also be dishonest with much.

Very little is a cereal bowl. Very little is one more throw. God's math runs from small to large, not the other direction. You do not get trusted with the crisis because you crushed the last crisis. You get trusted with the crisis because you sat at the table on a Tuesday without your phone.

Now, the single man. I said I would come back to you. You may have been sitting there thinking this is all about wives and kids and driveways. Your Tuesday has no witness. That is harder, not easier. Nobody is on the step waiting for you. So your disappearance is total. You can go a whole week without anyone knowing you were gone. There is no daughter learning not to knock. There is just a man, alone, becoming someone in the dark. The chapter would say your Tuesdays are the ones being watched most closely, because there is only One who sees them, and He does.

And the older man. Your kids are grown. The throw is not being asked for anymore. Here is your Tuesday. There is a man in your life, twenty seven years old, thirty one, who would take one call a week from you and does not know how to ask for it. He is in the truck. You know what the truck is because you sat in it. That is your Tuesday now. Do not tell me you are out of Tuesdays.

## SEGMENT 5: THE JOB [23:30]

**MAX:**
Every chapter in this program is going to give you a job. Here is the job from chapter one.

Last Tuesday is the day you answer for.

Not the best day you ever had. Not the worst. Not the day you are planning to have once things calm down. Last Tuesday. The actual one.

So do this with me, and do not do it with a version of you. Do it with the man who lived last Tuesday.

What time did you get up. What did you look at first, before your feet hit the floor. Who did you speak to in your house before you spoke to a screen. Where did you eat lunch, and with whom. What time did you get home. What did you do in the first ten minutes at home. Where were your hands at eight thirty. Where were your eyes at ten. And the hard one. What did the people in your house get from you last Tuesday that they could not have gotten from a roommate.

Write it down. Take one page. Left column is the hour. Right column is where you actually were. Not what you were doing. Where you were. A man can be washing dishes and be in Cleveland.

I did this exercise on the Tuesday before we recorded, and I am not going to pretend it was clean. I was in a good place until about eight forty five. And then I remembered a hard conversation I had coming the next morning, and I disappeared into rehearsing it. My wife said something to me and I said yeah, and I have no idea to this day what she said.

What did I do about it? Nothing, that night. That is the honest answer. The next morning I said to her, I was not here after eight forty five last night, and I know it. And she said, I know. She already knew. They always already know. But saying it out loud is the difference between a man and a boy. A boy hopes nobody noticed. A man names it.

Let me give you one more, from a different season, for the single men. Before I was married I lived alone for six years and I ate dinner standing at the kitchen counter most nights. Laptop open. Fork in one hand. I told myself it was efficient. I was very productive. And I am telling you now that standing at that counter was a way of never being anywhere. I had made a whole life out of being busy so I never had to sit down.

Sit down. You are on time. That was the first sentence out of my mouth today and I meant it literally.

Here is what I want you to notice about both of those confessions. Neither one is dramatic. I did not have an affair on Tuesday. I did not get fired. If this program were a highlight reel, I would have nothing to say this week. That is exactly why the chapter starts here. It wants your Tuesday because it is the only day you are not performing.

That is what the second mark means. Accepts responsibility. Not accepts blame. Accepts the day. This one is mine. All of it. The eight forty five included.

## SEGMENT 6: SETTLING [27:30]

**MAX:**
One more idea before we land it.

The chapter closes with a line that is also your carry for the week. Tuesday is where a man is made, or he settles.

Define settling. Settling is what happens when you stop asking the Tuesday question. It is not a decision. Nobody decides to settle. You just stop answering for the day, and eventually the day stops asking. The couch gets comfortable. The phone gets warm. Everyone learns to route around you. And here is the thing that should bother you. From the outside, the man who settled and the man who was made look almost identical on a Saturday. Same church. Same job. Same truck. Same jokes.

The difference is only visible on Tuesday. That is the whole thesis of the chapter in one line.

Now I want to be clear about the tone of this program, because there is a version of this that could make you feel bad for twenty four weeks, and that is not what we are doing and it is not what the book does. The last mark is that a real man expects the greater reward, God's reward. There is a payout for the man who gets out of the truck. It is not applause. Nobody films it. But a seven year old who stops waiting on the step because she does not have to anymore, because dad comes straight in now, that is a reward. Forty one years of coffee is a reward. A mother who picks up on the first ring because she knows it is Sunday, that is a reward. It is paid on a schedule you do not control, in a currency you do not get to pick. It is a better currency. It is the only one that holds.

Every week ends the same four ways. You will see them coming, and that is the point. Name it. Do this week. Tell a brother. Carry this.

Name it. Name your disappearance. Not your sin in general. Not your struggles. The specific place you go on a Tuesday night when you are technically in the room. Truck. Counter. Garage. Phone. News. Work. Agreeable. Say it out loud tonight, one sentence. I disappear into blank. If you cannot fill in the blank, ask the person who lives with you. They can. And if you are married, saying it to her out loud is the smallest courageous act you will do this week. Do it anyway.

Do this week. One meal without your phone in the room. Not face down on the table. Not in your pocket on silent. In another room. One meal. If you live alone, you sit down for it, at a table, fork in one hand and nothing in the other. That is the small yes. It is deliberately small, because we want a yes that survives the feeling, and the feeling will be gone by Thursday.

Tell a brother. Text one man this week about your Tuesday. Not a verse. Not a link. A sentence about your actual Tuesday. If you need a template, here it is. Started a twenty four week program on manhood. Week one is about ordinary Tuesdays. Mine was rough at eight forty five. What was yours. That is it. He will answer. Men answer that text.

Carry this. One sentence for the week. Tuesday is where a man is made, or he settles.
