# Voice handoff (for Vox)

Spec only. Do not buy ElevenLabs or any paid voice API. Do not clone a real person (not Aaron, not Rogan, not a pastor, not Wes Hart as a "voice").

Same two voices will later read the Wes Hart audiobook if it serves: Ellis as lead narrator, Mateo as second chair for dialogue and scripture when a second voice helps. That is a later call. Today is the table.

## Ellis Benn (lead at the table)

- **Pitch:** lower-mid baritone. Not radio-deep. Not a whisper-pastor.
- **Pace:** unhurried. About 10% slower than Mateo. He can sit in a silence for a full beat.
- **Warmth:** present without performing. Warmth is in the vowels and the wait, not in a smile you can hear.
- **Cadence:** Midwest/Oklahoma flattening. No preacher lope. No rising "can I get an amen" tail. Volume drops on the hard line instead of punching it.
- **Interrupts:** rarely. When he does, it is to stop a speech or to name the mark they are on. One short sentence, then he gives the floor back.
- **Scripture:** slower, not stained-glass. He does not announce it like a reading. He says it, then waits.
- **Laugh:** low, short, usually at himself. Never at Elena, Dana, or a man in the hypothetical row.

## Mateo Ruiz (second chair)

- **Pitch:** mid baritone, a half-step brighter than Ellis. Not tenor-cute.
- **Pace:** faster. Heat in the consonants. He runs a little, then catches himself.
- **Warmth:** easier laugh, quicker to tell on himself. Not comic relief.
- **Cadence:** American English, Tulsa-metro. No fake accent. No Spanglish bit. A family name or a short aside is fine. A persona is not.
- **Interrupts:** often, and that is the design. He cuts in when Ellis gets too clean, when a principle has no Thursday attached, or when he has already lived the mistake this week. He does not talk over a landing. After Ellis drops the hard line, Mateo waits, then asks the next question.
- **Scripture:** lets Ellis carry most of it. When Mateo reads, it is a sentence he needed, not a performance.
- **Laugh:** quicker, through the discomfort, then he lands. If the laugh would cost another man dignity, he does not take it.

## Who leads, who asks

- Ellis opens the week and closes it.
- Mateo asks "what did you actually do."
- Either man can say the creed. Ellis usually says it last.
- They do not interview each other. They talk like brothers who read the chapter.
- Wes Hart is the author they both read. Neither does an impression of him. Neither says "Wes would say." They say the thing.

## Anti-clone

If a take could be mistaken for Joe Rogan, a known pastor, a radio host, or Aaron, kill the take. Two ordinary men at a table. That is the whole brief.
