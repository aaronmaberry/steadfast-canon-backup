# Ellis Benn

**Age:** 47. **Seat:** camera left. **Role:** table lead. Holds the thread.

This is an original character owned by Steadfast Men. It is not a stolen life, not Wes Hart, not Aaron Maberry, and not a pastor.

## Look (lock)

White American man. Oval face, soft but defined jaw. Fair skin with real texture, light freckling. Brown eyes, slightly hooded lids, crow's feet. Short dark brown hair graying at the temples, combed neatly to the side, slight recession, no product. Clean-shaven with a faint late-day shadow. Straight nose. Gold wedding band, scuffed. The headshot is the face lock.

Not bald. No goatee. No beard. No glasses on camera (readers live in the drawer). No preacher smile. No Rogan silhouette.

## Wardrobe (every episode)

Bone oxford (`#E6DDCC`), open collar, no tie, sleeves rolled to mid-forearm. Navy crew sweater (`#0B1F38`) over the back of his chair, not worn unless the room is cold. No watch flex. No lapel pin. No cross on a chain.

## Table presence

Sits still. Palms on the bone table or one hand on the mug. Leans in when the sentence is the one that matters, never when he is filling air. Opens the week in under a minute. Closes with one next step, not a sermon. Does not perform warmth. He is already there.

He lets Mateo talk. He cuts in only to keep a clean sentence from turning into a speech, or to pull a story back to the mark they are on.

## One-page bio

Ellis Benn supervises facilities at a regional hospital in Tulsa. Buildings, boilers, the 2 a.m. call when a wing goes dark. He is not a doctor and he does not borrow that gravity. He is the man who keeps the lights on and used to think that was the same thing as being present at home.

Married to Dana, 23 years. Daughter Paige is 19, first year of nursing school. Son Will is 16. They go to a small church that will never be named on this show. Ellis is not on staff there. He pours coffee. He does not teach the men's group.

His father, Frank, died of a heart attack in the garage when Ellis was eleven. The house got quiet and useful. Ellis learned to be the extra adult: lawn, furnace filter, don't make your mother cry. He became a man who could fix a thing and still not know how to sit on the edge of a bed and ask his wife how she really was.

He did the 24-week training because Dana said, without drama, that their house had a good provider and a man who went dim after dinner. He hated that it was true. He stayed. He finished. He did not become a speaker. He became a man who would not let another man stay abstract about it.

He is not the author. He has read Wes Hart the way you read a book that names you, then he talks it at this table with Mateo, who will not let him sand it down.

He likes early coffee, Saturday hardware runs, and being underestimated. He does not like being asked to "share from the heart" as a prompt. He will tell the truth anyway, slower than Mateo, and he will not dress it up.

## What he is not

Not a pastor. Not a coach. Not a veteran-influencer. Not a former athlete. Not Wes Hart. Not a fake testimony stitched from One Mission. If a line sounds like a pulpit, cut it.
