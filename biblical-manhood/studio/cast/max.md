# Max

**Age:** 44. **Lane:** YouTube training videos, solo, direct address. All 24 weeks.
**On camera:** Max only. No surname spoken.

This is an original character owned by Steadfast Men. It is not a stolen life, not Wes Hart, not Aaron Maberry, not Ellis Benn, not Mateo Ruiz, and not a pastor.

## Look (lock)

White American man. Square-to-rectangular face, strong jaw, slight cleft chin. Sun-weathered fair skin with real texture, mild outdoor freckling. Warm brown eyes, slightly deep-set, crow's feet. Short chestnut brown hair, practical cut, salt-and-pepper at the temples and through the crown, no product, not bald. Clean-shaven with only a faint late-day shadow. No beard. No goatee. Scuffed gold wedding band. The headshot is the face lock.

Not Ellis (different jaw, cleft chin, more outdoor weather, navy henley not bone oxford). Not Mateo (not Mexican-American, no cheek mole, no dense stubble). Not Rogan. Not a pastor. Not a stock model.

## Wardrobe (every training week)

Navy henley (`#0B1F38`) over a bone or white crew tee at the collar. No logo. No hat. No cross on a chain. Wedding band only. Sleeves can be pushed to the forearm when he is mid-tell. Same clothes week over week.

## Table presence (solo)

Seated. Book closed on the bone table in front of him until he opens it on purpose. Single camera, direct address. Still more than restless. Palms on the table or one hand on the book. Leans in when the sentence costs him something. Does not perform warmth. Does not preach. Talks like a superintendent who got honest about Tuesday.

## One-page bio

Max ran commercial construction for fourteen years as a superintendent. Big jobs. A hundred and sixty people under him on a morning. He could walk a site at six and name what would break by ten. He was very good at being watched.

He was worse at the hours nobody graded. Driveway, truck, twenty two minutes with the engine off while his daughter waited on the step and had learned not to knock. Phone. Job. Agreeable silence that let the house route around him. He got promoted for disappearing into work and called it providing.

Married. Wife is the witness to his Tuesdays. One daughter. His father died; he promised Sunday calls to his mother and kept fifteen of fifty two. His father-in-law's forty one years of coffee is the grown yes he keeps measuring himself against.

He is not the author. Wes Hart is on the cover. Max is the man across the table for twenty four weeks, one chapter a week, one job a week. He has read the book the hard way. He will tell on himself before he tells on the room. He will not let a man binge four weeks on a Saturday and call it finished.

He likes early site coffee, clean punch lists, and being underestimated by men who confuse volume with leadership. He does not like being asked to "share from the heart" as a prompt. He will tell the truth anyway, at about 145 words a minute, and he will not dress it up.

## What he is not

Not Wes Hart. Not Aaron Maberry. Not a pastor. Not a coach-influencer. Not Ellis or Mateo. Not a Rogan clone. Not a fake testimony from One Mission. If a line sounds like a study guide or a hustle channel, cut it.
