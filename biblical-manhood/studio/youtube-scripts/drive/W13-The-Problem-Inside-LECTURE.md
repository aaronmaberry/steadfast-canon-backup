Picture a bubble in the paint on the fender of a first truck, the size of a thumbprint. Every spring a man sands it to bare metal, primes it, paints it. Every fall it is back, bigger, with two smaller ones beside it. Third year he takes it to a body man. The body man presses the bubble with his thumb. It gives. A little brown water runs out. Paint is not going to fix that, he says. It is coming from the inside. The rust was on the back of the panel, where paint never reached, eating its way out the whole time the man was dressing up the front.

## 2. THE CLAIM [04:00]

**MAX:**
Chapter fifteen is titled The Problem Inside. Here is the claim of the chapter, and it is the claim of this session.

[Slide: You've been painting over it. The problem inside has a name, and the name has a cure.]

You've been painting over it. The problem inside has a name, the name has a cure, and the cure has already been paid for. Say the word about yourself, or you will spend your life on Adam's excuse: the father You gave me, he did it.

Write that down under the four marks. I am going to define the terms so you do not hear this loosely.

The word is sin. Some avoid it because it sounds too harsh. They would rather say struggles, or issues, or baggage: soft words in a hard place. A struggle is something that happens to you. Sin is something you do. You manage the first and repent of the second. Others avoid it because churches swung it like a club at them when they were boys. The word does not belong to the men who misused it. It belongs to God, and in His mouth it is a diagnosis, not a beating. The body man was not attacking. He was the first one to tell the truth.

Sin is the bend in a man toward himself: he hides when he should stand up, blames when he should own, takes when he should give, goes quiet when he should speak. It is what Adam did in the garden and what you did last night. God described it to Cain before Cain ever raised a hand: sin is crouching at your door, it desires to have you, and you must rule over it. An animal. You are responsible and not helpless.

What the claim does not say. It does not say your father, your culture, or your wound did not matter. Those opened doors. What walked through them was already in you. It does not say you are only a victim. If your problem is entirely what was done to you, there is no way out; you cannot un-have your father. A man who believes his whole problem is his inheritance has found a way to never repent, because you cannot repent of what somebody else did. But if the problem is yours, it can be dealt with, because God deals with what is yours. He does not offer to fix your father. He offers to fix you.

Hold that. Scripture under it.

## 3. THE TEXT [07:00]

**MAX:**
Mark, chapter seven, verse twenty three. NIV. Jesus to religious men busy managing their outsides.

[Slide: Mark 7:23 NIV]

All these evils come from inside and defile a person.

Three observations. Take them down.

First observation. From inside, He says, not from your father or your culture or your wound. Adam had a perfect Father, a perfect place and work and wife, and no wound of any kind. He still reached for the fruit, or stood silent while it was reached for, then hid, then blamed. The problem inside a man did not come from his father. It came through his father, from a father before that, all the way back to a garden, and it would be in you if you had been raised by saints. Jeremiah called the heart deceitful above all things and beyond cure.

Second observation. Romans five, eight. But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us. While we were still sinners. Not while we were victims, or under-fathered. He named the real problem and died for it, and if you will name it too, you get the cure.

Third observation. James five, sixteen. Therefore confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed. And Psalm fifty one, ten, after David's worst: Create in me a pure heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me. You cannot paint over what is rusting from the inside. The metal has to be made new, and only God does that, and He keeps doing it.

Say it with me. All these evils come from inside.

So the text gives us the diagnosis. The claim gives us the relief. The problem is yours, which means it can be dealt with.

Four movements. Let's go.

## 4. MOVEMENT ONE: EVEN WITH A PERFECT FATHER [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. Even with a perfect father.

Suppose your father had been home every night, kind and present and strong, told you he loved you, and blessed you on your way into manhood, and you had brothers and a mentor besides. Would you be a good man? Better, probably. But good? Would you never lie to your wife, never look at what you should not, never quietly decide you are better than the man in the next pew?

You know the answer, because the first man had all of it. That does not sound like good news. It is. If the problem is entirely what was done to you, you are stuck painting forever. If the problem is yours, God offers to fix you. That is mercy dressed as a hard sentence.

I spent years sanding the front of my life after hard jobs and hard weeks. Early closes. Clean apologies that never named the real thing. Primer on the marriage. Paint on the temper. The bubble came back every fall because I was treating the inheritance as the whole story. My father opened doors. What walked through was already mine. Naming that felt foolish for about a minute. Then it felt like the first honest shop visit of my adult life.

I want the wounded man to hear this without hearing blame for his childhood. You are not being told your wound did not happen. You are being told the wound is not the whole diagnosis. I want the high performer to hear that competence on the outside is not righteousness on the inside. Both need the next movements.

Movement one, summarized. Write this down: even a perfect father would not have made you good. The rust is yours, which means it can be reached.

## 5. MOVEMENT TWO: SAY THE WORD [15:00]

**MAX:**
Movement two. Say the word about yourself.

Paul said it for every man: what I want to do I do not do, but what I hate I do. You want to be patient with your kids and you are not. You want to be present with your wife and you reach for the phone. Then he asks the only question that goes anywhere: Who will rescue me? Not what. Who.

I will not hand you a program. Most come down to trying harder, which is another coat of paint. Say the word about yourself. Not I've got some stuff. I sinned. Out loud, to God, and specifically. I lied to her about the money. David said silence wasted his bones. Then he confessed, and God forgave him, and the whole distance between the two was one honest sentence.

Know the difference between guilt and shame. Guilt says, I did a wrong thing. Shame says, I am a wrong thing. Guilt is the bubble in the paint, ugly and honest about where the rust is, and once you have confessed it has done its work. Shame is a liar, and it drives men straight back to whatever they reach for, because it is the exact feeling anger and appetite and absence were built to numb.

Turn; do not just feel bad. Repentance is not a feeling. It is a change of direction. A man can feel terrible about something for decades and never once turn. Confess it, make it right where you can, put something in place so that door is harder to walk through tomorrow, and get back to work. Then turn again tomorrow, and that is not failure. That is a Christian life from the inside: a man who keeps turning back toward the Father he keeps walking away from, and a Father who keeps receiving him.

Write this down: I sinned, not I struggle with. Guilt tells the truth. Shame lies. Turn, do not only feel.

Movement two, summarized. One honest sentence is the whole distance between wasting away and being forgiven.

## 6. MOVEMENT THREE: BRING A BROTHER IN [20:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. Bring a brother into it.

Sin lives in the dark, and it cannot survive being said to another man who loves you. That is why last week's twenty minutes matters this week. The first time a man named Wade said the word about himself to another man was in the twenty minutes, when his brother asked what was heavy. He started to say work and stopped, and after a long silence said, I scared my daughter last night. I did not touch her. I did not have to. I raised my voice and she went small, the way I used to go small, and I have been calling it my temper for years, and it is a sin. The other man was quiet a while. Then he said, Thank you for telling me, asked what Wade was going to do next, and prayed for him. Wade said he had expected to feel exposed and instead felt, for the first time in years, clean. James says a man like that is healed, not only forgiven.

If you do not have the brother yet, this week still asks for one confession to a man who will not leave. The asking and the confessing can land in the same seven days. Do not wait for the perfect circle. Sin does not wait for your scheduling.

I know what it costs to say it. I once told a brother on a job site lunch break that I had been lying about how fine I was after a failed bid, and that the storm I brought home was mine, not the owner's. He did not fix me. He prayed. I walked back to the trailer lighter than the apology I still owed at home. The drawer only opens when another man is in the room with a steady face.

Movement three, summarized. Write this down: confess to a brother, name the next move, let him pray. Dark cannot survive that.
