## 4. MOVEMENT ONE: WHAT A HUSBAND LAYS DOWN [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. What a husband lays down, mostly in pieces, on ordinary days.

He lays down his right to be right. You will be right a great deal. Most of the time it is worth nothing compared with what winning costs her. Not by pretending you are wrong. By deciding the point is not the point. Let's figure it out together. The second half of that sentence is the whole marriage.

He lays down his preferences. Where to eat, what to watch, how the dishes go, how the weekend goes. Cheerfully, without a scoreboard.

He lays down his phone. Face down when you walk in. Not in your hand while she is talking.

He lays down his Saturday, or whatever his day is. When the house needs you, the day loses without sulking.

He lays down his first minutes after work. Those belong to her, not the recliner.

He lays down his side of the story in front of the kids, parents, friends. Take her side. A wife whose husband defends her in public can survive a fight in private.

He lays down his mother's vote. When mother and wife disagree, your wife wins, and your mother hears you say so. You left. Act like it.

He lays down his sleep. When she is sick, when a child is up, you get up.

He lays down his anger. Not by stuffing it. Take it to the truck and to God and to your brother, and bring her what was under it.

He lays down his money. House money. Full standing, full knowledge, full say. Keeping her in the dark is not leading.

He lays down his plans. If you built the next years without her in the room, lay them down and make new ones with her in it.

What laying down is not. Not abdicating. A husband who stops leading and calls it letting her have her way is disappearing. She will feel abandonment even while she gets what she asks for. Not a doormat. Jesus said hard things to people He loved. Not self erasure. Lay down preferences, rights, comfort, pride. Do not lay down yourself.

Test: after you lay something down, is she stronger or weaker. More secure, more herself, more radiant: real. Carrying more because you checked out and called it humility: counterfeit.

I won a lot of kitchen points I should have thrown in the trash with the estimate. Being right felt like leadership. It was keeping score. The night I finally laid one down, the room changed in a way winning never did.

Movement one, summarized. Write this down: name what you grip hardest from the list.

## 5. MOVEMENT TWO: SHE NEEDS YOU [14:30]

**MAX:**
Movement two. What she actually needs. Chapter twenty four.

Steadiness. She needs to know you are not going anywhere. Strike the fine print. Say it out loud more than once: I'm not going anywhere, ever, and whatever this is, we're doing it together. Keep the oath when it hurts.

Presence. Be here when you are here. Phone down. Ask a question and wait for the whole answer. A person she is with, not a body she is near.

Providing without disappearing. The trap that catches the best men. Work asks for more. Giving to the work feels like giving to the family. You know you have started to disappear when she stops asking when you will be home, when you find out about your own house secondhand, when you are too tired at night to be anybody, when you told yourself she wants the lifestyle without asking her.

Dale asked one question at the kitchen table: What do you need from me that you're not getting? Renee did not have to think. Look up when I come into a room. He had provided an entire house and stopped lifting his eyes when she walked into it. He did not quit his job. He set a floor: supper at the table four nights, Sunday morning, bedtime prayer. He said when he would be home and was home then. He put the phone on the refrigerator the first hour. He quit playing for the scoreboard. This spring Dale heard Renee on the phone with the same sister from the same mudroom. She did not say provider. She said, He's here, and moved on like it was not news.

Safety. Not fists. The voice that drops a register. Three day silence. Sarcasm that draws blood. Truck that leaves fast. A wife who reads your weather before she speaks is not safe in her own house.

Pursued, still. Plan a Thursday. Ask her out with a specific evening and an ironed shirt. Look at her across a room the way you did the first year and let her catch you.

Leadership she can rest in. Not ruled. Not the only adult. Here's where we're going and how we'll get there. Pray with her out loud once a week, holding her hand.

Heard and known. Last week's chapter in one line.

Words. Out loud. Specific. In front of the children. You surpass them all is not you're a good wife.

Help that is not helping. The dishes are your dishes. A husband who helps out has told her the whole thing is hers and he is a volunteer.

Tenderness. Touch that is not about the bedroom. Hand on her back. Feet after a long day. Held while she cries without fixing the crying.

A man who is growing. Toward God and toward the four marks. Stuck is a weight she carries. Growing, even slowly, is a man she can follow.

Movement two, summarized. Write this down: the sentence you fear she would use about you to her sister.

## 6. MOVEMENT THREE: SEASONS AND THE REWARD [19:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. Seasons for both chapters, and the reward for the man who loses.

Newborn: take a shift, not I'll help when she asks. Kill the silent ledger. Do one unpaid thing without announcing it. Lay down the bedroom until she is ready. Lay down the gym for this year, not forever. Under a newborn she needs sleep more than anything you can buy, and you in the room at three even when you cannot feed him. Father sees the three o'clock.

Long marriage: lay down the retirement you planned alone. Ask what she wants the years to be. Lay down the remote. When she gets sick, laying down stops being a metaphor. I have watched a man bathe his wife every morning for years. She does not always know him. He knows her. He meant in sickness. Decades in, she needs to be seen as a woman not a fixture, plans made together, you at your checkups, because her quiet fear is finding you on the floor.

Divorced seat: you may be thinking about what you would not lay down when you had the chance. Hear it, grieve it, confess your part, do not stay there. Lay down your right to win with your children's mother. Kids need two parents who can be in a room together. Read the needs list honestly against the marriage that ended, not to bury yourself, so whatever comes next you are not the same man. Their mother still needs reliability, civility, backing in front of the kids.

Single: practice anyway. A man who has never lost a preference will not suddenly become good at it on a wedding night. Lay down where you eat. Lay down a Saturday for an older man who needs help. Practice presence, words, a call about her on your mother. Small deaths daily. Training ground.

The reward. Whoever loses their life will find it. The Father sees the laid down fight nobody witnessed, the night shift, the bath she may not remember. None of it is wasted. One day the Man who laid down everything will look at a husband who laid down a little and say well done. You did it the way I did.

The man on the wet floor was right that night. He laid it down anyway. His wife told me later that was the night she started trusting him with her fears, because a man who would give up a fight he was winning would not use what she told him against her.

Movement three, summarized. Write this down: one thing to lay down daily this week. One need to ask about.
