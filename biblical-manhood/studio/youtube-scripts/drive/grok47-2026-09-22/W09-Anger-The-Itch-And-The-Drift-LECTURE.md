Last week I asked you to name the chair. I asked you to write a letter you would not send. Some of you said the sentence out loud. Some of you are still deciding whether grief is allowed. Stay in the chair. Today we talk about where the pain goes when a man will not carry it to a Father.

A man took all three exits in one week. He would have told you he has a temper. He would have left it there.

First night. His daughter loaded the dishwasher wrong. He slammed the door hard enough to knock a glass off the top rack. The kitchen went quiet the way his kitchen has learned to go quiet.

Two nights later he was in the bathroom. The door was locked. His phone was in his hand for forty minutes. Nobody in the house needed anything from him in there. That was the point.

By the weekend he was on the couch with a game on. His wife asked what he thought about her sister taking the kids in July. He said whatever you think. He meant it. He was not in the room.

Three nights. Three exits. He would call them three bad moods. They were not. Under all three sat one thing he had not told anyone. The contract he had counted on for the fall had gone to somebody else. He had run the numbers. He was scared.

The chair at the end of the table never stays empty. Pain does not sit still in a man either. It has to go somewhere. It nearly always goes one of three directions. Out, as anger. Down, as appetite. Or away, as absence. Every man has a home exit. It is the one he takes when things get heavy. Most men think it is their personality. It is not. It is the way a boy learned not to feel something. That way wore into a habit. The habit answers to his name.

[Slide. The four marks]

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Write it down. Today the marks confront the exits. Passivity often looks like drift. Courage often looks like ninety seconds and a true sentence. Responsibility looks like telling the truth about the numbers instead of slamming the dishwasher.

## 3. The text [07:00]

**MAX:**
Ephesians, chapter four, verses twenty six and twenty seven. NIV. Primary text for the anger exit. It teaches the pattern for all three.

[Slide. Ephesians 4:26-27 NIV]

In your anger do not sin: Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry, and do not give the devil a foothold.

Three observations.

First. In your anger, it says. Anger is assumed. Then a deadline. Sundown. Then a warning. This is where the enemy gets a foot inside your house. He gets it through the anger you carried to bed and woke up with and carried to bed again, until it was the weather in your home.

Second. James, chapter one, verses fourteen and fifteen, for the itch.

Each person is tempted when they are dragged away by their own evil desire and enticed. Then, after desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, gives birth to death.

Dragged away is the feeling. Nobody plans to be up at two in the morning. The road ends in death. It is rarely a dramatic death. It is the slow death of a man's ability to be present, to want a real woman, to look his wife in the eye.

Third. Ecclesiastes, chapter ten, verse eighteen, for the drift.

Through laziness, the rafters sag; because of idle hands, the house leaks.

Nobody kicked a hole in that roof. It leaked because the man who should have been on the ladder was on the couch. By the time anybody names the drift, the ceiling is stained.

Write it down. Sundown deadline. Dragged away. Sagging rafters. Three exits. One house.

## 4. Movement one. Anger [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. Anger.

Anger is the exit that looks like strength. That is exactly why men like it.

Get underneath a man's anger and you nearly always find fear, shame, or helplessness. The money will not work. He is ashamed of something. He is watching something he loves go wrong and he cannot stop it. Every one of those feels like weakness. Anger is the one feeling that feels like power. So he grabs it the way a cold man grabs the nearest coat. The dishwasher did not make that man slam the door. The numbers did.

If anger is your exit, here is what is yours. Learn your ninety seconds. Anger in the body crests and starts to fall inside two minutes if you do not feed it. Walk to the mailbox and back. Do not say the sentence you have loaded. Then come inside and say what is under it. I am scared about the money. Your wife can work with a scared man. She cannot work with a storm. And do not let the sun go down on it. A man who masters this does not go soft. He becomes the strongest man in the room.

I have been the storm. After a failed bid years ago I came home and found something wrong with a cabinet door. It had been wrong for six months. Suddenly it could not be tolerated. I was not angry at the cabinet. I was angry at being helpless about the number. My kids learned the weather from the way I set my keys down. That is not strength. That is a coat grabbed in the cold.

Movement one, summarized. Write this down. Anger is often fear wearing power. Ninety seconds, then the true sentence.

## 5. Movement two. The itch [15:00]

**MAX:**
Movement two. The itch.

The second exit goes down, into appetite. Most of this is about the sexual kind. That kind has the most men by the throat. It applies to the bottle, the cart, the sports book, and the endless scroll. It is all one itch. Comfort now, at no cost, without having to be known.

The man locked in the bathroom with his phone was not chasing pleasure, mostly. He was chasing relief, from the numbers and the quiet kitchen. The screen asked nothing of him. It did not need him to be a man. That is the pull. That is the lie. Being needed as a man is the very thing that would heal him.

Flee, Paul tells the Corinthians. Not manage. Not moderate. Flee, the way Joseph ran from a house with a woman in it who wanted him. If the itch is your exit, here is what is yours. Tell one man the whole truth. Do not say I struggle with purity. Say what, how often, since when, and what it is costing. The itch lives on secrecy. It starts to die the day a brother knows. Cut the supply. Filters on every device. The password in that brother's hands. The phone charging in the kitchen overnight. A child's rule, yes, for a man acting like one at two in the morning. And deal with the loneliness, because that is what this is. There is a whole stretch on brothers later in this course. Its first line belongs here. The itch shrinks in men who are known.

I will not decorate this. If your home exit is the itch, the four marks are not a soft pep talk. Rejects passivity means you stop managing the secret. Accepts responsibility means you say the whole truth to one man. Leads courageously means you put the password in his hands before you feel ready. Expects God's reward means you quit collecting the small check the screen pays in the moment.

Movement two, summarized. Write this down. The itch is relief that refuses to be known. Secrecy is the fuel. A brother is the extinguisher.

## 6. Movement three. The drift [20:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. The drift.

The third exit is the quiet one. I worry about it most in men past their working years. Nobody notices it. Nothing seems wrong.

The drift is a man leaving without moving. He is in the house. He goes to work. He has not blown up and he has not blown it. He is just not there. His wife quit asking him things because the answers were fine and whatever you think. His kids learned early that Dad's body was on the couch and Dad was elsewhere. The drift is how a man becomes the full chair with nobody in it. That is the chair some of you grew up staring at.

Under it is usually a man who got hurt and decided, without saying so, that caring cost too much. Or a man who got tired, honestly tired, and let rest turn into retreat.

If the drift is your exit, here is what is yours. Name it to your wife. I have been in the house and I have been gone, and I am sorry. She knows. Hearing you say it will change something. Take one thing back. One evening a week with the screens dark and your attention in the room. One question a day you actually wait for the answer to. And go find the man who decided caring was not worth it. Take a brother or a counselor to help you look. That is not weakness. That is a man on the ladder.

I have watched superintendents who could run a hundred craftsmen become the quiet chair at home by forty five. No scandal. No explosion. Just fine and whatever you think, until the rafters sagged and everybody called it personality. It was not personality. It was an exit.

Movement three, summarized. Write this down. Drift is leaving without moving. Name it. Take one evening back. Get on the ladder.
