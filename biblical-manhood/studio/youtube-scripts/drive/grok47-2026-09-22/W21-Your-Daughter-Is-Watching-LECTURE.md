## 2. The claim [04:00]

**MAX:**
Chapter twenty six is titled Your Daughter Is Watching. Here is the claim.

[Slide. If you want to know what kind of man your daughter will marry, look at how you treated her mother this week.]

If you want to know what kind of man your daughter will marry, look at how you treated her mother this week.

Write that down. That is the man. Your daughter is watching. She is learning what a man is. She is learning what a woman deserves. She is learning what she is worth. She learns it before breakfast. She learns it whether or not you mean to teach.

Define the three lessons.

First lesson. What a man is. You are the definition. Every man she meets gets measured against you. If you are gentle, she will expect gentleness. If you are a storm, she will marry a storm. Or she will spend her life flinching at weather.

Second lesson. What a woman deserves. She learns it from how you treat her mother. Defend her. Thank her. Touch her gently. Listen to her. Or roll your eyes and go quiet for three days. She will choose a husband with those notes in her hand.

Third lesson. What she is worth. She learns it from how you look at her. Attention can be something she has to earn with grades and goals and pretty. Or attention is given because she is yours. A girl her father delights in grows up knowing her worth is not up for negotiation. A girl he does not delight in will go looking for a man to tell her what her father did not. The men who volunteer for that job are almost never the ones you would choose.

What the claim does not say. It does not say mothers do not matter. It says fathers are a definition whether they show up or not. It does not say you are doomed if yesterday was a storm. Wade's gladness came back like a muscle. It does not thin the no-daughter seat. Girls in your church are watching too.

Hold that. Scripture under it.

## 4. Movement one. The face in the bleachers [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. The face in the bleachers.

Wade learned the third lesson at a softball field. His daughter Nora struck out to end an inning. Before she walked back to the dugout she turned. She found his face in the bleachers. She read it. He had seen it a hundred times. He had never asked what she was looking for.

She was looking for what his face did when she failed. He was loud and proud on the hits. He also let his face fall on the strikeouts. Not in anger. Just the small drop of a man who wanted it for her. She had learned to check for the drop. Her worth, as far as she could tell, went up and down with the count.

On the drive home he said, I don't come to watch you hit. I come to watch you. Then he went to work on his face. That is harder than it sounds. You cannot fake gladness in front of a child. He decided before every pitch what his face would do after it. He made sure the face she found after a strikeout was the one she found after a double. It took a season. She does not look up after strikeouts anymore. She looks up after hits. She looks for somebody to share them with. Wade says that is the difference between a girl checking and a girl knowing.

I have worn the drop face. I wanted it for her so hard that my face taught her she was only as good as the result. Fixing a face is repentance with muscles. Decide before the moment. Glad she exists. Hit or miss.

Movement one, summarized. Write this down. Checking or knowing. What does your face do when she fails.

## 5. Movement two. What she needs from you [14:30]

**MAX:**
Movement two. What she needs from you.

Delight first, and on your face. Approval is for what she did. Delight is for who she is.

Her mother honored. The most important thing you will ever do for your daughter is love her mother where your daughter can see it. Thank her at the table. Take her side. Apologize to her in front of the children when you were wrong. If you are divorced, she is watching how you treat her mother across two houses. When she tries to get you to take her side against her mother, the sentence is this. Your mom loves you and she's doing her best, and I won't talk about her that way. She will relax when she hears it. She had been afraid she had to choose.

Protection. Keep. Guard. A fence with a gate. You at the gate. Phone known. Boy meets you first. Rescue plan taught early. Protect her from your moods.

Your words. What you call her, she will grow toward. Beautiful, and mean her whole self. Strong. Yours. Say I love you out loud every day. There is a boy coming who will say it to get something. It works on a girl who has been starving for it. Job gave his daughters an inheritance alongside their brothers. He put his name and his weight behind what they were worth.

Your time. Delight without time is only a sentiment. A standing breakfast. A drive with the radio off. A walk after supper that never stops.

Your steadiness when she pushes you away. Most daughters go through a stretch of wanting nothing to do with their fathers. Hurt fathers withdraw. She is not pushing because she does not need you. She is testing whether you will stay. Keep the breakfast in silence. Say I love you to a closed door. When she comes out the other side, she will remember who stayed.

Your faith where she can see it. Your eyes at home. Pray for her by name. Tell her you do. A daughter who has watched her father kneel knows what a man on his knees looks like. She will know to look for one. She notices where your eyes go. The pool. The restaurant. The screen. A father whose eyes wander teaches her that women are to be looked at. A father whose eyes stay home teaches her that she can expect a faithful man.

Movement two, summarized. Write this down. Delight. Honor mother. Protect. Words. Time. Steadiness. Faith. Eyes home.

## 6. Movement three. Every season and no daughter [19:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. Grown daughters, the divorced seat, and if you have no daughter.

If she is grown, she is still watching. She watches whether her father has changed. She watches whether he will show up for her children the way he did not for her. A grown daughter still needs her father's blessing. The window did not close when she left home. If you were not the father she needed, say so in those words. Say it without a but. If you have never told her you are proud of her, the call is overdue. It will mend something she did not know was still broken.

Divorced seat. You cannot be at the breakfast table every morning. Never speak against her mother. Not when your daughter baits you. Not when it is true. Not when you are tired. She is half her mother. Every word against the mother lands on the girl. Be there for everything you are allowed to be. Fight for some of the rest. Call on a schedule. The same night every week. Call through the months of fine and nothing. The night she finally talks for an hour is paid for by every night she did not. Make your house a home she lives in, not a place she visits. Her own room. Her own toothbrush in the cup. Be steady when the world is not. Your divorce shook her ground. A father who is present, honorable, and growing is the ground she stands on while it shakes.

If you have no daughter, there are girls in your church who do not have any of this. Be a man they can watch. Honor the women in your church where the girls can see it. Coach. Teach. Be the uncle who shows up. Do it in the light. Never alone with a girl who is not yours. Always visible. Always above reproach. That is part of what you are teaching her. A good man is safe. Do not thin this seat. Last week's single lanes stay loud into the father chapters. You stay in the room as a future father. You stay as a man who can show up for someone else's kid.

Movement three, summarized. Write this down. Season. Or no-daughter practice this week.
