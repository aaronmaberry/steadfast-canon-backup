## 2. The claim [04:00]

**MAX:**
Chapter twenty seven is titled Give Your Son a Name. Here is the claim.

[Slide. A boy answers to whatever he's called. If you don't name your son, the world will.]

A boy answers to whatever he's called. If you don't name your son, the world will.

Write that down. The only question is who is calling. God gave men the power to name. On a son it is enormous. What you call him, he becomes. What you do not call him, he will go looking for somebody else to call him. The world is full of names for boys. Almost none of them are good.

Define naming.

Naming is not the birth certificate. Naming is the habit of what you say over him. Under your breath. Off camera. When he fails. When he succeeds. When you are tired. The sigh is a name. The eye roll is a name. Why can't you be more like your sister is a name. I have sat with grown men who could still tell me the exact word their father used for them when they were small. Their voices changed when they said it.

What the claim does not say. It does not say mothers do not name. It says a father's names carry weight whether he means them or not. It does not say you are doomed if last month was sigh and eye roll. Ray fixed it with three sentences and a hand on a head. It does not thin the no-son seat. Boys on your street and in your church are being named by silence if not by you.

Hold that. Scripture under it.

## 4. Movement one. The names he's getting anyway [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. The names he is getting anyway.

He is being called things at school. Some of them stick for life. Lazy. Weird. Too small. Too much. He is being called things by a culture that has decided boys are a problem to be managed. That culture names them out of fear. He is being called things by his own failures. Every boy fails at everything. A boy with no one to tell him otherwise will decide the failures are his name. I can't. I'm bad at this. I always mess it up.

And he may be getting called things at home, by you, that you do not hear yourself saying.

Ray heard himself. His wife was playing back a video from their son's birthday. Off camera, under the singing, there was Ray's voice. What is wrong with you, bud? He did not remember saying it. That is how he knew he had said it a hundred times. On the screen his son's face did a small thing and then recovered. That is the way a boy's face does when he has been handed a name he is going to carry. Ray told me he watched those four seconds more times than he watched the rest of the party.

I have done the Ray thing with a different mouth. On a hospital job I used to call a young laborer by the mistake he made in week one. Not mean on purpose. Just the nickname that stuck because it was easy. He answered to it. He became smaller around me. When I finally used his real name for a month, his shoulders changed. A name is not a joke when it is the only word a boy hears.

Movement one, summarized. Write this down. The names he is getting anyway. School. Culture. Failure. And you, off camera.

## 5. Movement two. The three names [14:30]

**MAX:**
Movement two. The three names.

A boy needs three names from his father. Given over and over. From the first week of his life to the last week of yours.

The first is mine. The name of belonging. You're my son, and I'm not going anywhere. Before he has earned anything, he is yours. Nothing he does can unsettle it. Jesus received this name first at the Jordan. Possession first. A boy who knows he belongs can fail and come home. A boy who is not sure will audition forever.

The second is what I see in you. The name of character. Who he is rather than what he did. When he tells the truth about the broken window, you say, That's honesty. You're an honest man. You catch him being what God made him. You tell him what it is called. You speak the name a little ahead of him so he has something to grow into. A boy his father has called honest a hundred times will feel a lie sit wrong in his mouth. A boy who has only been called a liar never will.

Paul fathered in three verbs. Encouraging. Comforting. Urging. Encouraging says, I see it in you. Comforting says, when you fail, you're still mine. Urging says, now live up to it. A father who only urges raises a boy who cannot rest. A father who only comforts raises a boy who will not grow.

The third is man. The name of what he is for. It is the one most men never got. At some point your son needs to hear his father say it in a way he will remember. You're becoming a man, and here is what that means. Then say the four marks sentence. The same one we say every week. Word for word. He hears it from you before he hears a counterfeit. Every boy is asking whether he is a man yet. If his father does not answer first, the boy will take the question somewhere else. A girl. A fight. A paycheck. A truck. Answer first. On purpose. Out loud.

Movement two, summarized. Write this down. Mine. What I see in you. Man.

## 6. Movement three. How to give a name [19:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. How to give a name.

Naming is not a speech. It is a habit.

Every day, catch him and call it. Find one thing your son did that a man does. Name it. That's what a man does. Five seconds. The highest return five seconds in fathering.

Every week, give the blessing. Pick a night. Put your hand on his head. Say three sentences. Who he is. You're my son. I love you. I'm not going anywhere. What you see. This week I watched you tell the truth when it cost you. That's courage. What God says. God made you on purpose. He has work for you. I'll help you find it.

Every year, write the letter. On his birthday, one page. Who he is this year. What you have watched him become. What you see coming. Put it in a box for the day you tell him he is a man. A man holding a stack of letters in his father's handwriting, one for every year of his life, can walk into any room on earth.

In public, praise him. In private, correct him. This is my son. He finished the whole fence with me Saturday and didn't quit. When he has done wrong, take him aside. Shame in public is a name that sticks. Praise in public is a name that lifts.

When he fails, separate the act from the name. He told a lie. You say, That was a lie. You're not a liar. You're my son, who told a lie. Here is what my son does next. He goes and tells the truth to the person he lied to. You're a liar hands a boy a name he will fight or fulfill. You're my honest son who did a dishonest thing hands him a way back.

And always, tell him whose he is. Under your name is a bigger one. Tell him God made him on purpose. Tell him the Father who does not leave is his Father too. Your name over him is good. It is not enough. You will fail him. You will die. God's name over him holds. The best thing your name can do is point at it.

Ray did all of it, badly at first. Once he was looking for things a man does, his son turned out to be doing them all day. Then his son's little sister broke something and was crying. Ray heard the boy tell her, You're not bad. You did a bad thing. Go tell Mom. That is what a boy does with a name. He passes it on.

Movement three, summarized. Write this down. Daily catch. Weekly blessing. Yearly letter. Public praise, private correct. Separate act from name. Point to God's name.
