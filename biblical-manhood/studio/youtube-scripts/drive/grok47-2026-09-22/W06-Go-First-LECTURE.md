[Max standing, lectern or high stool camera right. Book closed. No music.]

**MAX:**
Sit down. You are on time.

Last week I asked you for a that is mine sentence with a period on it. Some of you provided something that was not money. Today, the third mark. Leads courageously. Go first.

This is Steadfast Men. The Way of Godly Men. My name is Max. Session six of twenty four. Same shape. Claim. Text. Movements. Application. Close. One session a week. Notes by hand. Do not binge.

The wind came through after midnight. By morning there was a tree across the street. A whole oak, roots up, crown in a neighbor's yard. Power is out. In every window up and down the block there is a face. Looking at the tree. Looking at the other windows. Doing the math about who is going to move.

Nobody is. Not because nobody cares. Every man on that street is thinking the same three things. Somebody should get out there. Somebody who knows what he is doing should get out there. It probably should not be me. So the faces stay in the windows and the tree stays in the street.

Then one door opens. A man in a coat over whatever he slept in. Work gloves. A saw. He does not call a meeting. He walks to the tree and starts looking at where to cut. For a stretch of seconds that feels longer than it is, he is the only man in the street.

Then the second door opens. Within a quarter hour there are six men and two saws. A truck is backed up to the curb. Somebody's wife brings out coffee she made on a camp stove. Nobody on that street was waiting for permission. They were waiting for a door.

I want you to feel the stretch of seconds when he is the only man in the street. That stretch is the whole chapter. Most men quit at four seconds and call it wisdom. Leadership is staying through eight. Last week we picked up weight. This week we move while carrying it. The third mark is not louder than the second. It is earlier.

The third mark of a man is that he leads courageously. Most men hear leadership and picture the head of the table, the title, the biggest voice in the room. That is not it. Leadership is the first door open, not the biggest title. It is the man who moves while everyone is still looking. He takes the stretch alone, so the second man has somewhere to go.

[Slide. The four marks]

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Write it down. Today's weight is on the third mark. Leads courageously.

## 2. The claim [04:30]

**MAX:**
Chapter six is titled Go First. Here is the claim.

[Slide. Leadership is the first door open, not the biggest title.]

Leadership is the first door open, not the biggest title.

Courageous leadership is not a title, a personality, or a volume. It is going first into what everyone else is waiting on. You stay exposed long enough for a second door to open, because the Lord Himself goes before you.

Write that down. Leadership is the first door open, not the biggest title. First does not get to be sure. First gets to be first.

What the claim does not say. It does not say you must love the front of the room. It does not say fear disqualifies you. It does not say you are actually alone in the street. It says timid is afraid and standing still, and courageous is afraid and moving.

Clear the three false definitions again so they cannot hide in your notes. Not a title. A husband can lead nothing. So can a father. So can an elder. So can a manager. Not a personality. Quiet men open doors. Not a volume. The loudest man is often the most frightened. Going first and taking people with you. Both halves. A loner goes first and leaves everyone behind. A manager says go and watches from the porch.

Hold that. Scripture under it.

## 3. The text [07:00]

**MAX:**
John, chapter thirteen, verses fourteen and fifteen. NIV.

[Slide. John 13:14-15 NIV]

Now that I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also should wash one another's feet. I have set you an example that you should do as I have done for you.

Three observations.

First. Jesus led the twelve for three years. The clearest lesson He gave them about it came with a towel around His waist. Nobody in that upper room was going to wash feet. It was the lowest job in the house. Every one of them was hoping the silence would pass. He got up. He poured the water. He knelt.

Second. An example. Not a memo. He did not explain how to serve. He went first at the thing nobody wanted to do, and then He said, now you.

Third. Watch me is the same pattern in Judges. Gideon with three hundred men in the dark. Watch me. Follow my lead. When I get to the edge of the camp, do exactly as I do. Watch me. That is leadership in two words, and it tells you why so few men want it. The man at the head of the table is safe. The man who goes first is exposed. If the water is cold, he finds out. If the saw is the wrong tool, everyone sees. If nobody follows, he is alone in the street holding a plan.

Write it down. Watch me. Example before memo.

Feet washing and Watch me belong together. One is the lowest job in the house. One is the edge of an enemy camp. Leadership is not choosing the photogenic one. It is going first at the thing the room is avoiding. If your leadership only shows up where you already look competent, it is not the third mark. It is a highlight reel.

Four movements. Let's go.

## 4. Movement one. Where first happens [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. Where first happens.

There may never be a tree across your street. Here is where going first actually happens.

The apology. Go first, and go clean. I am sorry I raised my voice. Stop. Not, I am sorry I raised my voice, and you know how you get. The and you is a hook. An apology with a hook in it is not an apology. It is an opening statement. She can hear the difference before you finish the sentence.

The prayer at your own table. There is a silence at a lot of tables when the food is set down. Everyone knows what is supposed to happen. Everyone is looking at the same man. Plenty of men have prayed in front of a room full of strangers and never once in front of the four people who share their last name.

The rubble. Nehemiah walked the broken wall at night, alone, before he said a word. Then he stood up in front of the people and said what all of them could see and none of them were saying.

Nehemiah two, verse seventeen. NIV.

[Slide. Nehemiah 2:17 NIV]

Then I said to them, You see the trouble we are in: Jerusalem lies in ruins, and its gates have been burned with fire. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, and we will no longer be in disgrace.

We have not been to church since spring. We are behind on the truck. Our son has been drinking and we have been pretending we do not smell it. Rubble that gets named is rubble you can rebuild from. Rubble nobody names becomes scenery.

The turn. Every marriage has cold spells where both people are waiting for the other one to turn. Turn first. You do not turn first because you are more wrong. You turn first because you are the man. The distance across a kitchen is the longest walk a lot of husbands never take.

The direction. Nehemiah did not say go rebuild the wall. He said come, and then he walked to the wall. Leadership says come and moves. Management says go and watches from the porch. The man who decides his family will be in church Sunday is the man in the car with the engine running while everyone else is still finding shoes.

The door has a different handle in every season. The single man goes first when he is the one in the group who says let's actually go Sunday, and drives. The new father goes first when he prays out loud over a crib, feels ridiculous, and does it again the next night. The divorced man goes first with the cleanest apology of his life to his children's mother, whatever she does with it. The father of a silent teenager goes first with one question in the truck. The older man goes first across the church lobby toward a younger one, because the younger one is never going to cross it.

Pick your seat and find your door handle. Do not admire all five and do none. Single. Sunday and the keys. New father. Prayer over a crib two nights in a row. Divorced. Clean apology to their mother. Father of a silent teenager. One question in the truck. Older. Lobby walk. Write the handle in the margin now so application is not abstract later.

Movement one, summarized. First happens in small rooms before big ones. Write this down. Name the room.

## 5. Movement two. What we are afraid of [15:30]

**MAX:**
Movement two. What we are afraid of.

Four things keep a man's hand off the door.

He is afraid of looking foolish. The prayer comes out wrong. The apology sounds rehearsed. He gets to the tree and does not know where to cut. He is afraid of being turned down. He turns first in the kitchen and she does not turn back. He says, son, I have noticed. He gets a shrug and a closed door. He is afraid of being wrong, so he sets no direction at all and calls it humility. And he is afraid of the cost, because there is one. The first man in the street loses his morning. The first man to apologize gives up the argument he was winning. First pays.

Scripture is honest about this. It never once tells a man to stop being afraid before he moves.

Second Timothy one, verse seven. NIV.

[Slide. 2 Timothy 1:7 NIV]

For the Spirit God gave us does not make us timid, but gives us power, love and self-discipline.

Not timid. It does not say not afraid. Timid is afraid and standing still. Courageous is afraid and moving. The man walking toward the tree with his heart going hard is not a lesser version of the brave man. He is the definition of one.

Cost inventory, because first pays and men pretend it does not. Looking foolish. Being turned down. Being wrong. Losing the morning. Giving up the argument you were winning. If your version of leadership never risks those, you have not gone first. You have managed optics. The Spirit does not make us timid. Power, love, self discipline. Moving while afraid is obedience, not personality.

I will tell on myself. I once let a marriage go quiet for four days because I did not want to look foolish starting the apology. I was protecting an argument I was winning. I was losing the kitchen. When I finally turned, the eight seconds before she turned back felt like a year. That stretch is what first feels like from the inside.

Married men. The kitchen turn is often the real tree across your street. The apology with no and you is often the real saw. Do not wait for a storm that knocks an oak down before you open a door you already see.

Movement two, summarized. Fear is not the disqualifier. Timidity is. Write this down. Afraid and moving.

## 6. Movement three. You are never actually first [20:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. You are never actually first.

Here is the part that changes the street. When Moses was handing a nation to Joshua, Joshua had to lead it across a river into a country full of walled cities. Moses did not tell him to be brave. He told him who was already out there.

Deuteronomy thirty one, verse eight. NIV.

[Slide. Deuteronomy 31:8 NIV]

The LORD himself goes before you and will be with you; he will never leave you nor forsake you. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged.

Goes before you. Every door you open, His is already open. Every hard sentence you start at your own table, He is in the room ahead of it. You are never actually first. You are the second door open, and the second door is a very different thing to be. It still costs. It is still exposed. But there is Someone in the street ahead of you, and He is not leaving.

That is why the fourth mark holds the third. A man cannot lead on nerve for long. Nerve runs out around the second door. What does not run out is a reward you are sure of. You go first because you are certain the One who went ahead of you will make the going worth it.

Hebrews twelve, verse two. NIV.

[Slide. Hebrews 12:2 NIV]

fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith. For the joy set before him he endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.

Pioneer. The one who goes first through the worst of it and cuts the trail so the rest can walk. He was alone in the street a lot longer than eight seconds. His friends slept, then scattered. And the writer tells you how He did it. For the joy set before Him. He went first with His eyes on a reward.

This is why session seven comes next and why the fourth mark keeps showing up inside the others. Rejects passivity needs a reward beyond comfort. Accepts responsibility needs a Father who sees the beam. Leads courageously needs a pioneer already in the street. Without God's reward, going first becomes a dare you can only win on adrenaline.

Movement three, summarized. You are the second door open. Write this down. The Lord goes before you.

## 7. Movement four. How to go first [24:30]

**MAX:**
Movement four. How to go first.

You need the first sentence, not the whole speech. Men stall trying to write the entire conversation before they open their mouths. I have been wrong about something. Let me pray. Son, I have noticed. After the first sentence, the room does most of the rest.

Small rooms before big ones. The man who wants to lead at church but goes quiet at his own table has it backwards. Lead in your truck. Lead in your kitchen. Lead in the group text with three friends. The big rooms are made of small ones.

Lead with a question. What has it been like being married to me this year? What are you worried about that you have not said? A question is a door you open and then hold.

Expect the eight seconds. Between your door opening and the second door, there is a stretch when you are alone in the street. Most men quit at four. That stretch is not failure. It is what first feels like from the inside.

And do it on an ordinary day. Trees across the street are rare. Dinner is tonight.

On a crew once, a bad pour was everybody's problem and nobody's move. I walked to the forms with a sawzall while six men watched from the truck. Eight seconds. Then two more doors. We saved what we could. Leadership that morning was not a title. It was a door.

First sentence practice out loud in this room. Silently if you need. I have been wrong about something. Let me pray. Son, I have noticed. Come, let us rebuild. We have not been to church since spring. Say the one that matches your rubble. Then stop writing the whole speech. The room does most of the rest after a clean open.

Movement four, summarized. First sentence. Small rooms. Eight seconds. Ordinary day. Write this down. Dinner is tonight.
