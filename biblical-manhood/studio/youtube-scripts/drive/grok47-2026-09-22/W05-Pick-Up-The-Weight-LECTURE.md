Sit down. You are on time.

Last week I asked you to name the stair you have been stepping over. I asked you to put the first screw in before noon. Some of you dialed. Some of you still have the drill across your knees. Stay in the chair. Today we take the second mark. Accepts responsibility. Pick up the weight.

This is Steadfast Men. The Way of Godly Men. My name is Max. Session five of twenty four. Same shape every week. Claim. Text. Movements. Application. Close. One session. Do the work. Come back. Notes by hand.

There is a wall in your house you cannot take out. It looks like the others. Same paint. Same nail holes where the school pictures hung. The joists above it come to rest on that wall. Everything upstairs is riding on it. The beds. The dressers. The kids asleep in them. Nobody has to think about it for that to be true. A contractor finds it in a minute. He knocks. He looks up. He checks which way the floor runs. He says, this one is carrying the second floor.

You can open up the kitchen without that wall. You can do it only if you put something else under the weight. The weight does not go away when you want a bigger room. It lands somewhere. If nothing is under it, the floor above comes down slowly. Nobody notices the sag until a bedroom door stops latching a year later.

Every house has weight that has to land somewhere. So does every family. So does every job. So does every life. Nobody asks whether the wall thinks it is fair. The weight is where the weight is.

The second mark of a man is that he accepts responsibility. A man looks at the weight in his life. He looks at the part that is his. He says, that lands on me. He does not say it because he caused all of it. He does not say it because the arrangement is fair. He says it because he is the wall. If he steps back, everyone upstairs finds out.

Last week's stair was the first mark. This week's wall is the second. They belong together. A man can reject passivity on a job and still refuse weight at home. He builds a beautiful case for why the weight is unfair. Do not leave session four's screw in the wall and skip session five's sentence. That is mine has to follow stop waiting. Leave it out and you become a man who moves once and then argues.

## 2. The claim [04:30]

**MAX:**
Chapter five is titled Pick Up the Weight. Here is the claim.

[Slide. Responsibility asks whose it is now, not only who caused it.]

Responsibility asks whose it is now, not only who caused it.

Responsibility is not the same thing as fault. A real man answers for what is his now. That includes weights he did not cause. He learns to tell his load from a crushing burden he was never meant to carry alone.

Write that down. Responsibility asks whose it is now, not only who caused it. Fault asks who caused it. Responsibility asks whose it is now. Fairness is a fine question for a courtroom. It is a useless one for a wall.

What the claim does not say. It does not say you caused everything heavy in your house. It does not say you should carry a wife's joy, a grown son's choices, and the church's health on one arm as a badge of honor. It does not say provision is only a paycheck. It says the weight with your name on it lands on you. Pointing does not move it.

I need the high performer and the crushed man in the same room for this claim. High performer. You may already be carrying three loads that are not yours. You call it leadership. Crushed man. You may be pointing at every weight that is yours. You call it honesty about fault. Both of you need the two words Paul uses. Both of you need the seventy. Neither of you gets to skip that is mine.

## 3. The text [07:00]

**MAX:**
Genesis, chapter four, verse nine. NIV.

[Slide. Genesis 4:9 NIV]

Then the LORD said to Cain, Where is your brother Abel? I do not know, he replied. Am I my brother's keeper?

Three observations.

First. The first words out of a man after the fall are the oldest sentence in the language of men. God asks what happened. Adam points. The woman You put here with me. Two fingers in one breath. One at his wife. One at God. None at himself. His son learned the grammar. Cain hands back a question.

Second. Yes. That is the whole answer to Am I my brother's keeper. Yes. It is the answer for every man who has ever said not my department. Not my kid. She knew what she was getting into. Nobody told me.

Third. Notice what both men were protecting. A version of the story in which the weight belonged to someone else. That is all blame is. It is a story with the weight moved.

We will hear Galatians six on load and burden. We will hear Numbers eleven on Moses and the seventy. We will hear First Timothy on household provision. We will hear Isaiah fifty three on the One who took it up. Primary text is the question and the true answer. Write it down. Yes. I am my brother's keeper. Not his savior. His keeper.

Keeper is a limited word on purpose. Savior is Jesus' job. Keeper is the man who does not pretend he does not know where his brother is. Cain's question tried to make ignorance sound like theology. It still does. Not my kid. Not my department. Not my problem. The grammar is ancient. The answer is still yes.

Four movements. Let's go.

## 4. Movement one. Not mine [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. Not mine.

Good men get stuck here because they have confused responsibility with fault. A man can be at no fault at all. The layoff. The diagnosis. His daughter's anxiety. The father who left him nothing but debts. Every one of those can still be his to carry.

I want you to feel how natural pointing is. It is the oldest grammar. It is also how a wall fails. While you are building the case for why the weight is not fair, upstairs is still riding on you.

Here is how not mine sounds, so you can hear yourself. Not my department at work when the mess is in your field. Not my kid when the forming is happening in your house. She knew what she was getting into when the temperature of the marriage is yours to answer for. Nobody told me when you are grown and the card is still unpaid.

For the wounded man. What was done to you matters. It is not the same sentence as what you do now. The course is not a verdict on the wound. It is a description of what is still available to you as a wall.

Illustration. I once spent three weeks documenting why a delay was the architect's fault. I was right. The owner did not need a brief. He needed a man who said the schedule is mine to recover. Being right about fault left the second floor sagging.

Good men love fault language because it feels like justice. I am not against justice. I am against using justice as a way to leave a bedroom door that will not latch. While you assemble the timeline of who caused what, children are still asleep upstairs. The wall does not get to file a grievance before it carries. Neither do you.

Movement one, summarized. Blame is a story with the weight moved. Write this down. Fault is not the same as responsibility.

## 5. Movement two. Five things with your name [14:30]

**MAX:**
Movement two. Five things with your name on them.

Here is what is yours. Not everything. The weight that is not yours comes soon. These five, without argument.

Your own life. Your body. Your money. Your word. Your habits. The state of your soul. Nobody else is going to get you to the doctor. Nobody else is going to pay the card down. Nobody else is going to open your Bible. If you are grown, these left your mother's hands a long time ago, whether or not she has noticed.

Your house. If you are married, the temperature of that marriage is yours. It is not split down the middle. You answer for whether it warmed or drifted. If there are children, their forming is yours. The school and the church can help. The boy will still grow up sounding like whoever was in the house.

Your work. The job in front of you, done the way you would do it if the owner were watching, because One is.

Your people. The brother who is struggling. The man at church whose wife just left. The kid down the street with no father in sight. Am I my brother's keeper? Yes. Not his savior. His keeper.

And the fifth weight, the heaviest and the first one men set down. What you have done wrong. Not what was done to you. What you did. The words at the dinner table. The years you were gone while you were home. The thing she does not know. That is yours. Setting it down does not make it lighter. It makes it heavier for everyone else in the house.

The weights shift with the season. The name on them does not. A single man's load is mostly himself, and it is heavier than he thinks, because nobody is home to notice when he sets it down. A new husband learns that a second person's peace now rides on him. A man with a newborn carries a wife who has not slept. A man with a boy who just got his license carries his own tongue. A divorced man is still the floor of two houses, whatever the paperwork says. A man with grown children carries the fifth weight most of all. The things he got wrong when they were small. He can still pick those up with a phone call.

Write the five in your notes with a blank after each. Own life. Blank. House. Blank. Work. Blank. People. Blank. What I did wrong. Blank. Fill one concrete noun in each blank before you leave tonight. If you cannot fill the fifth, ask the person who lives with you. They often remember the words at the dinner table better than you do.

Movement two, summarized. Five weights. The name on them does not move with the season. Write this down. List the five.

## 6. Movement three. Load, burden, and the seventy [19:30]

**MAX:**
Movement three. Load, burden, and the seventy.

Paul says two things a few lines apart that sound like they cancel out.

Galatians six, verse two. NIV.

[Slide. Galatians 6:2 NIV]

Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ.

Galatians six, verse five. NIV.

[Slide. Galatians 6:5 NIV]

for each one should carry their own load.

Carry each other's. Carry your own. Both, because he is using two different words. The first means a crushing weight, more than one back was built for. The second is a soldier's pack. It is a ship's cargo. It is the load a man is issued and expected to carry himself. Jesus used that second word when He said His burden is light.

A man who accepts responsibility can tell the two apart. He shoulders his own load without a speech about it. When a brother goes down under the crushing kind, he gets under it without being asked. Neither is optional. Neither is weakness.

Some men would rather lose a finger than make two trips from the car. They carry their lives the same way. A wife's happiness. The children's every choice. The church's health. Their own salvation. All on one arm, because setting any of it down feels like failure.

Moses got there. A whole nation in the desert was complaining about the menu. He said to God what most men are too proud to say.

Numbers eleven, verse fourteen. NIV.

[Slide. Numbers 11:14 NIV]

I cannot carry all these people by myself; the burden is too heavy for me.

God did not rebuke him. He gave him seventy elders. He said they would share the burden so Moses would not carry it alone. Read that as a man. There is weight God never assigned you. Picking it up is not responsibility. It is pride in work boots. Your wife's joy is not your load. Your grown son's choices are not your load. Carry what is yours. Ask for the seventy on what is not.

Pride in work boots is still pride. I have watched men refuse help on a crushing burden and call it being a rock. Their houses did not experience a rock. They experienced a man who would not make two trips and would not ask for elders. Moses' sentence is holy permission. The burden is too heavy for me. Say it without theater. Then name the seventy.

Movement three, summarized. Own load. Brother's burden. Ask for the seventy. Write this down. Two words for weight.

## 7. Movement four. Provide without disappearing [24:00]

**MAX:**
Movement four. Provide without disappearing.

Most men know one verse about providing. They have heard it preached as a paycheck.

First Timothy five, verse eight. NIV.

[Slide. 1 Timothy 5:8 NIV]

Anyone who does not provide for their relatives, and especially for their own household, has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever.

Read what the sentence is about. Household. Not payroll. The word takes in everyone under the roof. What a child needs from a father is not mostly money. A man can fund a house he is never in. He can pay for years of piano lessons and never once sit in a folding chair in a school cafeteria to hear his daughter play. The empty chair undoes the lessons.

Feed your family. That is the floor. It is only the floor. Presence is part of provision. A man who has traded all of the second for more of the first has not provided. He has found a very expensive way to disappear.

Connect this to week one if you were here. Disappearance into work with a bonus attached. Provision without presence is that disappearance with a verse taped to it. First Timothy will not let you reduce household to payroll. The empty chair undoes the lessons. Feed them. Be there. Stay there when it gets loud. The beam is not for admiration. It is for walking on.

What a household needs from the man carrying it is a floor that does not move. A teenager pushes because he is checking whether it holds. A wife brings the hard thing to a man she is sure will not buckle when she says it. You do not have to feel strong. You have to be there. You have to stay there. You have to not move when it gets loud. Upstairs, nobody thinks about the beam. They walk across the floor without testing it. That is what the beam is for.

How to pick it up. Say, that is mine. Out loud, to the person it concerns. Not, I can see how you would feel that way. That is mine. Stop at the period. That is mine, but is a different sentence. Everyone in the room can hear the difference. Adam's answer had a but in it before the word existed. Say the first part and close your mouth.

Own the outcome, not the effort. The roof does not grade on effort. Either the rain is coming in or it is not. A man who says I tried to get home for dinner is describing his intentions. His kids are describing the chair.

Carry your load, then get under a burden. In that order. The man who is helping everyone else while his own pack sits in the driveway is not generous. He is hiding. The people who can see it are the ones at home.

And ask for the seventy. Name the weight God never assigned you and hand it back to Him. Find two or three men and hand them the part that is crushing. That is not setting responsibility down. It is the most responsible thing you will do this year.

Isaiah fifty three, verse four. NIV.

[Slide. Isaiah 53:4 NIV]

Surely he took up our pain and bore our suffering, yet we considered him punished by God, stricken by him, and afflicted.

Took up. Bore. He picked up a weight He had not earned, over a wreck He did not make. He never once said the woman You put here with me. The cross is that is mine, spoken by the one man in history who could have said not mine and been right. For the joy set before Him He endured the cross. That is the fourth mark living inside the second. You do not shoulder weight because you are strong. You do not shoulder it because it is fair. You shoulder it because the Father sees the beam nobody upstairs ever thanks, and He is the one who pays.

I will tell on myself. There was a season I funded every lesson and missed every recital. I called it providing. My daughter called it an empty chair. Both were true. Only one was the wall.

How to pick it up again, slower, because this is the application seed. Say that is mine out loud. Stop at the period. Own the outcome, not the effort. Carry your load, then get under a burden. Ask for the seventy. If you reverse the order and become everyone's helper with a pack in the driveway, you are hiding. Home always knows.

Movement four, summarized. Presence is part of provision. Write this down. Expensive disappearance is still disappearance.

That matters for the man who thinks responsibility is only for people who caused the mess. It matters for the man who thinks accepting weight means pretending the mess was fair. Jesus did neither. He did not pretend. He did not point. He carried with His eyes on a reward.

A crisis bigger than one back is burden language. A season that floors you is burden language. Confusing them produces two failures. Soft men set down load and call it humility.
