## 1. OPEN [00:00]

[Max standing, lectern or high stool camera right. Book closed. No music.]

**MAX:**
Sit down. You are on time.

Last week I asked you for a first door. Some of you prayed at your own table. Some of you said a clean apology. Some of you stayed through eight seconds and heard a second door. Good. Stay in the chair. Today we open the fourth mark, because the other three cost something now, and a man will only keep paying if he is sure the payout is real.

This is Steadfast Men. The Way of Godly Men. My name is Max. Session seven of twenty four. Same shape. Claim. Text. Movements. Application. Close. One session a week. Notes by hand. Do not binge.

I want to put a picture in front of you before I state the claim. A folding table at a yard sale. A cardboard box on it. Somebody has written FREE on the side in marker. Inside: trophies. A little gold man swinging a bat with one arm chipped off. A bowling pin on a marble base. A plaque for top salesman from a company that does not exist anymore. Somebody's whole life of Saturdays is in that box. Proudest nights. Radio up on the drive home. Nobody is taking them. By noon the box goes back in the garage. Next spring it goes to the curb.

I do not tell you that to make you sad. I tell you because every one of those was once a reward a man lived for, and it worked. For a night, it worked. That is the thing about rewards you can hold. They all work, for a while, and then you need another one.

The fourth mark of a man is that he works for God's pay, not the room's. It is the one that makes the other three hold. Refusing to wait, picking up the weight, and going first all cost something now. Take this mark away and the other three turn into a brave face on a bad deal. Why get up off the step if no one is keeping score? Why say that is mine when blame costs nothing later? Why open the door first if this street is all there is? The other three marks are questions until this one answers them.

[Slide: the four marks]

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Write it down. Today's weight is on the fourth mark. Works for God's pay, not the room's.

## 2. THE CLAIM [04:30]

**MAX:**
Chapter seven is titled Live for a Reward That Lasts. Here is the claim of the chapter, and it is the claim of this session.

[Slide: Every man lives for a reward. The only question is which one.]

Every man lives for a reward. The only question is which one. Live for the reward that lasts, God's pay, or you will spend a life collecting checks that clear for a night and end up with a box on a folding table.

Write that down under the four marks. I am going to define the terms so you do not hear this loosely.

A reward is whatever a man is actually steering toward when nobody is preaching at him. Not what he says he values on Sunday. What he checks on the drive in. What he will burn an evening to collect. What he will quit for when it stops paying.

God's pay is not a health plan and not a bigger house. It is the Father who sees in secret, the inheritance Colossians names, the Well done and Come and share your master's happiness that Matthew records. Welcome. More entrusted. His joy thrown open. A son who wants his Father glad he came home.

What the claim does not say. It does not say applause, money, being wanted, being right, a father's nod, comfort, or being remembered are evil. Most are good in their place. It says every one of them has a bottom, and every one of them is not God. It does not say working for God's pay is mercenary. Look at what the reward is. It is Him.

Hold that. Scripture under it.

## 3. THE TEXT [07:00]

**MAX:**
Matthew, chapter six, verses nineteen through twenty one. NIV. Jesus never told men to stop wanting a reward. He told them to want a better one, and He was specific about where to keep it.

[Slide: Matthew 6:19-21 NIV]

Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moths and vermin destroy, and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where moths and vermin do not destroy, and where thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.

Three observations. Take them down.

First observation. Moths, vermin, thieves. He is describing the yard sale. Earth treasure works until it does not. Heaven treasure is not a mood. It is a location the moth cannot reach.

Second observation. A few lines earlier in the same sermon, He tells you how the other kind gets stored. When you give to the needy, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, so that your giving may be in secret. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you. Every other reward on the list needs an audience. This one requires you to lose the audience on purpose.

Third observation. Colossians three, twenty three and twenty four. Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving. Whatever you do. The invoice. The diaper. The spreadsheet. The reward is not reserved for men on a stage. It is for the man on the ladder and the man on the night shift and the man doing dishes with nobody watching, if he is doing it for the Lord. Not the job. Who it is for.

Say it with me. Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.

So the text gives us the vault. The claim gives us the diagnosis. Small rewards steer a man until he trades up.

Four movements. Let's go.

## 4. MOVEMENT ONE: WHAT YOU ARE ALREADY LIVING FOR [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. What you are already living for.

You do not have to decide whether to live for a reward. You already do. Every man does, all day. The only question is which one. Here are the usual ones. Write the list.

Applause, even if it is only a nod from a room. Money, or more exactly the number, the one you check on the drive in. Being wanted, which is the reward the single man is quietest about and the married man forgets he is still chasing. Being right, which is the reward a young husband will burn a whole evening to collect. The father's nod, whether he is alive to give it or not. More men are still working for that one than will say so. Comfort, which is what the new father is living for around week six and the retired man most afternoons. And being remembered. The box of trophies. The name on something. The hope that what you built outlasts you by a little.

None of these are evil. Here is what they have in common. Every one of them has a bottom, and every one of them is not God. Applause fades before you reach the parking lot. The number goes up and the feeling does not come with it. The father's nod, if it finally comes, turns out to be smaller than you made it. Comfort is the one that never says no and never says enough. You can live your whole life collecting rewards that work for a night and end up with a box on a folding table.

Let me tell on myself so you know I am not teaching from the balcony. For years on hospital jobs I lived for the number and the nod from the owner. Early close. Clean inspection. Text chain of congratulations. I would drive home with the radio up and still feel empty by the driveway, because the check had already cleared in the parking lot of the job trailer. Site Max got paid. Home Max was broke. Same man. Wrong vault.

I want the high performer to hear this without flinching. Your competence is not the problem. Collecting your whole reward from competence is the problem. I want the wounded man to hear that chasing the father's nod is not vanity. It is unfinished business aiming at the wrong person. Both need the next movements.

Movement one, summarized. Write this down: you are already living for a reward. Name which one before you pretend you are above the list.

## 5. MOVEMENT TWO: TREASURE THAT KEEPS [15:00]

**MAX:**
Movement two. Treasure that keeps.

The widow's driveway shoveled before her kitchen light comes on. The check with no name on it. The good word about a man who will never hear that you said it. When nobody sees, one Person still does, and He is the one who keeps the account.

That is how heaven treasure gets stored. Not by louder religion. By secret obedience. Jesus said men who do good for the crowd have received their reward in full. The moment you are checking who saw, you have been paid, and it was a small check.

I watched a superintendent stay late for three weeks training a young foreman who would eventually take his chair. Nobody clapped. The owner never knew. The young man did. Years later that foreman told me the old man's reward was not the raise he never got. It was sleeping like a man who had put something in a vault the moth could not touch. That is Colossians on a job site. Whatever you do. Who it is for.

Notice what secret treasure does to a Tuesday. It frees you from performing the cereal bowl. Last week we talked about going first while exposed. This mark funds that exposure. You can open the door without an audience if the Father is the audience. You can lose an argument you could win if being right stopped paying enough to be worth the damage. You can stay in the marriage and the church and the town when leaving would be easier and you could justify it.

Write this down: every other reward needs an audience. This one requires you to lose the audience on purpose.

Movement two, summarized. Moths own the yard sale. The Father owns the secret.

---

## 6. MOVEMENT THREE: THE REWARD IS HIM [20:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. The reward is Him.

Is that mercenary, working for God the way you would work for a bonus? Look at what the reward is.

Matthew twenty five, verse twenty one. NIV. His master replied, Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful with a few things. I will put you in charge of many things. Come and share your master's happiness.

Well done. More to do. Come and share my happiness. That is not a payout. That is a welcome. The reward for serving Him is more of Him, closer, with more entrusted and His joy thrown open to you. A man who wants that is not a mercenary. He is a son who wants his Father to be glad he came home.

Moses understood the trade. He grew up in the palace, with the biggest treasure room in the world down the hall, and he walked out of it to stand with slaves. Hebrews eleven, twenty six. He regarded disgrace for the sake of Christ as of greater value than the treasures of Egypt, because he was looking ahead to his reward.

Looking ahead. That is the whole posture. Not gritting his teeth. Looking past the treasure room to something worth more, and choosing accordingly. A man can walk away from almost anything if he is looking at something better. He cannot walk away from much of anything if all he can see is what he is leaving.

Be clear about what this does not promise, because the counterfeit is loud. God's pay is not a health plan. It is not a bigger house or a smoother year or a guarantee that your kids turn out. It does not promise the man who stays a warmer marriage by spring, or the man who forgives an apology back. Men who preached that have left wreckage. Paul, who had been beaten and shipwrecked, put it this way in Second Corinthians four. Our light and momentary troubles are achieving for us an eternal glory that far outweighs them all. So we fix our eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen.

Light and momentary. From a man with scars. He could say it because he had done the math with the right numbers, and the troubles were not being subtracted. They were achieving something. The reward does not cancel the hard years. It is what the hard years are building.

Movement three, summarized. Write this down: Well done is a welcome, not a bonus check. Looking ahead is how a man leaves Egypt without living in regret.

## 7. MOVEMENT FOUR: WHAT IT DOES TO A MAN [24:00]

**MAX:**
Movement four. What it does to a man, and how to live for it.

You can tell a man who is working for God's pay without asking him. He does good that nobody sees and does not mention it later. He loses arguments he could win. He stays when leaving would be easier. He takes the low place at the table and the low job on the crew without checking who noticed. He forgives, not because the other man deserves it but because the account he cares about is not the one between him and that man. And he plants what he will not taste. He prays for grandchildren who do not exist yet. He teaches a boy to work who will teach his own boy after he is gone.

None of that makes sense if the payout is here. All of it makes sense if it is there.

How to live for it. Five practices from the chapter. Write them.

Fix your eyes daily. Paul's word is fix, not glance. Before your feet hit the floor, one sentence: what I do today is seen, and it counts.

Do one good thing a week that no one will ever know about. Not your wife. Not your brother. Not the men you meet with. If it leaks, it was not the one.

Rehearse well done. Say the words to yourself, in His voice, on the way home from a day nobody thanked you for. That is not vanity. That is remembering who you work for.

Notice when you are performing. The moment you are checking who saw, you have been paid.

And pray with heaven in view. Most men pray for Thursday. Pray for Thursday, and then lift your eyes past it.

Put the four marks on one man and stand back. He does not step around the thing that needs doing. He says that is mine and stops at the period. He opens the first door while the street is still watching. And he does all of it with his eyes on a reward the yard sale cannot touch. There is an old word for that kind of man. Steadfast.

Seats, blunt. Young man: your applause addiction will train you to quit when the room goes quiet. Trade up now. Single man: being wanted is a loud reward. Secret obedience is quieter and stronger. Married man: being right has burned enough evenings. Older man: being remembered is already trying to steer you. Plant what you will not taste. Wounded man: the father's nod you are still chasing is too small a vault. High performer: your early closes have already paid you in full more times than you admit.

Movement four, summarized. Write this down: steadfast is four marks with eyes on a reward the curb cannot claim.
Before application, I want to walk the fourth mark one more time so it does not stay abstract.

The other three marks ask a man to pay now. Stop waiting on a Tuesday when the couch is easier. Pick up the weight when blame would be cheaper. Go first when the window face is safer. If there is no God's pay, those payments are a bad deal, and eventually a man stops making them. That is why this chapter sits where it sits. It funds the course.

Say it plainly for the seats. Young man: you are being trained to live for applause that dies in the parking lot. Trade up before the habit hardens. Single man: being wanted will steer your evenings unless a better treasure sits higher. Married man: being right has already cost you intimacy you cannot buy back with flowers. Father: the nod you never got will make you either harsh or hollow with your kids unless you take that account to the Father. Older man: being remembered is already writing your speeches. Plant trees you will not sit under. Wounded man: small rewards feel like justice. They are still too small. High performer: you have been paid in full more times than you admit, and you are still hungry. That hunger is evidence, not failure.

Rehearse the vault language until it is yours. Moths. Vermin. Thieves. Secret. Father who sees. Inheritance from the Lord. Well done. Come and share. Looking ahead. Light and momentary achieving eternal glory. Fix your eyes. One secret good. Notice when you are performing. Pray past Thursday.

I want you to feel the difference between a reward that needs an audience and a reward that requires you to lose the audience. The first makes you a performer of manhood. The second makes you a son at work. Site Max can collect the early close. Home Max still has to decide who the dishes are for. The invoice can be for the Lord. The diaper can be for the Lord. The spreadsheet can be for the Lord. Colossians did not reserve the inheritance for men with microphones.

Do not confuse God's pay with a prosperity slogan. Paul had scars when he said light and momentary. The hard years are not a bug in the system. They are achieving something if your eyes are fixed on what is unseen. That does not make pain good. It makes pain not wasted.

If last week's first door felt expensive, this week tells you why you can keep opening doors. The clap may never come. The Father still sees. The yard sale still cannot touch what He keeps.

Write one sentence on your card under the four marks: what I do today is seen, and it counts. Put the card where your eyes go in the morning. When the small reward starts steering you on Wednesday, you will have a sentence ready that is larger than the nod you are tempted to chase.

Live for a reward that lasts. Then get back to work. That is how the chapter ends, and I cannot improve it. Trade up. Then pick up the invoice, the diaper, the shovel, the first door, and do them for the Lord.

May He fix your eyes where the treasure is, and may you hear one day the words that make every unseen thing worth it.

Let me say the fourth mark without poetry one more time so nobody leaves with a slogan.

Every man lives for a reward. Applause. The number. Being wanted. Being right. The father's nod. Comfort. Being remembered. Name yours. Trade up. Store treasure where moths cannot reach. Lose the audience on purpose. Work the invoice and the diaper as unto the Lord. Want Well done as welcome, not as a bonus. Look ahead like Moses. Fix your eyes daily. Do one secret good. Rehearse Well done on the thankless drive home. Notice when you are performing. Pray past Thursday. That is the chapter. That is the mark. That is how the other three keep paying.

If you try to stop waiting without God's pay, you will eventually decide the couch is smarter. If you try to pick up the weight without God's pay, blame will look like wisdom. If you try to go first without God's pay, the eight seconds will feel like stupidity. The fourth mark is not decorative. It is structural.

I have watched men stay in hard marriages, hard churches, and hard towns because they were looking ahead, and I have watched more gifted men leave easier fights because the only reward they could see was comfort. Looking ahead is not denial. It is math with eternal numbers. Second Corinthians says the troubles are achieving something. That sentence can hold a man when the yard sale cannot.

For the man who thinks secret obedience is small: the widow's driveway, the unnamed check, the good word never reported back to you, those are how heaven treasure is stored. Jesus said the crowd-payout is received in full. Small check. Do not cash it if you want the greater one.

For the man who fears this is mercenary: sons want their Father glad. Well done. More entrusted. Share my happiness. That is family language, not contractor language. You are not hiring God. You are coming home.

Write the list of small rewards again on the back of your card. Circle the one that steered you this month. Draw an arrow to Father who sees. That arrow is the week.

One last pass for the man who will try to keep both vaults open.

You cannot. The heart follows treasure. Matthew six is not suggesting a balanced portfolio. Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also. If the number is still the treasure, your heart will be on the drive in. If the Father who sees is the treasure, your heart can stay in a secret driveway before dawn.

Steadfast is the old word for the man who stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's, with his eyes off the curb box. Live for a reward that lasts. Then get back to work. Secret good this week. Small reward named to a brother. Card sentence in the morning. That is the job.

I will not add a fifth small reward to chase called spiritual reputation. If you do the secret good and then fish for a way to be seen as the kind of man who does secret goods, you have been paid. The Father who sees in secret is enough audience. Keep the secret. Name the small reward to a brother. Carry the question which reward. Come back next week ready for the empty chair, because many small rewards are attempts to fill what a chair left empty.

Last line before the job. Every man lives for a reward. The only question is which one. If yours has been small, you took the nearest one because it paid tonight. Trade up. The Father who sees in secret does not lose the account. Fix your eyes. Do the secret good. Tell the brother the small reward, not the secret. Then get back to work on an ordinary Tuesday with heaven in view.

Session eight will ask about the empty chair. Hear the link now. Many small rewards are a man trying to collect from applause, numbers, and nods what a father never put in his chest. Trade up anyway. God's pay is not a substitute dad. It is God. Still trade up.
