## 1. Open [00:00]

[Max standing, lectern or high stool to his right. Book on the lectern, closed. Wide shot of the room, then to camera. No music.]

**MAX:**
Sit down. You are on time.

Last week I told you manhood is proven on ordinary Tuesdays, not highlight reels. Some of you went home and did the worksheet. Some of you named a disappearance out loud. Some of you are still deciding whether this course is for you. Good. Stay in the chair. Twenty four weeks is still too long to do for anyone else. The first seat is yours.

This is Steadfast Men. The Way of Godly Men. My name is Max. Session two of twenty four. We are still working one chapter a week. Same shape every time. I state a claim. I put Scripture under it. I teach it in movements. I give you an application. I close the same four ways. By the end of the course you can run the close without me.

How to take this class has not changed. Watch one session. Do the work. Come back in a week. Do not binge four of these on a Saturday. Notes by hand. Four or five sentences you can say back without looking.

Before I state the claim, I need to name something the book names in the first paragraph of chapter two. A man sat across from me at a diner a few years back. Midlife. Not angry. Tired the way a man gets tired when he has been aiming at a target that will not hold still. He said, I feel like I have been handed six instruction manuals for being a man. Every one of them contradicts the other five. I am supposed to act like that makes sense.

I have heard that sentence since, from men in every season. Young men. Young fathers. Men who quietly quit listening. Older men watching the picture they lived by get mocked on a grandchild's screen. Same diner sentence. Different booths.

I told him what I am going to tell you for the next half hour. The confusion is in the world. The gap you feel is real. You are not crazy.

Some of you needed that sentence last week after the cereal bowl. Some of you need it because you have been carrying the hum for twenty years and calling it personality. Some of you need it because another man handed you this course and you are still deciding whether the confusion is in you. Settle that before I state the claim. The first seat is still yours.

Here is the course in one sentence. You will hear it every session, word for word.

[Slide. The four marks]

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Write it down. Four marks. Rejects passivity. Accepts responsibility. Leads courageously. Expects the greater reward, God's reward. Next week we take that sentence apart mark by mark. This week we deal with the noise that makes the sentence hard to hear. A lot of you heard it last week. By Wednesday the manuals were louder than the marks.

## 2. The claim [04:00]

**MAX:**
Chapter two is titled You Are Not Crazy. Here is the claim of the chapter. It is the claim of this session.

[Slide. The confusion is in the world. You were handed crossed maps.]

The confusion is in the world. You were handed crossed maps.

Write that down under the four marks. The fact that you keep getting turned around is evidence against the maps. It is not evidence against you. I am going to define the terms so you do not hear this loosely.

Crossed maps means you were handed pictures of manhood that do not share a north. Be strong. Be vulnerable. Lead. Get out of the way. Your instincts are dangerous. Your failure to act on them is what is wrong with your marriage. One voice says manhood is a costume. Another says it is the most important thing about you. A third says it is whatever you want it to be. A fourth says it was settled long ago and you are simply failing at it. You can hold all of those in your hands. You cannot walk by them.

What the claim does not say. It does not say you have no work to do. It does not say every feeling you have about yourself is accurate. It does not say the world is the only problem and you are fine. It says the first problem is not that you are defective for wanting a clear picture. The first problem is that the picture you were handed does not hold together. You have been blaming yourself for the fact that it will not.

I need that line to land for the high performer and for the wounded man alike. The high performer hears permission to stop hitting every room's target. The wounded man hears that naming broken maps is not the same as escaping responsibility. Both need the next movements. Neither needs another week of self-verdict dressed up as manhood.

Hold that. Now Scripture under it.

## 3. The text [06:30]

**MAX:**
Genesis, chapter one, verse twenty seven. NIV. The book opens the true picture on the first page of the Bible.

[Slide. Genesis 1:27 NIV]

So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them.

Three observations. Take them down.

First observation. Male and female is on purpose. God made human beings in His image, male and female, and He called it good before anyone had done anything with it. Your manhood is not a problem to solve. It is not a residue the species should have outgrown. It is a way of carrying the image of God. It was His idea.

Second observation. The text does not start in your chest. It starts with what God did. A lot of the manuals on the shelf start with what a man feels. Some of that is true. Feeling wild does not leave a mark by itself. Only what a man does leaves a mark. Genesis one puts the foundation under the doing.

Third observation. If God made it, then the instinct that manhood is a real thing and not a costume is right. That instinct is not vanity. It is not nostalgia. It is recognition. You were meant to be handed a picture by other men. Most of us were not. We have been guessing ever since. Wanting somebody to tell you plainly what a man does is the right want.

Say it with me. The book presses this. The confusion is in the world. The gap you feel is real. You are not crazy for wanting a north.

The text gives us the ground. The claim gives us the diagnosis. Crossed maps are the disease. You are not the disease.

Four movements. Let's go.

## 4. Movement one. The picture you were handed [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. The picture you were handed.

Every man alive today was handed a picture of manhood that does not hold together. Most of us have spent years trying to make it hold anyway. We blame ourselves when it would not. I want to look at that picture plainly. Then you can stop blaming yourself for the fact that it does not work.

Start with home. The book points ahead to a later chapter called The Empty Chair. For now it is enough to say this. Whoever sat at the end of your childhood table taught you what a man was before you could read. For most of us the lesson came in fragments. A father who was there and silent. A father who was gone. A father who was a storm. Even a good father left gaps. You started with a piece of a picture. You went out to fill in the rest.

The world had plenty to offer. None of it was consistent. The culture told you manhood is a problem. It said the part of you that wants to build and compete and protect and take charge is residue. Then the same culture mocked men who were passive, who could not commit, who would not grow up. It never noticed it had asked for exactly that.

The screen gave you three options. The fool. That is the father in every sitcom. The brute. Villain, or sometimes the hero. And the lone hero. Every boy wanted to be him. Here is the trouble with the lone hero. He has no tired wife. No father who calls twice a year. No son who will not talk. No church. No brothers. No ordinary week. He is a man with no house. You were trained to admire him while you were living in one.

The marketplace said a man is what he earns. The internet said a man is what he watches. Some corners of the church gave you spiritual leader so soft you could not have told a grown man what to do on a weekday. Other corners gave you warrior and king so loud there was no room for a mortgage, a sick mother, and a shift at the plant. The shelf of books for men tends to start inside the chest. Feeling wild. That does not leave a groove on a life by itself.

Put the fragment from home next to the manuals from the world. That is what most men are trying to live by. It was never a picture. It is six maps that do not share a north. Every bearing contradicts the last. A man who tries gets turned around and calls the dizziness his own fault.

Let me tell on myself so you know I am not teaching from the balcony. When I was about thirty two I kept three different versions of myself running. Site Max. Church Max. Home Max. Site Max could walk a hospital job at six in the morning and tell you what would fail by ten. Church Max nodded in the right places and never said a hard thing at a table of men. Home Max was tired and agreeable. None of them was quite me. I thought the exhaustion was the job. It was the costume changes.

Here is why the lone hero map is especially cruel. It trains you to admire a man who never has to do Tuesday. Then you go home to a house that needs Tuesday. You feel like a failure for needing a house. The book is not asking you to hate strength. It is asking you to stop calling a man with no house the ideal while you are trying to keep one standing.

The church corners matter because they sound holy. Soft spiritual leader can become a way of never saying a hard thing. Loud warrior king can become a way of never doing a quiet thing. Both leave a mortgage unpaid in different ways. Both leave a sick mother without a call. Both leave a shift at the plant looking like it is beneath the real work of manhood. It is not beneath it. It is most of it.

Movement one, summarized. You were handed crossed maps. Write this down. Six maps that do not share a north are not a picture you failed to live. They are maps you cannot walk by.

## 5. Movement two. What the crossed maps do [15:30]

**MAX:**
Movement two. What the crossed maps do to a man.

I have watched those maps do three things to men. You have probably felt all three.

They make a man wait. When you do not know what a man is supposed to do, the safest move is no move. A lot of male passivity is not laziness. It is a man standing very still because every direction he could go has been declared wrong by somebody. He is not on the tailgate because he is lazy. He is on the tailgate because he is confused. He has decided confused men should not drive. That is the first mark touching down early. Rejects passivity. Passivity often starts as confusion wearing a safety vest.

They make a man perform. If you cannot find the real target, you learn to hit whatever target the room wants. You are one man at church and another at work and another with your buddies and another with your wife. None of them is quite you. The costume changes wear you out. A performer is not a liar exactly. He is a man who never got a stable picture and learned to borrow one from whoever was watching.

They make a man quietly ashamed. This is the one I see most. Hand a man contradictory instructions he cannot live up to. He does not usually conclude the instructions are broken. He concludes he is. He carries a low hum of failure under everything. He does not talk about it, because talking about it would confirm it. The man at the diner had that hum. Most of the men I sit with do. It is not a sign something is wrong with them. It is the sound of a good man trying to obey six maps at once.

Notice something about those three outcomes. Waiting looks like humility. Performing looks like competence. Shame looks like seriousness. None of them looks like the boy on the couch with the blinds down. That is why this chapter is hard to hear. The maps do not produce cartoon passivity. They produce respectable men who cannot find north.

I sat with a superintendent on a hospital job who could run a hundred craftsmen. He could not tell you what he believed a man was supposed to do at eight thirty at home. He was not lazy. He was mapped to death. Every room had a different north. He hit every one of them. He went home empty.

Here is what I want to say to that hum, slowly. Your instinct that manhood is real is right. Your sense that most of what you have heard does not hold together is sound. Wanting a plain picture is the right want. The answer, when it comes, is simpler and harder than anything on the shelf. Simpler, because it fits in one sentence. Harder, because the sentence has to be lived on ordinary days. No feeling in your chest can do that for you. No burst on a big day can do that for you.

Movement two, summarized. Wait. Perform. Shame. Write this down. The hum is the sound of a good man trying to obey six maps at once.

## 6. Movement three. One pass through the room [21:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. One pass through the room.

The contradiction lands differently depending on where you are standing. Whatever your season, you are not alone in it. I am going to name the seats the book names. Find yours. Not the dinner party version. The actual one.

The young man. You hear from school that your restlessness is a disorder. From your phone that a man is whatever gets attention. From youth group that a man is a servant leader. From the older boys that a man is whoever has had the most girls. You are trying to be all of it at once. You think the fact that you cannot is a problem with you. It is not. The maps are the problem.

The young father. You are supposed to be present and provide. Lead and defer. Stay strong for a newborn and stay in touch with your own feelings. All in the same week. You are failing at most of it because it cannot all be done. The hum under your exhaustion is not evidence you are a bad father.

The man in midlife. You have quietly given up. You have decided manhood is whatever you happen to be doing, mostly working and coming home tired. You have stopped listening because every voice contradicted the last. Your resignation looks like peace. It is not. It is a man who folded the maps and sat on them.

The older man. You lived by a picture handed to you long ago. You are watching that picture get mocked on every screen your grandchildren hold. You have begun to wonder whether the thing you built your life on was a mistake. It was not. Some of what you were handed was true and some was not. The true parts are still true. You need somebody to sort it with you. You do not need somebody to tell you it was all wrong.

Same maps. Different seasons. None of them alone.

For the high performer. You are good at being watched. The manuals love you because you can hit whatever target the room wants. That is not the same as having a north. If you can succeed in four rooms and still feel the hum, the maps are still broken. Success is not the cure for crossed maps. It is often the costume that hides them longest.

For the single man. Your unstructured hours are not the advantage you think they are. Without a witness, the manuals get louder and the performance gets cheaper. Nobody is on the step waiting. That makes the diner sentence easier to ignore and harder to heal. Write your sentence anyway.

For the wounded man. Something happened, to you or by you, and the hum got louder. Hear me. Naming crossed maps is not the same as denying what you did or what was done. It is clearing the fog so you can see which weight is actually yours. We pick up weight in session five. Tonight we set down what was never yours to carry as a verdict on your manhood.

Movement three, summarized. Same maps, different seasons. Write this down. None of them alone.

## 7. Movement four. What comes next [25:00]

**MAX:**
Movement four, and it is short. What comes next.

I am going to hand you a picture. The frame of it is one sentence. Next week, The Four Marks, puts that sentence in your hands on purpose. You already know the words. Next week we take them apart so you can see how each mark looks on a man in the middle of an ordinary week.

It does not start in your chest. It starts with what you do. It has room for the man with the mortgage and the shift at the plant. It has room for the single man at the back table. It has room for the divorced man in the apartment. It has room for the old man who just turned in his badge. It does not require you to feel wild. It requires you to get up.

I am not going to pretend next week will feel like a finish line. A sentence you can carry in your pocket is still a sentence you have to live. A man with a stable picture is a different kind of tired than a man with six maps. One is the tiredness of work. The other is the tiredness of contradiction. We are after the first kind.

Before you leave tonight, hear the book's last word on this chapter. The tiredness you feel is not failure. It is the tiredness of a man who has been carrying a contradiction. Strength spent on the wrong load. If the hum has been under your life a long time, let it get quieter tonight. Not because you have solved anything. Because you have seen the maps for what they are. Set them down. Soon you will have something that holds.

The fourth mark already peeks through here. Expects the greater reward, God's reward. Rest from carrying what was never yours is part of that reward. Courage to pick up what is comes next.

May God give you rest from carrying what was never yours to carry, and the courage to pick up what is. That is the book's blessing at the end of the chapter. I am not going to improve it.

Movement four, summarized. Simpler and harder. Write this down. The answer fits in one sentence and has to be lived on ordinary days.

## 8. Application. Name the manuals [27:00]

**MAX:**
Application. Every session gives you a job. Here is the job from chapter two.

[Slide. Name two contradictory instructions and where each came from.]

The shame lifts when you can see the maps for what they are. Say out loud two of the contradictory instructions you were handed about being a man, and where each one came from.

Not abstract. Concrete. Be a provider and be home for dinner. Be a warrior and never raise your voice. Lead your house and do not overstep. Feel everything and man up. Write them. Say where they came from. Home. Screen. Church. Locker room. Boss. Book. Phone.

A man who has been performing for six audiences needs to hear his own voice. Write down in one sentence what you actually believe a man is supposed to do. Do it before next week's marks land. Keep it. You will compare it later. Do not polish it for church. Write the sentence you would say if nobody was grading you.

Walk the page with me. Left side. Instruction one, and the source. Instruction two, and the source. Right side. Your one sentence. If you cannot find two contradictions, ask the person who lives with you. Ask what they have watched you try to obey at the same time. They can usually name them faster than you can.

I did this the week before we recorded. My two contradictions. From the job site, a man never shows weakness. From a small group years ago, a man shares everything or he is hiding. Both cannot be the whole truth. My one sentence before the marks. A man shows up for what is his when it costs him. Next week we will see how that sentence either matches the marks or needs to be rewritten.

For the single man. Your manuals are often louder because there is no wife and no kid to force a Tuesday test. Your sentence still matters. Write it alone at a table. God sees it.

For the older man. You may think the manuals are a young man's problem. They are not. The grandchildren's screens are handing them new maps every day. You are one of the few men in the room with evidence about which old pieces were true. Your sentence this week is not nostalgia. It is sorting. Write what still holds.

That is what accepting responsibility looks like before we name the second mark formally. This one is mine to see clearly. The maps too.
