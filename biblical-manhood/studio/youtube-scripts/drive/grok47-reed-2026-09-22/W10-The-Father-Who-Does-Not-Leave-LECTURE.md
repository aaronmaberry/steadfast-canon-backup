## 1. Open [00:00]

[Max standing, lectern or high stool camera right. Book closed. No music.]

**MAX:**
Sit down. You are on time.

Last week I asked you to name your exit. Anger. Itch. Drift. Some of you walked to the mailbox. Some of you told a brother the whole truth. Some of you are still saying fine. Stay in the chair. Today we take the pain to a Father who does not leave.

This is Steadfast Men. The Way of Godly Men. My name is Max. Session ten of twenty four. Same shape. Claim. Text. Movements. Application. Close. One session a week. Notes by hand. Do not binge.

Stand near the doors of any grade school at three and watch the kids come out. They are not looking at the cars. They are looking for one car. You can tell from the sidewalk which ones find it. Their backpacks slide down their shoulders. They quit looking. They are talking before the door is open. Some do not find it. They look at every car. One ends up on the curb with a teacher who keeps checking her phone while the line empties.

I know men who have been in that pickup line every school day for years. Twenty minutes early. Radio off. They swore their kids would never be the one on the curb. They have kept that promise. They have also never once been the kid who comes through the doors and finds the car. Nobody was in the line for them. They learned to walk home.

This chapter is about a Father you can stop looking for. He is in the line before the bell. And because I know that for some of you the word father is the whole problem, that is where we have to start.

[Slide. The four marks]

A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

Write it down. Today the fourth mark and the whole course hang on whether the Father you are working for is the original or a poisoned copy of your dad.

## 3. The text [07:00]

**MAX:**
John, chapter fourteen, verse nine. NIV. Scripture turns the picture around through the only Man who has seen the Father.

[Slide. John 14:9 NIV]

Jesus answered: Don't you know me, Philip, even after I have been among you such a long time? Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father. How can you say, Show us the Father?

Three observations.

First. Whatever Jesus is like, the Father is like. Not whatever your dad was like. Whatever Jesus is like. That is the correction.

Second. Luke, chapter fifteen, verse twenty. The boy who left, spent everything, and came home smelling of pigs with a speech ready.

But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him.

You only see somebody a long way off if you have been watching the road. Grown men in that world did not run. This one did. He is not waiting for you to get it together.

Third. John, chapter fourteen, verse eighteen.

I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you.

Orphans is the word He chose, because that is what the disciples were about to feel like, and some of you have felt it since you were a boy. And He says, not you. I will come to you.

Matthew, chapter three, verse seventeen belongs here too. At Jesus's baptism, before a sermon or a healing.

This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased.

Three things every boy needs to hear from his father, and Jesus heard them at the start, not the finish. That is the order the Father works in. He blesses first and sends you to work second.

Write it down. Seen Me, seen the Father. Long way off. Not orphans.

Four movements. Let's go.

## 4. Movement one. The trouble with the word [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. The trouble with the word.

What God is your Father does inside a man depends on the chair at the end of his childhood table.

If the chair was empty, Father means someone who is not coming. You believe God exists the way you believe your dad exists. Somewhere. Doing something. Not here.

If it was full and silent, Father means someone in the room with nothing to say to you. You pray the way a man talks to a newspaper.

If it was dangerous, Father means a mood you read before you speak. You spend your Christian life working out what God is angry about today, fairly sure it is you.

If it was good, you are ahead of most. But your father got tired and missed things, so somewhere in you sits the assumption that God does too.

You held the only picture of a father you had up to heaven and assumed that is what was there. That picture has been poisoning your prayers for years. The work of this session is not to pretend the copy was fine. It is to set the copy down.

I did this badly for a long time. I could preach responsibility and still pray like a man filing a report with a silent supervisor. God was real. God was distant. God was grading. That was not theology. That was a chair I had baptized.

Movement one, summarized. Write this down. Name which false father you have been praying to. Not coming. Silent. Dangerous. Or good with a gap.

## 5. Movement two. What He is like [15:00]

**MAX:**
Movement two. What He is like.

He comes toward you. While he was still a long way off. Watching the road. Running. Not folded arms at the house.

He tells you who you are before you have done anything. Blesses first. Sends second. If you are in Christ that voice is over you right now. Your own father may have made you earn what should have been given. This one does not.

He carries you. Moses said it. A father carries his son the whole way, even when the boy gets heavy. He knows what you need before you ask. The need does not put Him off. The man who learned that needing is dangerous should sit with that a long time.

He disciplines you, because you are His. Hebrews calls the hard season God treating you as a son. The man with a dangerous father needs to hear that discipline and rage were never the same thing. It is not proof He left. It is proof He stayed.

And He does not leave. Never will I leave you. Never will I forsake you. I will not leave you as orphans. I will come to you.

Put that next to last week's exits. He is not afraid of your anger. He is not disgusted by your itch. He has not drifted from you while you drifted from Him. Bring Him the exit first, before the brother and the counselor, and do not clean it up on the way. Father, I am about to blow up at my kids. Father, I am reaching for the phone again. He is the one watching the road.

Movement two, summarized. Write this down. Comes toward. Blesses first. Carries. Disciplines as a son. Does not leave.

## 6. Movement three. The hardest thing for a man to do [20:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. The hardest thing for a man to do.

This ought to be the easiest chapter in the book. For a lot of men it is the hardest. A man who has fathered himself since he was a boy does not know how to be fathered. He would tell you he believes God is a Father. But when it comes to actually being carried, actually letting somebody else hold the weight, everything in him locks. He has held his own weight so long that leaning feels like falling.

I know God's my Father. I don't know how to be anybody's son. Nobody ever showed me the receiving end. That is the honest sentence. I would rather you say it than fake one.

Here is what works in men who learn to be sons late.

Call Him Father out loud. Not God. Not Lord. Father, alone, when it is awkward. Paul says the Spirit in you already cries Abba, Father. Abba is a small boy's word. You are allowed to use it. It will feel strange in your mouth. Say it until it does not.

Bring Him your exit. We covered that.

Let Him tell you who you are. Read something He said about His sons every day and say it back as true of you. A man who was never told who he was needs telling many times. The Father is patient.

Let yourself be carried. Sometime this year something will be too heavy. Your instinct will be to grit it out alone. Instead, say out loud, I cannot carry this. Carry me. Then let a brother help. That is not a man failing. That is a man learning to be a son, which every real man is first.

And stay in the house. The Word, where the Father talks. The church, where His other children are. Prayer, where He listens. A man who only visits on holidays never learns what His voice sounds like on an ordinary afternoon.

Movement three, summarized. Write this down. Leaning feels like falling until you practice being a son.
