Picture a man with kids in high school and his name on two vans. Saturday mornings he still drives to his father's place and hands the man the wrench. The disposal quits. A gutter pulls loose. He does the work while his father stands close enough to tell him how. His father has never once said good. He says should have used a longer screw. Same house. Two ways out. One boy slammed the door. One boy never really left. Neither was handed off. Neither could tell you when he became a man.

## 4. Movement one. What a handoff is, and the counterfeits [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. What a handoff is, and the counterfeits.

For most of history, boys did not become men by accident. Men made them men, with words and a weight and a welcome. The older men took the boy, put him through something, told him what a man was, gave him a man's responsibility, and brought him into their company. He woke up a man because the men had said so and meant it.

A boy who is not handed off does not stop wanting it. He goes looking, and the world has counterfeits ready. Sex is the first, and it does not work. There is no older man in it, no word, no weight, only a girl who never signed up to make anybody a man. A paycheck is the second. A boy with a paycheck is still a boy. The third is the slam. The fight, the drink, the birthday that is supposed to change something, the leaving in the middle of an argument. Every one is a boy trying to hand himself off, and you cannot. A handoff is received, from men. The fourth is the quietest, and it is the orbit. A man who was never handed off can spend his whole adult life circling the house he grew up in, waiting for someone inside it to tell him he can stop. And a man who does not know he is a man does not take a man's weight, because he is still waiting to be told it is his. Everything else this course asks of a man rests on this.

I lived the orbit longer than I want to admit. Not always physically. Professionally. Emotionally. Waiting for a nod from men who were never going to give it, while younger men on my crews needed a hand on a shoulder and a sentence I was too busy auditioning to say.

Movement one, summarized. Write this down. Four things make a handoff. Four counterfeits fake one. Nobody hands himself off.

## 5. Movement two. How a man receives it now [14:30]

**MAX:**
Movement two. How a man receives it now.

Hear it from the Father first. He speaks identity before performance, and He has already said the words over you, this is my son, whom I love. If you are in Christ that is a settled fact. The handoff you never got from your dad you have from your Father. Start there or nothing else holds.

Then ask an older man to say it. It will feel awkward. Do it anyway. Find a man older than you who is living what this course describes and tell him the truth. Nobody ever told me I was a man. Would you? Then let him put a hand on your shoulder and say it. More older men than you would guess have waited their whole lives to be asked for something real.

Be welcomed by brothers. A few men who know you and count you as one of them. If no circle pulled you in, go find one that will.

Take the weight before you feel ready. David said act like a man, and it is a command. Solomon did not feel like one. He was young and scared and told God so. David told him to act like one anyway, and the acting was part of the becoming.

And mark it. Write down the date. The day you decided. The day the older man said it. The day you asked God in the dark. Jacob got a limp. You will settle for a date inside the cover of your Bible. But a man who cannot point to when he became a man will always half-wonder whether he did.

Some fathers will never hand you off. Not because you have not earned it. Because they never received it themselves, or because keeping you a boy is the only way they know to stay a man. You can honor that father and stop orbiting him in the same week. You go because he is your father, not because you are still waiting. That is leaving without slamming. You take the word from the Father and from men who will give it.

I know a man who heard it from an older brother in his church after they swapped a water heater. Washing up at the utility sink, the older man said, I have watched you a long time. You are a good man. I would trust you with anything I own. The younger man dried his hands twice. That was a handoff, and it did not come from the chair. God arranges men like that for the ones who ask.

If you are the older man in the room, you are the other half of the solution. Saying so is the whole job. Most of the handoffs in Scripture were a man, a word, a hand on a shoulder, and a go. You can do that at a utility sink this week.

Movement two, summarized. Write this down. Father first. Ask an older man. Take weight. Mark the date. Older men, say it.

## 6. Movement three. The knob and the slam [19:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. The knob and the slam. Chapter twelve opens at the front door.

One man walks in without knocking. Gray in his beard. Mortgage across town. In the refrigerator before anyone has said hello, his wife still out on the porch. Another man has not stood on that porch in years. He can quote you the fight that ended it. He would tell you he is free of the place, and he is not. A third man may still have a key on his ring, but he knocks and waits, and when the door opens he says, Hey, Dad.

The third man left home. The other two are still standing in the doorway. One with his hand on the knob. One with his back turned. Neither one knows it.

If your mother's opinion still outranks your wife's in your own house, you have not left. If your father can make you feel like a boy with one comment, and you arrange your decisions to keep that comment from coming, you have not left. If you are single, well into your thirties, and every major decision still gets cleared with your parents, you have not left. You moved out. That is not the same thing. I am not telling you to stop listening. A wise man takes counsel his whole life. I am telling you that a life that orbits somebody else's kitchen never gets a center of its own.

Now the slam. Some of you left houses no boy should have had to live in. I will not tell you that you should have stayed. I will not send you back to a man who is still dangerous. But a slammed door does not stay behind you. The man who cut his father off in fury has his father in every room of his life, because fury is a kind of attention, and your life organizes itself around whatever you are furious at. He is more bound to his dad than the son who calls every Sunday. He just cannot see the rope, because it is made of anger instead of affection. And it teaches your children how to leave you. Kids watch how their father treats his father. That is the only manual they get.

Movement three, summarized. Write this down. Knob or slam. Both are still in the doorway. The knock is the design.

## 7. Movement four. Honor, forgiveness, and the knock [23:30]

**MAX:**
Movement four. Honor when he was not honorable, and the knock.

Honor does not mean pretending. You do not have to call your father good if he was not. Scripture never asks you to lie. Honor is how you treat the office, not how you grade the performance. A soldier salutes the rank when he cannot respect the man wearing it, and he is not a hypocrite.

With a hard father, honor looks like telling the truth about him without mocking him. It looks like providing for him in old age even if he never provided for you. It looks like praying for him, even if the whole prayer is, Father, I do not know how to pray for this man, but You know him.

And it looks like forgiving him. Forgiveness is not saying it did not matter. It is not trust. You can forgive a man and still never leave your children alone with him. It is not always a relationship. Some tables are not safe. Forgiveness is a man deciding that the debt his father owes him will be settled by God and not by him. He stops collecting. He stops making his peace wait on an apology. Romans, chapter twelve, verse nineteen says leave room for God's wrath. Read that as relief, not threat. You do not have to be the one who makes him pay. Joseph named the wrong. He said you intended to harm me. Then he said what God did with it anyway. Honesty about the harm. Trust in what God was doing. No bill presented.

If your father is alive and it is safe, there may be a conversation to have. The knock, in four parts. First, what you needed and did not get. One sentence, no list. Second, what you are grateful for. Even a hard father usually left you something. Third, what you are going to do. I am going to raise my kids differently, and I am done carrying this against you. Fourth, the door. I would like to know you as a man, if you are willing. Do not make an apology the price of the door staying open. How he answers is his weight, not yours.

I know a man who did this with a father who had never said a kind word to him. He sat in the truck a long while before he knocked. He said the four things on the porch and stopped talking. His father did not apologize. He looked past him at the field across the road for a long time. Then he said, You eat yet, and turned toward the kitchen, leaving the door open behind him. Supper was the closest thing to an embrace the old man had in him. The son took it, because he had quit needing more than his father could give.

If your father is dead, or too dangerous to approach, say the four things anyway, to God and to a brother. The words need to leave you even if they cannot reach him.

Seasons, short. Single man. Decide, then tell them with love. Young father. Say in front of your mother, we are doing it my wife's way. Divorced man. Do not go home emotionally and make your mother your confidante. Older man on the other side of the door. Be the father in Jesus's story. Make the call that does not get answered. If he knocks, set another plate.

Movement four, summarized. Write this down. Honor the office. Tell the truth. Hand the debt to God. Knock when you come home.
