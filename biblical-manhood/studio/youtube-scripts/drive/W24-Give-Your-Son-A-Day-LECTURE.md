## 2. THE CLAIM [04:00]

**MAX:**
Chapter twenty nine is titled Give Your Son a Day He Will Not Forget. Here is the claim.

[Slide: The day is the marker. The Tuesdays are the handoff.]

Most men never got handed off, and spent their lives waiting for a sentence that never came. Do not let your son wait. Give him a day. Not a party but a marker: men, a word, a weight, a welcome. The day is the marker. The Tuesdays are the handoff. A stone with no story is only a rock.

Write that down. Define day.

A day is not a birthday dinner with cake. A day is men around a boy saying, on purpose and out loud, you're a man now, here's what that means, and you're one of us. God builds markers into His people. Rocks on a riverbank so a boy in another generation asks his father a question and gets a story. Most Christian families have none for their boys. Birthdays and graduations, and no day that marks the thing that matters most. So he guesses. He picks a marker from whatever is lying around. Counterfeits, reached for because nobody handed him the real one.

What the claim does not say. It does not say the ceremony changes everything by itself. Alton knew that. The week after he called the boy to pour a footing. It does not say you missed the window forever. Missed it? Do it anyway. It does not open Part five of the book. This is still one lecture. One day. One handoff. The series closes here on purpose.

## 4. MOVEMENT ONE: FIVE STONES AT FIRST LIGHT [10:00]

**MAX:**
Movement one. Five stones at first light.

Grandfather Alton went in first, then the father, then an uncle, then the man the father meets with every week for twenty minutes, then another brother. Nobody spoke. They set the stones at the boy's feet, a stack you could see from the bridge.

Alton read from Joshua. Each man spoke. The uncle told the boy the worst thing he had ever done and what a man does afterward. His father put a hand on his shoulder and said the three names he had been saying over him for years, and then the fourth: You're becoming a man. That's what today is. Here's what that means. And he said the sentence. Then he handed the boy a card with it in his own hand.

Then Alton took a brass plumb bob out of his coat, heavy for its size, the boy's name and the date cut into it, put it in the boy's hand, and told him what it was for.

The boy carries it every day. His father has seen him take it out in a parking lot and just hold it. The stones are still on the bank. He walks down there sometimes.

I never got a day. I got a license, a first job, and a handshake at graduation that meant good luck more than you're one of us. For years I treated that absence like weather. Something that just happened. Then I watched a boy hold a plumb bob in a parking lot and realized weather is what you call it when you do not want to build a marker. If you never got one, you can still give one. Giving it will heal something in you that receiving it would have.

Movement one, summarized. Write this down: men, word, weight, welcome. A day he can point to.

## 5. MOVEMENT TWO: THE DESIGN [14:30]

**MAX:**
Movement two. The design of a day that works.

When: as he starts to cross. You will know it by the voice, the shoulders, the way he has begun to watch men. Missed the window? Do it anyway.

Who: the father and two to four men the boy respects who will be in his life going forward. Not a crowd. A crowd makes it a performance. If you have no men, you have a year to go get them, and you know how from the brothers weeks.

Where: outside, early, somewhere with weight. A hill he has climbed, or water he was baptized in. The place should ask something of him just to get there, if only a walk in the cold and dark.

Before: he knows something is coming but not what. You meet the men once and ask each to prepare one true thing: a story of what a man is from his own life, one failure and what he did afterward, one charge for the boy. Five minutes each.

The morning: you arrive by going somewhere, in quiet, so the weight settles on him. One man reads the Word. The men speak in turn and the boy stands and takes it. He will remember every word. The father gives the four marks, the sentence and a line on each, the card in his own hand, and which mark he is weakest at himself. The father gives the name, hand on the shoulder, the three and then the fourth. If you have never said it before, you will probably cry. Let them see it. A boy who watches his father weep while naming him learns something no ceremony can teach. Then the token, weighted rather than expensive: a knife, a Bible with the date inside, a great grandfather's watch, a tool. Then the prayer, every man's hand on him, the father last. Then somebody says it plainly: You're one of us now. Then breakfast, where the men talk to him like a man for the first time, and he notices.

Movement two, summarized. Write this down: when, who, where, prepare, morning order. Card. Token. One of us.

## 6. MOVEMENT THREE: THE PLUMB BOB AND THE DAY AFTER [19:00]

**MAX:**
Movement three. The plumb bob, and the day after.

Here is what Alton told the boy.

This is a plumb bob. I hung it on every wall of the house your dad grew up in. You hang it next to what you're building and wait for it to stop swinging, and when it settles it points straight down. It doesn't care what you think is straight. Gravity doesn't have opinions.

You'll build a lot, and some of it will be crooked, and you won't be able to tell, because everything around it is crooked too. Then you hang this and wait. God's Word is your plumb line. It doesn't move. Hold your life up next to it, let it settle, and see where you're off.

And it's heavy for its size on purpose. A light bob swings forever. A heavy one settles fast. A man's supposed to be like that, steady and weighted and hard to knock off true. Your name's on it so you remember who you're supposed to be when you're not sure.

The boy has already held that bob next to a decision or two, his father says. Whether to go along with something at school. Whether to tell the truth about a grade. It is a piece of brass. It is also this whole book, in his pocket.

Choose your own token, and say what it means out loud when you hand it over.

The ceremony changes nothing by itself. I have watched fathers pour everything into the morning and go back on Monday to treating their son like a boy, and the boy learns the day was a nice thing his dad said once. What makes it real is that from then on the men treat him differently. More hill. His opinion asked and weighed. A man's jobs, a man's correction, and when he fails, a man's standard and a man's grace. The day is the marker. The Tuesdays are the handoff. Give him the day without them and you have given him a stone with no story.

Alton built for the county for a working lifetime. He says the retirement dinner was the best night of his career and the next morning the worst of his life. The dinner said well done and the morning said nothing. Markers are stones on a riverbank, not the land. The land is Tuesday. Alton knew it, which is why the next week he called the boy to come help pour a footing. The day said you're a man. The week after started making him one.

Movement three, summarized. Write this down: token with meaning spoken. Day after equals handoff. Footing next week.
