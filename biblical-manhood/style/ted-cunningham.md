## Voice

### Sentence length and rhythm

He talks like a standup who also has a three-point outline.

- **Setup is conversational and often long.** Run-on with “and,” “so,” “right?,” “okay.” He restates the premise until the room is with him.
- **Punchline is short.** After the wind-up: “I am a dog.” “You’re fired.” “For a good time, call home.” “You need a paycheck and a few jokes.” “Land the plane, please.”
- **Thesis after the laugh is one clean sentence.** “Enjoying life and marriage, according to Ecclesiastes 9:9, is a decision. It’s not an outcome.” “Compatibility is something you create.” “Character trumps chemistry every time” (trailer snippet). “Jesus is my source of life, not you.”
- **Misquote → correction** is a signature gag: he recites “Endure life with your wife, all your miserable days,” waits for the church “hmm,” then: “That’s not what it says.” Same Ecclesiastes 9:9 bit in talks 1, 2, and 7.
- **Lists** as scaffolding: 5 restraints (#5); 12 NSTs / romance-prep steps (#2); “38 benefits of laughter” then jokes he’ll give 42, actually gives ~15 (#4).
- **Call-and-response.** “All God’s people said—” / “Amen.” “Raise your right hand. Repeat after me.” “How many of you remember…?” “Can I get an amen on that one?” (clip). He turns the room into the tour.

Interview mode (#3, #6, #7) is slightly tighter: he still tells the full bit, but Daly/Fuller interrupt, so punchlines land faster and he names the craft (“callback,” “humor muscle,” “low-hanging fruit”).

### Punchline placement

He **teaches the mechanic out loud** (#1, #2, #3): comedy is “premise, punchline, premise, punchline.” The **callback** is the thing he wants couples to steal: bring an earlier punchline off a new premise 10–20 minutes later (or years later in a marriage). He credits learning this from comedians **Jason Earls and John Branyan** (#2) and from studying comedy “the last year and a half” (#1).

Structure of a bit, seen repeatedly:

1. Ordinary irritation (parking, construction, foodie restaurant, Christmas décor, toilet chain).
2. His stubborn or IFB reading of it.
3. Amy’s move (bicep massage, Andes candy, mint-leaf enthusiasm).
4. Self-own (“I am a dog”).
5. Name the tool: “That’s a callback.”
6. Land the doctrine: decision, character, Jesus as source.

He also **warns that humor is a delivery system**: “while you’re laughing, I’m cramming truth down your throat” (#4). Guests describe the ride as laugh → unexpected hit → hug → hit again (#3).

### Self-deprecation vs punching down

**He punches himself and his tribe first.** Independent, fundamental, premillennial, KJV-only Baptist childhood; “guilt-prone nature”; he sees road signs as laws, Amy sees them as suggestions; he is not a foodie; he hated a couples massage; he “listens real fast”; David Jones to his face: wanting to become a better listener “will be refreshing” (#5). Ethel Barrymore / Mark Batterson: learn to laugh **at yourself** (#4).

**Amy is a character, not a dummy.** She is “way better at this than I am” (#1). She is the queen of the house (#1). She has a “silent, patriotic laugh” (hand on heart, nothing comes out) (#4). Foodie, passionate, Assembly of God “set free in Jesus.” Jokes run **with** her (mint leaf, parking rewards). He explicitly fences **biting, sarcastic, tearing-the-flesh humor** (#3): “We’re not laughing at you, we’re laughing with you.” *A Love That Laughs* and the FoF close both say never at the spouse’s expense.

**Edges that do punch out** (do not import blindly):

- Class/taste: Venti Caramel Macchiato vs Folgers and flip phones; Ethan Allen vs cinder-block shelves; “I don’t want her to go to community college” as the formula joke (#4) — he then says 25% of the room is irritated, which is the point, but it still punches a social rung.
- The “successful woman” with the “ginormous chip on your shoulder” (#1): pastoral confrontation, not a wife gag, but the laugh is at her expense.
- Spanking-nostalgia “how many of you remember…” chain (#4).
- “Karen” apology bit (#5): he apologizes for the culture’s use of the name, then still uses it.
- Microwave men / crockpot women; “show up naked” as the entire wives’ list (#2). Clean by tour standards; coarse for a mixed-age men’s classroom.

### How he handles marriage / sex / conflict

**Marriage:** enjoyment is a **choice**, not a season you wait for. “You don’t have to choose between life and a wife.” First seven years: in love and committed, D-word removed, but they did not enjoy it until they decided to (#1). Date night + “annual abandon” as scheduled joy, plus daily micro-fun. “Stuck” marriages (5–6 on a 10) are his church’s target, not only crisis (#7). WHFC, per him, preaches marriage every second Sunday of the month (#7).

**Sex (#2, trailer):** Hebrews 13:4. He grew up honoring **purity** but not **marriage**. “Sex is dirty, nasty, and ugly. And you should save it for the one you love” (trailer). Amy: “I will be your only fantasy.” Creative marriage-bed ideas “without bringing anything from the world into it.” Men: all-day prep (NST = non-sexual touch, Greg Smalley 12/day, call her, domestic work, playlist, candles, remove kid distractions). Women: one-line punchline. Mature-audience disclaimer on the FoF Date Night DVD. **Not** a purity-culture scare talk and **not** a Playboy talk; it is date-night coaching.

**Conflict:** turn the irritation into a callback instead of a fight (#1–3). Fight as teammates, not opponents (book jacket). **Do not** teach jokes to a couple who needs Hope Restored (#3). Anger is a **secondary** emotion over core fears/buttons (control, failure, judgment for him; disconnection for Amy) (#6). Gary Smalley lines he repeats: unresolved anger is drinking poison expecting the other to get sick; you never bury anger dead, you bury it alive (#1, #6). Ephesians 4:26 read via Psalm 4:4: when angry, **be silent and sleep**, don’t stay up and fight (#5). “The issue is rarely ever the issue” (#1). Worst apology: “I’m sorry you feel that way” (#6).

**In-laws / leaving:** Genesis 2:24. To the mom at a wedding who says she’s gaining a daughter: “Nope, you’re losing a son. And it’s time for you to back away” (#1). Not sampled: the Pinelake in-laws sermon (`mKsZwfPPmqE`).

## Do / don’t for Steadfast Men writers

Blend this **onto** the Steadfast spine (the four marks: Stop Waiting, Pick Up the Weight, Go First, Work for God's Pay). Do not replace the arc with Date Night Comedy.

### Do

- **Callback across sessions**, not inside one gag. If session 02 uses a concrete family image, bring it back in 15 and 24. That is his actual craft, and it is original if the stories are ours.
- **Self-own first.** The man is the punchline (stubborn parking, listening too fast, empty soul), not his wife, not “women,” not the wounded dad in row three.
- **One short thesis after the laugh.** Then sit in it. “Enjoying life with her is a decision” can sit next to “pick up the weight” without swapping the creed.
- **Observational, domestic, named.** Original stories from Aaron’s / Steadfast men’s world (work, sons, the Hill Country, the group), not Ted’s Andes/mint/Macchiato set.
- **Laugh as medicine that helps other medicine go down** (#4). Use it to open men, then hit passivity, depravity, 1 Peter 3:7. He is explicit that sorrow also teaches (Eccl 7) and that some rooms must be dead serious (#3).
- **Teammates, not opponents** on conflict. Fits Cindy Hawks “25 ways” and “live with her according to knowledge.”
- **Jesus as source of life**, spouse and kids as overflow. Fits the gospel close in 24 and Trophy Child’s “Jesus is the priority relationship.”
- **Fence biting humor in the writers’ room** the way he does on air.
- **Match humor to the cluster.** Sessions 02–09 (father wound, mother wound, depravity) are closer to his “don’t joke at Hope Restored” rule. Sessions 15–20 (wife, sex, sons, daughters) can carry more of his lightness **if** the four marks stay the spine.
- **Lead with companionship/image**, then depravity — his ordination anecdote (#7) is useful **pacing** advice, not a denial of sessions 08–09.

### Don’t
