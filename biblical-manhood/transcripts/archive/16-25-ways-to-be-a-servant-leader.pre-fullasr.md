Hey guys, good morning. I am super excited and really quite honored to be with y'all. I admire and respect you so much for all you carry, for prioritizing coming and thinking about how to be a better man. Men that go after manhood are really the heroes of our nation. Our nation needs more men like you. I bring greetings from the women. I'm hoping this morning helps you translate women's perspective, maybe gain insight into your wives or future wives.

A lot of times I wonder if you think women try to mix you up, send mixed messages. We typically don't do that on purpose. Many times we're sending mixed messages because we ourselves are confused.

Case in point: years ago my second daughter was a sophomore, volleyball, made JV. Before district they moved her up for a tournament at Georgetown High School. I go, excitable personality, they put her in, she does well, they don't take her out. I'm wound up. Break between sets, I run to the ladies room. Man coming in the foyer. I think I know that guy, huggable well. I greet him, full body hug around the neck. While I'm up around his neck I think: yeah, I don't know him. I think he doesn't know that I don't know him. Small town, I'll never see him again. I'll let him think he can't remember who I am. So good to see you. Run out. In the bathroom laughing. Come back, he's sitting with the varsity parents. Not going back to a small town. I go explain, babbling. He arranges his face like if I put this face on she'll think I know what she's talking about and maybe it will stop soon. I walked off. I didn't do that on purpose. I was confused. If you feel confused by the women in your world, mixed messages, it very likely is us. It's not you.

25 ways to be a servant leader from a woman's perspective. Tim said I could change it up. I combined some points so I could add about five more. Really like 30. The honeydew list keeps getting longer.

1. A servant leader keeps leading and initiating even when it doesn't go well. God has given you spiritual and social leadership in society and families. That responsibility is firmly, heavily on your shoulders. Sometimes as you act on it, it doesn't go well. Imperfect man trying to lead an imperfect woman: sometimes not well received. Backlash. We as women, like Eve, can be deceived. Temptation to lock in on the inner narrative, clamp down, not receive leadership well. Y'all like Adam tend to know the right thing but go passive. Resistance and backlash play into that. Well I give up, this is impossible. One of the most courageous things a man can do is keep trying to lead, keep initiating, keep moving into the life of his wife, give input and direction and partner with her, even when the tempest is at full fury, even when the shell around the emotions seems fragile. Be brave. A real man keeps initiating and leading even when it doesn't go well.

2. A servant leader stays alert to the blessing flow within the family. Jesus-style leadership is not top down. Culture's leadership is authoritarian, benefits flowing to the leader. Jesus: not so among you. Biblical leadership is more bottom up. I'm here to serve. Benefits from me to you. Measure: which way are the blessings flowing? Occasional blessing-flow direction check. Easy to fall into selfish patterns. No dashboard oil light. Put it on the calendar. Am I giving more benefit or receiving more? Tim takes the most inconvenient option, least comfortable chair, worst car in the family, most burnt piece of chicken when I grill and set the whole thing on fire. Little things. Then financial, time, entertainment decisions: flow toward us. That is not natural. It has to be intentional.

3. A servant leader accepts spiritual responsibility for his family by personally committing to his own spiritual growth and by investing in the growth of his family. Greatest blessing you can give is your own spiritual growth and commitment to Jesus. 1 Corinthians 11:1: follow me as I follow Christ. That's a tough thing to say. Tim has taught biblical manhood 20 years. So much life change that women started coming: you've got to teach the part for women. My husband's changing so much. It's inspired me to be a better wife. When you invest in your own growth and lead by example, we respond. You create the DNA, the ethos. Set the standard by example and communication: church, praying together, initiating spiritual discussions, discipling children. I'm most grateful for Tim's dad. Grew up without a dad. Came to faith in junior high. Plugged into church in high school. Church man: whatever they offer, he's there. Sunday night, Wednesday night. Non-negotiable. Passed to Tim. Even dating we were not going to miss church. On vacation we find a church. That's who we are. Father to father, hopefully to our sons.

4. A leader is willing to say I'm sorry and forgive me. How do you feel when your boss apologizes and owns up? Some of you: that is never going to happen. You don't want to be that guy. Your wife and children are under your leadership. Own your stuff. It melts my heart when Tim apologizes. A couple weeks ago: I'm sorry, I think I came across really critical of you. Completely unnecessary. I cannot remember what the conversation was about. When he said that, it's over. You will never regret those words.

5. A servant leader lets his wife and children into the interior of his life. Not natural. You don't typically start sentences with I feel. Men have a secret nonverbal language. Movies: two men, profound moment, eye contact, a nod, nothing said. We use lots of words. Suggestions: tell stories. Stories where he failed, struggled. Glimpse of the internal world without going in the back door. Will you pray for me about this? Forward thinking. What's concerning him, weighty.

6. A servant leader seeks to understand his wife and interacts out of that understanding. 1 Peter 3:7: live with your wife in an understanding way, with knowledge. Seeks. Doesn't mean you understand. [Manslator video: Fine means stay home. I'm fine means not fine. Ready in 5 minutes means 30.] Or you could work at it. Last time I spoke at the end of biblical sexuality I unpacked women's brains: web, cauldron, pendulum, gift. Go listen to that. We're just so different. Picture of a man's day versus a woman's day: y'all through the day, we're up and down. My daughter Kate at 24: men socially swap libraries, information, information. Exhausting. Then I realized they can hold all that because their heads are empty. She meant: at any given time I have a million things going on. What that person's feeling, what I'm feeling, people not in the room, to-do list. Meme: a woman's mind is a browser with 25,500 tabs open. It's a challenge. It's a command.

7. A servant leader includes his wife in envisioning the future. Y'all are the bearers of destiny, responsible for the big picture. Don't do it in a vacuum. If you plan the big picture and don't involve her, it's hard for her to own. From business you know that. Mention it once, in and out for a woman: diapers, carpool, teenager with a broken heart. Lost in the browser tabs. Second reason: she's smart. Different smart. Maybe more smart. I got the short end of the stick on common sense. I'm not stupid. Valedictorian, magna cum laude, all A's in graduate school. I may not connect all the dots all the time, but I have lots of dots. When Tim includes me I feel valuable. Suggestion: a weekend away once a year, getting away to get it together. Workbook: Getting Away to Get It Together by Bill Wellons on Amazon.

8. A servant leader frequently tells his wife what he likes about her and praises her often in public. Proverbs 31:28: her children rise up and call her blessed, and her husband also, and he praises her. Words, adjectives: kind, smart, pretty, great smile, sense of humor, diligent, industrious, thoughtful, eye for detail, big picture, artistic, creative, logical, frugal, generous, earnest, light-hearted, enthusiastic, intense, gentle, healthy, talented, organized, free spirit, decisive, flexible, leader, servant, multitasker, single focused, sensitive, tough, resilient. Most great weaknesses have a corresponding incredible strength. Praise her in private and in public. You have no idea what it does for a woman to hear her husband say in a matter-of-fact way to someone else something he appreciates. That's for real. He's not just making that up.

9. A servant leader prays with and for his wife on a regular basis. More to intimacy than sex. As a man and woman move toward God they draw toward each other. Bonus: when you draw together in intimacy you're drawing closer to sex. Not the only reason.

10. A servant leader encourages his wife to grow as an individual and ensures time for his wife to pursue her own interests. Tim has more confidence in my gifts than I have in myself. He's not only encouraged but shoved me to pursue interest, skills, gifting.

If you have children, your wife's heart is tethered to her children. When you engage family life and children, you're engaging the heart of your wife. All of this gives you points.

11. A servant leader discusses household responsibilities with his wife and makes sure they are reasonably distributed. Housework is not just her work. It's y'all's deal. Big picture, you're responsible. Factor work, childcare. Clearly distribute. When I'm clear about my piece I'm not disappointed; I'm appreciative when you help. Trend: wives working part-time or full-time. Combining paid and unpaid work, women work about seven hours a week more than men. Improvement: 25 years ago it was 15. Not because men took on more house responsibility. Women lowered their standards for the house. Protect your wife. Reasonable distribution.

12. A servant leader provides financially for his family's basic living expenses and consults his wife on all major financial decisions. Before the fall Adam was given responsibility to provide. After the fall, Genesis 3, still that responsibility, just harder. Doesn't mean a wife can't work and contribute. Responsibility for making sure the family is provided for is on the husband's shoulders. Countercultural recommendation: when planning core living expenses, his means should cover that. Not to limit women. To give them freedom. If your salary covers essentials, women have more flexibility: stay home with kids, volunteer, flex, older parents, emergencies. A family whose high school son was permanently disabled in a car accident: because they had planned this way, the wife could quit working and take care of him.

13. A servant leader keeps his family financially sound and out of harmful debt. Romans 13:8: no outstanding debt. Debt is killing people. Financial Peace University at Hill Country. On your shoulders.

14. A servant leader makes sure he and his wife have drawn up a will and arranged a well-conceived plan for their children in case of death. Women don't want to die, not because they're worried about being dead, because they're worried about their children after. Remove one of the tabs from their mental browser.

15. A servant leader follows through with commitments he has made to his wife and family. Psalm 15:4: a man of integrity keeps his oath even when it hurts. Family time: you're not going to ditch that when you get a better offer. Faithful in little things, trustworthy in big things. She starts reading all your behavior: he does what's in our best interest, not his.

16. A servant leader anticipates the different stages his marriage and his children will pass through. Stages for men, stages for women, lots to do with hormones. Marriage stages: before kids, young children, school age, teenage, young adult, empty nest, grandparents. Anticipate stressors. A lot of couples have no idea how the world changes when kids come. Kids' developmental stages: what they can learn. Relationships with couples a little further down the road. Watch their mess-ups.

17. A servant leader manages the schedule of the home and anticipates the pressure points. You may not feel welcome into managing the schedule. Put on your armor and step in. Many times through 32 years of marriage I have lost my mind taking on commitments way beyond what I can do. Tim has saved me. Every time you say yes to something, you're also saying no to something else. How are we going to have family time if the family isn't around?

18. A servant leader deals with distractions so he can talk with his wife and family. TV, computer, phone: turn them off, shut them down, pay attention.

19. A servant leader initiates meaningful family traditions and regular fun family outings. Value in being a fun husband and dad: we know we're your favorite people. Kate in high school: Dad's kind of weird because he just wants it to be just us. Our kids know Tim would rather be with us than anybody else. Traditions layer on that. Wives can help with creativity. Shocked at goofy traditions from when kids were little that they still want in their 20s.

20. A servant leader takes the lead in establishing with his wife sound biblically supportable family values. Actually discuss them, decide, write them down. We frame ours. Tim taught Real Family Values years ago. Find it online. Transfer those to your kids.

21. A servant leader takes the time to give his children practical instructions about life, which in turn gives them confidence with their peers. Manners, laundry, checkbook, how you resolve conflict with authority or peers. Fail: we didn't teach the girls how to change a tire. Ellen in college, flat tire, guy friends don't know either. Middle-aged guy in Florida, disgusted, changed it. You guys need to teach our kids.

22. A servant leader explained sex to his child in a way that gives them a wholesome perspective. Not easy. Resource: Passport to Purity, Family Life, Dennis Rainey. Amazon. He did it with the guys, I did it with the girls. Particularly sons: talk about porn. Epidemic. Some of you struggle. Your sons do or will. Tim has an ongoing conversation: warning, concerns, and if you're struggling I will help you. Don't avoid me because you're afraid of what I'll say.

23. A servant leader joins a small group of men dedicated to improving their skills as a man, husband, and father. Intentionality in developing as a man screams I love you to your wife. You can't do this alone. Dependent by design. Friday mornings, Guadalajara, Hill Country men at six o'clock. Encourages me so much. Some of y'all call it HCBC AM.

24. A servant leader asks for help when he or his family is floundering. Most of us flounder at some point. A courageous man gets help for himself, wife, marriage, kids. Doesn't let pride, passivity, or shame keep him from taking care of his people.

25. A servant leader keeps starting over. This stuff is impossible. You will fail. Servant leadership requires supernatural enablement. Walk with Jesus every morning. Yield control to the Holy Spirit. Invariably you'll take back control and botch it. Keep starting over, keep yielding. If you take a hundred steps and stumble on every other step but keep starting over, you've still taken 50 steps of progress. The most meaningful hero is not unscathed. It's the guy beat to a pulp who still gets up because he's living for something bigger than himself. You are those kind of men. Legacy in this church family. Hill Country men keep starting over. Heroes to us. Honor to serve under your leadership as your sister in Christ, by your side, partnering for something way bigger than ourselves.

[Prayer.] Father, I thank you for these men. They cling to you. Holy Spirit, empower them. Transform them, grow them, make them even more excellent. They would excel still more in what you've called them to be. We trust you. You will for sure work in our lives. We thank you and celebrate you. In Jesus' name. Amen.
