Tim Hawks:

Morning. For those joining us in evening groups, good evening. For groups meeting all over, including Gonzalez, hello.

Last week we started with Dave Barry on men versus guys. Let me continue. Guys struggle with communicating feelings. A guy is reading the newspaper. The phone rings. He answers, listens ten minutes, hangs up, resumes reading. His wife: Who was that? Phil Wonkerman's mom. Phil is an old friend they haven't heard from in 17 years. Well? Well what? What did she say? She said Phil is fine. He's trying to finish Calvin and Hobbes. She will not let up, district-attorney questions, until she has the entire story: Phil just got out of prison after a murder he committed when he became a drug addict because of guilt when his wife died in a freak submarine accident while Phil was having an affair with a nun, but now he's straightened out, a trapeze artist, almost through surgical sex change, engaged to a prominent member of the Grateful Dead. So in other words he is fine, which is exactly what the guy told her. She wants every detail. That's happened to me, and I just don't understand it.

Last week we began the methodology for our definition of manhood. Today we put that definition together, begin memorizing it, and incorporating it. Methodology: the two Adams. First Adam from Eden; second Adam, Jesus Christ. Two masculine identities. Adam's response leads to a masculine destiny of death and destruction for himself, family, and people around him. Manhood set on an earthly course is typically the model of a declining culture. Jesus: manhood set on a heavenly course, a life-giving being. Follow that example and the destiny leads to life and rewards for himself and those around him. Push the two examples together and four characteristics pop out.

A. The first Adam fell into passivity. The second Adam rejected passivity. When Eve was tempted, Adam was with her. He was not aggressively out disobeying God. He was passive. The serpent tempted his wife. She became deceived. Adam knew what she was about to do was not the will of God, and he stood by. That plunged the human race into destruction.

In contrast, more masculinity in the manger than in Eden. Jesus lays aside heavenly glory and comfort and parachutes into a broken world to save it. Not passive. Moving out to make a difference.

Watch Jesus in another garden. Eden versus Gethsemane. Jesus to his disciples: I'm in anguish, facing the cross. Would you sit here and pray for me? How often does Jesus ask them a favor? Typically he's doing them the favor. In his greatest need they go passive. Tired. Long day. Fall asleep. Three times he wakes them. They're not against him. They give in to feelings and fatigue while Jesus prays and seeks the Father as he saves the world. A real man rejects passivity.

B. The first Adam disregarded his responsibilities. The second Adam accepts responsibility. Same three responsibilities every man has.

1. A will to obey. Whose will: mine, what I feel, think, guts, strategies, intellect, or revealed will? Adam gave into his own will. God had given direction. Because of selfishness he plunged himself, wife, and family into sin. Jesus in Gethsemane: I don't want this. Suffering, dying, becoming sin, separated from you, Father, that's not my preference. But not my will, your will be done. By accepting the Father's will he changed human history. 2.2 billion people look to Jesus as Savior, shepherd, guide, example, and hope.

2. A work to do. God put Adam in the garden to cultivate, keep, care. Produce is available. One restriction. Also raise offspring, perpetuate the race, represent God. He experiments with a better lot by disobeying. Jesus has a work: save the world. It costs everything. He leans in. All men have a role: change the culture, lead families, work in the world. Or we say my work is me: feelings, desires, how I look, how people treat me.

3. A woman to love. Adam was given Eve. Rather than protect her even if she didn't want it, lead her even if she didn't want it, he chose not to love her that way. One of the most unloving moments in history: he watched her commit spiritual suicide and used her to see if God would kill them. Jesus has a woman to love too. Ephesians 5: the bride of Christ is the church. Jesus gave his life for his church.

Rejects passivity, accepts responsibility. Men can run a company, raise an army, build a dam, govern a nation, invent a microchip. Why is it so hard to lead a family, establish a budget with a proactive view of the future, romance a wife through the whole marriage, teach a son and invest character, establish a division of labor, sit down and work through how we run this family as a team? You can put together an org chart at work but not at home. Why is walking with God so hard? Domestic responsibilities: men tend to become passive and not accept them.

C. The first Adam abandoned his post of leadership. The second Adam chose to lead courageously. Adam hides. God calls. "I was afraid, so I hid." Not courageous. He didn't face his wife with truth. He doesn't face his Maker. He blames.

Write this in big bold letters and never apologize: Men were created to lead. When men lead effectively for the right cause, society prospers, women and children thrive.

A Danish journalist and an American journalist discussed the refugee crisis in Germany and abuse of women. Men in Cologne dressed in miniskirts and marched in solidarity. The Danish female commentator: that's our problem, men are too feminine. Men ought to be in the streets protecting their women, not putting on a miniskirt. A woman calling out Europe: men have adopted a feminized approach. People will wake up: we need men. The cry: where are the men? A 12-year-old girl in the mall dressed like the pictures, 13- and 14-year-old boys bumping, giggling, touchy. Where is her father? She's learning she can get masculine attention by letting boys touch her. Where are the fathers of those boys? Abuse in government and on Wall Street: where are the men to stand up and say that's not right?

Every situation Jesus walks into, people find a direction, a path, someone to follow. Called to lead courageously in three areas.

1. Direction. He gave direction with his life. Follow me. Not: you go do that while I pontificate. We complain about national debt while personal debt grows. We complain about morals and don't speak into our families, don't turn off or skip what we object to. We are not talking about the gift of leadership. We're talking about taking care of business, owning your deal. You may not be strong with money, but if you take a home loan you're expected to pay it off. You may not feel visionary. Understand what you've committed to and lead out.

2. Protection. John 10:11: "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep." In the garden, soldiers come, Peter cuts off the high priest's ear, Jesus protects Peter by healing the man, then the cross. What do I need to do to protect people in my world? Sacrifices, time, laying aside goals.

3. Provision. He fed them, feeding of the 5,000. Fed them with his word. Provided in every way.

Ephesians 5:23: "The husband is the head of the wife as Christ is the head of the church." The head has eyes, ears, brain. The head provides direction for the body. Charting direction for family, life, business, church, country. The husband is not to create the direction. Direction is given by God in revelation. God gave you the woman because she can contribute significantly to figure out the direction. At the end of the day you're responsible to make sure the family has a noble, biblical direction, and you'll stick to it. Driving, she's tired, pull in and eat, and you're thinking that's not in the budget. If everybody wants to, I don't want to be the bad guy. Coward. We put together a plan. Honey, if everybody's too tired, I'll make dinner. We've committed.

Verse 25: "Husbands, love your wives as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her..." He feeds and cares for his body as Christ does the church. Feed: provision. Care: protect, watch over, keep safe. Same three: direction, protection, provision. Jesus for his bride. You as a man do that as well.

The greatest obstacle to a masculine vision is our feelings. Not bad parenting, lack of father's blessing, the woman you're leading, rebellious kids, government, church. What's inside you. Feelings-driven leadership revolves around a personal agenda. Truth-driven direction revolves around a transcendent cause. Physical: I'm too tired, this is too early, I can't get up. Emotional: awkward to look a man in the eye and shake his hand. Perception: I didn't get the respect I deserve. A guy threw a chair through a window at work because of perceived abuses. Four jobs. Everybody treats him badly. Operating on feelings. Master your feelings. Set them aside for truth. Greatest struggle of your life.

Jesus is 30, baptized, Father's blessing: this is my son, the one I love, in him I am well pleased. Spirit descends. First thing: Matthew 4, the Spirit led him into the wilderness to be tempted. Before the masculine vision he faces feelings. Three temptations. Physical: 40 days fasting, stones into bread. Power and respect: throw yourself off the temple, force angels, prove you're somebody. Sell out to have it all: kingdoms of the world if you bow down. What am I willing to compromise so I can have it all, be on top, boardroom, applause? He overcomes all three. That takes courage.

Years ago I was convicted I was taking my wife for granted. Plan: take Cindy to a nice place. I'm tight, so that was hard. Apologize: you're wonderful, best wife, I've been off doing my thing. Dinner going well. Holy Spirit: open your mouth. Satan: why ruin this evening? This may be a three or four hour conversation. Visceral lump in my throat. I didn't say anything. Looked in the mirror: Tim, you are a coward. You're letting feelings interfere with your masculine destiny. Your biggest enemy is can you say no to your feelings to say yes to something much greater.

D. The first Adam sought a greater reward. The second Adam expected the greater reward, God's reward. Men don't operate simply because they should. We're wired around a cause because we think it will make things better. Adam hears that eating may improve his station. Jesus expects the greater reward from God.

Twofold motivation: it will make life better here and now for you and people around you. On days when seeds won't produce fruit for years, you need an eternal perspective: someone looking down who will reward this. Investing in a three-year-old may not pay dividends until he's 33. A setback because you will not travel a dishonest path. Hanging in with a woman through menopause, fighting for the marriage. In the moment, without a greater reward, the temptation is this isn't worth it, give up.

Every Christian will stand before the judgment seat of Christ and give an account for every thought, word, deed. Not hell or not. Eternal value of what you did. Wood, hay, straw: burns up, you suffer loss. Or precious, something to put at Jesus' feet in worship. Empty-handed or not.

Hebrews 12:1-2: throw off everything that hinders and the sin that so easily entangles. Run with perseverance. Fix our eyes on Jesus, author and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy set before him endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God. Looking to the Father's applause.

Hebrews 11:24-26: Moses refused to be known as the son of Pharaoh's daughter. Chose mistreatment with the people of God rather than the pleasures of sin for a short time. Regarded disgrace for the sake of Christ as of greater value than the treasures of Egypt because he was looking ahead to his reward. Traded gold, girls, and chariot for sandals in the desert. Led Israel through the desert into the promised land.

2 Corinthians 2:9: no eye has seen, no ear has heard, no mind conceived what God has prepared for those who love him.

2 Timothy 4:6-8: Paul poured out like a drink offering, departure at hand, waiting to be beheaded. "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith. Now there is in store for me the crown of righteousness which the Lord, the righteous judge, will award to me on that day, and not only to me but also to all who have longed for his appearing." Five crowns in the Bible for faithfulness.

John 10:10: the thief comes to steal, kill, and destroy. I come that they may have life, and have it to the full.

Definition: when cut from a biblical mold, the real man is one who rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Challenge: memorize it. Wrestle with it. Am I rejecting passivity, accepting responsibility, leading courageously, expecting God's greater reward? We'll talk marriage, sons, daughters, work, journey, sexuality.

Two images. First: Adam in a plowed field, kneeling, holding the body of his youngest son Abel, brains and blood on the ground, killed by Cain out of anger. This is my fault. I disobeyed God and put death in place. Is that the manhood, the legacy you want?

Second: a youth pastor in Little Rock asks boys, how would you define a real man? Robert Lewis's son Garrett: a real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward. Where did you learn that? I learned it from my dad. Masculinity that breathes life into the next generation. Which model do you want to follow?
