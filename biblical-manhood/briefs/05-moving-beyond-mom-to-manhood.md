# 05 Moving Beyond Mom to Manhood

- title: Moving Beyond Mom to Manhood
- speaker: Tim Hawks (host opens; Waterboy clip)
- date: 2025-10-10
- source: transcript (YouTube ASR). Audio ASR skipped; substantial transcript already present.

## Core claims
- Mother wound is not "mama." It is unhealthy emotional intimacy with mother that later makes a son threatened by women's influence or overidentified with it, lacking a positive masculine image.
- Two wrong responses: overly dominant/hostile toward women (Genesis 3); overly passive/feminized, frozen when women "bark" (last week's Ahab/Jezebel frame).
- Passive-side traits: wait not initiate; prefer group/women's approval; shun risk; struggle to decide and second-guess; high emphasis on feelings and what makes him happy; can be controlled by how a woman feels about him; often marries a "mother wife" and expects women to take care of him.
- Cycle: wife eventually wants a man, loses respect; he feels worse and grows more passive; some marriages in the room have broken up over this. Hawks: a man's greatest need is not a woman's love but her respect.
- Pink Floyd "Mother" as grown men still asking mama whether to build a wall, run for president, trust the government, or whether a girl is good enough.
- Two breaks every son must make: postpartum (physical cord cut at birth) and post-power (move from being nurtured to taking care of women: protect, provide, lead). Post-power is not disrespect; disrespect is the mother-wound pushback. Irresponsible/carefree becomes thoughtful, responsible, self-controlled, contributing.
- Margaret Mead: the biggest societal question is appropriate roles for men; men either build or destroy; they are not neutral.
- Jesus and Mary model the break without disrespect: Luke 2 (age 12, "my Father's house," Mary personalizes "why have you treated us like this," Joseph silent); John 2 (Cana: "Dear woman, why do you involve me? My time has not yet come"); Matthew 12 (who is my mother? whoever does the Father's will); John 19 (on the cross he assigns John to care for Mary). Mary lost her boy and gained a man.
- Masculine motto: do more, say less. Do not call mom and announce she is smothering you.

## Practices
- Personal masculine focus: the problem is not women; answer who am I as a man.
- Establish masculine boundaries (finances, teens, Christmas/mom criticizing wife, laundry, mom talking to the coach). Grow up; do not wait for her to stop.
- Relationships with other men for cheerleading and accountability; a friend who does not accept excuses (call dad; you have a phone and ten numbers).
- If married and the issue is mom, tell your wife you recognize it and will take responsibility. Do not let wife and mom fight while you sit out.
- Fathers: call sons away from mom to manhood. Direct; specific (transcendent cause, masculine vision, code of conduct / what the last name means); employ ceremony. Church has a ministry for 11-12-year-old boys.
- Group question: will I begin to move beyond mom to manhood?
