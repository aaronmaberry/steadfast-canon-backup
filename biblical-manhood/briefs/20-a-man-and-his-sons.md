# 20 A Man and His Sons

- title: A Man and His Sons
- speaker: Tim Hawks
- date: Apr 1, 2026
- source: transcript

## Core claims
- Keep working the manhood plan (Nate has the questions). Without a plan you are not going anywhere.
- Need a plan to raise sons to maturity because of internal depravity (raise them further than you), external culture (self-centered manhood), and time (finite formative years). Proximity is not parenting (sidelines, car, video games). Kids' activities are a market; smartphones are sold as safety. Volleyball with his D1 daughter was bounded by church, family, and not attending every tournament.
- Dig-set-spike. Dig = father's character. Tone sets the family atmosphere (chilly, combative, or boring usually tracks dad). If home is boring next to career or golf, sons will leave. Love spoken, time enjoyed, affection toward wife creates warmth. Sons drink dad's aspirations (athletics, money, worship criticism, Wall Street vs Bible). Hawks' dad: State Farm salesman, Bible open every morning. Father power: kids come out loving dad; use it early with time and "doing him," not only his activities. Neglected, love becomes pain, then teenage anger. Plan so you do not neglect by accident.
- "Catch of a Lifetime" (Reader's Digest): 11-year-old hooks a huge bass two hours before season; dad makes him release it. Son, 34 years later an architect, still uses that ethics lesson on estimates and corners. Little-thing honesty is how big-thing honesty is formed.
- Set = father's training and instruction. Three treasures: (1) manhood vision: reject passivity, accept responsibility, lead courageously, expect God's reward; (2) code of conduct tied to the family name (four to six values: courage, faith, obedience, honesty, hard work, relational integrity). Anchor each in Scripture so kids obey God, not only dad. 1950s kept practices after dropping the Bible; 1960s asked why keep them. Real Family Values series (1998) on the church player. Prov 4: David teaching Solomon wisdom; almost none of us heard this from dad; we are called to give it; (3) transcendent cause: if vision never exceeds the self you become small. Seek first the kingdom and his righteousness.
- Spike = father's ceremonies. We ceremony what we think matters (graduation, wedding, funeral) but not manhood. Gangs, hazing, and frat rites fill the vacuum. Hawks' dad on a pay phone in Oklahoma: "I trust you" after oil-rig work; he heard "you are a man." You can do better. Robert Lewis, Raising a Modern-Day Knight (knight process). Memorable ceremonies: costly (time, effort, other men); ascribe value; employ symbols (rings, diplomas, swords, crests); empower a life with vision (life looked like this; now it looks like that). Example: five dads, sons at 13, camp with grandfathers; north star = Bible; backpacks filled with rocks for rising responsibility, then dads ran beside them: we will help you, do not go alone. Lewis stages on the outline (page/squire/knight/roundtable); church offers Journey to Manhood (Nate closes). Book still helps teens, college, pre-marriage.
