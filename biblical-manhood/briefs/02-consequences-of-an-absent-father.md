# 02 Consequences of an Absent Father

- title: Consequences of an Absent Father
- speaker: Tim Hawks
- date: 2025-09-19
- source: faster-whisper small.en from local audio (no YouTube caption track)

## Core claims
- First masculinity wound is the father wound: an ongoing emotional, social, or spiritual deficit ordinarily met in a healthy relationship with dad that must now be overcome by other means. It does not expire at age 18.
- "Absent" also means distant: physically gone, emotionally checked out, or chemically blurred. Something is missing between father's heart/mind and son's: love, blessing, and communication of what it means to be masculine.
- Results of the wound: (1) Anger expresses the pain. Boys start with dad as north star; repeated rejection becomes hurt, then anger. Some can name the day they stopped seeking dad's approval. (2) Addictions and obsessions suppress or numb the pain (alcohol, porn, obsessive exercise, video games as adult escape, workaholism). (3) Lostness: listlessness, indecisiveness, experiencing everything but convicted by nothing. No inherited masculine convictions to die for.
- Levels of wounding differ. Tim's dad, Richard Fields Hawks: consistent (provider, present, no divorce talk), walled off (touch as hitting, sarcasm instead of "I love you"), respected (county commissioner, deacon chair) but hard to access. Grandfather was an alcoholic who fathered three families. Tim left with both deficits and positives.
- Workaholism gets praised even through wrecked marriages. Masculinity is a paycheck, but more than a paycheck. Ted Turner at a graduation, Success magazine cover: "Dad, is this enough for you?"
- Every son needs from dad: lifelong positive memories to draw on in failure; direction with solid why answers (how to think, decide, reason); conviction through modeling (walk matches talk); his father's heart (love, affirmation, blessing). If Jesus needed the Father to speak those words into adult life, so do you.

## Scripture
- Ephesians 6:4. Fathers, do not exasperate your children (slow simmer that boils over through neglect or abuse). Bring them up in the teaching and nurture of the Lord.
- Matthew 3:16-17. Jesus' baptism: "This is my son" (affirmation), "the one I love," "in him I am well pleased" (blessing).
