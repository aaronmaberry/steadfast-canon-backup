# 22 A Man and His Life Journey

- title: A Man and His Life Journey
- speaker: Tim Hawks
- date: Apr 15, 2026
- source: transcript (YouTube ASR captions)

## Core claims
- Recite: a real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects a greater reward, God's reward. Men do not refuse directions; they refuse to stop once moving. Younger men: this will feel less relevant and help more.
- Life-cycle map: Daniel Levinson, Seasons of a Man's Life (1978); 40 men, 10 years. Hawks still finds it valid. Spring 0-22 childhood/adolescence (he would move the top to 25 because of delayed adulthood); summer 17-45 early adulthood (he would start ~21); fall 40-65 middle adulthood; winter 60+ late adulthood. Transitions are where men read the map and shape the rest.
- Spring question: who am I? Body, family, place, spiritual roots. Trigger into adulthood: I cannot keep living like this. Bankrolled sons delay it. Dads: work, earn, talk money and college on merit early; do not underwrite a school you cannot afford.
- Summer question: what will I do? Work, marriage, family. Crisis when the job fights your wiring (Jay last week). Ability to change tracks is capped by appetite and debt / a wife's upscale expectations.
- Fall question: is that all there is? Midlife. Blame circumstances (new wife, new career, sail the world) or ask whether you seized a masculine calling deeper than getting established. Men become more relationally sensitive: how kids and wife see you starts to matter more.
- Winter question: what do I do with the rest of my life? Youth culture moves you off center stage. Elevate your game (mentor, invest) or descend into selfishness (shells, golf, TV). Decision affects legacy (next week) and length of life; many men die within a couple of years of retirement without a plan to keep going.
- Life-stage map: Robert Hicks, The Masculine Journey (Hebrew words for man); Robert Lewis added ages. Fail a stage's challenge and you still advance, carrying it; stacked challenges crater a life.
- Adam, creation, 0-20. Challenge: will I accept who God created me to be? Ps 139:13-16; Eph 2:10. Parents: what does it mean to be me, how should it be used, for whom? Do not tell kids they can be anything (daughter could not dunk; millennials disappointed they are not Zuckerberg).
- Zakar, phallic, 13-25. Challenge: will I master my sexual drive or will it master me? Culture lies that you cannot (sex sells). Hicks: men in their 50s-60s still living below the belt. Porn: researchers could not find daily-abstaining teens; ED in 30s from overstimulation; 95k-man Reddit quitting porn for relational reasons, not morals. Addiction: the mind can restart the chemical. 1 Thess 4:3-8: learn to control your body. Seminary prof prayed with an 80-year-old asking not to become a dirty old man. Filters, skip ads, Celebrate Recovery; it will not fade on its own.
- Gibbor, warrior, 20-40. Prov 20:29: glory of young men is their strength. Challenge: will I develop a roadmap? Life in the fight; competition, little comradeship. Napoleon to Moscow without a plan home; Alexander at 32 with no kingdoms left. Football concussion story: playing so hard you do not feel the wounds (marriage, kids, partners) you are picking up.
- Enosh, wounded, 40-50. Challenge: will I change or live in Woundedville? Re-evaluate priorities, heal marriage and kids, or keep plowing.
- Ish, mature, 50-60. If prior challenges are faced: less insecure, relationships steadier, sex drive governed, deeper meaning. Read Ps 23 as David here. Strategic mentoring, shape for sage.
- Zaken, sage, 60+. Prov 20:29: gray hair the splendor of the old. Standard-bearers. Moses began at 80; Abraham at 75. Letter to Lewis from Isaac Block, 91, care home, diabetes and heart trouble, writing a book for grandchildren on the Christian life; 13 children, all in Christ, many in ministry. That is the aim. Legacy next week.
