## Core claims
- Manhood is in a state of confusion. Since WWII, culture has feminized masculinity. God gave male and female different operating systems that match their physiology. Men do not have a "feminine side"; chasing one produces something weird, not male or female.
- Society: no American rite of passage. Until the industrial revolution, boys learned manhood beside fathers. Factory hours pulled dad away; boys were then socialized mainly by women (school). Spiritual revolution: late 1800s West dropped revealed truth for opinion. 1960s-70s cultural/feminist turn treated gender as a construct ("only how they are raised"). Brain research in the 80s-90s showed different wiring; culture still denies it. Hawks cites Target 2015 boy/girl aisles as the live experiment.
- Family dynamics shape the definition: patriarchy, matriarchy, divorce, abuse.
- Dad himself: Newsweek "Dad is Destiny"; biological father is the largest factor in a child's social, spiritual, and intellectual outcomes. 40% of US boys raised without their biological father. "The Trouble with Boys": a reliable predictor of high-school success is a man to look up to; a boy without a father figure is an explorer without a map. True for girls too; they learn femininity by playing off dad's masculinity.
- Confused men create major problems. Garrison Keillor: manhood treated as a problem to overcome. Time: "Are Men Really That Bad?" Margaret Mead: the central problem of every society is to define appropriate roles for men. Men are wired with destiny; unused, masculine energy turns inward. 81% of US violent crime and 63% of property crime committed by men.
- Confused men settle for less. Conventional model: a man's one purpose is to earn a living. Father-in-law retired, bought a fishing boat he barely used, ended on daytime TV watching a stock ticker. Generations: builders work; boomers work and play; millennials work to play. Delayed manhood: gap years into the 30s; wives calling about 32-year-olds on World of Warcraft.
- No lofty vision of manhood. 1 Cor 13:11: when I became a man I put childish ways behind me. Men describe manhood as progress (love a woman, father, family name, ministry) or regress (frat stories, past athletic glory). Robert Lewis of Fellowship Bible Church originated the study (credit given; Raising a Modern-Day Knight). Seminar: nearly all had taught sons baseball and school help; few how to treat a woman; none a noble definition of masculinity.
- The Bible has insight. There is no book of manhood; the story assumes it. Non-Bible hearers: ask whether it corresponds to reality.
- Manhood develops by addressing unfinished past (men delay; wounds leak like a nuclear-waste canister into grumpiness, laziness, addiction, broken relationships; next week: dad) and a written personal manhood plan in semester two (hand Tim a plan to graduate).
